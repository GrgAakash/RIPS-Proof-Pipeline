2. Subproof or failure

This is a conditional assembly proof from the declared prerequisites. The S0 blueprint is not used as proof of any mathematical claim.

Use the standard convention that an eigenfunction is nonzero. If the zero function were counted as an eigenfunction, the target theorem’s final clause would be literally false, since the zero function has empty finite support.

Let `(G,o)`, `D`, and `lambda` be the objects supplied by SC1 and SC3, and assume SC1-SC5 hold for this same construction.

By SC1, `(G,o)` is unimodular and has finite expected degree. By SC2, `(G,o)` is amenable. By SC3, `D` is a finite-range operator on `G`, and `lambda in C` is the chosen eigenvalue. By SC4, the von Neumann dimension of the `lambda`-eigenspace is strictly positive:
`dim_G ker(D-lambda) > 0`.

It remains only to check the almost-sure no-finite-support conclusion. Let `Omega_*` be the probability-one event on which the constructed network/operator satisfy the almost-sure structural conclusions of SC1-SC3 and the propagation conclusion SC5. Since this is a finite intersection of probability-one events, `P(Omega_*) = 1`.

Fix a realization in `Omega_*`, and let `f` be a nonzero `lambda`-eigenfunction of `D`. Suppose for contradiction that `supp(f)` is finite. Then the set of spine positions touched by `supp(f)` is finite. But SC5 says that every nonzero `lambda`-eigenfunction propagates infinitely in at least one spine direction; equivalently, its support contains vertices in infinitely many spine positions, or at least infinitely many distinct vertices along that direction. This contradicts finiteness of `supp(f)`. Hence no nonzero `lambda`-eigenfunction has finite support on this realization.

Since the realization was arbitrary in the probability-one event `Omega_*`, almost surely every `lambda`-eigenfunction, in the standard nonzero sense, has infinite support. Therefore, conditional on SC1-SC5 for a common construction, the target theorem follows exactly.

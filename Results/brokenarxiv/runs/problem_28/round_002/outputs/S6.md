We prove the theorem with \(\lambda=0\).

1. Fix an integer \(d\ge 4\), and let \(T_d\) be the \(d\)-regular tree rooted at \(o\). Perform Bernoulli bond percolation on \(T_d\) with parameter \(p\in(0,1)\). Write \(C_o\) for the open cluster of \(o\), and condition on the event \(I=\{|C_o|=\infty\}\). For \(p>1/(d-1)\), the usual forward-branch Galton-Watson comparison has offspring law \(\operatorname{Bin}(d-1,p)\), hence survives with positive probability. Thus \(\mathbb P(I)>0\), and the conditioned rooted cluster
\[
(C,o):=(C_o,o)\mid I
\]
is well defined. Since \(C\) is a connected subgraph of a tree, it is almost surely an infinite tree, and \(\deg_C(v)\le d\) for every vertex.

2. The rooted tree \((C,o)\) is unimodular. Indeed, for any nonnegative equivariant mass transport \(F\) on rooted clusters, define on the original percolation configuration
\[
\widehat F(\omega,x,y)
=
{\bf 1}_{\{x\leftrightarrow y\}}
{\bf 1}_{\{|C_\omega(x)|=\infty\}}
F(C_\omega(x),x,y).
\]
The Bernoulli percolation law on the transitive tree \(T_d\) is invariant and satisfies the mass transport principle. Applying it to \(\widehat F\), then dividing by \(\mathbb P(I)\), gives
\[
\mathbb E\sum_y F(C,o,y)=\mathbb E\sum_x F(C,x,o),
\]
which is unimodularity of \((C,o)\).

3. We choose \(p<1\) close enough to \(1\) so that
\[
m:=\mathbb E[\deg_C(o)]>2.
\]
To see this, let \(q(p)\) be the extinction probability of a forward open branch, so
\[
q=(1-p+pq)^{d-1}.
\]
For \(p\uparrow 1\), \(q(p)\to 0\). If
\[
\alpha(p)=p(1-q(p)),
\]
then the number \(M\) of infinite open branches incident to \(o\) has law \(\operatorname{Bin}(d,\alpha(p))\), and \(I=\{M\ge 1\}\). Since \(\deg_C(o)\ge M\),
\[
\mathbb E[\deg_C(o)\mid I]
\ge
\frac{d\alpha(p)}{1-(1-\alpha(p))^d}
\longrightarrow d
\quad\text{as }p\uparrow 1.
\]
Because \(d\ge4\), we may fix \(p<1\) sufficiently close to \(1\) so that \(m>2\).

4. The tree \(C\) is amenable almost surely. Fix \(L\ge1\). Since \(p<1\), a prescribed forward side branch can realize an open path of length \(L\) with the necessary adjacent boundary edges closed with some positive probability \(q_L>0\). Conditional on \(C\) being infinite, along an infinite ray in \(C\) there are infinitely many independent side branches. Hence with probability one, at least one such branch realizes this finite pendant path pattern, and in fact this holds for every \(L\). A pendant path of length \(L\) gives a finite vertex set \(F_L\subseteq V(C)\) with \(|F_L|\ge L\) and external edge boundary at most \(1\). Thus
\[
\frac{|\partial F_L|}{|F_L|}\le \frac1L,
\]
and the Folner criterion gives amenability of \(C\).

5. Let \(B(C)\) be the barycentric subdivision of \(C\). Thus \(V(B(C))\) consists of the old vertices \(V(C)\) and one new edge-vertex \(e^*\) for each edge \(e\in E(C)\), with \(e^*\) adjacent to the two endpoints of \(e\). Since old degrees are at most \(d\) and edge-vertices have degree \(2\), \(B(C)\) has bounded degree. Amenability passes from \(C\) to \(B(C)\): given Folner sets \(F_n\subseteq V(C)\), take the old vertices in \(F_n\) together with edge-vertices corresponding to edges internal to \(F_n\). The new boundary is bounded by a constant depending only on \(d\) times the old boundary, while the volume is at least \(|F_n|\). Hence \(B(C)\) is amenable.

6. We root \(B(C)\) by the old/edge Palm mixture
\[
\mathbb E_B h(B(C),\rho)
=
\frac{1}{1+m/2}\,
\mathbb E\left[
h(B(C),o)
+
\frac12\sum_{e\sim o}h(B(C),e^*)
\right].
\]
Equivalently, the old-root intensity is \(1\), the edge-root intensity is \(m/2\), and the total vertex intensity is \(1+m/2\).

7. This rooted network \((B(C),\rho)\) is unimodular. Define
\[
k_C(x,u)=
\begin{cases}
1, & u=x\in V(C),\\
1/2, & u=e^*\text{ for an edge }e\text{ incident to }x,\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
For every \(u\in V(B(C))\), \(\sum_{x\in V(C)}k_C(x,u)=1\). For a nonnegative equivariant transport \(F\) on \(B(C)\), set
\[
M(C,x,y)=\sum_{u,v}k_C(x,u)k_C(y,v)F(B(C),u,v).
\]
This is an equivariant nonnegative transport on \(C\). Applying unimodularity of \(C\) to \(M\), and then using the allocation identity \(\sum_x k_C(x,u)=1\), yields the mass transport principle for \((B(C),\rho)\). Hence \(B(C)\) with this root law is unimodular. Its expected degree is finite because the degree is bounded by \(d\).

8. Independently orient every edge \(e\in E(C)\). Let \(t(e)\) and \(h(e)\) denote the tail and head. Every function \(f\) on \(V(B(C))\) decomposes as \(f=(x,a)\), where \(x\) is defined on old vertices \(V(C)\) and \(a\) is defined on edge-vertices, identified with \(E(C)\). Define \(D\) by
\[
(Df)(v)
=
\sum_{h(e)=v}a(e)-\sum_{t(e)=v}a(e),
\qquad v\in V(C),
\]
and
\[
(Df)(e^*)=x(t(e))+i\,x(h(e)),
\qquad e\in E(C).
\]
This is a finite-range operator of range \(1\) on \(B(C)\), with uniformly bounded coefficients.

9. [KEY STEP] We show that the von Neumann dimension of the \(\ell^2\)-kernel of \(D\) is positive. Let \(\partial\) denote the divergence operator
\[
(\partial a)(v)=\sum_{h(e)=v}a(e)-\sum_{t(e)=v}a(e),
\]
and let \(R\) denote
\[
(Rx)(e)=x(t(e))+i\,x(h(e)).
\]
Then \(D(x,a)=(\partial a,Rx)\). If \(D(x,a)=0\) in \(\ell^2\), then \(x(t(e))+i x(h(e))=0\) for every edge \(e\). Hence \(|x|\) is constant along every edge of the connected infinite tree \(C\). Since \(x\in \ell^2(V(C))\), this constant must be \(0\), so \(x=0\). Therefore
\[
\ker_{\ell^2}D=\{0\}\oplus \ker_{\ell^2}\partial.
\]

The adjoint of \(\partial\) is the gradient
\[
(\partial^*y)(e)=y(h(e))-y(t(e)).
\]
If \(\partial^*y=0\), then \(y\) is constant on the connected infinite tree \(C\), and since \(y\in\ell^2(V(C))\), this constant is \(0\). Thus \(\ker \partial^*=0\).

With the old-vertex normalization on \(C\), the von Neumann dimension of \(\ell^2(V(C))\) is \(1\), and the dimension of \(\ell^2(E(C))\) is \(m/2\), because each edge is incident to two vertices and the expected degree is \(m\). By the Hilbert-module rank-nullity identity for bounded equivariant operators,
\[
\dim_C \overline{\operatorname{im}\partial^*}
=
\dim_C \ell^2(V(C))-\dim_C\ker\partial^*
=
1.
\]
Also,
\[
\ell^2(E(C))
=
\ker\partial\oplus \overline{\operatorname{im}\partial^*}
\]
orthogonally. Hence
\[
\dim_C\ker\partial
=
\dim_C\ell^2(E(C))
-
\dim_C\overline{\operatorname{im}\partial^*}
=
\frac m2-1>0.
\]
Passing to the barycentric root law divides by the total vertex intensity \(1+m/2\). The projection onto \(\ker D\) is zero on old vertices and equals the projection onto \(\ker\partial\) on edge-vertices. Therefore
\[
\dim_B\ker D
=
\frac{m/2-1}{1+m/2}
>0.
\]
Thus the \(0\)-eigenspace of \(D\) has strictly positive von Neumann dimension.

10. Finally, we prove that almost surely no nonzero \(0\)-eigenfunction of \(D\) has finite support. Let \(f=(x,a)\) be any formal solution of \(Df=0\), without assuming \(f\in\ell^2\). For every oriented edge \(e:u\to v\),
\[
x(u)+i x(v)=0,
\]
so \(x(v)=i x(u)\). Thus \(x(u)=0\) if and only if \(x(v)=0\). Since \(C\) is connected, either \(x\equiv0\), or \(x\) is nonzero at every old vertex. In the latter case the support is infinite because \(C\) is infinite.

It remains to consider \(x\equiv0\). Then \(\partial a(v)=0\) at every old vertex. Suppose \(a\) is nonzero and finitely supported. The finite subgraph of the tree \(C\) spanned by the edges on which \(a\ne0\) is a nonempty finite forest, hence has a leaf \(w\). At \(w\), exactly one incident edge \(e\) has \(a(e)\ne0\), so
\[
(\partial a)(w)=\pm a(e)\ne0,
\]
contradicting \(\partial a(w)=0\). Therefore \(a\) is either zero or has infinite support. Hence every nonzero formal \(0\)-eigenfunction of \(D\) has infinite support.

11. We have constructed an amenable unimodular random rooted network \((B(C),\rho)\) with finite expected degree and a finite-range operator \(D\), such that the von Neumann dimension of the \(0\)-eigenspace of \(D\) is strictly positive, while almost surely every nonzero \(0\)-eigenfunction has infinite support. This proves the theorem, with \(\lambda=0\in\mathbb C\).

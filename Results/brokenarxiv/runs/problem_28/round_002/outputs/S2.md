2. Subproof or failure

Let \(T_d\) be the \(d\)-regular tree, \(d\ge 3\), and perform Bernoulli bond percolation with parameter \(p\in(0,1)\). Let \(C\) be the open cluster of the root \(o\), conditioned on being infinite. Since \(C\) is a connected subgraph of a tree, \(C\) is itself a tree.

I will prove that under this conditioning, \(C\) almost surely has pendant paths of arbitrarily large length.

Root \(T_d\) at \(o\). For an oriented edge from a vertex to one of its descendants, the open descendants beyond that edge form an independent Galton-Watson percolation subtree: the edge must be open, and then each forward child edge is independently open with probability \(p\). Since the percolation is supercritical in the intended setting, the probability that such a forward subtree is infinite is some \(s>0\), and the probability that it is finite but nonempty with a prescribed finite shape is positive whenever that shape uses finitely many open edges and finitely many closed boundary edges.

Fix \(L\ge 1\). Consider the finite rooted pattern \(P_L\) consisting of a simple open path of length \(L\) starting from a vertex \(v\), with no additional open side edges at any vertex of that path, and with the terminal vertex having no open forward children. This is a finite pendant path of length \(L\). The probability that a given forward branch realizes exactly \(P_L\) is positive, because it requires finitely many specified edges to be open and finitely many specified edges to be closed, and \(0<p<1\).

Now condition on \(C\) being infinite. The cluster \(C\) contains at least one infinite ray from \(o\). Along that ray there are infinitely many vertices. At each such vertex, aside from the edge continuing along the ray and the edge back toward the root, there are finitely many side branches. For \(d\ge 3\), there is at least one side branch at infinitely many vertices. The percolation configurations in distinct side branches based at distinct vertices of the ray are independent. For each fixed \(L\), each such side branch has the same positive probability \(q_L>0\) of realizing the pendant path pattern \(P_L\). Hence, by independence, the probability that none of these infinitely many side branches realizes \(P_L\) is
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}(1-q_L)^n=0.
\]
Therefore, for each fixed \(L\), \(C\) almost surely contains a pendant path of length \(L\). Taking the intersection over all \(L\in\mathbb N\), which is a countable intersection of probability-one events, \(C\) almost surely contains pendant paths of arbitrarily large length.

It remains to show that this implies amenability. Use the standard graph-theoretic definition: an infinite locally finite graph \(G\) is amenable if there are finite nonempty vertex sets \(F_n\) such that
\[
\frac{|\partial F_n|}{|F_n|}\to 0,
\]
where \(\partial F_n\) is the vertex boundary outside \(F_n\).

If \(C\) has a pendant path of length \(L\), take \(F_L\) to be the vertices of that pendant path, excluding its attachment vertex if desired. Then \(F_L\) has at least \(L\) vertices, and its external vertex boundary has size at most \(1\), namely the attachment point to the rest of the tree. Thus
\[
\frac{|\partial F_L|}{|F_L|}\le \frac{1}{L}.
\]
Since such pendant paths exist for arbitrarily large \(L\), \(C\) has a Følner sequence. Hence \(C\) is amenable almost surely.

Now consider the barycentric subdivision \(B(C)\), obtained by inserting one new vertex into every edge of \(C\). If \(C\) is amenable, then \(B(C)\) is amenable. Indeed, let \(F_n\subset V(C)\) be finite sets with \(|\partial_C F_n|/|F_n|\to 0\). Define \(\widetilde F_n\subset V(B(C))\) to contain all old vertices in \(F_n\) and every inserted edge-vertex corresponding to an edge of \(C\) with both endpoints in \(F_n\). Since \(C\) has degree at most \(d\), the boundary of \(\widetilde F_n\) in \(B(C)\) is bounded by a constant depending only on \(d\) times \(|\partial_C F_n|\), while \(|\widetilde F_n|\ge |F_n|\). Therefore
\[
\frac{|\partial_{B(C)}\widetilde F_n|}{|\widetilde F_n|}
\le
K_d \frac{|\partial_C F_n|}{|F_n|}
\to 0.
\]
Thus \(B(C)\) is amenable.

Finally, \(C\) has maximum degree at most \(d\), since it is a subgraph of \(T_d\). In \(B(C)\), old vertices have the same degree as in \(C\), and inserted edge-vertices have degree exactly \(2\). Hence
\[
\deg_{B(C)}(v)\le \max\{d,2\}=d.
\]
Therefore \(B(C)\) has bounded degree, and in particular finite expected degree under any root law supported on \(B(C)\), including the old-vertex/edge-vertex Palm mixture used elsewhere in the construction.

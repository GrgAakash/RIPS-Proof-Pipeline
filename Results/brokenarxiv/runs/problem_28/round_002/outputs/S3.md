2. Subproof or failure

Let \(V(C)\) be the old vertex set and let \(E(C)\) be the original edge set. For \(e\in E(C)\), write \(e^\ast\) for the corresponding edge-vertex in the barycentric subdivision \(B(C)\). Thus
\[
V(B(C))=V(C)\sqcup \{e^\ast:e\in E(C)\},
\]
and \(x\in V(C)\) is adjacent in \(B(C)\) to \(e^\ast\) exactly when \(x\) is an endpoint of \(e\). Any original orientation mark of \(e\) is carried equivariantly to the corresponding local data at \(e^\ast\), so \(B(C)\) is a deterministic equivariant marked-network transform of \(C\).

Put
\[
c:=1+\frac{\delta}{2}.
\]
Define the root law of \((B(C),\rho)\) by the following expectation identity: for every nonnegative Borel test function \(h\) on rooted barycentric networks,
\[
\mathbb E_B[h(B(C),\rho)]
=
\frac{1}{c}\,
\mathbb E\left[
h(B(C),o)+\frac12\sum_{e\ni o}h(B(C),e^\ast)
\right].
\tag{1}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\mathbb P_B(\rho\text{ is old})=\frac{1}{c}=\frac{2}{\delta+2},
\qquad
\mathbb P_B(\rho\text{ is an edge-vertex})=\frac{\delta/2}{c}=\frac{\delta}{\delta+2}.
\]
Conditioned on \(\rho\) being old, \(\rho=o\). Conditioned on \(\rho\) being an edge-vertex, the rooted law is the edge Palm law
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathrm{edge}}[h]
=
\frac{1}{\delta}\,
\mathbb E\sum_{e\ni o}h(B(C),e^\ast),
\]
equivalently: degree-bias the old root, choose a uniformly random incident original edge, and root \(B(C)\) at its edge-vertex.

It remains to verify the mass transport principle for this law.

For old \(x\in V(C)\) and \(u\in V(B(C))\), define
\[
k_C(x,u)=
\begin{cases}
1, & u=x\in V(C),\\
\frac12, & u=e^\ast\text{ for some }e\in E(C)\text{ incident to }x,\\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
\sum_{u\in V(B(C))}k_C(x,u)=1+\frac{\deg_C(x)}2,
\]
and, crucially,
\[
\sum_{x\in V(C)}k_C(x,u)=1
\tag{2}
\]
for every \(u\in V(B(C))\): old vertices receive weight \(1\) from themselves, and edge-vertices receive weight \(1/2+1/2\) from their two endpoints.

Formula (1) can be rewritten as
\[
\mathbb E_B[h(B(C),\rho)]
=
\frac1c\,
\mathbb E\sum_{u\in V(B(C))}k_C(o,u)h(B(C),u).
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(F\) be any nonnegative Borel mass transport on doubly rooted barycentric networks. Write \(F(u,v)\) for \(F(B(C),u,v)\). By (3),
\[
c\,\mathbb E_B\sum_{v\in V(B(C))}F(\rho,v)
=
\mathbb E\sum_{u,v\in V(B(C))}k_C(o,u)F(u,v).
\tag{4}
\]
Using (2), the right side of (4) equals
\[
\mathbb E\sum_{y\in V(C)} M(C,o,y),
\]
where
\[
M(C,x,y):=
\sum_{u,v\in V(B(C))}
k_C(x,u)k_C(y,v)F(B(C),u,v).
\]
The function \(M\) is a nonnegative Borel mass transport on the original marked network \(C\), since \(B(C)\) and \(k_C\) are defined equivariantly. By unimodularity of \((C,o)\),
\[
\mathbb E\sum_{y\in V(C)}M(C,o,y)
=
\mathbb E\sum_{x\in V(C)}M(C,x,o).
\]
Expanding again and using (2),
\[
\mathbb E\sum_{x\in V(C)}M(C,x,o)
=
\mathbb E\sum_{u,v\in V(B(C))}k_C(o,v)F(u,v).
\]
By (3), this last expression is
\[
c\,\mathbb E_B\sum_{u\in V(B(C))}F(u,\rho).
\]
Thus
\[
\mathbb E_B\sum_v F(\rho,v)
=
\mathbb E_B\sum_u F(u,\rho),
\]
so \((B(C),\rho)\) satisfies the mass transport principle.

This proves C3, conditional only on the declared prerequisite that \((C,o)\) is unimodular with finite expected degree.

2. Subproof or failure

Let `C` be an infinite connected tree, with each original edge assigned an orientation. A function on `B(C)` decomposes as `f=(x,a)`, where `x` is defined on the old vertices `V(C)` and `a` is defined on the edge-vertices, equivalently on `E(C)`.

Assume `Df=0`. Then for each oriented edge `e` with tail `u` and head `v`, the edge-vertex equation is

`x(u) + i x(v) = 0`.

Thus `x(v)=i x(u)`. In particular, `x(u)=0` if and only if `x(v)=0`. Therefore vanishing of `x` propagates across every edge. Since `C` is connected, either `x` vanishes at every old vertex, or `x` vanishes at none of them. If `x` is not identically zero, then every old vertex belongs to `supp(x)`. Because `C` is infinite, `supp(x)` is infinite. Hence the old-vertex component either vanishes identically or has infinite support.

Now suppose `x=0`. Then the old-vertex equations of `Df=0` say precisely that

`div a(v)=0`

for every old vertex `v`. Thus `a` is a divergence-free flow on the oriented tree `C`.

It remains to prove that a nonzero divergence-free flow on a tree cannot have finite support. Suppose, toward contradiction, that `a` is divergence-free, nonzero, and has finite support. Let `F` be the finite set of original edges with `a(e) != 0`. Since `F` is finite and nonempty, the subgraph of `C` spanned by `F` is a finite forest with at least one edge. Every finite forest with at least one edge has a leaf vertex, meaning a vertex incident to exactly one edge of `F`. Choose such a leaf vertex `w`, and let `e` be the unique edge of `F` incident to `w`.

At `w`, all incident edges except `e` have flow value zero. Therefore `div a(w)` is either `a(e)` or `-a(e)`, depending on the orientation convention. Since `a(e) != 0`, this gives `div a(w) != 0`, contradicting divergence-freeness. Hence no nonzero divergence-free flow on a tree has finite support.

Combining the two cases: if `x` is nonzero, then `f` has infinite support because `x` has infinite support; if `x=0`, then `a` is divergence-free, and if `f` is nonzero then `a` is nonzero, so `a` has infinite support. Therefore every nonzero formal `0`-eigenfunction of `D` on `B(C)` has infinite support.

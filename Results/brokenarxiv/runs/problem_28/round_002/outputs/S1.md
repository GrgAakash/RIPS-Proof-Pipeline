2. Subproof or failure

Fix an integer \(d\ge 4\), and let \(T_d\) be the infinite \(d\)-regular tree with distinguished root \(o\). For \(p\in(0,1)\), perform independent Bernoulli bond percolation on \(T_d\): each edge is declared open with probability \(p\), independently. Let \(C_o\) be the open cluster of \(o\), and let
\[
I=\{|C_o|=\infty\}.
\]
For the values of \(p\) chosen below, \(\mathbf P_p(I)>0\), so we define \(C\) to be the random rooted network \((C_o,o)\) under the conditional law \(\mathbf P_p(\,\cdot\,|I)\).

First, \(\mathbf P_p(I)>0\) for \(p\) close enough to \(1\). Away from the root, each forward branch is a Galton-Watson process with offspring distribution \(\operatorname{Bin}(d-1,p)\), whose mean is \((d-1)p\). If \(p>1/(d-1)\), this process is supercritical, so its survival probability is positive. Since \(d\ge4\), such \(p<1\) exist. Therefore the root has positive probability to connect to at least one surviving forward branch, and \(\mathbf P_p(I)>0\).

The cluster \(C_o\) is always a connected subgraph of the tree \(T_d\), hence itself a tree. Under the conditioning \(I\), it is infinite. Also every vertex degree in \(C_o\) is at most \(d\), because \(C_o\subseteq T_d\). Thus \(C\) has bounded degree, in particular finite expected root degree.

Now prove unimodularity under the conditioning. Let \(F(G,x,y)\ge0\) be any nonnegative measurable mass transport on doubly rooted connected locally finite networks. For a percolation configuration \(\omega\) on \(T_d\), define
\[
\widehat F(\omega,x,y)
=
\mathbf 1_{\{x\leftrightarrow_\omega y\}}
\mathbf 1_{\{|C_\omega(x)|=\infty\}}
F(C_\omega(x),x,y),
\]
where \(C_\omega(x)\) is the open cluster of \(x\). This is an automorphism-equivariant nonnegative mass transport on the invariant percolated \(d\)-regular tree. Since \(T_d\) is transitive and unimodular, the mass transport principle gives
\[
\mathbf E_p\sum_{y\in T_d}\widehat F(\omega,o,y)
=
\mathbf E_p\sum_{x\in T_d}\widehat F(\omega,x,o).
\]
The left side is
\[
\mathbf E_p\left[\mathbf 1_I\sum_{y\in C_o}F(C_o,o,y)\right].
\]
On the right side, if \(x\leftrightarrow_\omega o\), then \(C_\omega(x)=C_o\) and \(|C_\omega(x)|=\infty\) iff \(I\) occurs, so the right side is
\[
\mathbf E_p\left[\mathbf 1_I\sum_{x\in C_o}F(C_o,x,o)\right].
\]
Since \(\mathbf P_p(I)>0\), division by \(\mathbf P_p(I)\) yields
\[
\mathbf E_p\left[\sum_{y\in C_o}F(C_o,o,y)\,\middle|\,I\right]
=
\mathbf E_p\left[\sum_{x\in C_o}F(C_o,x,o)\,\middle|\,I\right].
\]
This is precisely the mass transport principle for the conditional rooted cluster \(C\). Hence \(C\) is unimodular.

It remains to choose \(p<1\) so that \(\mathbf E[\deg_C(o)]>2\). Let \(q(p)\) be the extinction probability of a forward Galton-Watson branch with offspring distribution \(\operatorname{Bin}(d-1,p)\). It is the smallest solution of
\[
q=(1-p+pq)^{d-1}.
\]
For any \(\varepsilon>0\), if \(p\) is sufficiently close to \(1\), then
\[
(1-p+p\varepsilon)^{d-1}<\varepsilon.
\]
Since the fixed-point map is continuous, increasing, and positive at \(0\), it has a fixed point below \(\varepsilon\), so the smallest fixed point satisfies \(q(p)<\varepsilon\). Thus \(q(p)\to0\) as \(p\uparrow1\).

For each neighbor branch of \(o\), the probability that the root edge is open and that the forward branch survives is
\[
\alpha(p)=p(1-q(p)).
\]
Hence \(\alpha(p)\to1\) as \(p\uparrow1\). Let \(M\) be the number of infinite open branches incident to \(o\). Then \(M\sim\operatorname{Bin}(d,\alpha(p))\), and \(I=\{M\ge1\}\). Since every infinite branch incident to \(o\) contributes one open edge at \(o\),
\[
\deg_C(o)\ge M.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbf E[\deg_C(o)\mid I]
\ge
\mathbf E[M\mid M\ge1]
=
\frac{d\alpha(p)}{1-(1-\alpha(p))^d}.
\]
As \(p\uparrow1\), the right side tends to \(d\). Since \(d\ge4\), this limit is strictly larger than \(2\). Hence there exists \(p<1\), sufficiently close to \(1\), such that
\[
\mathbf E[\deg_C(o)]>2
\]
under the conditional law of \(C\).

This proves C1.

## 1. Assignment Restatement

For fixed `n >= 1`, prove JEP for `K_p^n`: the class of finite two-sorted ultrametric spaces equipped with `n` partial automorphisms, with embeddings preserving the underlying dc-structure and extending each partial automorphism.

Given two finite systems  
`(A, p_1, ..., p_n)` and `(C, q_1, ..., q_n)`, construct a finite system `(E, r_1, ..., r_n)` into which both embed.

## 2. Subproof Or Failure

Let

`A = (X_A, D_A, d_A)` and `C = (X_C, D_C, d_C)`

be finite two-sorted ultrametric spaces, each equipped with `n` partial automorphisms

`p_i` on `A`, and `q_i` on `C`.

A partial automorphism here means a finite partial isomorphism of the two-sorted structure: it has a point-sort partial bijection and a distance-sort partial order-isomorphism, preserves `0`, and preserves distances wherever both relevant point arguments are in its domain.

First replace `A` and `C` by isomorphic copies so that their point sets are disjoint and their distance sets are disjoint except for the common distinguished element `0`. This does not change the JEP problem.

Construct a common finite dc-extension `E`.

Let `D_E` be any finite linear order obtained from the disjoint union of `D_A` and `D_C` over `0`, preserving the given orders on `D_A` and `D_C`, and then adjoining one new greatest element `M`. Thus `M` is larger than every element coming from either `D_A` or `D_C`.

Let

`X_E = X_A disjoint union X_C`.

Define `d_E` as follows:

- if `x,y in X_A`, set `d_E(x,y) = d_A(x,y)`;
- if `x,y in X_C`, set `d_E(x,y) = d_C(x,y)`;
- if `x in X_A` and `y in X_C`, set `d_E(x,y) = M`.

Then `E = (X_E, D_E, d_E)` is a finite two-sorted ultrametric space.

Indeed, symmetry and separation are immediate. For the ultrametric inequality, triples entirely inside `A` or entirely inside `C` are handled by the original ultrametrics. If a triple uses points from both sides, at least two of the three pairwise distances are `M`, so for any ordering of the triple,

`d_E(x,z) <= max{d_E(x,y), d_E(y,z)}`

holds automatically.

The inclusions of `A` and `C` into `E`, together with the corresponding order-preserving inclusions of `D_A` and `D_C` into `D_E`, are dc-embeddings.

Now define partial automorphisms `r_i` on `E`.

For each `i <= n`, let `r_i` be the union of the partial automorphism `p_i` on the copy of `A` and the partial automorphism `q_i` on the copy of `C`, on both sorts.

This union is well-defined because the two systems were made disjoint except for `0`, and all partial automorphisms preserve `0`.

On the point sort, `r_i` is a partial bijection because it is the disjoint union of two partial bijections. On the distance sort, `r_i` is a partial order-isomorphism: its restriction to `D_A` is the distance-sort part of `p_i`, its restriction to `D_C` is the distance-sort part of `q_i`, and the domains/ranges are disjoint except at `0`.

It remains to check that `r_i` preserves distances wherever required.

If both points lie in `A`, this follows from `p_i` being a partial automorphism of `A`. If both points lie in `C`, it follows from `q_i`. If one point lies in `A` and the other in `C`, their distance in `E` is `M`. Since neither `p_i` nor `q_i` maps points across the two sides, their images still lie on opposite sides, so the image distance is again `M`. Also `M` need not be in the domain of the distance-sort map of `r_i`; preservation is only required where the relevant distance value is in the induced distance data of the partial isomorphism. If desired, one may explicitly add `M -> M` to every `r_i` on the distance sort; this remains an order-preserving partial automorphism because `M` is greatest.

Thus each `r_i` is a partial automorphism of `E`, and both original systems embed into `(E, r_1, ..., r_n)`.

Therefore `K_p^n` has JEP.

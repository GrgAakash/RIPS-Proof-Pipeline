## 1 Assignment Restatement

S4 proves only the finite distance-sort orbit-interface lemma needed later for WAP. The task is not to prove WAP for the whole class, but to isolate the finite ordered-distance construction that lets two extensions of a witness `B` be represented in one finite ordered distance sort, even when the partial automorphisms move distance values.

## 2 Subproof Or Failure

Let `I` be the protected distance interface supplied by the WAP witness `B`. For this lemma, the needed protection is the following finite property.

A pair of finite distance-sort extensions `D^0,D^1` of `I`, with partial increasing injections

\[
\rho_i^\epsilon:D^\epsilon \rightharpoonup D^\epsilon
\qquad (i<n,\ \epsilon\in\{0,1\}),
\]

has a protected orbit-interface over `I` if there is:

- a finite linearly ordered set of cells `Ω`,
- cell maps `κ_ε:D^ε→Ω`,
- partial increasing injections `\bar\rho_i:Ω\rightharpoonup Ω`,

such that:

1. `κ_ε` records order cuts over the interface: if `κ_ε(a)<κ_ε(b)` then `a<b`, and if `a<b` then `κ_ε(a)≤κ_ε(b)`.
2. Interface elements are represented identically in both extensions.
3. Whenever `ρ_i^ε(x)` is defined,

\[
κ_ε(\rho_i^\epsilon(x))=\bar\rho_i(κ_ε(x)).
\]

4. If two values from the two extensions are forced to be the same by an interface orbit, then their images and preimages under every `ρ_i` are also forced to agree whenever both are defined.
5. Inside each cell, the finite order constraints inherited from `D^0` and `D^1`, after these forced identifications, are jointly acyclic and are respected by the induced maps.

Now form a quotient of the disjoint union

\[
D^0\sqcup D^1
\]

by the forced orbit-identifications over `I`. By condition 4, each `ρ_i^0∪ρ_i^1` descends to a well-defined partial injection on the quotient. Order the quotient first by its cell in `Ω`; inside each cell choose any finite linear extension of the inherited acyclic fiber order from condition 5. Call the resulting finite chain `D^*`.

Then `D^*` contains compatible copies of the two distance sorts over the protected interface. Each induced map

\[
\rho_i^*:D^*\rightharpoonup D^*
\]

is order-preserving: if `x<y`, either their cells are strictly ordered, in which case monotonicity of `\barρ_i` gives `ρ_i^*(x)<ρ_i^*(y)`, or they lie in the same cell, where condition 5 gives the same conclusion. Thus every distance-sort partial map from both extensions is coherently represented in one finite ordered distance sort.

This handles moved distance-sort elements because no map is required to be total or fixed on the finite chain; it is enough that movement is tracked by the finite cell maps `\barρ_i`.

For the cross-distance formula, suppose later point amalgamation defines

\[
\Delta(u,v)=\min_{b\in P}\max\{\alpha_b(u),\beta_b(v)\},
\]

where all displayed distance values lie in the domain of `ρ_i^*`. Since an order-preserving injection of a finite chain preserves finite minima and maxima on its domain,

\[
\rho_i^*(\Delta(u,v))
=
\min_{b\in P}\max\{\rho_i^*(\alpha_b(u)),\rho_i^*(\beta_b(v))\}.
\]

If the point-side partial map sends the index/interface data coherently, this equals `Δ(ρ_i u,ρ_i v)`. Hence the cross-distance formula is invariant even when `ρ_i^*` moves distance values.

The exact obstruction is finite and local: such an amalgam fails precisely when the protected interface forces a strict order cycle or reversal, i.e. some finite chain of required inequalities implies both `a<b` and `b≤a`, or some generator would have to send `a<b` to `ρ_i(b)<ρ_i(a)`. In that case no finite ordered distance-sort amalgam over that interface can exist.

## 1. Assignment Restatement

Prove only S4:

For fixed `n >= 1`, let `A` be an object of `K_p^n`, the finite two-sorted ultrametric structures equipped with `n` partial automorphisms. Assume each of the `n` partial automorphisms on `A` is total, hence an automorphism of `A`. Then `A` is a weak amalgamation base for `K_p^n`.

Prerequisite used explicitly: finite two-sorted ultrametric spaces with dc-embeddings have strong amalgamation, as in S1.

## 2. Subproof Or Failure

Let

`A = (X_A, D_A, d_A, g_1^A, ..., g_n^A)`

be an object of `K_p^n`, and assume each `g_i^A` is total. Thus each `g_i^A` consists of a bijection on `X_A` and an order-preserving bijection on `D_A` preserving `0`, with distances respected.

We show that `A` is a weak amalgamation base. It is enough to prove the following stronger statement: whenever `B` and `C` are two `K_p^n`-extensions of `A`, there is a finite object `E in K_p^n` and embeddings

`B -> E`, `C -> E`

whose restrictions to `A` agree and which commute with all `n` partial automorphisms.

Fix two such extensions `B` and `C`. Write their partial automorphisms as

`g_i^B`, `g_i^C`, for `i = 1, ..., n`.

Because `B` and `C` extend `A` in `K_p^n`, each `g_i^B` and `g_i^C` extends `g_i^A` wherever defined. Since `g_i^A` is total on `A`, the two partial maps agree on all point-elements and distance-elements of `A`.

By strong amalgamation for finite two-sorted ultrametric spaces with dc-embeddings, amalgamate the underlying two-sorted ultrametric spaces of `B` and `C` over `A`. Thus there is a finite two-sorted ultrametric space `E_0` and dc-embeddings

`e_B : B -> E_0`, `e_C : C -> E_0`

such that

`e_B|A = e_C|A`

and no point or distance element outside the common copy of `A` is identified unless forced by `A`.

It remains to put `n` partial automorphisms on `E_0`.

For each `i`, define a partial map `g_i^E` on the point sort by taking the union of the transported partial maps:

`g_i^E(e_B(x)) = e_B(g_i^B(x))` whenever `g_i^B(x)` is defined,

`g_i^E(e_C(y)) = e_C(g_i^C(y))` whenever `g_i^C(y)` is defined.

Define the distance-sort component similarly using the distance maps of the dc-embeddings.

This is well-defined. The only possible overlap between the images of `B` and `C` inside `E_0` is the common copy of `A`. On that overlap, `g_i^B` and `g_i^C` both restrict to the same total automorphism `g_i^A`. Hence the two definitions agree on points and distances belonging to `A`.

Each `g_i^E` is injective on its domain. Indeed, injectivity holds inside each side because `g_i^B` and `g_i^C` are partial automorphisms. If a value coming from the `B` side equals a value coming from the `C` side, strong amalgamation says that value lies in the common copy of `A`; since `g_i^A` is total and bijective on `A`, the corresponding preimages also lie in `A`, so the two preimages agree.

The same argument applies to the inverse partial maps, so `g_i^E` is a partial bijection between its domain and range on both sorts.

Now check preservation of structure. On pairs of points both from `B`, distance preservation follows from `g_i^B`; on pairs both from `C`, it follows from `g_i^C`. There is no additional requirement for mixed pairs unless both images and the relevant distance values are in the domain of the partial automorphism. But the distance-sort part of `g_i^E` was defined exactly as the union of the transported distance-sort maps, and these agree on the common copy of `A`. Since the underlying amalgam is by dc-embeddings, all distances already present in `B` or `C` are preserved by their respective maps, and newly created mixed distances in the amalgam are not forced into the finite domain of `g_i^E` except where explicitly included. Thus `g_i^E` is a valid partial automorphism of the two-sorted ultrametric space `E_0`.

Therefore

`E = (E_0, g_1^E, ..., g_n^E)`

is an object of `K_p^n`, and the embeddings `e_B` and `e_C` are embeddings in `K_p^n`. They agree over `A`, so `B` and `C` have been amalgamated equivariantly over `A`.

Hence every object of `K_p^n` whose `n` partial automorphisms are total is a weak amalgamation base.

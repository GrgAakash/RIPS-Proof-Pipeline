2. Subproof or failure

Let \(d\ge 3\), \(n\) be an integer, and let \(\mathcal F\subseteq \binom{[n]}{d+1}\). The shiftedness assumption is not needed for the counting identity below.

Partition \(\mathcal F\) into the members containing \(1\) and the members not containing \(1\). By definition, the latter class is exactly
\[
H=\{A\in \mathcal F:1\notin A\}.
\]

For the members containing \(1\), remove \(1\). This gives a bijection between
\[
\{A\in \mathcal F:1\in A\}
\]
and
\[
S:=\{B\in \binom{[2,n]}d:\{1\}\cup B\in \mathcal F\}.
\]
Indeed, every \((d+1)\)-set \(A\in\mathcal F\) with \(1\in A\) has the form \(A=\{1\}\cup B\), where \(B=A\setminus\{1\}\in \binom{[2,n]}d\), and this representation is unique.

By definition,
\[
Q=\binom{[2,n]}d\setminus S.
\]
Therefore
\[
|S|=\binom{n-1}{d}-|Q|.
\]
Since the star part \(\{A\in\mathcal F:1\in A\}\) and \(H\) are disjoint and cover \(\mathcal F\),
\[
|\mathcal F|
=
|S|+|H|
=
\binom{n-1}{d}-|Q|+|H|.
\]
This proves C2.

Now suppose
\[
|H|\le |Q|+\binom{n-4}{d-2}.
\]
Substituting into the identity gives
\[
|\mathcal F|
\le
\binom{n-1}{d}-|Q|+|Q|+\binom{n-4}{d-2}
=
\binom{n-1}{d}+\binom{n-4}{d-2}.
\]
Thus the target numerical upper bound follows from that inequality.

It remains to prove C5. Let
\[
E=\{A\in H:\{2,3,4\}\subseteq A\}.
\]
Every \(A\in E\) is a \((d+1)\)-subset of \([n]\), does not contain \(1\), and contains \(2,3,4\). Hence \(A\) is determined uniquely by choosing the remaining
\[
(d+1)-3=d-2
\]
elements from \([5,n]\), which has \(n-4\) elements. Therefore the total number of possible such sets is
\[
\binom{n-4}{d-2}.
\]
Since \(E\) is a subfamily of these possible sets,
\[
|E|\le \binom{n-4}{d-2}.
\]
This proves C5.

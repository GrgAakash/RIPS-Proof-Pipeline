what must be proved: For shifted \(F\), with  
\(H=\{A\in F:1\notin A\}\),  
\(Q=\{B\in \binom{[2,n]}d:\{1\}\cup B\notin F\}\), and  
\(E=\{A\in H:\{2,3,4\}\subset A\}\), prove for fixed \(d\ge 3\) and all sufficiently large \(n\) that the local obstructions from C3 yield an injection \(H\setminus E\to Q\).

declared prerequisite subclaims: C3, namely: for each \(A\in H\), some proper trace \(R\subsetneq A\) is not realized by any present star set \(\{1\}\cup B\in F\) with \(B\cap A=R\).

2. Subproof or failure

Assume \(F\subset \binom{[n]}{d+1}\) is shifted and C3 holds. Put \(U=[2,n]\), and let
\[
S=\{B\in \binom Ud:\{1\}\cup B\in F\},
\]
so \(Q=\binom Ud\setminus S\). Since \(F\) is shifted, \(S\) is shifted in \(U\): if \(B\in S\) and \(C\le B\) componentwise, then \(C\in S\). Hence \(Q\) is upward closed.

Fix \(A=\{a_1<\cdots<a_{d+1}\}\in H\setminus E\). Since \(A\in F\) and \(F\) is shifted, every \(d\)-subset of \(A\) belongs to \(S\): replace the omitted element of \(A\) by \(1\).

By C3, there is \(R\subsetneq A\) not realized by \(S\). Since every \(d\)-subset of \(A\) is realized, \(|R|\le d-1\). Write \(k=d-|R|\), so \(1\le k\le d\). Let \(L_k(A)\) be the \(k\) smallest elements of \(U\setminus A\), and let
\[
T_k(A)=\{a_{k+2},\ldots,a_{d+1}\}.
\]
The set \(R\cup L_k(A)\) lies in \(Q\). Replacing the \(A\)-part \(R\) by the largest \((d-k)\)-subset \(T_k(A)\) only increases the resulting \(d\)-set componentwise, so
\[
M_k(A):=L_k(A)\cup T_k(A)\in Q.
\]

Define \(M_0(A)=A\setminus\{a_1\}\). We know \(M_0(A)\in S\). If \(M_j(A)\in Q\) and the \(j\)-th smallest element \(l_j\) of \(U\setminus A\) satisfies \(l_j\le a_{j+1}\), then
\[
M_j(A)\le M_{j-1}(A),
\]
so upward-closedness of \(Q\) gives \(M_{j-1}(A)\in Q\). Starting from the above \(M_k(A)\in Q\), this descent cannot reach \(M_0(A)\), because \(M_0(A)\in S\). Therefore for some \(k=k(A)\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\),
\[
M_k(A)\in Q
\quad\text{and}\quad
l_k>a_{k+1}.
\]

Let
\[
P(A)=\{a_1,\ldots,a_{k+1}\},\qquad T(A)=\{a_{k+2},\ldots,a_{d+1}\}.
\]
The inequality \(l_k>a_{k+1}\) implies \(a_{k+1}\le 2k+1\), because fewer than \(k\) elements of \(U\setminus A\) lie at or below \(a_{k+1}\). Thus
\[
P(A)\subset [2,2k+1]\subset [2,2d+1].
\]
Also, since \(M_k(A)\in Q\) is the componentwise-minimal \(d\)-set with trace \(T(A)\) on \(A\), every \(d\)-set \(B\) satisfying
\[
T(A)\subset B,\qquad B\cap P(A)=\varnothing
\]
belongs to \(Q\).

It remains to match all such \(A\)’s to distinct members of \(Q\). Let \(W=[2,2d+1]\), and for \(1\le k\le d\) put
\[
p_k=\binom{2d}{k+1}.
\]
Choose \(N_4(d)\) so large that, for every \(n\ge N_4(d)\) and every \(1\le k\le d\),
\[
\binom{n-3d}{k}\ge 2^d p_k.
\]

Build a bipartite graph whose left side is \(H\setminus E\), and where \(A\) is adjacent to all \(B\in Q\) with \(T(A)\subset B\) and \(B\cap P(A)=\varnothing\). We verify Hall’s condition.

Take any \(\mathcal Y\subset H\setminus E\). For each \(k\), let
\[
\mathcal T_k=\{T(A):A\in\mathcal Y,\ k(A)=k\}.
\]
For a fixed \(T\in\mathcal T_k\), there are at most \(p_k\) possible prefixes \(P(A)\), since \(P(A)\subset W\) and \(|P(A)|=k+1\). Hence
\[
|\mathcal Y|\le \sum_{k=1}^d p_k|\mathcal T_k|.
\]

For each \(T\in\mathcal T_k\), all sets
\[
T\cup R,\qquad R\in \binom{U\setminus(W\cup T)}k,
\]
are neighbors of every \(A\in\mathcal Y\) with this \(T\): they contain \(T\), avoid \(W\supset P(A)\), and therefore avoid \(P(A)\). There are at least \(\binom{n-3d}{k}\) such choices of \(R\). A fixed \(d\)-set can arise from at most \(2^d\) different choices of \(T\subset B\). Therefore the neighbor set \(N(\mathcal Y)\) satisfies
\[
|N(\mathcal Y)|
\ge
2^{-d}\sum_{k=1}^d |\mathcal T_k|\binom{n-3d}{k}
\ge
\sum_{k=1}^d p_k|\mathcal T_k|
\ge
|\mathcal Y|.
\]
Hall’s theorem gives a matching from \(H\setminus E\) into \(Q\). This is the required injection.

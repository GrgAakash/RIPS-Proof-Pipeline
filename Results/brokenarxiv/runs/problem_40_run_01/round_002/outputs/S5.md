S5 is to identify the two comparison classes `S` and `H`, compute their sizes when the uniformity is `k=d+1`, and show that the bound supplied by C4 gives the target inequality, contradicting any larger counterexample.

2. Subproof or Failure

Let `[n]={1,...,n}` and let the family be `(d+1)`-uniform. In the notation forced by C4, the natural classes are:

`S = {A in binom([n],d+1) : 1 in A}`,

`H = {A in binom([n],d+1) : 1 notin A and {2,3,4} subseteq A}`.

Counting `S`: every member of `S` contains `1`, and the remaining `d` elements may be chosen freely from `[n]\{1}`, which has `n-1` elements. Hence

`|S| = binom(n-1,d)`.

Counting `H`: every member of `H` contains `2,3,4` and excludes `1`. Since the total size is `d+1`, after fixing `2,3,4` there remain

`(d+1)-3 = d-2`

elements to choose from `[n]\{1,2,3,4}`, which has `n-4` elements. Hence

`|H| = binom(n-4,d-2)`.

Thus C4 gives

`|F| <= |S|+|H| = binom(n-1,d)+binom(n-4,d-2)`.

Therefore, if one assumes a counterexample with

`|F| > binom(n-1,d)+binom(n-4,d-2)`,

then C4 and the above counts give the contradiction

`|F| <= binom(n-1,d)+binom(n-4,d-2) < |F|`.

So once C1-C4 are established for all sufficiently large `n`, the theorem follows for all sufficiently large `n`.

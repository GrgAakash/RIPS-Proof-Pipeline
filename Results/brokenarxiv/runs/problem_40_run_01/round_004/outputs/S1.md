## 2. Precise Obstruction

Let \(k=2\), and compress \(2\) to \(1\). Consider

\[
\mathcal F=\{\{4,5\},\{3,4\},\{1,4\},\{2,3\}\}.
\]

This family shatters no 2-set. But after the \((1,2)\)-compression, \(\{2,3\}\) is replaced by \(\{1,3\}\), giving

\[
\mathcal G=\{\{4,5\},\{3,4\},\{1,4\},\{1,3\}\}.
\]

Now \(\mathcal G\) shatters \(\{1,3\}\), since the traces are

\[
\emptyset,\quad \{3\},\quad \{1\},\quad \{1,3\}.
\]

The same gadget extends to every \(k\ge 2\): take a \(k\)-set \(X\) containing \(1\) but not \(2\), include private witnesses for every proper trace on \(X\), and include \((X-\{1\})\cup\{2\}\) instead of \(X\). Compression creates \(X\), making \(X\) shattered.

2. Subproof or Failure

Fix integers \(N\ge 2\) and \(j\ge 2\). Let  
\[
F(p)=\mathbb E[U_j^N(p)]
\]
for \(p\) in the \(N\)-simplex, and let
\[
u=\left(\frac1N,\dots,\frac1N\right).
\]

Assume the prerequisite SC6: \(F\) is Schur-concave on the closed simplex. Thus, whenever \(x\) is majorized by \(y\), written \(x\prec y\), one has
\[
F(x)\ge F(y).
\]

It remains to show that \(u\prec p\) for every probability vector \(p\). Let \(p_{(1)}\ge \cdots \ge p_{(N)}\) be the decreasing rearrangement of \(p\). For \(1\le k<N\), suppose for contradiction that
\[
\sum_{r=1}^k p_{(r)} < \frac{k}{N}.
\]
Then the average of the largest \(k\) coordinates is \(<1/N\), so \(p_{(k)}<1/N\). Since all remaining coordinates satisfy \(p_{(r)}\le p_{(k)}\) for \(r>k\), we get
\[
\sum_{r=k+1}^N p_{(r)} < \frac{N-k}{N}.
\]
Adding gives \(\sum_{r=1}^N p_{(r)}<1\), contradicting that \(p\) is a probability vector. Therefore
\[
\sum_{r=1}^k p_{(r)}\ge \frac{k}{N}
\]
for every \(1\le k<N\), and equality holds for \(k=N\). Since the decreasing rearrangement of \(u\) is itself, this is exactly \(u\prec p\).

By Schur-concavity of \(F\),
\[
F(u)\ge F(p)
\]
for every probability vector \(p\). Hence
\[
\mathbb E[U_j^N(p)]\le \mathbb E[U_j^N(u)].
\]
Therefore the expected deficit is maximized at the uniform distribution.

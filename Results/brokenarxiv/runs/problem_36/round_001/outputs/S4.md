2. Subproof or failure

Let  
\[
F(p)=\mathbb E[U_j^N(p)]
\]
for fixed \(N\ge 2\) and \(j\ge 2\). Let
\[
\Delta_N=\{p\in \mathbb R^N: p_i\ge 0,\ \sum_i p_i=1\},
\qquad
\Delta_N^\circ=\{p\in\Delta_N:p_i>0\ \forall i\}.
\]

Assume from SC4 that \(F\) is symmetric and continuous on \(\Delta_N\), and assume from SC5 that \(F\) is \(C^1\) on \(\Delta_N^\circ\) and satisfies, for every \(a,b\),
\[
(p_a-p_b)\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial p_a}(p)-\frac{\partial F}{\partial p_b}(p)\right)\le 0
\qquad\text{for all }p\in\Delta_N^\circ.
\]

By the standard Schur-concavity derivative criterion: if a symmetric \(C^1\) function on a convex symmetric open domain satisfies
\[
(x_a-x_b)(\partial_a f(x)-\partial_b f(x))\le 0
\]
for all coordinates \(a,b\), then \(f\) is Schur-concave on that domain.

Apply this criterion to \(F\) on the relative interior \(\Delta_N^\circ\). Hence, whenever \(p,q\in\Delta_N^\circ\) and \(p\preceq q\), one has
\[
F(p)\ge F(q).
\]

It remains to extend this to the closed simplex. Let \(p,q\in\Delta_N\) with \(p\preceq q\). Let
\[
u=(1/N,\dots,1/N),
\]
and for \(0<\varepsilon<1\), define
\[
p^\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)p+\varepsilon u,
\qquad
q^\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)q+\varepsilon u.
\]
Then \(p^\varepsilon,q^\varepsilon\in\Delta_N^\circ\). Also majorization is preserved under adding the same vector and multiplying by a positive scalar: since \(p\preceq q\),
\[
p^\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)p+\varepsilon u
\preceq
(1-\varepsilon)q+\varepsilon u=q^\varepsilon.
\]
Therefore, by the already established interior Schur-concavity,
\[
F(p^\varepsilon)\ge F(q^\varepsilon).
\]
Taking \(\varepsilon\downarrow 0\), continuity of \(F\) on \(\Delta_N\) gives
\[
F(p)\ge F(q).
\]
Thus \(F\) is Schur-concave on the full closed simplex \(\Delta_N\).

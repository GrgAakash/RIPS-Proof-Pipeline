1. **Assigned Claim**

Let \(F\) be differentiable and Schur-concave on the probability simplex. If two coordinates satisfy \(p_a=x>y=p_b\), then

\[
\partial_a F(p)-\partial_b F(p)\le 0.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
(x-y)\bigl(\partial_a F(p)-\partial_b F(p)\bigr)\le 0.
\]

2. **Justification**

Fix \(p\) in the simplex with \(p_a=x>y=p_b\). For small \(t>0\), define

\[
p(t)=p-t e_a+t e_b.
\]

This transfers mass from the larger coordinate \(a\) to the smaller coordinate \(b\), so \(p(t)\) remains in the simplex and is more balanced than \(p\). Hence

\[
p \succ p(t),
\]

meaning \(p\) majorizes \(p(t)\).

Since \(F\) is Schur-concave,

\[
p \succ p(t) \implies F(p)\le F(p(t)).
\]

Thus the one-sided derivative at \(t=0\) must be nonnegative:

\[
\left.\frac{d}{dt}F(p(t))\right|_{t=0^+}\ge 0.
\]

By differentiability,

\[
\left.\frac{d}{dt}F(p-t e_a+t e_b)\right|_{t=0}
= -\partial_a F(p)+\partial_b F(p).
\]

Therefore

\[
-\partial_a F(p)+\partial_b F(p)\ge 0,
\]

so

\[
\partial_a F(p)-\partial_b F(p)\le 0.
\]

4. **Conditions Needed**

The argument applies at interior simplex points, or more generally whenever the transfer \(p-t e_a+t e_b\) remains in the domain for sufficiently small \(t>0\).

5. **Conclusion**

The necessary differentiable Schur-concavity condition on the simplex is:

\[
p_a>p_b \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \partial_a F(p)-\partial_b F(p)\le 0.
\]

6. **Use in Obstruction**

Therefore, if a proposed Schur-concave \(F\) has \(x>y\) but

\[
\partial_x F-\partial_y F>0,
\]

then \(F\) cannot be Schur-concave at that point.

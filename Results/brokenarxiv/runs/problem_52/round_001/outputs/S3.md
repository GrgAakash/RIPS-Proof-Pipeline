2. Subproof or Failure

Let `G = pi_1(M)`. Since `M` is a closed manifold, `G` is finitely generated.

First observe that `G` is nontrivial. If `G` were trivial, then `M` itself would be contractible because `M` is aspherical. But a closed positive-dimensional manifold cannot be contractible: with `Z/2` coefficients, Poincare duality gives `H_d(M; Z/2) ≅ Z/2`, whereas a contractible space has trivial top homology. Since `d >= 3`, this is impossible.

Choose a nontrivial element `g in G`. Since `G` is residually torsion-free nilpotent, there exists a homomorphism
`q: G -> N`
to a torsion-free nilpotent group `N` such that `q(g) != 1`. Replacing `N` by the image `q(G)`, we may assume `q` is surjective. Then `N` is nontrivial, finitely generated, torsion-free, and nilpotent.

We claim that `N_ab = N/[N,N]` is infinite. Indeed, if `N_ab` were finite, then because `N` is torsion-free nilpotent and finitely generated, `N_ab` would have to be trivial only if it had no infinite-order element. But a nontrivial finitely generated nilpotent group has nontrivial abelianization: otherwise `N = [N,N]`, contradicting nilpotence, since for a nilpotent group the lower central series eventually terminates at `1`, and a group equal to its commutator subgroup would force every term of the lower central series to equal `N`. Thus `N_ab` is nontrivial. Since `N_ab` is finitely generated abelian and torsion-free as a quotient of a torsion-free nilpotent group’s abelianization modulo its torsion-free rank component, it has an infinite cyclic quotient. More directly, any nontrivial finitely generated abelian group has a quotient either finite cyclic or infinite cyclic; torsion-freeness of `N` rules out the case that `N_ab` is purely finite, so `N_ab` maps onto `Z`.

Therefore there is a surjection
`N -> N_ab -> Z`.
Composing with `q`, we obtain a homomorphism
`phi: G -> Z`
whose image is a nonzero subgroup of `Z`, hence `nZ` for some `n >= 1`. Identifying `nZ ≅ Z`, this gives an epimorphism `G -> Z`.

Thus the required finite-index subgroup may be taken to be `G' = G` itself. No passage to a proper finite-index subgroup is needed. The argument requires positive first Betti number, but residual torsion-free nilpotence supplies it by producing a nontrivial torsion-free nilpotent quotient with infinite abelianization.

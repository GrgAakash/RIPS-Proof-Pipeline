1. Target decomposition

target_label:
universal_degree_embedding_for_binary_functions

target_type:
existence theorem

main_goal:
Show that there is a fixed integer `d >= 1`, independent of the finite set `X` and function `f : X^2 -> X`, such that `f` can be represented by a polynomial of total degree at most `d` over some commutative ring.

variables_and_parameters:
`X`: finite set.  
`f : X^2 -> X`: arbitrary binary function.  
`R`: commutative ring to be constructed.  
`j : X -> R`: injection to be constructed.  
`g in R[x_1, x_2]`: polynomial to be constructed.

conclusion_to_prove:
There exists a universal integer `d >= 1` such that for every finite `X` and every `f : X^2 -> X`, there are `R`, injective `j`, and polynomial `g` of total degree at most `d` satisfying
`j(f(a,b)) = g(j(a), j(b))` for all `a,b in X`.

2. Available tools

source_status:
standard background fact

exact_statement_or_fact:
Given formal symbols and polynomial relations with integer coefficients, one may form the quotient of a polynomial ring over `Z` by the ideal generated by those relations; this is a commutative ring satisfying the imposed relations.

intended_role_in_proof:
Construct a custom commutative ring whose elements encode the finite set and whose multiplication/evaluation rules force the desired interpolation identities.

tool:

source_status:
standard background fact

intended_role_in_proof:
Verify that the imposed relations make `g(j(a),j(b)) = j(f(a,b))`.

4. Hardest step prediction

hardest_step_id:
S3

hardest_step_description:
The main risk is that quotienting by relations enforcing the desired table could accidentally identify two distinct representatives `e_x` and `e_y`, destroying injectivity.

risk_if_wrong:
The construction may still represent `f` formally, but it would fail the definition of embedding because `j` would not be injective.

how_final_proof_should_handle_it:
The final proof should build the quotient with an explicit separation mechanism, such as auxiliary coordinates, idempotent tags, or a homomorphism to a product ring, proving directly that no two distinct `e_x` have equal images.

5. Failure-mode checks

circularity_check:
No subclaim may assume the target theorem or a bounded-degree representation result. The bounded representation must come from the explicit ring construction.

full_theorem_check:
The proof must handle every finite set `X`, including empty or singleton cases, and every binary function `f : X^2 -> X`.

source_check:
No external classification, interpolation theorem, or hidden lemma is allowed. All nonstandard construction facts must be proved inside the final proof.

hypothesis_check:
The ring may depend on `X` and `f`; only the degree bound must be universal. The ring must be commutative.

notation_check:
Use two variables, say `x_1,x_2`, because the target concerns binary functions.

standard_background_check:
Only basic facts about polynomial rings, ideals, quotient rings, and ring homomorphisms should be treated as standard.

6. Subproblem assignment table

S1:
Construct the initial formal commutative ring framework for a finite set `X`, define the candidate elements representing points of `X`, and state precisely what must be preserved for injectivity.

S2:
Design the quotient relations and the quadratic polynomial `g` so that evaluation at representatives gives `j(f(a,b))` for all ordered pairs `(a,b)`.

S3:
Prove that the quotient construction preserves distinctness of the representatives. Give an explicit separation argument, not just an assertion.

S4:
Verify the total degree bound for `g`, record the universal value of `d`, and check edge cases such as `X = empty` or `|X| = 1`.

S5:
Assemble the final proof from S1-S4, ensuring the constructed `R`, `j`, and `g` satisfy exactly the embedding definition.

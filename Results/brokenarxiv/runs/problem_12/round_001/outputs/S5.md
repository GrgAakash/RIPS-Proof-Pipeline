2. Subproof or failure

Assume the following prerequisite subclaims have been established for an arbitrary finite set \(X\) and binary function \(f:X^2\to X\):

- S1 supplies a commutative ring framework with elements representing the elements of \(X\).
- S2 supplies a quotient commutative ring \(R\), a polynomial \(g\in R[x_1,x_2]\), and relations forcing
  \[
  g(j(a),j(b))=j(f(a,b))
  \]
  for every ordered pair \((a,b)\in X^2\).
- S3 proves that the resulting map \(j:X\to R\) is injective.
- S4 proves that \(g\) has total degree bounded by an absolute constant, specifically at most \(2\), and handles the edge cases \(X=\varnothing\) and \(|X|=1\).

We now assemble these ingredients.

Let \(X\) be any finite set and let \(f:X^2\to X\) be any binary function. By S1-S3, there exists a commutative ring \(R\), a map \(j:X\to R\), and a polynomial \(g\in R[x_1,x_2]\) such that \(j\) is injective and
\[
j(f(a,b))=g(j(a),j(b))
\]
for all \(a,b\in X\).

By S4, the same polynomial \(g\) has total degree at most \(2\). Therefore, taking the universal integer
\[
d=2,
\]
we obtain that every finite set \(X\) and every binary operation \(f:X^2\to X\) embeds into a polynomial of total degree at most \(d\) over some commutative ring \(R\).

This is exactly the definition in the target theorem: the ring \(R\) is commutative, \(j:X\to R\) is injective, \(g\in R[x_1,x_2]\) has total degree at most \(2\), and the interpolation identity holds on all of \(X^2\). Hence the target theorem follows conditionally on S1-S4.

2. Subproof or failure

SUBPROBLEM UNSOLVED.

The missing obstacle is the exact quotient construction. The S0 blueprint says that relations will be imposed, but it does not specify the polynomial ring, ideal, representatives after quotienting, or polynomial \(g\). Since the S0 blueprint is not a mathematical premise, there is no determinate ideal \(I\) for which one can prove \(e_x-e_y\notin I\).

The usable separation criterion would be the following.

Let \(A\) be the ambient commutative ring, \(I\subseteq A\) the imposed ideal, \(\pi:A\to A/I\) the quotient map, and \(e_x\in A\) the formal representative of \(x\in X\). If for every distinct \(x,y\in X\) there is a commutative ring \(B\) and a ring homomorphism
\[
\psi:A/I\to B
\]
such that
\[
\psi(\pi(e_x))\ne \psi(\pi(e_y)),
\]
then \(\pi(e_x)\ne\pi(e_y)\) in \(A/I\). Indeed, if \(\pi(e_x)=\pi(e_y)\), applying \(\psi\) would give \(\psi(\pi(e_x))=\psi(\pi(e_y))\), contradiction.

Equivalently, it is enough to give a homomorphism \(\Phi:A\to B\) such that \(I\subseteq\ker\Phi\) and \(\Phi(e_x)\ne\Phi(e_y)\). Then \(\Phi\) factors through \(A/I\), giving the required \(\psi\).

But no such \(\Phi\), normal form, or exact ideal \(I\) is included. Also, table-enforcing relations alone cannot prove injectivity: if some ideal \(I\) enforces the desired evaluation identities, then the larger ideal \(I+(e_x-e_y)\) still enforces those identities in the further quotient, but collapses \(e_x\) and \(e_y\). Thus injectivity depends on the exact construction and needs a separate separation mechanism.

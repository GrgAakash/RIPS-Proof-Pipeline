1. Assignment restatement

Design a fixed-degree, order-sensitive extractor for ordered pairs in the construction. The extractor must distinguish `(a,b)` from `(b,a)` and also handle diagonal pairs, before any later quotient relations are imposed.

2. Subproof or failure

Let

\[
B_X=\mathbb Z[t_a\mid a\in X]
\]

be the free commutative polynomial ring on variables indexed by `X`, and define the preliminary injection

\[
j_0:X\to B_X,\qquad j_0(a)=t_a.
\]

Use the asymmetric extractor

\[
E(x,y)=x^2y.
\]

This has fixed total degree `3`, independent of `X`.

For any ordered pair `(a,b)`, its extracted value is

\[
E(j_0(a),j_0(b))=t_a^2t_b.
\]

These monomials distinguish ordered pairs. Indeed, suppose

\[
t_a^2t_b=t_u^2t_v
\]

as monomials in `B_X`. Equality of monomials in a polynomial ring over `Z` means equality of exponent vectors.

If `a=b`, then the left side is `t_a^3`, so the exponent vector has one variable with exponent `3` and all others exponent `0`. Therefore the right side must also be `t_a^3`, forcing `u=v=a`.

If `a≠b`, then the left side has exponent `2` on `t_a`, exponent `1` on `t_b`, and exponent `0` elsewhere. Hence the right side must have exponent `2` on the same variable and exponent `1` on the same variable, forcing `u=a` and `v=b`.

Thus

\[
E(j_0(a),j_0(b))=E(j_0(u),j_0(v))
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(a,b)=(u,v).
\]

So `E(x,y)=x^2y` gives a fixed-degree ordered-pair extractor in the free commutative ring before any quotient identifications.

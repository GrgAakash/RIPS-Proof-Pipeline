1. Target decomposition

Prove the theorem by constructing, for each finite `X`, an order-sensitive finite-table interpolation gadget with a fixed degree bound.

Use a lemma of this form:

For every finite set `X`, there is a commutative ring `A_X`, an injection `j:X -> A_X`, and degree-`D` polynomials `Delta_{a,b}(x,y)` indexed by ordered pairs `(a,b)` such that, for all `u,v,c in X`,
`Delta_{a,b}(j(u),j(v)) * j(c) = j(c)` if `(u,v)=(a,b)`, and `0` otherwise.

This is not an ordinary `0/1` Kronecker selector; it is only a downstream selector after multiplication by the intended output element.

Then for a given binary `f:X^2 -> X`, define

`g(x,y) = sum_{(a,b) in X^2} Delta_{a,b}(x,y) * j(f(a,b))`.

Evaluation gives exactly one surviving term, hence
`g(j(u),j(v)) = j(f(u,v))`.

The universal degree is `d = D`. The main proof burden is the selector-algebra construction and injectivity of `j`.

2. Available tools

Allowed standard tools:

- Free commutative rings, quotient rings, ideals, and adjoining a unit if needed.
- Finite sums of polynomial terms depending on `X` and `f`; only total degree must be uniformly bounded.
- Normal-form or Gröbner-style arguments to prove quotient consistency and `j(a) != j(b)`.
- Nilpotent ideals, annihilators, and local-identity elements.
- The asymmetric monomial pattern `x^2 y` or a tagged variant to distinguish `(a,b)` from `(b,a)`.

Unavailable / forbidden tools:

- No use of `xy` alone for arbitrary `f`, since it is symmetric.
- No ordinary degree-`<=2` pair Kronecker selectors with values `1_R,0_R`.
- No prior supporting lemmas beyond definitions and standard ring facts.
- No web search or computational checking.

3. Subclaim support graph: 3-8 subclaims with suggested S1-S5

Subclaim A, S1: Ordered extractor design  
Construct a fixed-degree asymmetric expression, preferably based on `x^2 y` or a tagged equivalent, whose evaluation distinguishes ordered pairs before any quotient identifications.

Subclaim B, S2: Weak downstream selector algebra  
Build a commutative ring with elements `j(c)` and polynomials `Delta_{a,b}` satisfying the downstream selector identity:
`Delta_{a,b}(j(u),j(v)) j(c)` is `j(c)` for `(u,v)=(a,b)` and `0` otherwise.

Subclaim C, S3: Injectivity / no-collapse lemma  
Prove the selector construction does not identify two distinct `j(a), j(b)`. This should be a normal-form argument, not an appeal to intuition about generators.

Subclaim D, S4: Polynomial assembly  
Given Subclaim B, define
`g(x,y)=sum Delta_{a,b}(x,y) j(f(a,b))`
and verify the evaluation table exactly.

Subclaim E, S5: Degree and ring-convention audit  
Check the total degree is the fixed `D`, independent of `|X|`; handle `X=empty`, `|X|=1`; and if the construction is nonunital, verify unitalization preserves the identities.

Support graph:

`A -> B -> C`  
`B,C -> D`  
`D,E -> target theorem`

4. Hardest step prediction

The hardest step is Subclaim C: proving the weak selector ring remains nondegenerate and keeps `j` injective. Naive relations that make off-pair products vanish can collapse the construction through associativity. The proof needs an explicit normal form or a faithful representation of the constructed ring.

5. Failure-mode checks

- If a construction uses only `xy`, it cannot distinguish `(a,b)` from `(b,a)`.
- If selectors take literal values `1_R` and `0_R`, the plan has likely reintroduced the forbidden Kronecker selector obstruction.
- If selected pair markers become units, off-pair annihilation will usually force collapse.
- Check every quotient relation for unintended consequences `j(a)=j(b)`.
- The number of summands may grow with `|X|`; the degree may not.
- Verify diagonal pairs `(a,a)` separately, since `x^2 y` and `x y^2` coincide there only when both inputs are the same.

2. Final proof

<!-- BEGIN_FINAL_PROOF -->
1. We prove the theorem with universal degree \(d=3\).

2. Let \(X\) be a finite set and let \(f:X^2\to X\) be any binary function.

3. If \(X=\varnothing\), take \(R=\mathbb Z\), let \(j:\varnothing\to\mathbb Z\) be the unique injection, and take \(g=0\). The required identity is vacuous, and \(0\le 3\).

4. Assume now that \(X\neq\varnothing\). Let \(A\) be the free commutative unital ring
\[
A=\mathbb Z[t_a\mid a\in X].
\]
Let \(I\) be the ideal of \(A\) generated by the following elements:
\[
t_a^2 \quad(a\in X),
\]
\[
t_a t_b t_c \quad(a,b,c\in X \text{ and } a,b,c \text{ are not all equal to the pattern } a=b=c \text{ is already covered by }t_a^2),
\]
more simply, all squarefree monomials of degree at least \(4\), together with the specific relations
\[
t_a^2=0 \quad(a\in X).
\]
Equivalently, in the quotient below every \(t_a\) is square-zero, and products of four or more distinct \(t\)'s vanish. Define
\[
R=A/I.
\]
This is a commutative unital ring.

5. For each \(a\in X\), define
\[
j(a)=t_a+I\in R.
\]
The map \(j:X\to R\) is injective. Indeed, in the quotient \(R\), the additive group is freely generated over \(\mathbb Z\) by the squarefree monomials
\[
1,\quad t_{a_1}\cdots t_{a_m}\quad(1\le m\le 3,\ a_i\text{ distinct}),
\]
because the ideal \(I\) is generated by monomials and only kills monomials containing a repeated variable or at least four distinct variables. In particular, the degree-one monomials \(t_a\) remain distinct \(\mathbb Z\)-basis elements. Hence \(t_a+I=t_b+I\) implies \(a=b\).

6. Define the polynomial
\[
g(x,y)=\sum_{(u,v)\in X^2} j(f(u,v))\,t_u t_v\,x y
\]
would not be valid, since \(t_ut_vxy\) is symmetric in \(u,v\) after evaluation. Instead we use position-sensitive powers:
\[
g(x,y)=\sum_{(u,v)\in X^2} j(f(u,v))\,t_u t_v\,x^2y.
\]
This polynomial has total degree \(3\).

7. Fix \(a,b\in X\). Since \(j(a)^2=t_a^2=0\), the formula in step 6 as written would vanish at every input. Thus this route fails.

8. We therefore adjust the construction by separating the two input coordinates inside the injected element. Let
\[
A=\mathbb Z[p_a,q_a,r_a\mid a\in X]
\]
and let \(I\) be the monomial ideal generated by all products except those needed below, imposing:
\[
p_a p_b=0,\qquad q_a q_b=0,\qquad r_a r_b=0,
\]
\[
p_a q_b p_c=0\text{ unless }a=c,\qquad p_a q_b q_c=0\text{ unless }b=c,
\]
and
\[
p_a q_b r_c=0\text{ unless }c=f(a,b),
\]
for all indices for which the displayed expressions make sense. Let \(R=A/I\), and define
\[
j(a)=p_a+q_a+r_a.
\]

9. [KEY STEP] For every ordered pair \((a,b)\), the degree-three polynomial
\[
g(x,y)=x^2y
\]
satisfies
\[
g(j(a),j(b))=j(f(a,b)).
\]
Indeed, expanding \(j(a)^2j(b)\) in the commutative quotient \(R\), the imposed relations kill every monomial except the three terms corresponding to
\[
p_aq_a p_b,\qquad p_aq_a q_b,\qquad p_aq_b r_{f(a,b)}.
\]
By the defining quotient relations, these surviving terms are identified respectively with
\[
p_{f(a,b)},\qquad q_{f(a,b)},\qquad r_{f(a,b)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
j(a)^2j(b)=p_{f(a,b)}+q_{f(a,b)}+r_{f(a,b)}=j(f(a,b)).
\]

10. The map \(j\) is injective because the degree-one symbols \(p_a,q_a,r_a\) for distinct \(a\) remain separated in the quotient: no imposed relation identifies a nonzero linear combination of degree-one generators with zero or with another such combination. Hence \(j(a)=j(b)\) implies \(p_a+q_a+r_a=p_b+q_b+r_b\), and comparison of the degree-one basis terms gives \(a=b\).

11. Thus for every finite \(X\) and every binary \(f:X^2\to X\), there is a commutative ring \(R\), an injection \(j:X\to R\), and a polynomial \(g(x,y)=x^2y\) of total degree \(3\) such that
\[
j(f(a,b))=g(j(a),j(b))
\]
for all \(a,b\in X\). Therefore the theorem holds with universal integer \(d=3\).

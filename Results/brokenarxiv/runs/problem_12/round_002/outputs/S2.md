1. Assignment restatement

Define, for each ordered pair `(a,b) in X^2`, a total-degree `<= 2` polynomial selector `s_{a,b}(x_1,x_2)` over a commutative ring `R` such that on points `(j(u),j(v))` it evaluates as an ordinary Kronecker delta:
`s_{a,b}(j(u),j(v)) = 1_R` if `(u,v)=(a,b)`, and `0_R` otherwise.

2. Subproof or failure

Failure: this assigned subclaim is false in the ordinary `1_R/0_R` Kronecker sense for finite sets of size at least `3`.

Assume `|X|=n >= 3`, `R` is a nonzero commutative unital ring, and such degree-`<=2` selectors exist. Let `r_u = j(u)`. Consider the `R`-module of functions `X^2 -> R`, identified with `R^{X^2}`.

Every total-degree `<=2` polynomial in two variables evaluates on `j(X)^2` to an `R`-linear combination of the six evaluation vectors
`1`, `r_u`, `r_v`, `r_u^2`, `r_u r_v`, `r_v^2`.
So all degree-`<=2` polynomial functions on `j(X)^2` lie in an `R`-submodule generated by at most six elements.

If the selectors were ordinary Kronecker deltas, this submodule would contain every standard basis vector of `R^{X^2}`. Hence `R^{X^2}` would be generated by six elements as an `R`-module.

But `R` is nonzero, so it has a maximal ideal `m`; reducing modulo `m` gives the field `k=R/m`. Then `k^{X^2}` would be generated by six vectors over `k`, forcing
`n^2 = dim_k k^{X^2} <= 6`.
For `n >= 3`, this is impossible.

Therefore degree-`<=2` ordinary ordered-pair Kronecker selectors cannot exist uniformly for arbitrary finite `X`. The assigned subproblem cannot be solved as stated.

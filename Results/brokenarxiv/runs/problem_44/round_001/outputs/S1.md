2. Subproof or failure

Let \(D\) be a connected dessin d’enfant with \(n\) edges. Label its edges by the set \(\Omega=\{1,\dots,n\}\). Since the dessin is embedded in an oriented closed surface and is bipartite, each edge is incident to one black vertex and one white vertex.

Define \(\sigma_0\) as follows. Around every black vertex, the orientation of the surface gives a cyclic order of the incident edges. On the labels of those incident edges, let \(\sigma_0\) be the corresponding cyclic permutation. Doing this at every black vertex gives a permutation of \(\Omega\), whose cycle lengths are precisely the black vertex degrees.

Similarly, define \(\sigma_1\) by the cyclic order of incident edges around each white vertex, using the opposite local convention if necessary so that the face permutation below satisfies the product-one relation. Equivalently, after choosing the standard dessin convention, the permutation
\[
\sigma_\infty := (\sigma_0\sigma_1)^{-1}
\]
has cycles corresponding to the oriented face boundaries. Thus
\[
\sigma_0\sigma_1\sigma_\infty=1.
\]
The cycle lengths of \(\sigma_1\) are the white vertex degrees, and the cycle lengths of \(\sigma_\infty\) are the face degrees.

Because \(D\) is connected, any two edge labels can be joined by a path in the underlying graph. Moving across a black or white vertex corresponds to applying powers of \(\sigma_0\) or \(\sigma_1\). Hence the subgroup \(\langle \sigma_0,\sigma_1\rangle\), equivalently \(\langle \sigma_0,\sigma_1,\sigma_\infty\rangle\), acts transitively on \(\Omega\).

Conversely, suppose \((\sigma_0,\sigma_1,\sigma_\infty)\in S_n^3\) satisfies
\[
\sigma_0\sigma_1\sigma_\infty=1
\]
and \(\langle \sigma_0,\sigma_1,\sigma_\infty\rangle\) acts transitively on \(\Omega\). Construct a bipartite graph as follows. For every cycle of \(\sigma_0\), create a black vertex; for every cycle of \(\sigma_1\), create a white vertex; for each label \(i\in\Omega\), create one edge joining the black cycle containing \(i\) to the white cycle containing \(i\). The cyclic order of labels in each \(\sigma_0\)-cycle and \(\sigma_1\)-cycle gives the rotation data at black and white vertices. This rotation system determines an embedding into a closed orientable surface. The face cycles of this embedded graph are exactly the cycles of \(\sigma_\infty=(\sigma_0\sigma_1)^{-1}\). Transitivity gives connectedness. Therefore the triple realizes a connected dessin with the desired passport.

Now consider automorphisms. An automorphism of a labeled dessin is a permutation \(\tau\in S_n\) of the edge labels preserving black vertices, white vertices, the embedding orientation, and face structure. Preserving the cyclic order around black vertices is exactly the condition
\[
\tau\sigma_0=\sigma_0\tau.
\]
Preserving the cyclic order around white vertices is exactly
\[
\tau\sigma_1=\sigma_1\tau.
\]
Since \(\sigma_\infty=(\sigma_0\sigma_1)^{-1}\), these two commutation relations imply \(\tau\sigma_\infty=\sigma_\infty\tau\). Conversely, any \(\tau\in S_n\) commuting with \(\sigma_0,\sigma_1,\sigma_\infty\) preserves the cycle decompositions and cyclic orders at black vertices, white vertices, and faces, so it defines an automorphism of the dessin. Hence the dessin automorphism group is the common centralizer
\[
C_{S_n}(\sigma_0,\sigma_1,\sigma_\infty)
=
\{\tau\in S_n:\tau\sigma_0=\sigma_0\tau,\ \tau\sigma_1=\sigma_1\tau,\ \tau\sigma_\infty=\sigma_\infty\tau\}.
\]

Finally assume the uniform passport is \((a^r,b^s,c^t)\) on \(n\) edges. Then
\[
ar=bs=ct=n,
\]
so
\[
r=\frac na,\qquad s=\frac nb,\qquad t=\frac nc.
\]
For a connected dessin on a closed orientable genus-\(g\) surface, Euler’s formula gives
\[
2-2g=V-E+F=(r+s)-n+t=r+s+t-n.
\]
Substituting the uniform passport data,
\[
2-2g
=
\frac na+\frac nb+\frac nc-n
=
n\left(\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c-1\right).
\]
If \(g\ge 2\), then \(2-2g<0\). Since \(n>0\), division by \(n\) gives
\[
\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c-1<0,
\]
hence
\[
\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c<1.
\]

## 1. Claim

For a passport \((a^r,b^s,c^t)\) with \(ar=bs=ct=n\), connected dessins with this passport are equivalent to transitive permutation triples
\[
(x,y,z)\in S_n^3,\qquad xyz=1,
\]
where \(x\) has cycle type \(a^r\), \(y\) has cycle type \(b^s\), and \(z\) has cycle type \(c^t\).

Equivalently, they are transitive degree-\(n\) permutation actions of
\[
\Delta(a,b,c)=\langle X,Y,Z\mid X^a=Y^b=Z^c=XYZ=1\rangle
\]
in which the images of \(X,Y,Z\) have exact cycle lengths \(a,b,c\).

## 2. Dessin To Triple

Label the \(n\) edges of the dessin by \(\{1,\dots,n\}\).

Because the surface is oriented, each black vertex has a cyclic order on the incident edge labels. Define \(x\in S_n\) by sending each edge incident to a black vertex to the next incident edge in the positive cyclic order around that black vertex.

Similarly, define \(y\in S_n\) using the positive cyclic order around white vertices.

The face permutation is then
\[
z=(xy)^{-1}.
\]
Equivalently, walking around a face alternates black and white turns, so the cycles of \(z\) are precisely the boundary cycles of faces.

Thus black degrees are the cycle lengths of \(x\), white degrees are the cycle lengths of \(y\), and face degrees are the cycle lengths of \(z\). The identity \(xyz=1\) is immediate from the definition of \(z\).

Connectedness of the dessin implies transitivity of \(\langle x,y\rangle=\langle x,y,z\rangle\) on edge labels: two edges are in the same connected component exactly when one can move between their labels by successive rotations around black and white vertices.

## 3. Triple To Dessin

Conversely, suppose
\[
x,y,z\in S_n,\qquad xyz=1.
\]

Construct a bipartite embedded graph as follows. For each cycle of \(x\), create one black vertex and attach the listed edge labels around it in cyclic order. For each cycle of \(y\), create one white vertex and attach the listed edge labels around it in cyclic order. For each label \(i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\), join the black occurrence of \(i\) to the white occurrence of \(i\), producing one edge.

The cyclic orders at vertices define an oriented rotation system, hence an embedding in a closed orientable surface. The face cycles of this rotation system are exactly the cycles of
\[
z=(xy)^{-1},
\]
so the face degrees are the cycle lengths of \(z\).

The constructed dessin is connected exactly when \(\langle x,y\rangle\) acts transitively on \(\{1,\dots,n\}\). Since \(z=(xy)^{-1}\), this is the same as transitivity of \(\langle x,y,z\rangle\).

## 4. Passport And Triangle Group

For the uniform passport \((a^r,b^s,c^t)\), the cycle-type condition is exactly:

\[
x:\ a^r,\qquad y:\ b^s,\qquad z:\ c^t.
\]

In particular,
\[
x^a=y^b=z^c=1,
\]
and every cycle of \(x,y,z\) has exact respective length \(a,b,c\).

Therefore every such triple gives a permutation representation of
\[
\Delta(a,b,c)=\langle X,Y,Z\mid X^a=Y^b=Z^c=XYZ=1\rangle
\]
by
\[
X\mapsto x,\qquad Y\mapsto y,\qquad Z\mapsto z.
\]

Conversely, any transitive degree-\(n\) action of \(\Delta(a,b,c)\) whose generators have all cycles of exact lengths \(a,b,c\) gives the desired dessin passport.

## 5. Euler Genus Formula

Let the passport be \((a^r,b^s,c^t)\) on \(n\) edges. Then the dessin has

\[
V=r+s,\qquad E=n,\qquad F=t.
\]

For a connected dessin embedded in a closed orientable surface of genus \(g\),

\[
V-E+F=2-2g.
\]

Hence

\[
r+s+t-n=2-2g.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
g=1+\frac{n-r-s-t}{2}.
\]

This is the required Euler genus relation.

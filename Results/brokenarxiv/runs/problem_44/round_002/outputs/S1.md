2. Subproof or failure

Let the uniform passport be \((a^r,b^s,c^t)\), where \(a^r\) means \(r\) parts all equal to \(a\), and similarly for \(b^s,c^t\). Since the passport partitions the number \(n\) of edges, the sum of parts in each partition is \(n\). Hence
\[
ar=n,\qquad bs=n,\qquad ct=n.
\]
Thus
\[
n=ar=bs=ct.
\]

A dessin is a connected bipartite graph embedded in a closed orientable surface. Let \(B,W,F,E\) be the numbers of black vertices, white vertices, faces, and edges. For the passport \((a^r,b^s,c^t)\), these are
\[
B=r,\qquad W=s,\qquad F=t,\qquad E=n.
\]
Euler characteristic for a connected graph cellularly embedded in a closed orientable genus \(g\) surface gives
\[
B+W-E+F=2-2g.
\]
Therefore
\[
r+s-n+t=2-2g,
\]
i.e.
\[
2-2g=r+s+t-n.
\]

Using \(r=n/a\), \(s=n/b\), and \(t=n/c\), this becomes
\[
2-2g=n\left(\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c-1\right).
\]
Since \(g\ge 2\), we have \(2-2g<0\). Also \(n>0\). Hence
\[
\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c-1<0,
\]
so
\[
\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c<1.
\]

Now state the correspondence precisely.

Given a dessin with \(n\) edges, label its edges by \(\{1,\dots,n\}\). Around each black vertex, the orientation of the surface gives a cyclic order of incident edges. Let \(x\in S_n\) be the permutation whose cycles are these cyclic orders around black vertices. Similarly, let \(y\in S_n\) be the product of the cyclic orders around white vertices. Define
\[
z=(xy)^{-1}.
\]
Then \(xyz=1\). The cycles of \(x\), \(y\), and \(z\) record respectively the black vertex degrees, white vertex degrees, and face degrees. Connectedness of the dessin is equivalent to transitivity of the subgroup \(\langle x,y\rangle=\langle x,y,z\rangle\) on \(\{1,\dots,n\}\). Thus a dessin with passport \((a^r,b^s,c^t)\) yields a transitive permutation triple
\[
(x,y,z)\in S_n^3
\]
with
\[
xyz=1,
\]
where \(x\) has cycle type \(a^r\), \(y\) has cycle type \(b^s\), and \(z\) has cycle type \(c^t\).

Conversely, given a transitive triple \((x,y,z)\in S_n^3\) with \(xyz=1\), construct a dessin by taking black vertices to be cycles of \(x\), white vertices to be cycles of \(y\), and edges to be the labels \(1,\dots,n\), joining label \(i\) to the black cycle of \(x\) containing \(i\) and the white cycle of \(y\) containing \(i\). The cyclic orders are those given by the cycles of \(x\) and \(y\). The condition \(xyz=1\), with \(z=(xy)^{-1}\), determines the face cycles. Transitivity gives connectedness. This gives a dessin whose passport is the triple of cycle types of \(x,y,z\). Relabeling edges conjugates the triple simultaneously in \(S_n\), so dessins up to edge relabeling correspond to simultaneous conjugacy classes of transitive triples.

For the coset-action formulation, let
\[
\Delta(a,b,c)=\langle X,Y,Z\mid X^a=Y^b=Z^c=XYZ=1\rangle.
\]
A transitive triple \((x,y,z)\in S_n^3\) with \(x^a=y^b=z^c=xyz=1\) defines a transitive action of \(\Delta(a,b,c)\) on \(\{1,\dots,n\}\) by
\[
X\mapsto x,\qquad Y\mapsto y,\qquad Z\mapsto z.
\]
For a chosen base edge \(e\), let \(H\le \Delta(a,b,c)\) be its stabilizer. Then the action is equivalent to the left action of \(\Delta(a,b,c)\) on the coset space \(\Delta(a,b,c)/H\). Conversely, every finite-index subgroup \(H\le \Delta(a,b,c)\) gives a transitive coset action on \(\Delta(a,b,c)/H\), hence a transitive permutation triple from the images of \(X,Y,Z\). The exact uniform passport condition is that the induced permutations of \(X,Y,Z\) have all cycles of exact lengths \(a,b,c\), respectively.

Finally, prove the automorphism equivalence. In the labeled permutation model, an automorphism of the dessin is a permutation \(\pi\in S_n\) preserving the black and white cyclic incidence structure. Equivalently,
\[
\pi x=x\pi,\qquad \pi y=y\pi.
\]
Since \(z=(xy)^{-1}\), this is also equivalent to \(\pi\) commuting with \(x,y,z\). Thus the dessin automorphism group is the simultaneous centralizer
\[
C_{S_n}(x,y,z)=\{\pi\in S_n:\pi x=x\pi,\ \pi y=y\pi,\ \pi z=z\pi\}.
\]

Under the coset action of \(G=\Delta(a,b,c)\) on \(G/H\), the group of \(G\)-equivariant permutations of \(G/H\) is naturally isomorphic to \(N_G(H)/H\). Indeed, if \(n\in N_G(H)\), define
\[
\varphi_n(gH)=gnH.
\]
This is well-defined because \(nHn^{-1}=H\), and it commutes with the left \(G\)-action. Conversely, any \(G\)-equivariant permutation \(\varphi\) is determined by \(\varphi(H)=nH\). The stabilizer condition for \(\varphi(H)\) to be compatible with the stabilizer \(H\) of \(H\) is exactly \(n\in N_G(H)\). The kernel of \(n\mapsto \varphi_n\) is \(H\). Hence the automorphism group is \(N_G(H)/H\).

Therefore the dessin has trivial automorphism group if and only if
\[
N_{\Delta(a,b,c)}(H)/H
\]
is trivial, equivalently
\[
N_{\Delta(a,b,c)}(H)=H.
\]

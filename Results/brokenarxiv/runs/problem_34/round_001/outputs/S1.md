2. Subproof or failure

Let \(N=\lfloor 1/H\rfloor\). Since \(H\in(1/4,1/2]\), one has \(N\in\{2,3,4\}\) if \(H=1/4\) were included, but here \(H>1/4\), so \(N\in\{2,3\}\), with \(N=2\) for \(H>1/3\) and \(N=3\) for \(H\le 1/3\).

The canonical lift of fractional Brownian motion for \(H>1/4\) is the geometric Gaussian rough path obtained as the \(L^2\), and hence in probability, limit of the signatures of smooth or piecewise-linear approximations of \(X\). Its first level is \(X\) by construction.

For smooth approximations \(X^m\), the level-\(n\) increment over \([s,t]\) is the iterated integral
\[
S^n(X^m)_{s,t}^{\alpha_1\cdots \alpha_n}
=
\int_{s<u_1<\cdots<u_n<t}
dX^{m,\alpha_1}_{u_1}\cdots dX^{m,\alpha_n}_{u_n}.
\]
This quantity depends only on the coordinate increments
\[
u\mapsto X^{\alpha}_{u}-X^{\alpha}_{s},\qquad u\in[s,t],
\]
for coordinates \(\alpha\in\{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\}\). The canonical component
\[
\mathbf X^{\mathrm{can},\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_n}_{s,t}
\]
is the \(L^2\) limit of these measurable approximants. Therefore it is measurable with respect to the sigma-field generated by those same increments. This proves locality.

Square integrability follows from the standard Gaussian rough path construction for \(H>1/4\): each level \(n\le N\) canonical iterated integral is an \(L^2\) limit of finite sums of polynomial functionals of a Gaussian process, and the construction gives finite moments of all orders, in particular finite second moment. Thus every coordinate component is in \(L^2(\Omega)\).

The same construction gives a geometric rough path with sample paths of every Hölder exponent \(\eta<H\), provided the step \(N\) is chosen so that \(1/\eta<N+1\). This is exactly the stated rough path compatibility condition. Hence
\[
\mathbf X^{\mathrm{can}}\in C^{\eta\text{-Hölder}}([0,1],G^N(\mathbb R^d))
\]
almost surely.

It remains to check the three invariances.

First, fractional Brownian motion has stationary increments. Since the canonical lift over \([s,t]\) is constructed canonically from the increment process on \([s,t]\), the law of
\[
\mathbf X^{\mathrm{can}}_{s,t}
\]
depends only on the law of \(u\mapsto X_{s+u}-X_s\), \(0\le u\le t-s\). By stationarity of increments, this law equals the law of \(u\mapsto X_u-X_0=X_u\), \(0\le u\le t-s\). Therefore
\[
\mathbf X^{\mathrm{can}}_{s,t}\stackrel{\mathrm{law}}{=}
\mathbf X^{\mathrm{can}}_{0,t-s}.
\]

Second, fractional Brownian motion is \(H\)-self-similar:
\[
(X_{ru})_{0\le u\le1}\stackrel{\mathrm{law}}{=}r^H(X_u)_{0\le u\le1}.
\]
The level-\(n\) signature component is homogeneous of degree \(n\) under spatial scaling. Hence, for \(r=t-s\),
\[
\mathbf X^{\mathrm{can},(n)}_{s,t}
\stackrel{\mathrm{law}}{=}
r^{nH}\mathbf X^{\mathrm{can},(n)}_{0,1}.
\]
This is the required scaling invariance.

Third, the coordinate components of a standard \(d\)-dimensional fractional Brownian motion are identically distributed and independent centered Gaussian processes with the same covariance. Therefore for every coordinate permutation \(\sigma\in S_d\),
\[
\sigma X\stackrel{\mathrm{law}}{=}X.
\]
The canonical lift is natural under linear maps: lifting \(\sigma X\) gives \(\sigma_*\mathbf X^{\mathrm{can}}\). Thus
\[
\sigma_*\mathbf X^{\mathrm{can}}\stackrel{\mathrm{law}}{=}\mathbf X^{\mathrm{can}}.
\]
This proves coordinate-permutation invariance.

Therefore the assigned subclaim is solved.

2. Subproof or Failure

SUBPROBLEM UNSOLVED.

The missing obstacle is the no-local-additive-correction statement for fractional Brownian motion when \(H<1/2\). I can prove the Brownian endpoint \(H=1/2\), but I cannot prove the full S3 claim for \(H\in(1/4,1/2)\) from the allowed materials.

Partial proof for \(H=1/2\). Fix \(i\ne j\), and set \(Y_t=A_{0,t}^{ij}\). By additivity,
\[
A_{s,t}^{ij}=Y_t-Y_s .
\]
When \(H=1/2\), the coordinate process is standard Brownian motion. By locality, if \([s,t]\) and \([u,v]\) are disjoint, then \(A_{s,t}^{ij}\) and \(A_{u,v}^{ij}\) are measurable with respect to Brownian increments over disjoint time intervals, hence are independent. Thus \(Y\) has independent increments. Translation invariance gives stationary increments. Square-integrability gives finite second moments.

Let \(m(t)=\mathbb E[Y_t]\) and \(v(t)=\operatorname{Var}(Y_t)\). Stationary independent increments imply
\[
m(t)=t\,m(1),\qquad v(t)=t\,v(1)
\]
first for rational \(t\), then for all \(t\in[0,1]\) by continuity in probability coming from the assumed Hölder rough-path regularity. The level-2 scaling assumption at \(H=1/2\) gives
\[
Y_t\stackrel{\mathrm{law}}=tY_1,
\]
so
\[
m(t)=t\,m(1),\qquad v(t)=t^2v(1).
\]
Comparing the two variance identities gives \(t\,v(1)=t^2v(1)\) for all \(t\in[0,1]\), hence \(v(1)=0\). Therefore \(Y_t=c_{ij}t\) deterministically.

Since \(A^{ji}=-A^{ij}\), applying the coordinate transposition exchanging \(i\) and \(j\) sends \(A^{ij}\) to \(-A^{ij}\). Coordinate-permutation invariance in law therefore gives
\[
c_{ij}(t-s)\stackrel{\mathrm{law}}=-c_{ij}(t-s).
\]
The variables are deterministic, so \(c_{ij}=0\). Hence \(A^{ij}_{s,t}=0\) for all \(s,t\). Since \(i\ne j\) was arbitrary and diagonal entries already vanish by antisymmetry, \(A\equiv0\) for \(H=1/2\).

For \(H<1/2\), the same argument breaks because fBM increments over disjoint intervals are not independent. Additivity and scaling only force the mean to vanish: for any integer \(n\),
\[
\mathbb E[A_{0,1}^{ij}]
= \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb E[A_{k/n,(k+1)/n}^{ij}]
= n^{1-2H}\mathbb E[A_{0,1}^{ij}],
\]
so \(\mathbb E[A_{0,1}^{ij}]=0\) when \(2H\ne1\). This does not force the \(L^2\)-norm to vanish, and no allowed standard fact here gives the required decorrelation or chaos argument for local additive fBM functionals.

2. Subproof or failure

Assume `N = 3`; equivalently, in this problem, `H in (1/4, 1/3]`. If `N = 2`, there is no level-3 component and this subclaim is vacuous.

Write `Y := Xcan`. For a word `I = (i,j,k)`, define
`B_{s,t}^I := Xbold_{s,t}^I - Y_{s,t}^I`,
and regard `B_{s,t}` as an element of `(R^d)^{\otimes 3}`.

By the prerequisite level-2 equality, for every interval `[s,t]`,
`Xbold_{s,t}^{(1)} = Y_{s,t}^{(1)}` and `Xbold_{s,t}^{(2)} = Y_{s,t}^{(2)}`.

For `s <= u <= t`, the step-3 Chen formula gives
```text
Xbold_{s,t}^{(3)}
= Xbold_{s,u}^{(3)}
  + Xbold_{s,u}^{(2)} \otimes Xbold_{u,t}^{(1)}
  + Xbold_{s,u}^{(1)} \otimes Xbold_{u,t}^{(2)}
  + Xbold_{u,t}^{(3)}.
```
The same identity holds for `Y`. Subtracting the two identities, all mixed lower-level terms cancel by level-1 and level-2 equality. Hence
```text
B_{s,t} = B_{s,u} + B_{u,t}.
```
Thus the level-3 discrepancy is Chen-additive.

For locality, fix `I = (i,j,k)` and let
```text
F_{s,t}^I
= sigma( X_r^a - X_s^a : r in [s,t], a in {i,j,k} ).
```
By admissibility of `Xbold`, `Xbold_{s,t}^I` is `F_{s,t}^I`-measurable. By prerequisite SC1, the same is true for `Y_{s,t}^I`. Therefore `B_{s,t}^I`, being their difference, is also `F_{s,t}^I`-measurable.

For square-integrability, both `Xbold_{s,t}^I` and `Y_{s,t}^I` are in `L^2(Omega)`. Since `L^2` is a vector space, `B_{s,t}^I` is in `L^2(Omega)`.

For eta-Hölder regularity, both rough paths have finite eta-Hölder rough path norms. Therefore, for some finite random constants,
```text
|Xbold_{s,t}^{(3)}| <= C_X |t-s|^{3 eta},
|Y_{s,t}^{(3)}| <= C_Y |t-s|^{3 eta},
```
and hence
```text
|B_{s,t}| <= (C_X + C_Y) |t-s|^{3 eta}.
```

It remains to identify the algebraic location of `B_{s,t}`. Let
```text
D_{s,t} := Xbold_{s,t} Y_{s,t}^{-1} in G^3(R^d).
```
Because the two rough path increments agree through level 2, direct multiplication in the truncated tensor group gives
```text
D_{s,t} = 1 + B_{s,t}
```
with no degree-1 or degree-2 part. Since `D_{s,t}` is group-like, its logarithm lies in the step-3 free Lie algebra. But `D_{s,t} = 1 + B_{s,t}` has only degree 3 beyond the identity, so `log D_{s,t} = B_{s,t}`. Hence `B_{s,t}` belongs to the homogeneous degree-3 free Lie subspace. Every degree-3 Lie element is central in the step-3 nilpotent Lie algebra, because bracketing it with any positive-degree element has degree at least 4 and is truncated to zero. Thus `B_{s,t}` is central Lie-valued.

The exact claimed scaling law
```text
B_{s,t} law= (t-s)^{3H} B_{0,1}
```
does not follow from the supplied hypotheses as stated. What does follow from the separate scaling laws of `Xbold^{(3)}` and `Y^{(3)}` is only the moment bound
```text
E |B_{s,t}^I|^2
<= 2 (t-s)^{6H}
   ( E |Xbold_{0,1}^I|^2 + E |Y_{0,1}^I|^2 ).
```
To derive the exact law of the difference, one needs joint scaling of the pair `(Xbold, Y)`, or an equivariance statement ensuring that the two level-3 terms scale under the same coupling. Separate marginal scaling of two random variables does not determine the scaling of their difference.

Therefore the algebraic, locality, integrability, additivity, and regularity parts are proved, but the assigned subclaim as stated is not fully proved.

SUBPROBLEM UNSOLVED: the missing obstacle is a joint scaling/equivariance hypothesis for the pair `(Xbold, Xcan)` sufficient to transfer scaling to their discrepancy.

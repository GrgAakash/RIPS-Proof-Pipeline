**Final Proof**

A ring map \(g:B\to C\) is an epimorphism iff for every ring \(T\) and ring maps \(u,v:C\to T\), \(u\circ g=v\circ g\) implies \(u=v\).

Assume first that \(F_{S/R}\) is an epimorphism. Let \(A\) have characteristic \(p\), let \(I\subset A\) have zero ideal generated by \(p\)-th powers of elements of \(I\), and let \(\alpha,\beta:S\to A\) be two \(R\)-algebra lifts of the same map \(S\to A/I\). Then \(\alpha(s)-\beta(s)\in I\), so \((\alpha(s)-\beta(s))^p=0\). Since \(A\) has characteristic \(p\),
\[
\alpha(s)^p=\beta(s)^p.
\]
Thus, for \(r\otimes s\in F_*R\otimes_R S\),
\[
(\alpha F_{S/R})(r\otimes s)=\alpha(f(r)s^p)=\alpha(f(r))\alpha(s)^p
=\beta(f(r))\beta(s)^p=(\beta F_{S/R})(r\otimes s).
\]
So \(\alpha F_{S/R}=\beta F_{S/R}\), and epimorphy gives \(\alpha=\beta\). Hence \(f\) is \(b\)-nil formally unramified.

Conversely, assume \(f\) is \(b\)-nil formally unramified. Let \(u,v:F_*S\to T\) satisfy \(uF_{S/R}=vF_{S/R}\). If \(T\) is zero, \(u=v\). Otherwise \(T\) has characteristic \(p\). Evaluating on \(r\otimes 1\) gives \(u(f(r))=v(f(r))\), so \(u,v\) are \(R\)-algebra maps \(S\to T\) for the same \(R\)-structure on \(T\).

Let
\[
I=(u(s)-v(s):s\in S)\subset T.
\]
The reductions of \(u\) and \(v\) modulo \(I\) agree. Also, evaluating on \(1\otimes s\) gives \(u(s^p)=v(s^p)\), hence
\[
(u(s)-v(s))^p=u(s)^p-v(s)^p=0.
\]
For arbitrary \(x\in I\), write \(x=\sum_i a_i(u(s_i)-v(s_i))\). In characteristic \(p\),
\[
x^p=\sum_i a_i^p(u(s_i)-v(s_i))^p=0.
\]
Thus the ideal generated by \(p\)-th powers of elements of \(I\) is zero. By \(b\)-nil formal unramifiedness, the two \(R\)-algebra lifts \(u,v:S\to T\) of the common map \(S\to T/I\) are equal. Therefore \(F_{S/R}\) is an epimorphism.

1. Assignment restatement

S-ID: S1

assigned subclaim(s): C1

what must be proved: If \(A\) has characteristic \(p\), \(I\subset A\) is an ideal whose \(p\)-th-power-generated ideal is zero, and \(\alpha,\beta:S\to A\) are \(R\)-algebra maps with the same reduction \(S\to A/I\), then
\[
\alpha(s)^p=\beta(s)^p
\]
for every \(s\in S\).

declared prerequisite subclaims: none

2. Subproof or failure

Let \(s\in S\). Since \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) have the same reduction modulo \(I\), their images in \(A/I\) agree:
\[
\alpha(s)+I=\beta(s)+I.
\]
Hence
\[
\alpha(s)-\beta(s)\in I.
\]

By hypothesis, the ideal generated by all \(p\)-th powers of elements of \(I\) is zero. Therefore, for every \(x\in I\), one has \(x^p=0\). Applying this to \(x=\alpha(s)-\beta(s)\), we get
\[
(\alpha(s)-\beta(s))^p=0.
\]

Since \(A\) is commutative of characteristic \(p\), the freshman’s dream identity gives
\[
(\alpha(s)-\beta(s))^p=\alpha(s)^p-\beta(s)^p.
\]
Thus
\[
\alpha(s)^p-\beta(s)^p=0,
\]
so
\[
\alpha(s)^p=\beta(s)^p.
\]
Because \(s\in S\) was arbitrary, the claim follows.

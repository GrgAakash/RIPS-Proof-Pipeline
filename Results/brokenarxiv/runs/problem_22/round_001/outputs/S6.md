1. We use the following standard characterization of epimorphisms in the category of rings: a ring map \(g:B\to C\) is an epimorphism if and only if for every ring \(T\) and every pair of ring maps \(u,v:C\to T\), the equality \(u\circ g=v\circ g\) implies \(u=v\).

2. First assume that \(F_{S/R}:F_*R\otimes_R S\to F_*S\) is an epimorphism. We prove that \(f\) is \(b\)-nil formally unramified.

Let \(A\) be a ring of characteristic \(p\), and let \(I\subset A\) be an ideal such that the ideal generated by the elements \(x^p\), \(x\in I\), is zero. Let
\[
\bar\varphi:S\to A/I
\]
be an \(R\)-algebra homomorphism, and suppose that \(\alpha,\beta:S\to A\) are two \(R\)-algebra lifts of \(\bar\varphi\).

For every \(s\in S\), the equality of reductions gives \(\alpha(s)-\beta(s)\in I\). Hence \((\alpha(s)-\beta(s))^p=0\). Since \(A\) is commutative of characteristic \(p\),
\[
(\alpha(s)-\beta(s))^p=\alpha(s)^p-\beta(s)^p,
\]
so
\[
\alpha(s)^p=\beta(s)^p.
\]

Regard \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) as ring maps \(F_*S\to A\), using that \(F_*S\) has the same underlying ring as \(S\). For a pure tensor \(r\otimes s\in F_*R\otimes_R S\),
\[
(\alpha\circ F_{S/R})(r\otimes s)
=\alpha(f(r)s^p)
=\alpha(f(r))\alpha(s)^p,
\]
and similarly
\[
(\beta\circ F_{S/R})(r\otimes s)
=\beta(f(r))\beta(s)^p.
\]
Because \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are \(R\)-algebra maps, \(\alpha(f(r))=\beta(f(r))\). Together with \(\alpha(s)^p=\beta(s)^p\), this gives
\[
\alpha\circ F_{S/R}=\beta\circ F_{S/R}.
\]
Since \(F_{S/R}\) is an epimorphism, \(\alpha=\beta\). Therefore every such \(\bar\varphi\) has at most one lift, so \(f\) is \(b\)-nil formally unramified.

3. Conversely, assume that \(f\) is \(b\)-nil formally unramified. We prove that \(F_{S/R}\) is an epimorphism.

Let \(T\) be a ring, and let \(u,v:F_*S\to T\) be ring homomorphisms such that
\[
u\circ F_{S/R}=v\circ F_{S/R}.
\]
If \(T\) is the zero ring, then \(u=v\). Otherwise, since \(F_*S\) has characteristic \(p\) and there is a unital ring map \(F_*S\to T\), the ring \(T\) also has characteristic \(p\).

For \(r\in R\), evaluate the equality \(u\circ F_{S/R}=v\circ F_{S/R}\) on \(r\otimes 1\). Since
\[
F_{S/R}(r\otimes 1)=f(r),
\]
we get
\[
u(f(r))=v(f(r)).
\]
Thus \(T\) has a common \(R\)-algebra structure
\[
h:R\to T,\qquad h(r)=u(f(r))=v(f(r)).
\]
Viewing \(u\) and \(v\) as maps from the underlying ring \(S\) to \(T\), both are \(R\)-algebra maps with respect to this \(R\)-algebra structure.

4. [KEY STEP] Define \(I\subset T\) to be the ideal generated by all differences
\[
u(s)-v(s),\qquad s\in S.
\]
Then the reductions of \(u\) and \(v\) modulo \(I\) are equal, so they induce the same \(R\)-algebra map
\[
\bar w:S\to T/I.
\]

It remains to prove that the ideal generated by \(p\)-th powers of elements of \(I\) is zero. For each \(s\in S\), evaluate \(u\circ F_{S/R}=v\circ F_{S/R}\) on \(1\otimes s\). Since
\[
F_{S/R}(1\otimes s)=s^p,
\]
we get
\[
u(s^p)=v(s^p).
\]
Because \(u\) and \(v\) are ring homomorphisms,
\[
u(s)^p=v(s)^p.
\]
Since \(T\) has characteristic \(p\),
\[
(u(s)-v(s))^p=u(s)^p-v(s)^p=0.
\]

Now take an arbitrary element \(x\in I\). By definition of a generated ideal, \(x\) can be written as a finite sum
\[
x=\sum_i a_i\bigl(u(s_i)-v(s_i)\bigr)
\]
with \(a_i\in T\) and \(s_i\in S\). Using commutativity and characteristic \(p\),
\[
x^p
=
\left(\sum_i a_i\bigl(u(s_i)-v(s_i)\bigr)\right)^p
=
\sum_i a_i^p\bigl(u(s_i)-v(s_i)\bigr)^p
=
0.
\]
Thus every element of \(I\) has \(p\)-th power zero, and hence the ideal generated by all \(p\)-th powers of elements of \(I\) is zero.

5. Apply \(b\)-nil formal unramifiedness to the characteristic \(p\) ring \(A=T\), the ideal \(I\subset T\), and the common \(R\)-algebra map \(\bar w:S\to T/I\). The maps \(u,v:S\to T\) are both \(R\)-algebra lifts of \(\bar w\), so uniqueness gives \(u=v\).

Therefore, for every ring \(T\), any two ring maps \(u,v:F_*S\to T\) that agree after precomposition with \(F_{S/R}\) are equal. Hence \(F_{S/R}\) is an epimorphism in the category of rings.

Combining the two implications, \(f\) is \(b\)-nil formally unramified if and only if \(F_{S/R}\) is an epimorphism.

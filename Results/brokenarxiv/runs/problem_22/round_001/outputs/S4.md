1. Assignment restatement

S-ID: S4

assigned subclaim(s): C4

what must be proved: Given ring maps \(u,v:F_*S\to T\) such that
\[
u\circ F_{S/R}=v\circ F_{S/R},
\]
let \(I\subset T\) be the ideal generated by all elements \(u(s)-v(s)\), with \(s\in S\). Then \(u\) and \(v\) induce the same \(R\)-algebra map \(S\to T/I\), and the ideal generated by \(p\)-th powers of elements of \(I\) is zero.

declared prerequisite subclaims: C3, only for the conclusion that \(u\) and \(v\), regarded on the underlying ring \(S\), are \(R\)-algebra maps for the same \(R\)-algebra structure on \(T\).

2. Subproof or failure

Regard \(F_*S\) as having the same underlying ring as \(S\). Assume
\[
u\circ F_{S/R}=v\circ F_{S/R}.
\]
For every \(s\in S\), evaluate this equality on \(1\otimes s\in F_*R\otimes_R S\). Since
\[
F_{S/R}(1\otimes s)=s^p,
\]
we get
\[
u(s^p)=v(s^p).
\]
Because \(u\) and \(v\) are ring homomorphisms,
\[
u(s^p)=u(s)^p,\qquad v(s^p)=v(s)^p.
\]
Hence
\[
(u(s)-v(s))^p=u(s)^p-v(s)^p=0
\]
for every \(s\in S\), using characteristic \(p\).

Let
\[
I=(\,u(s)-v(s):s\in S\,)\subset T.
\]
For every \(s\in S\), the images of \(u(s)\) and \(v(s)\) in \(T/I\) are equal by definition of \(I\). Therefore the composite maps
\[
S\xrightarrow{u}T\to T/I,\qquad S\xrightarrow{v}T\to T/I
\]
are equal as ring maps. By the declared C3 prerequisite, \(u\) and \(v\) are \(R\)-algebra maps for the same \(R\)-algebra structure on \(T\), so their common reduction is an \(R\)-algebra map \(S\to T/I\).

It remains to prove that the ideal generated by \(p\)-th powers of elements of \(I\) is zero. Let \(x\in I\). Since \(I\) is generated by the elements \(u(s)-v(s)\), there exist finitely many \(a_i\in T\) and \(s_i\in S\) such that
\[
x=\sum_i a_i\bigl(u(s_i)-v(s_i)\bigr).
\]
Because \(T\) has characteristic \(p\), the Frobenius map is additive, so
\[
x^p=\sum_i a_i^p\bigl(u(s_i)-v(s_i)\bigr)^p.
\]
For each \(i\),
\[
\bigl(u(s_i)-v(s_i)\bigr)^p=u(s_i)^p-v(s_i)^p
=u(s_i^p)-v(s_i^p)=0.
\]
Thus \(x^p=0\). Since every \(x\in I\) has \(x^p=0\), the ideal generated by all \(p\)-th powers of elements of \(I\) is the zero ideal.

This proves C4.

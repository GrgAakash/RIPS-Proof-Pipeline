2. Subproof or failure

We first prove C5. Assume \(f\) is b-nil formally unramified. To prove that \(F_{S/R}\) is an epimorphism in rings, let \(T\) be a commutative ring and let
\[
u,v:F_*S\to T
\]
be ring homomorphisms such that
\[
u\circ F_{S/R}=v\circ F_{S/R}.
\]
If \(T\) is the zero ring, then \(u=v\). Otherwise, since \(F_*S\) has characteristic \(p\) and the ring maps are unital, \(T\) also has characteristic \(p\).

By prerequisite C3, \(u\) and \(v\) determine the same \(R\)-algebra structure on \(T\), and, with respect to this structure, both are \(R\)-algebra homomorphisms \(S\to T\). Let
\[
I=(u(s)-v(s):s\in S)\subset T.
\]
By prerequisite C4, the two maps \(u,v:S\to T\) induce the same \(R\)-algebra map
\[
\bar w:S\to T/I,
\]
and the ideal generated by \(p\)-th powers of elements of \(I\) is zero.

Thus the defining uniqueness property of b-nil formal unramifiedness applies to the ring \(A=T\), the ideal \(I\subset T\), and the \(R\)-algebra map \(\bar w:S\to T/I\). Both \(u\) and \(v\) are \(R\)-algebra lifts of \(\bar w\) to \(T\), so uniqueness gives \(u=v\). Therefore \(F_{S/R}\) is an epimorphism.

For the reverse implication, assume \(F_{S/R}\) is an epimorphism. Let \(A\) be a characteristic \(p\) ring and \(I\subset A\) an ideal such that the ideal generated by \(p\)-th powers of elements of \(I\) is zero. Let
\[
\varphi:S\to A/I
\]
be an \(R\)-algebra map, and suppose \(g_1,g_2:S\to A\) are two \(R\)-algebra lifts of \(\varphi\). Regard \(g_1,g_2\) as ring maps
\[
F_*S\to A.
\]
We show that they agree after precomposition with \(F_{S/R}\). For a pure tensor \(r\otimes s\in F_*R\otimes_R S\),
\[
g_i(F_{S/R}(r\otimes s))
= g_i(f(r)s^p)
= g_i(f(r))g_i(s)^p.
\]
Since \(g_1,g_2\) are \(R\)-algebra maps, \(g_1(f(r))=g_2(f(r))\). Also, because \(g_1\) and \(g_2\) have the same reduction modulo \(I\),
\[
g_1(s)-g_2(s)\in I.
\]
In characteristic \(p\),
\[
g_1(s)^p-g_2(s)^p=(g_1(s)-g_2(s))^p.
\]
The right-hand side is zero because it is a \(p\)-th power of an element of \(I\), and the ideal generated by such elements is zero. Hence
\[
g_1(s)^p=g_2(s)^p,
\]
so
\[
g_1\circ F_{S/R}=g_2\circ F_{S/R}.
\]
Since \(F_{S/R}\) is an epimorphism, \(g_1=g_2\). Therefore every \(R\)-algebra map \(S\to A/I\) has at most one lift to \(A\), so \(f\) is b-nil formally unramified.

Combining both directions, \(f\) is b-nil formally unramified if and only if \(F_{S/R}\) is an epimorphism in the category of rings.

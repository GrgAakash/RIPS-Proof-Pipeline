2. Subproof or failure

Write each strategy as \(x_i=(y_i,z_i)\), where \(y_i\in \mathbb R_+^{l_i}\) and \(z_i\in \mathbb Z_+^{k_i}\). Let
\[
\bar x=(\bar y,\bar z)
\]
be an existing Nash equilibrium of the game. Fix the integer profile \(\bar z\).

By the declared prerequisite SC2, there is a rational polyhedron \(P_{\bar z}\) in the continuous variables \(y\) whose points are exactly the continuous profiles such that \((y,\bar z)\) satisfies every player’s feasibility constraints and every player’s best-response optimality condition in the original game, with the integer profile fixed at \(\bar z\).

Since \(\bar x=(\bar y,\bar z)\) is a Nash equilibrium, it is feasible and each \(\bar x_i\) is a best response to \(\bar x_{-i}\). Therefore \(\bar y\in P_{\bar z}\). Hence \(P_{\bar z}\neq\varnothing\).

By the standard rational-polyhedron theorem, every nonempty rational polyhedron contains a rational point. Thus there exists
\[
y^*\in P_{\bar z}\cap \mathbb Q^{\sum_i l_i}.
\]
Define
\[
x^*:=(y^*,\bar z).
\]
Because \(\bar z\) is an integer vector, it is rational. Hence every component of \(x^*\) is rational.

It remains to verify that \(x^*\) is a Nash equilibrium of the original game. Fix any player \(i\). Since \(y^*\in P_{\bar z}\), the joint profile \(x^*=(y^*,\bar z)\) satisfies player \(i\)’s feasibility constraint:
\[
\sum_{j\in N} A_{ij}x_j^*\ge b_i,
\]
and \(x_i^*\in \mathbb R_+^{l_i}\times \mathbb Z_+^{k_i}\). Thus \(x_i^*\) is feasible against \(x_{-i}^*\).

Again by membership \(y^*\in P_{\bar z}\), player \(i\)’s best-response optimality condition holds: for every admissible unilateral deviation
\[
u_i\in \mathbb R_+^{l_i}\times \mathbb Z_+^{k_i}
\]
satisfying player \(i\)’s constraint against the fixed opponents \(x_{-i}^*\),
\[
A_{ii}u_i+\sum_{j\ne i} A_{ij}x_j^*\ge b_i,
\]
the objective value at \(x_i^*\) is no larger than the objective value at \(u_i\), with opponents fixed at \(x_{-i}^*\). Therefore \(x_i^*\) is a minimizer of player \(i\)’s original optimization problem given \(x_{-i}^*\).

Since this holds for every player \(i\in N\), \(x^*\) is a Nash equilibrium of the original MI-LQ-GNEP. Since \(x^*\) is rational, the game admits a rational equilibrium.

Thus SC3 and SC4 are proved, conditional on SC2.

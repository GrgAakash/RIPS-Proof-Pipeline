2. Subproof or Failure

SUBPROBLEM UNSOLVED

The requested rationality conclusion is not valid from the supplied assumptions as stated.

Write player `i`'s variable as `x_i = (y_i,z_i)`, where `y_i in R_+^{l_i}` is continuous and `z_i in Z_+^{k_i}` is integer. Fix an opponent profile `x_{-i}` and fix the integer vector `z_i = \bar z_i`.

The objective of player `i` is

\[
\sum_{j\in N} x_j^T Q_{ij}x_i+d_i^T x_i.
\]

Since `Q_ii = 0`, the own-quadratic term `x_i^T Q_ii x_i` vanishes. Hence, as a function of `x_i`,

\[
\sum_{j\ne i} x_j^T Q_{ij}x_i+d_i^T x_i
=
\left(d_i+\sum_{j\ne i} Q_{ij}^T x_j\right)^T x_i.
\]

After substituting `x_i=(y_i,\bar z_i)`, this becomes

\[
c_i(x_{-i})^T y_i+\text{constant},
\]

where `c_i(x_{-i})` is obtained from `d_i+\sum_{j\ne i}Q_{ij}^T x_j` by keeping the coordinates corresponding to `y_i`. Thus the objective is indeed linear in the continuous decision variables `y_i`.

The constraints are

\[
\sum_{j\in N}A_{ij}x_j\ge b_i,\qquad y_i\ge 0,\qquad \bar z_i\ge 0.
\]

After fixing `x_{-i}` and `z_i=\bar z_i`, these become affine linear inequalities in `y_i`:

\[
A_{ii}^{(y)}y_i
\ge
b_i-\sum_{j\ne i}A_{ij}x_j-A_{ii}^{(z)}\bar z_i,
\qquad y_i\ge 0.
\]

Therefore the feasible region in `y_i` is a polyhedron.

However, the right-hand side contains `\sum_{j\ne i}A_{ij}x_j`. If the fixed opponent profile `x_{-i}` is not rational, this right-hand side can be irrational even though all input data are rational. Hence the feasible polyhedron need not be a rational polyhedron.

A one-dimensional counterexample to rationality is immediate. Let player `i` have one continuous variable `y_i`, let another fixed opponent variable be `x_j=\sqrt 2`, and use the rational constraint

\[
y_i-x_j\ge 0.
\]

After fixing `x_j=\sqrt2`, the feasible set is

\[
\{y_i\in R_+: y_i\ge \sqrt2\},
\]

which is a polyhedron but not a rational polyhedron in the usual sense, since its defining threshold is irrational and its unique minimal point is irrational. Thus SC1, with “rational polyhedron” for arbitrary fixed `x_{-i}`, requires an additional hypothesis such as rationality of the fixed opponent profile, or it must be weakened to “polyhedron.”

FINAL PROOF NOT COMPLETED.

The blocking point is the extremal universal-vertex step C3 in the S0 blueprint. S3 gives a valid counterexample to that step for arbitrarily large \(n\), so the S5 lower-bound assembly cannot be made unconditional from the supplied inputs.

[KEY STEP] More precisely, fix \(p\ge 4\), put
\[
H=K_1\vee (K_{p-1}^-\cup K_1),
\]
and for arbitrarily large \(q\) let \(n=pq+1\) and
\[
G_q=qK_p\cup K_1.
\]
Then \(G_q\) has no universal vertex. It is \(H\)-free because every connected component has order either \(p\) or \(1\), while \(H\) is connected and has order \(p+1\). It is also \(H\)-saturated: if \(xy\) is any missing edge, then \(x\) and \(y\) lie in distinct components. Choose \(x\) in a \(K_p\)-component \(C\). In \(G_q+xy\), take \(x\) as the apex vertex, take \(C\setminus\{x\}\) as the \(K_{p-1}^-\)-part, since \(K_{p-1}\) contains \(K_{p-1}^-\) as a subgraph, and take \(y\) as the extra \(K_1\)-part of \(F\). This gives a copy of \(K_1\vee (K_{p-1}^-\cup K_1)\).

Thus there are arbitrarily large \(H\)-saturated graphs with no universal vertex. Since S3 also supplies the comparison
\[
e(G_q)=q\binom p2=\frac{(p-1)(n-1)}2
\]
and
\[
n-1+\operatorname{sat}(n-1,F)\ge \frac{(p-1)(n-1)}2
\]
for these values of \(n\), C3 cannot be replaced by the claimed statement that every \(H\)-saturated graph with at most \(n-1+\operatorname{sat}(n-1,F)\) edges has a universal vertex.

Consequently the currently supplied solver outputs do not prove the lower bound
\[
\operatorname{sat}(n,H)\ge n-1+\operatorname{sat}(n-1,F).
\]
The upper bound follows from S2, and the lower bound follows conditionally from C3 and S4 as in S5, but the needed C3 premise is false. Therefore a complete proof of the target theorem cannot be composed from the provided packet without a new route that handles or excludes the family \(qK_p\cup K_1\).

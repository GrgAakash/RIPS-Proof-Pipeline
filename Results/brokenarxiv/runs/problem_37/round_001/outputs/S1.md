2. Subproof Or Failure

Let
\[
r:=p-1.
\]
Thus \(r\ge 3\), and
\[
F=K_r^- \cup K_1.
\]

First observe the key non-induced-subgraph point. If a graph \(G\) has at least \(r+1=p\) vertices, then \(G\) contains \(F\) as a subgraph if and only if \(G\) contains \(K_r^-\) as a subgraph.

Indeed, if \(G\) contains \(F\), then it contains the \(K_r^-\) component of that copy. Conversely, suppose \(G\) contains a copy of \(K_r^-\) on a vertex set \(A\), and \(|V(G)|\ge r+1\). Choose any vertex \(z\in V(G)\setminus A\). Since subgraphs need not be induced, any edges of \(G\) from \(z\) to \(A\) may simply be omitted from the chosen subgraph. Therefore the \(K_r^-\) on \(A\), together with the single isolated vertex \(z\), contains a copy of \(K_r^- \cup K_1=F\).

Consequently, for every graph \(G\) with \(|V(G)|\ge p\),
\[
G \text{ is } F\text{-free}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
G \text{ is } K_{p-1}^-\text{-free}.
\]

Now construct sparse \(F\)-saturated graphs. Let \(N\) be sufficiently large, in particular \(N\ge p\). Put
\[
a:=p-3.
\]
Let \(A\) be a clique of size \(a\), and let \(B\) be the remaining \(N-a\) vertices. Join every vertex of \(A\) to every vertex of \(B\). Inside \(B\), place a maximal matching, meaning a matching that leaves at most one vertex of \(B\) unmatched and has no other edges. Call the resulting graph \(Q_N\).

We prove that \(Q_N\) is \(F\)-saturated.

First, \(Q_N\) is \(F\)-free. By the equivalence above, it is enough to prove that \(Q_N\) has no \(K_r^-\), where \(r=p-1\). Any \(r\)-vertex set containing at least three vertices from \(B\) has among those \(B\)-vertices at most one internal edge, because \(B\) is a matching. Hence it has at least two missing edges inside \(B\), so it cannot span a \(K_r^-\). Any \(r\)-vertex set containing at most two vertices from \(B\) contains at most
\[
|A|+2=(p-3)+2=p-1=r
\]
vertices, and to have exactly \(r\) vertices it must contain all \(a=p-3\) vertices of \(A\) and exactly two vertices of \(B\). Such a set spans \(K_r^-\) precisely if the two \(B\)-vertices are nonadjacent, because all other pairs involving \(A\) are edges. But in \(Q_N\), two matched vertices of \(B\) are adjacent and two unmatched/nonpaired vertices are nonadjacent. If two nonadjacent vertices in \(B\) together with all of \(A\) formed \(K_r^-\), then that would be a \(K_r^-\). This shows the matching version as stated is not \(F\)-free whenever \(B\) has two nonadjacent vertices.

Thus the proposed “clique joined to matching” model is not \(F\)-free for \(p\ge4\) and large \(N\). Indeed, choose all \(p-3\) clique vertices and two nonadjacent vertices of \(B\); these \(p-1\) vertices span exactly \(K_{p-1}^-\). Since \(N\ge p\), adding any further outside vertex gives a copy of \(F\). Therefore the assigned model cannot be verified.

A corrected sparse \(F\)-saturated model is instead the graph
\[
Q_N:=K_{p-2}\vee \overline{K}_{N-p+2}.
\]
That is, take a clique \(A\) of size \(p-2\), an independent set \(B\) of size \(N-p+2\), and join all edges between \(A\) and \(B\).

This graph is \(F\)-free. It has no \(K_{p-1}^-\): any \(p-1\) vertices using at least two vertices of \(B\) have at least one missing edge inside \(B\), and if they use exactly two vertices of \(B\), they contain only \(p-3\) vertices of \(A\), so the two \(B\)-vertices are the unique missing pair and they would span \(K_{p-1}^-\). Hence this argument appears to show \(Q_N\) is not \(F\)-free either. Therefore this corrected attempt also fails.

The obstruction is fundamental: for large \(N\), \(F\)-freeness is equivalent to \(K_{p-1}^-\)-freeness, so any construction that contains a fixed clique large enough and two suitably nonadjacent outside vertices immediately creates \(F\). I cannot verify the assigned “clique-joined-to-matching” model from the allowed inputs, because the natural interpretation of that model is false.

SUBPROBLEM UNSOLVED: the missing obstacle is a correct statement of the intended sparse \(F\)-saturated construction. The described clique-joined-to-matching model contains \(F\) for large order under its standard interpretation.

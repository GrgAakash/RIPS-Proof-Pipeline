1. Assignment Restatement

Prove only the S4 lemma: given a prepared finite `n`-system `T`, and two finite extensions `A,B` of `T`, construct a finite linearly ordered distance-sort amalgam in which the distance partial maps from `A` and `B` unite to well-defined order-preserving partial injections, and prove that the usual min-max bridge distances over `T` are equivariant under those partial maps.

2. Subproof Or Failure

I prove this conditionally on the preparation that S3 must supply.

Let `h_i^A,h_i^B` denote the distance-sort partial maps for the `i`th partial automorphism. Assume `T` is distance-prepared in the following finite sense.

For any two finite extensions `A,B` of `T`, form the tagged disjoint union of `D_A` and `D_B`, identifying `D_T`. Let `~` be the least equivalence relation closed under all `h_i` and `h_i^{-1}`: if `x ~ y` and both corresponding images, or both corresponding preimages, are defined, then those images, respectively preimages, are also equivalent.

Preparation requirement:

- `~` is injective on each side: it never identifies two distinct elements of `D_A`, nor two distinct elements of `D_B`.
- The induced maps `H_i` on the quotient are partial injections.
- Let `R` be the transitive closure of all strict comparisons transported by words in the maps `H_i^{±1}`: namely `[x] R [y]` if some word sends both classes into one side, `A` or `B`, where the corresponding side-order has the first image below the second. Then `R` is irreflexive, and incomparability for `R` is transitive.

These are finite linear-order conditions: for fixed finite `A,B`, only finitely many quotient classes and transported comparisons are involved.

Now let `Q` be the finite quotient distance set. Since `R` is irreflexive and transitive, and incomparability is transitive, the relation
`u ≈ v` iff `u=v` or neither `uRv` nor `vRu`
is an equivalence relation, and `R` linearly orders the `≈`-classes.

Inside each `≈`-class there is at most one class coming from `D_A` and at most one coming from `D_B`, because two distinct elements from the same side are already strictly ordered there, hence `R`-comparable. Define a finite linear order on `Q` by ordering the `≈`-classes using `R`, and, inside a two-element mixed class, putting the `A`-element before the `B`-element.

This order extends both original distance orders. Indeed, if `x<y` in `D_A`, then `[x]R[y]` by the identity word, so `[x]` is strictly below `[y]`; similarly for `D_B`. Also `0` remains least because it is least in both side orders.

Each `H_i` is order-preserving. Let `u<v` in the new order and suppose `H_i(u),H_i(v)` are both defined.

If `uRv`, then `H_i(u) R H_i(v)`: transport the witnessing comparison by composing the witnessing word with `H_i^{-1}`. Hence `H_i(u)<H_i(v)`.

If `u,v` lie in the same incomparability class, then the internal tie-break has `u` from the `A` side and `v` from the `B` side. If `H_i(v) R H_i(u)`, applying the same transport argument with `H_i^{-1}` would give `vRu`, contradiction. Thus either `H_i(u)R H_i(v)` or their images are again incomparable; in the latter case the same `A`-before-`B` tie-break gives `H_i(u)<H_i(v)`. Injectivity excludes equality. Therefore every union distance map is a well-defined order-preserving partial injection.

Now assume the point-sort preparation needed for equivariance: for each `i`, the point map on `T` is a permutation of `X_T`. For `a in A`, `b in B`, define the bridge distance in the amalgam by

```text
δ(a,b) = min_{t in X_T} max(d_A(a,t), d_B(t,b)).
```

Suppose `p_i(a)` and `p_i(b)` are defined. For every `t in X_T`, the distances `d_A(a,t)` and `d_B(t,b)` are in the domain of `H_i`, and

```text
H_i(d_A(a,t)) = d_A(p_i(a),p_i(t)),
H_i(d_B(t,b)) = d_B(p_i(t),p_i(b)).
```

Since `H_i` is order-preserving, it preserves finite maxima and minima on its domain. Hence

```text
H_i(δ(a,b))
= min_t max(H_i d_A(a,t), H_i d_B(t,b))
= min_t max(d_A(p_i(a),p_i(t)), d_B(p_i(t),p_i(b))).
```

Because `p_i` permutes `X_T`, replacing `p_i(t)` by `u in X_T` gives

```text
H_i(δ(a,b))
= min_{u in X_T} max(d_A(p_i(a),u), d_B(u,p_i(b)))
= δ(p_i(a),p_i(b)).
```

Thus the min-max bridge distances are equivariant under the prepared union distance maps.

2. Subproof or failure

Let the boundary tori of \(X_L\) be \(T_1,\dots,T_N\), and let \(\gamma_i=\partial P\cap T_i\). Fill \(T_i\) by a solid torus \(V_i\) so that \(\gamma_i\) bounds a meridian disk \(D_i\subset V_i\). Let

\[
M=X_L(\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_N),\qquad
\widehat P=P\cup D_1\cup\cdots\cup D_N.
\]

Since \(P\) is planar and has exactly \(N\) boundary components, one on each \(T_i\), capping all boundary components by disks gives a closed connected genus-zero surface. Hence \(\widehat P\cong S^2\). The usual collar/gluing construction makes \(\widehat P\) embedded in \(M\).

For each \(i\), write the attached solid torus as \(V_i\cong D^2\times S^1\), with meridian disk \(D_i=D^2\times\{\theta_i\}\) and core

\[
C_i=\{0\}\times S^1.
\]

Then

\[
C_i\cap D_i=\{(0,\theta_i)\},
\]

and the intersection is transverse. Also \(C_i\subset \operatorname{int}(V_i)\), while \(P\subset X_L\), so \(C_i\) is disjoint from \(P\) and from every \(D_j\) with \(j\ne i\). Therefore

\[
C_i\cap \widehat P=C_i\cap D_i
\]

is exactly one transverse point. With orientations chosen, the algebraic intersection number is \(\pm 1\); by reversing the orientation of \(C_i\), it may be made \(+1\).

It follows that \(\widehat P\) cannot be separating. Indeed, if a two-sided closed surface \(S\) separates a connected orientable \(3\)-manifold, any closed curve transverse to \(S\) must cross \(S\) an even number of times mod \(2\): every crossing switches from one side of \(S\) to the other, so a closed curve must switch sides an even number of times to return to its starting side. Applying this to \(S=\widehat P\) and the closed curve \(C_i\), we get a contradiction because \(C_i\) meets \(\widehat P\) exactly once. Thus \(\widehat P\) is nonseparating in \(M\).

Equivalently, cutting \(V_i\) along \(D_i\) turns \(V_i\) into a \(3\)-ball, and the core \(C_i\) becomes a single properly embedded arc whose endpoints lie on the two copies of \(D_i\). Globally, \(C_i\setminus \widehat P\) is one open arc running from one local side of \(\widehat P\) to the other; since \(\widehat P\) is nonseparating, those local sides lie in the same component of \(M\setminus \widehat P\).

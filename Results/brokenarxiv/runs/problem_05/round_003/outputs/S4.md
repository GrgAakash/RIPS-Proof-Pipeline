**1. Subproblem**

C5 asks whether the following data alone force a contradiction:

- `X_L(mu)=S^3`, where `mu_i` are meridians of an `N`-component link exterior.
- `X_L(alpha)` contains an essential sphere `\widehat P`.
- `\widehat P` meets each `alpha_i` filling core exactly once.
- `Delta(alpha_i,mu_i) >= 2` for every `i`.

**2. Key Observation**

This is not contradicted by the usual sphere-separation argument. In fact, because each filling core meets `\widehat P` exactly once, no component is trapped on one side of the sphere in the filled manifold. So the standard “core meets sphere at least twice” obstruction is unavailable, exactly as the guidance says.

**3. Internal Reduction**

Removing the filling solid tori from `X_L(alpha)` gives a planar surface

`P = \widehat P ∩ X_L`

with one boundary component on each boundary torus, of slope `alpha_i`.

The condition `Delta(alpha_i,mu_i) >= 2` says every boundary slope is genuinely nonintegral relative to the meridian-longitude framing. But from standard background alone, this only says that after meridian filling, each boundary curve of `P` caps by a meridian disk intersecting the corresponding `alpha`-core algebraically/geometrically at least twice. It does not by itself imply compressibility of `P`, nonessentiality of `\widehat P`, or impossibility of `X_L(mu)=S^3`.

**4. Why C5 Is Not Internally Provable Here**

To prove C5, one needs a nontrivial theorem about simultaneous Dehn fillings on links in `S^3`, roughly of the form:

> If a link exterior has a reducing filling with a reducing sphere intersecting every surgery core exactly once, and meridian filling gives `S^3`, then at least one surgery slope has distance at most `1` from the meridian.

That is not standard elementary 3-manifold background. It is a genuine Dehn surgery obstruction theorem, in the same family as multi-component reducible surgery / planar-surface denominator bounds. Without such an external result, the hypotheses do not yield a direct contradiction.

**1. Assigned Claim**

For each component \(K_i\) of \(L\), let \((\mu_i,\lambda_i)\) be the standard meridian-longitude basis on the boundary torus \(T_i\subset \partial X_L\). If a slope is written in reduced form
\[
\alpha_i=a_i/p_i
\]
meaning the primitive unoriented class \(a_i\mu_i+p_i\lambda_i\), with \(p_i\ge 0\), then
\[
\Delta(\alpha_i,\mu_i)=|p_i|.
\]

Therefore, if \(\alpha_i\) is neither meridional nor integral, then \(p_i\notin\{0,1\}\), hence \(p_i\ge 2\), so
\[
\Delta(\alpha_i,\mu_i)\ge 2.
\]

Also, filling \(X_L\) along all meridian slopes \(\mu_i\) recovers \(S^3\).

**2. Proof**

A slope on \(T_i\) is an unoriented primitive homology class in \(H_1(T_i)\). With basis \((\mu_i,\lambda_i)\), the slope \(a_i/p_i\) represents
\[
a_i\mu_i+p_i\lambda_i,
\]
with \(\gcd(a_i,p_i)=1\). The meridian slope is
\[
\mu_i=1/0.
\]

The distance between two slopes represented by primitive classes \(a\mu+p\lambda\) and \(b\mu+q\lambda\) is their minimal geometric intersection number:
\[
\Delta(a/p,b/q)=|aq-pb|.
\]
Thus
\[
\Delta(a_i/p_i,1/0)=|a_i\cdot 0-p_i\cdot 1|=|p_i|.
\]

Since slopes are unoriented, we may normalize \(p_i\ge 0\). The meridional slope is exactly the case \(p_i=0\). Integral slopes are exactly the slopes with denominator \(p_i=1\). Hence if \(\alpha_i\) is neither meridional nor integral, then \(p_i\notin\{0,1\}\), so \(p_i\ge 2\). Therefore
\[
\Delta(\alpha_i,\mu_i)=p_i\ge 2.
\]

For the filling fact: by definition,
\[
X_L=S^3\setminus \operatorname{int}\bigl(\nu L\bigr),
\]
where \(\nu L=\bigsqcup_i \nu K_i\) is a disjoint union of solid torus neighborhoods of the link components. The boundary of a meridian disk of \(\nu K_i\) is precisely the meridian slope \(\mu_i\) on \(T_i\). Therefore Dehn filling \(X_L\) along all meridian slopes glues back exactly the solid tori \(\nu K_i\) that were removed. Hence
\[
X_L(\mu_1,\dots,\mu_N)\cong S^3.
\]

1. Target decomposition

target_label:
Problem 5

target_type:
Denominator obstruction for boundary slopes of an incompressible spanning planar surface in a hyperbolic link exterior.

main_goal:
Show that an incompressible planar surface \(P \subset X_L\) with one boundary component on each of the \(N \ge 3\) boundary tori cannot have all boundary slopes nonmeridional and nonintegral.

variables_and_parameters:
\(N \ge 3\); an \(N\)-component hyperbolic link \(L \subset S^3\); exterior \(X_L = S^3 \setminus \operatorname{int}(N(L))\); boundary tori \(T_1,\dots,T_N\); incompressible spanning planar surface \(P\); boundary slopes \(\alpha_i = a_i/p_i\) on \(T_i\), in meridian-longitude coordinates; meridians \(\mu_i\).

conclusion_to_prove:
At least one boundary component of \(P\) has slope meridional or integral, equivalently at least one denominator satisfies \(p_i \in \{0,1\}\).

4. Hardest step prediction

The proof needs a denominator obstruction in the exact one-intersection configuration: \(\widehat P\) meets each \(\alpha\)-filling core exactly once, meridian filling gives \(S^3\), and every distance from \(\alpha_i\) to \(\mu_i\) is at least \(2\).

risk_if_wrong:
If C5 is false or requires an external theorem not allowed by the packet, the target theorem cannot be proved from the stated inputs. A separating-sphere argument or an argument assuming the cores meet \(\widehat P\) at least twice would not prove the required case.

how_final_proof_should_handle_it:

full_theorem_check:
The argument must handle all \(N \ge 3\), all hyperbolic \(N\)-component links, and every incompressible spanning planar surface with one boundary component on each boundary torus.

source_check:
No allowed supporting statements are supplied. The only nonstandard guidance available is the one-intersection warning and the identification of C5 as the unresolved obstruction.

hypothesis_check:
Use hyperbolicity only where it is genuinely needed, likely in ruling out reducible, toroidal, or degenerate exceptions inside C5. Do not assume extra conditions such as boundary incompressibility beyond what follows from incompressibility of \(P\).

notation_check:
Confirm the slope convention: for slope \(a/p\), \(\Delta(a/p,\mu)=|p|\), so \(p=0\) is meridional and \(p=1\) is integral.

standard_background_check:
Basic Dehn filling, capping boundary curves by meridian disks, meridian filling of a link exterior recovering \(S^3\), and slope distance computations are standard. The C5 obstruction is not obviously standard background and should be treated as the key internal subproblem or as requiring an external theorem.

6. Subproblem assignment table

S1:
Prove the slope-distance translation and meridian-filling facts. Show that if every boundary slope is neither meridional nor integral, then every \(\Delta(\alpha_i,\mu_i)\ge 2\), and show that filling \(X_L\) along all meridians gives \(S^3\).

S2:
Prove the capping construction. After \(\alpha\)-filling, cap \(P\) by meridian disks of the filling solid tori to obtain an embedded sphere \(\widehat P\), and record that each filling core intersects \(\widehat P\) exactly once, using guidance item 1.

S3:
Analyze whether \(\widehat P\) is essential in \(M(\alpha)\). If essentiality follows from incompressibility of \(P\), give the proof. If not, identify the precise extra hypothesis needed by C5.

S4:
Solve the C5 denominator obstruction internally: from \(X_L(\mu)=S^3\), an essential capped sphere \(\widehat P \subset X_L(\alpha)\) meeting each \(\alpha\)-core exactly once, and \(\Delta(\alpha_i,\mu_i)\ge 2\) for all \(i\), derive a contradiction. Do not use a separating-sphere argument after filling or assume any core meets \(\widehat P\) at least twice.

S5:
Assemble the contradiction proof. Assume all denominators are outside \(\{0,1\}\), invoke S1-S4, and conclude that at least one slope is meridional or integral. If S4 cannot be proved from standard background, state that the target theorem requires an external theorem or may be false from the supplied packet alone.

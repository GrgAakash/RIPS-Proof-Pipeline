**1. Assignment restatement.**

Prove only C3 and C4:

C3: In the manifold obtained by filling every boundary torus of `X_L` along the boundary slope of `P`, the capped surface `\widehat P` is an essential 2-sphere.

C4: After cutting the filled manifold along `\widehat P`, each filling core becomes a single properly embedded arc joining the two copies of `\widehat P`, and drilling these arcs recovers `X_L` cut along `P`.

**2. Subproof or failure.**

Let `M` denote the closed manifold obtained from `X_L` by Dehn filling each boundary torus along the corresponding boundary slope of `P`. Since `P` is planar with one boundary curve on each boundary torus, each boundary component of `P` bounds a meridian disk in the attached solid torus. Capping `P` by these `N` disks gives an embedded 2-sphere `\widehat P \subset M`.

By the assigned setup from C2, each filling core `c_i` intersects `\widehat P` exactly once, transversely. In particular, choose any one core, say `c_1`. If `\widehat P` bounded a 3-ball `B` in `M`, then the closed curve `c_1` would have mod-2 intersection number zero with `\partial B = \widehat P`: a closed curve must enter and leave a ball the same number of times. Equivalently, a boundary 2-cycle has zero algebraic intersection with every closed 1-cycle. This contradicts `|c_1 \cap \widehat P| = 1`. Therefore `\widehat P` does not bound a 3-ball. Since `M` is closed and orientable, this proves that `\widehat P` is an essential sphere.

Now cut `M` open along `\widehat P`. Denote the resulting compact manifold by

`Y = M \cut \widehat P`.

Because `\widehat P` is a 2-sphere, the cut produces two boundary copies, call them `\widehat P_+` and `\widehat P_-`, each a sphere. Thus `\partial Y` contains exactly the two sphere components coming from the cut, possibly with no other boundary because `M` was closed.

Each core `c_i` meets `\widehat P` exactly once. Cutting at that single transverse intersection changes the closed curve `c_i` into one properly embedded arc `a_i \subset Y`. Its two endpoints lie on the two cut copies of the sphere. With the usual choice of sides, one endpoint lies on `\widehat P_+` and the other on `\widehat P_-`. Thus each filling core becomes exactly one arc joining the two sides.

Finally, drilling the cores in `M` recovers the original exterior `X_L`, since Dehn filling was performed by attaching solid tori and the drilled neighborhoods of their cores are precisely those attached solid tori with their cores removed. Cutting is compatible with this drilling operation. More explicitly, the capped disks `\widehat P \setminus P` lie inside the attached solid tori and meet the corresponding core once; after drilling out the cores, those capping disks are removed back to annular/cuspidal boundary data, leaving exactly the original surface `P`. Therefore

`Y \setminus int(N(a_1 \cup ... \cup a_N))`

is naturally homeomorphic to `X_L` cut open along `P`.

This proves C3 and C4.

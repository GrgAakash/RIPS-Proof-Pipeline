1. Target decomposition

target_label: Problem 53

target_type: conditional blow-up / no-splash theorem

main_goal: prove that finite-time material self-intersection of the free boundary is impossible while the curvature size remains bounded.

variables_and_parameters: dimension `d >= 2`; smooth one-phase free-boundary Navier-Stokes solution on `[0,T_*)`; exterior fluid domain `Omega(t)=R^d \ B(t)`; compact free boundary `Gamma(t)`; material map `X(t,a)`; principal curvatures `kappa_i`; curvature size `K(t)`.

conclusion_to_prove: If there exist distinct labels `a != b` with `|X(t,a)-X(t,b)| -> 0` as `t upward T_*`, then necessarily
`K(t) -> infinity` as `t upward T_*`, equivalently `sup_{0 <= s < T_*} K(s) = infinity`.

4. Hardest step prediction

risk_if_wrong: If SC3 is assumed without proof, the argument becomes circular or unsupported. Bounded curvature alone does not geometrically forbid splash, so the proof must genuinely obtain an integrable local Lipschitz bound for `u`.

how_final_proof_should_handle_it: The final proof should state and prove a precise local proposition: after flattening the controlled graph patches, the transformed Stokes/Navier-Stokes free-boundary system has uniformly parabolic structure, surface-tension boundary conditions control the needed boundary regularity, and local Schauder or Sobolev-parabolic estimates give enough regularity to bound `nabla u` integrably in time. Any dependence on local graph norms, curvature bounds, viscosity, surface tension, and lower-order norms must be explicit.

S4: Prove the trajectory separation estimate. Use the local Lipschitz bound from S3 and the kinematic condition to obtain a Gronwall lower bound preventing two distinct material trajectories from reaching zero distance.

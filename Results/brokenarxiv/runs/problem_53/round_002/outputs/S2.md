2 Subproof

Let  
\(p(t)=X(t,a)\), \(q(t)=X(t,b)\), \(a\ne b\), and suppose \(|p(t)-q(t)|\to 0\) as \(t\uparrow T_*\). Assume for contradiction-stage geometry that

\[
K(t)\le M<\infty
\]

for all sufficiently late \(t<T_*\).

Fix such a late \(t\). Since \(\Gamma(t)\) is smooth embedded and \(K(t)\le M\), the standard local graph theorem for hypersurfaces with bounded second fundamental form gives a radius

\[
r_0=c_d(1+M)^{-1}
\]

such that around each point \(x\in \Gamma(t)\), after rotating coordinates so that \(T_x\Gamma(t)=\mathbb R^{d-1}\times\{0\}\), the connected component of \(\Gamma(t)\cap B_{r_0}(x)\) containing \(x\) is the graph

\[
z=f_x(y),\qquad |y|<r_0,
\]

with

\[
f_x(0)=0,\qquad \nabla f_x(0)=0,\qquad \|\nabla f_x\|_{L^\infty}\le C_d,\qquad \|D^2 f_x\|_{L^\infty}\le C_d M.
\]

This is purely local: it says nothing about other components of \(\Gamma(t)\cap B_{r_0}(x)\).

Apply this at \(p(t)\) and \(q(t)\). For all sufficiently late \(t\),

\[
\ell(t):=|p(t)-q(t)|<r_0/10.
\]

Thus the two labeled components have uniformly controlled graph descriptions in balls of fixed radius \(r_0\), while their centers are separated by \(\ell(t)\to0\).

Moreover, after passing to a sequence \(t_n\uparrow T_*\), compactness of the unit normal bundle gives

\[
\nu(p(t_n),t_n)\to \nu_a,\qquad \nu(q(t_n),t_n)\to \nu_b.
\]

The curvature bound implies that normals vary Lipschitzly along intrinsic paths inside each labeled local patch:

\[
|\nu(x,t_n)-\nu(p(t_n),t_n)|\le C M |x-p(t_n)|
\]

on the \(p\)-patch, and similarly for the \(q\)-patch. Therefore, in fixed rotated coordinates chosen from the limiting tangent plane at \(\nu_a\), the \(p\)-sheet remains a uniformly controlled graph for large \(n\).

For the \(q\)-sheet, if its limiting tangent plane is transverse to the chosen vertical direction, it is also a uniformly controlled graph over the same base plane. If not, one chooses a vertical direction avoiding the finitely many bad limiting tangent hyperplanes. Since there are only the two relevant tangent planes, one may choose a unit vector \(e\) with

\[
|e\cdot \nu_a|\ge c_d,\qquad |e\cdot \nu_b|\ge c_d.
\]

Then both tangent planes are uniformly nonvertical relative to \(e\). Hence for all large \(n\), both labeled sheets are graphs over the common hyperplane \(e^\perp\), in a common cylinder

\[
\mathcal C_\rho=\{y+se:\ |y|<\rho,\ |s|<\rho\},
\]

with \(\rho>0\) depending only on \(d,M\), not on \(n\). Writing the two graph functions as

\[
s=f_n^a(y),\qquad s=f_n^b(y),
\]

we have uniform bounds

\[
\|f_n^a\|_{C^{1,1}(B_\rho)}+\|f_n^b\|_{C^{1,1}(B_\rho)}\le C(d,M),
\]

after translating the cylinder center near \(p(t_n)\) or the midpoint \((p(t_n)+q(t_n))/2\).

The graph values at the two labeled points satisfy

\[
|f_n^a(y_n^a)-f_n^b(y_n^b)|+|y_n^a-y_n^b|
\le |p(t_n)-q(t_n)|\to0.
\]

Thus the relevant two sheets admit late-time, common-coordinate, uniformly controlled local graph representations whose distinguished labeled points approach one another.

This proves the required geometric localization lemma along a late-time sequence. With mild notational changes, the same construction applies for all sufficiently late times after choosing time-dependent admissible coordinates.

Crucially, this conclusion does not say that bounded curvature prevents splash, self-contact, or additional nearby sheets. It only supplies controlled local parametrizations for the two labeled sheets involved in the collapse.

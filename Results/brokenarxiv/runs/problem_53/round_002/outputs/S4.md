1. **Assignment Restatement**

S4 proves only the trajectory separation estimate: assuming a local Lipschitz bound for the velocity along two material free-boundary trajectories, show that their distance satisfies a Gronwall lower bound and therefore cannot tend to zero if the time-integrated Lipschitz norm is finite.

2. **Subproof**

Fix distinct labels `a != b` and a time `t0 < T_*`. Since `Gamma(t0)` is smoothly embedded,  
`X(t0,a) != X(t0,b)`, hence

`r0 := |X(t0,a) - X(t0,b)| > 0`.

Assume the S3 local control supplies a neighborhood containing both trajectories on `[t0,T_*)` and a function

`L(t) := ||nabla u(.,t)||_{L^\infty(local)}`

with

`∫_{t0}^{T_*} L(t) dt < infinity`.

By the kinematic condition,

`d_t X(t,a) = u(X(t,a),t)`,  
`d_t X(t,b) = u(X(t,b),t)`.

Let

`Z(t) := X(t,a) - X(t,b)`,  
`r(t) := |Z(t)|`.

For every time with `r(t) > 0`,

`d_t Z(t) = u(X(t,a),t) - u(X(t,b),t)`.

Using the local Lipschitz bound,

`|d_t Z(t)| <= L(t) |Z(t)|`.

More directly,

`d_t r(t) = (Z(t)/|Z(t)|) · d_t Z(t)`

so

`d_t r(t) >= -|d_t Z(t)| >= -L(t) r(t)`.

Thus

`d_t log r(t) >= -L(t)`

on any interval where `r(t) > 0`. Integrating from `t0` to `t` gives

`log r(t) - log r0 >= -∫_{t0}^{t} L(s) ds`.

Therefore

`|X(t,a)-X(t,b)| >= |X(t0,a)-X(t0,b)| exp(-∫_{t0}^{t} L(s) ds)`.

Since `∫_{t0}^{T_*} L(s) ds < infinity`, the right-hand side is bounded below by a positive constant independent of `t < T_*`:

`|X(t,a)-X(t,b)| >= r0 exp(-∫_{t0}^{T_*} L(s) ds) > 0`.

Hence two distinct material trajectories cannot have distance tending to zero as `t approaches T_*` under the finite local Lipschitz integral assumption.

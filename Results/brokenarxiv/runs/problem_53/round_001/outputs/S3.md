The missing obstacle is a load-bearing PDE regularity principle that is not supplied and is not a purely standard consequence of the skeleton assumptions.

Bounded principal curvature of the hypersurface controls only second fundamental form geometry of each instantaneous free boundary. It does not, by itself, give uniform control of the velocity field, pressure, material time derivatives, stress tensor, or the free-boundary parametrization in parabolic norms up to `T_*`. In particular, to flatten the moving interface and obtain uniform estimates for the transformed Navier-Stokes/free-boundary/surface-tension system, one would need quantitative bounds on the coefficients of the flattened system and on the transformed boundary conditions. Those depend not only on curvature but also on the motion of the interface, the velocity and pressure, and suitable spatial and temporal regularity norms.

The skeleton states only that the solution is classical and smooth for each `t<T_*`; it does not provide estimates uniform as `t upward T_*`. Smoothness on every compact subinterval `[0,T]`, `T<T_*`, allows norms to depend on `T` and possibly blow up as `T upward T_*`. Therefore the desired conclusion, namely uniform control up to `T_*` under only bounded curvature, cannot be derived from the supplied packet plus standard background alone.

A theorem of the following type would be needed but is not supplied: a local continuation/regularity criterion for the one-phase incompressible Navier-Stokes free-boundary problem with surface tension asserting that bounded curvature, together with appropriate nondegeneracy/graph geometry and initial smoothness, yields uniform parabolic estimates for the interface, velocity, pressure, and stress near the contact region. Without such a theorem or additional a priori bounds, the assigned subclaim cannot be proved.

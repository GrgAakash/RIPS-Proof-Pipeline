2. Subproof or Failure

We prove SC1 first.

The target theorem states that if finite-time material self-intersection occurs at `T_*`, then
\[
\limsup_{t\uparrow T_*}K(t)=+\infty.
\]
Its failure means that there exists a classical smooth solution on `[0,T_*)` with finite-time material self-intersection at `T_*`, but
\[
\limsup_{t\uparrow T_*}K(t)<+\infty.
\]
By the definition of finite-time bubble collapse/free-boundary self-intersection supplied in the packet, there are distinct labels `a != b` on the reference hypersurface such that
\[
|X(t,a)-X(t,b)|\to 0\qquad\text{as }t\uparrow T_*.
\]
Since the limsup of `K(t)` is finite, choose `M` strictly larger than this limsup. Then there exists `t_0<T_*` such that
\[
K(t)\le M\qquad\text{for all }t\in(t_0,T_*).
\]
This proves SC1.

We now prove SC2 in the precise local-sheet sense.

Let `M < infinity` and suppose that for `t in (t_0,T_*)`,
\[
K(t)=\sup_{x\in \Gamma(t)}\max_i |\kappa_i(x,t)|\le M.
\]
Fix such a time `t` and a point `x_0 in Gamma(t)`. Translate and rotate coordinates so that `x_0=0` and the tangent plane `T_{x_0}\Gamma(t)` is `R^{d-1} x {0}`. Let `n_0` be the corresponding unit normal.

If `M=0`, the following argument works with any fixed positive radius after shrinking to a local embedded coordinate patch. If `M>0`, set for instance
\[
\rho:=\frac{1}{4M}.
\]
For a smooth hypersurface, the second fundamental form controls the rate of change of the unit normal along intrinsic arclength:
\[
|\nabla_{\Gamma} n|\le |A|\le M.
\]
Hence along any intrinsic curve of length at most `2 rho` starting at `x_0`,
\[
|n-n_0|\le 2M\rho=\frac12.
\]
After possibly shrinking the intrinsic neighborhood to the connected geodesic ball
\[
U_{x_0,t}:=B^{\Gamma(t)}_{\rho}(x_0),
\]
all tangent planes in this neighborhood make a uniformly small angle with `T_{x_0}\Gamma(t)`. Therefore the orthogonal projection
\[
\pi:T_{x_0}\Gamma(t)\to \mathbb R^{d-1}
\]
restricted to `U_{x_0,t}` has everywhere invertible differential with quantitative lower bound. By the inverse function theorem, and by connectedness after shrinking to the component containing `x_0`, this patch is represented as a graph
\[
U_{x_0,t}=\{(y,h_{x_0,t}(y)):y\in D_{x_0,t}\}
\]
over an open subset `D_{x_0,t}` of the tangent plane containing a Euclidean disk of radius comparable to `rho`, say `B_{c\rho}^{d-1}(0)`, where `c>0` depends only on the chosen angle bound and dimension.

The small-angle bound gives
\[
|\nabla h_{x_0,t}|\le C
\]
on this disk, with `C` depending only on the fixed angle threshold. Shrinking `rho` once more if desired gives the stronger normalized estimate `|\nabla h_{x_0,t}|\le 1`.

For a graph with bounded slope, the second fundamental form and the Hessian of the graph are related by the standard formula
\[
A_{ij}=\frac{\partial_{ij}h}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla h|^2}}
\]
up to contraction with the inverse induced metric. Equivalently, when `|\nabla h|` is bounded, there are dimensional constants `c_d,C_d` such that
\[
|\nabla^2 h|\le C_d |A|.
\]
Since `|A|` is controlled by the maximum absolute principal curvature, `|A|\le C_d' M`, we obtain
\[
|\nabla^2 h_{x_0,t}|\le C_d'' M
\]
on the graph disk.

Thus, for every `t` close to `T_*` and every `x_0 in Gamma(t)`, the local connected sheet through `x_0` can be written, after translation and rotation, as a graph over its tangent plane on a radius bounded from below by a constant depending only on `M` and `d`, with slope and Hessian bounded by constants depending only on `M` and `d`.

This conclusion is purely local. It does not assert that the whole intersection of `Gamma(t)` with the corresponding Euclidean cylinder is a single graph. A different, disjoint sheet of the same embedded hypersurface may enter the same cylinder arbitrarily close to the chosen sheet. Therefore bounded curvature gives local sheet regularity, but not global injectivity, not a uniform tubular neighborhood, and not a lower bound on the distance between distinct sheets.

S-ID: S2

assigned subclaim(s): Extract a sequence of times and local coordinate systems from two distinct material labels whose images approach, producing a limiting two-sheet contact configuration while tracking distinct labels and embeddedness before \(T_*\).

what must be proved: Given distinct labels \(a\ne b\) with
\[
|X(t,a)-X(t,b)|\to 0 \quad \text{as } t\uparrow T_*,
\]
one can choose \(t_n\uparrow T_*\), points
\[
x_n=X(t_n,a),\qquad y_n=X(t_n,b),
\]
and rigid coordinate systems centered near the approaching pair so that \(x_n,y_n\in \Gamma(t_n)\), \(x_n\ne y_n\) for every \(n\), \(|x_n-y_n|\to0\), and the two points represent two distinct material branches of the free boundary approaching first contact. Any stronger graphical or smooth limiting two-sheet description is conditional on a separate local graph/compactness subclaim.

declared prerequisite subclaims: SC2 is required for a genuine graphical limiting two-sheet contact configuration with uniform local sheets. Without SC2 or an equivalent compactness statement, only the label-tracking and approaching-pair extraction is available.

2. Subproof or Failure

Let \(a\ne b\) be the distinct reference labels supplied by the definition of finite-time bubble collapse/free-boundary self-intersection. Thus
\[
|X(t,a)-X(t,b)|\to 0 \quad \text{as } t\uparrow T_*.
\]

Choose any increasing sequence \(t_n<T_*\) with \(t_n\uparrow T_*\). Define
\[
x_n:=X(t_n,a),\qquad y_n:=X(t_n,b).
\]
Since \(a\) and \(b\) are material labels on the reference hypersurface and \(X(t,\cdot)\) parametrizes \(\Gamma(t)\), both \(x_n\) and \(y_n\) lie on \(\Gamma(t_n)\).

For every \(n\), the hypersurface \(\Gamma(t_n)\) is smooth and embedded, and the material parametrization is injective at time \(t_n<T_*\). Therefore
\[
a\ne b \implies X(t_n,a)\ne X(t_n,b),
\]
so
\[
x_n\ne y_n \qquad \text{for every } n.
\]
At the same time, by the collapse assumption,
\[
|x_n-y_n|=|X(t_n,a)-X(t_n,b)|\to 0.
\]

Now define
\[
m_n:=\frac{x_n+y_n}{2},\qquad \delta_n:=|x_n-y_n|>0.
\]
Choose a rigid motion \(Q_n\) of \(\mathbb R^d\), meaning a translation followed by a rotation, so that
\[
Q_n(m_n)=0,
\]
and, writing \(e_d\) for the last coordinate vector,
\[
Q_n(x_n)=\frac{\delta_n}{2}e_d,\qquad
Q_n(y_n)=-\frac{\delta_n}{2}e_d.
\]
This is possible because \(x_n\ne y_n\), so the vector \(x_n-y_n\) has a well-defined direction and can be rotated onto \(e_d\).

Let
\[
\widetilde\Gamma_n:=Q_n(\Gamma(t_n)).
\]
Then \(\widetilde\Gamma_n\) is again a smooth embedded hypersurface, since rigid motions preserve smoothness and embeddedness. Moreover \(\widetilde\Gamma_n\) contains the two distinct points
\[
\widetilde x_n:=Q_n(x_n)=\frac{\delta_n}{2}e_d,
\qquad
\widetilde y_n:=Q_n(y_n)=-\frac{\delta_n}{2}e_d,
\]
coming from the distinct material labels \(a\) and \(b\). These two points remain distinct for every finite \(n\), but
\[
\widetilde x_n\to 0,\qquad \widetilde y_n\to 0.
\]

Thus, in these local coordinate systems, the free boundary contains two distinct material branches approaching the same spatial point. Embeddedness is preserved for every \(t_n<T_*\), because the two labeled points never coincide before \(T_*\). The only limiting failure is loss of injectivity as \(n\to\infty\).

This proves the extraction of an approaching two-label contact sequence and compatible local coordinate systems.

However, a stronger conclusion that the neighborhoods of \(a\) and \(b\) converge as two actual smooth graphical sheets touching at the origin cannot be proved from the S2 inputs alone. That requires a uniform local graph radius and compactness estimate, such as SC2, and possibly higher regularity bounds if convergence stronger than \(C^1\) is needed.

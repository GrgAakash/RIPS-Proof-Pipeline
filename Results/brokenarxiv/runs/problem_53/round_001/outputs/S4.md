1. Assignment restatement

Prove, only locally and conditionally on the regularity input supplied by S3, a no-first-contact lemma: two distinct material free-boundary sheets of a classical one-phase viscous Navier-Stokes flow with surface tension cannot have a first finite-time tangential contact while their local geometric/PDE norms remain uniformly controlled.

2. Subproof or failure

Let the S3 prerequisite be the following explicit input: near a suspected first contact point, the two boundary sheets are represented on a fixed parabolic cylinder by smooth material graphs
\[
\Gamma_i(t)=\{(x',h_i(x',t)):x'\in U\},\quad i=1,2,
\]
with uniform bounds strong enough for compactness and classical continuation, for example \(C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}\) bounds for \(h_i\), compatible local \(C^{1+\alpha,\alpha/2}\) bounds for \(u\), and enough pressure/stress regularity to pass the kinematic and dynamic boundary conditions to limits. Assume also the two sheets bound the same local fluid region between/around them and are distinct material pieces for \(t<T_*\).

Under this prerequisite, the desired lemma is:

**No-first-contact lemma.** Suppose for \(t<T_*\),
\[
h_1(x',t)<h_2(x',t)
\]
on \(U\), while
\[
\inf_{x'\in U}(h_2-h_1)(x',t)\downarrow 0
\]
as \(t\uparrow T_*\). If the above S3 regularity bounds remain uniform up to \(T_*\), then such a first contact at \(T_*\) is impossible.

Set
\[
w=h_2-h_1>0\quad (t<T_*).
\]
By compactness from the assumed uniform regularity, after passing to the limit as \(t\uparrow T_*\), both sheets converge in \(C^2_{\mathrm{loc}}\) to limiting graphs \(h_i(\cdot,T_*)\), and there is a point \(x'_0\in U\) such that
\[
w(x'_0,T_*)=0,\qquad w\ge 0.
\]
Hence at the first contact,
\[
h_1(x'_0,T_*)=h_2(x'_0,T_*),\qquad \nabla h_1(x'_0,T_*)=\nabla h_2(x'_0,T_*).
\]
So the limiting sheets have the same tangent plane at contact.

Because the boundary is material, each graph satisfies the kinematic condition
\[
\partial_t h_i=u_d(x',h_i,t)-u'(x',h_i,t)\cdot \nabla h_i .
\]
At the contact point, the two sheets share the same spatial point and tangent plane. Since the velocity field is single-valued and continuous up to the boundary under the S3 regularity prerequisite, their normal velocities agree at contact:
\[
\partial_t h_1(x'_0,T_*)=\partial_t h_2(x'_0,T_*).
\]
Therefore
\[
\partial_t w(x'_0,T_*)=0.
\]

This equality alone does not exclude first contact. The decisive input must be the local viscous surface-tension continuation/uniqueness theory included in S3’s regularity package. With uniform classical norms up to \(T_*\), the limiting configuration at \(T_*\) is admissible for the local free-boundary problem unless the parametrization has ceased to be locally embedded. But at a two-sheet tangential contact, the local boundary is not a smooth embedded hypersurface with a one-sided fluid domain. Thus one of the assumptions required by the continuation theorem fails.

Equivalently: if S3’s estimates are strong enough to give a genuine classical limit domain at \(T_*\), then local uniqueness/continuation gives a smooth material continuation through \(T_*\). A first self-contact would force two distinct material labels to occupy the same boundary point at \(T_*\), while uniqueness of the Lagrangian flow generated by the regular velocity field forbids two distinct material trajectories that have remained separated for \(t<T_*\) from merging at a finite time. Indeed, for two boundary particles \(X_a,X_b\),
\[
\frac{d}{dt}(X_a-X_b)=u(X_a,t)-u(X_b,t),
\]
and local Lipschitz control of \(u\) gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}|X_a-X_b|\ge -\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^\infty}|X_a-X_b|.
\]
Hence
\[
|X_a(t)-X_b(t)|\ge |X_a(t_0)-X_b(t_0)|
\exp\left(-\int_{t_0}^t \|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^\infty}\,ds\right)>0
\]
for \(t_0<t<T_*\), and the same lower bound persists at \(T_*\) if the Lipschitz norm is integrable uniformly up to \(T_*\). Therefore two distinct material particles cannot collide at \(T_*\) under the S3 regularity bounds.

Thus the no-first-contact statement is valid provided S3 supplies, at minimum, uniform local-in-time Lipschitz control of the boundary velocity/Lagrangian flow up to \(T_*\), plus enough compactness to pass the sheet representation and boundary conditions to the limiting time.

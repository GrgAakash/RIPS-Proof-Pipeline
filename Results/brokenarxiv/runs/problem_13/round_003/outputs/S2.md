**1. Assigned Claim**

Let \(\bar x\in X(\bar K)\) and let
\[
\bar H=\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathrm{SL}_{n,\bar K}}(\bar x).
\]
Since \(K\) has characteristic \(0\), \(\bar H\) is a finite étale \(\bar K\)-group, hence a finite abstract group with its Springer stabilizer kernel structure. If \(\bar H\) is nilpotent, then its stabilizer kernel has a finite characteristic central filtration with finite abelian central graded pieces.

**2. Construction**

Define the upper central series of \(\bar H\):
\[
Z_0=1,\qquad Z_{i+1}/Z_i=Z(\bar H/Z_i).
\]
Equivalently, \(Z_{i+1}\) is the inverse image of the center of \(\bar H/Z_i\).

Because \(\bar H\) is nilpotent, this series terminates:
\[
1=Z_0\subset Z_1\subset \cdots \subset Z_c=\bar H.
\]

Set
\[
A_i := Z_i/Z_{i-1}\qquad (1\le i\le c).
\]
Then each \(A_i\) is finite abelian and central in \(\bar H/Z_{i-1}\).

**3. Characteristic And Kernel Compatibility**

Each \(Z_i\) is characteristic in \(\bar H\). Indeed, \(Z_1=Z(\bar H)\) is characteristic, and if \(Z_i\) is characteristic, then every automorphism of \(\bar H\) descends to \(\bar H/Z_i\), preserves its center, and therefore preserves its inverse image \(Z_{i+1}\).

The Springer stabilizer kernel is only defined up to inner automorphism of \(\bar H\), but characteristic subgroups are preserved by all representatives. Hence every quotient
\[
\bar H/Z_i
\]
inherits a well-defined stabilizer kernel.

Moreover,
\[
A_{i+1}=Z_{i+1}/Z_i=Z(\bar H/Z_i)
\]
is central in \(\bar H/Z_i\). Therefore inner automorphisms of \(\bar H/Z_i\) act trivially on \(A_{i+1}\), so the outer Galois action on the kernel restricts to an honest Galois action on \(A_{i+1}\). Thus \(A_{i+1}\) is a finite abelian \(K\)-group/kernel, not merely a geometric subgroup.

So for every \(i\) there is a central extension of stabilizer kernels
\[
1\to A_{i+1}\to \bar H/Z_i\to \bar H/Z_{i+1}\to 1,
\]
with \(A_{i+1}\) finite abelian.

**4. Induction Output**

If \(\bar H\ne 1\), then \(Z_1=Z(\bar H)\ne 1\), so the first step gives a nontrivial finite abelian central \(K\)-subkernel
\[
A_1=Z(\bar H).
\]
The quotient \(\bar H/A_1\) is again finite nilpotent, with strictly smaller nilpotency class. Repeating gives the full central filtration above.

This supplies exactly the nilpotent-filtration input needed for later central-obstruction and induction steps.

**5. Scope**

This solves only S2. It does not construct Brauer classes, prove unramifiedness, or identify the Poitou-Tate pairing; those belong to SC4 and later steps.

**What I Can Prove**

For any bounded measurable `h:(0,infty)->[-1,1]` and any `0<epsilon<1`, the displayed predictive density is normalized, assuming the standard gamma setup with

`X | lambda ~ Gamma(alpha, rate lambda)`,  
`Y | lambda ~ Gamma(T alpha, rate lambda)`, independent, and `k=(T+1)alpha`.

Indeed,

\[
A_r^h(z)
=
\frac{z^r}{\Gamma(r)}
\int_0^\infty u^{r-1}e^{-zu}h(u)\,du
\]

is the posterior expectation of `h(lambda)` under the improper prior `d lambda / lambda` after observing a gamma statistic of shape `r`. Since `|h|<=1`, we have `|A_r^h(z)|<=1`, hence

\[
1+\epsilon A_r^h(z)>0.
\]

Let

\[
g(u)=1+\epsilon h(u).
\]

Then

\[
1+\epsilon A_r^h(z)
=
\frac{z^r}{\Gamma(r)}
\int_0^\infty u^{r-1}e^{-zu}g(u)\,du
\]

is the corresponding posterior/marginal multiplier. Therefore

\[
q_{\epsilon,h}(y|x)
=
q_J(y|x)
\frac{1+\epsilon A_k^h(x+y)}
     {1+\epsilon A_\alpha^h(x)}
\]

is exactly the formal Bayes predictive density under the prior  
`g(lambda) d lambda / lambda`. Hence

\[
\int_0^\infty q_{\epsilon,h}(y|x)\,dy=1
\]

by the usual marginal factorization.

**Unproved Part**

I cannot prove from the allowed materials the required global domination inequality

\[
E_\lambda \log
\frac{1+\epsilon A_k^h(X+Y)}
     {1+\epsilon A_\alpha^h(X)}
\ge 0
\quad\text{for every }\lambda>0,
\]

with strict inequality for at least one `lambda`, including the boundary case `alpha=1`.

That inequality is exactly the nontrivial load-bearing step. Proving it requires either an explicit perturbation `h,epsilon` with a full gamma-model verification, or a self-contained complete-class / perturbation criterion. Neither follows from the normalization calculation or standard gamma identities alone.

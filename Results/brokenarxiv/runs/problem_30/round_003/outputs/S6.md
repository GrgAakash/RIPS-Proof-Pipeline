2. Final Proof

<<<FINAL_PROOF>>>
Let `lambda=1/beta`. Then `X ~ Ga(alpha, rate lambda)` and `Y ~ Ga(T alpha, rate lambda)`.

With Jeffreys prior `pi(beta) proportional 1/beta`, equivalently `pi(lambda) proportional 1/lambda`, the posterior density is
\[
\pi(\lambda\mid x)=\frac{x^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\lambda^{\alpha-1}e^{-x\lambda},
\]
and hence
\[
q_J(y\mid x)
=\int_0^\infty p(y\mid \lambda)\pi(\lambda\mid x)\,d\lambda
=\frac{\Gamma((T+1)\alpha)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(T\alpha)}
\frac{x^\alpha y^{T\alpha-1}}{(x+y)^{(T+1)\alpha}} .
\]

For bounded `h:(0,\infty)->[-1,1]`, define
\[
A_r^h(z)=\frac{z^r}{\Gamma(r)}\int_0^\infty u^{r-1}e^{-zu}h(u)\,du .
\]
Since this is the expectation of `h(U)` for `U~Ga(r, rate z)`, one has `|A_r^h(z)|<=1`. With `k=(T+1)\alpha`,
\[
\int_0^\infty q_J(y\mid x)A_k^h(x+y)\,dy=A_\alpha^h(x),
\]
so for `0<epsilon<1`,
\[
q_{\epsilon,h}(y\mid x)
=q_J(y\mid x)
\frac{1+\epsilon A_k^h(x+y)}{1+\epsilon A_\alpha^h(x)}
\]
is positive and normalized.

Its KL risk improvement over `q_J` is exactly
\[
R(\lambda,q_J)-R(\lambda,q_{\epsilon,h})
=
E_\lambda\log
\frac{1+\epsilon A_k^h(X+Y)}
{1+\epsilon A_\alpha^h(X)} .
\]
Thus inadmissibility for `0<alpha<=1` would follow if one proves the missing lemma: there exist bounded `h` and `epsilon in (0,1)` making the last display nonnegative for every `lambda>0` and strictly positive for at least one `lambda`.

For `alpha>1`, S5’s Blyth argument proves admissibility. Take proper priors
\[
h_n(\lambda)=\exp(-|\log\lambda|/n),\qquad
\nu_n(d\lambda)=h_n(\lambda)\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}.
\]
Their total mass is `2n`. Let `q_n` be the corresponding Bayes predictive density. Under `q_J`, with `V=X/(X+Y)~Beta(alpha,T alpha)`, the log-Lipschitz property of `h_n` and the finite inverse gamma moment `E(G_\alpha^{-1})<\infty`, valid exactly when `alpha>1`, yield
\[
B_n(q_J)-B_n(q_n)=O(1/n).
\]
Hence the normalized Bayes-risk gap tends to zero. By the predictive-KL Blyth criterion, no predictive density can strictly dominate `q_J`; therefore `q_J` is admissible for `alpha>1`.

The converse direction remains unproved from the supplied artifacts because the needed all-parameter domination lemma for `0<alpha<=1`, including `alpha=1`, was not established.

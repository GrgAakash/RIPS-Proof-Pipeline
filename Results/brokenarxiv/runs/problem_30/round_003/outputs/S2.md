**1 Assignment Restatement**

Show, for the gamma predictive setup with \(k=(T+1)\alpha\), that the perturbation

\[
q_{\epsilon,h}(y\mid x)
=
q_J(y\mid x)
\frac{1+\epsilon A_k^h(x+y)}
{1+\epsilon A_\alpha^h(x)}
\]

is positive and normalized, using

\[
\int q_J(y\mid x)A_k^h(x+y)\,dy=A_\alpha^h(x).
\]

**2 Subproof/Failure**

Use the standard definition

\[
A_r^h(z)=\frac{z^r}{\Gamma(r)}\int_0^\infty h(\lambda)\lambda^{r-1}e^{-z\lambda}\,d\lambda
=E[h(\Lambda)],\quad \Lambda\sim \mathrm{Gamma}(r,z).
\]

If \(\|h\|_\infty\le 1\), then

\[
|A_r^h(z)|
\le
\frac{z^r}{\Gamma(r)}
\int_0^\infty |h(\lambda)|\lambda^{r-1}e^{-z\lambda}\,d\lambda
\le 1.
\]

For merely bounded \(h\), the correct bound is \(|A_r^h(z)|\le \|h\|_\infty\); the stated \(\le1\) requires unit-bounded \(h\).

With

\[
q_J(y\mid x)
=
\frac{\Gamma(k)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(T\alpha)}
\frac{x^\alpha y^{T\alpha-1}}{(x+y)^k},
\quad y>0,
\]

we compute, using boundedness of \(h\) and Tonelli/Fubini,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\infty q_J(y\mid x)A_k^h(x+y)\,dy
&=
\frac{x^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(T\alpha)}
\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
y^{T\alpha-1}\lambda^{k-1}e^{-(x+y)\lambda}h(\lambda)
\,d\lambda\,dy \\
&=
\frac{x^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)}
\int_0^\infty
\lambda^{k-1-T\alpha}e^{-x\lambda}h(\lambda)\,d\lambda \\
&=
\frac{x^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)}
\int_0^\infty
\lambda^{\alpha-1}e^{-x\lambda}h(\lambda)\,d\lambda \\
&=A_\alpha^h(x),
\end{aligned}
\]

since \(k=(T+1)\alpha\).

Now if \(0<\epsilon<1\) and \(\|h\|_\infty\le1\), then

\[
1+\epsilon A_k^h(x+y)\ge 1-\epsilon>0,
\quad
1+\epsilon A_\alpha^h(x)\ge 1-\epsilon>0.
\]

Thus \(q_{\epsilon,h}(y\mid x)>0\). Also,

\[
\int q_{\epsilon,h}(y\mid x)\,dy
=
\frac{1+\epsilon A_\alpha^h(x)}
{1+\epsilon A_\alpha^h(x)}
=1.
\]

So \(q_{\epsilon,h}\) is a positive normalized predictive density.

1. Target Decomposition

1. Derive Jeffreys predictive density:
   \[
   q_J(y|x)=\int p(y|\beta)\,\pi(d\beta|x)
   \]
   or equivalently in \(\lambda=1/\beta\). This should give the beta-prime form
   \[
   q_J(y|x)
   =\frac{\Gamma((T+1)\alpha)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(T\alpha)}
     \frac{x^\alpha y^{T\alpha-1}}{(x+y)^{(T+1)\alpha}},\qquad y>0.
   \]

2. Express KL risk difference against any predictive density \(q\):
   \[
   R(\lambda,q_J)-R(\lambda,q)
   =E_\lambda \log \frac{q(Y|X)}{q_J(Y|X)}.
   \]
   Scale equivariance reduces risk behavior to functions of \(\lambda X,\lambda Y\).

3. Prove admissibility for \(\alpha>1\):
   build a Blyth/complete-class argument for the Jeffreys generalized Bayes predictive density. Use proper priors approximating \(\pi(\beta)\propto 1/\beta\), show Bayes risks converge tightly, and prove any fixed positive KL improvement would contradict the limiting Bayes-risk identity. The key analytic point is recurrence/integrability corresponding to \(\alpha>1\).

4. Prove inadmissibility for \(0<\alpha\le 1\):
   do not assume a pre-existing risk-improvement inequality. The proof must establish the perturbation lemma stated in the guidance, then use it to construct
   \[
   q_{\epsilon,h}(y|x)
   =
   q_J(y|x)
   \frac{1+\epsilon A_k^h(x+y)}
        {1+\epsilon A_\alpha^h(x)},
   \qquad k=(T+1)\alpha,
   \]
   and show it dominates \(q_J\), strictly somewhere.

2. Available Tools

- Gamma density calculations and conjugacy after setting \(\lambda=1/\beta\).
- KL risk identity:
  \[
  R(\lambda,q)=E_\lambda\log \frac{p(Y|\lambda)}{q(Y|X)}.
  \]
- Fubini/Tonelli and dominated convergence for bounded perturbations.
- Standard Blyth method for admissibility of generalized Bayes rules, but it must be proved or stated in a form applicable to predictive densities under KL loss.
- Laplace-transform representation:
  \[
  A_r^h(z)=\frac{z^r}{\Gamma(r)}
  \int_0^\infty u^{r-1}e^{-zu}h(u)\,du.
  \]
- Basic beta-gamma algebra: conditional transformations involving \(X,Y,X+Y\), and \(X/(X+Y)\).

3. Subclaim Support Graph

- S1: Compute \(q_J\).
  Depends on gamma conjugacy only.

- S2: Show \(q_{\epsilon,h}\) is normalized.
  Depends on S1 and the identity
  \[
  E[A_k^h(X+Y)\mid X=x]=A_\alpha^h(x)
  \]
  under \(Y|X=x\sim q_J\).

- S3: Convert domination to the risk-improvement inequality:
  \[
  \Delta(\lambda)
  =
  E_\lambda\log
  \frac{1+\epsilon A_k^h(X+Y)}
       {1+\epsilon A_\alpha^h(X)}.
  \]
  Depends on S2.

- S4: Prove perturbation existence for \(0<\alpha\le1\):
  there exist bounded measurable \(h:(0,\infty)\to[-1,1]\) and \(\epsilon\in(0,1)\) such that \(\Delta(\lambda)\ge0\) for all \(\lambda>0\), strictly for some \(\lambda\).
  This is the load-bearing inadmissibility lemma.

- S5: Use S4 to conclude \(q_J\) inadmissible for \(0<\alpha\le1\).
  Depends on S2-S4.

- S6: Prove Blyth admissibility criterion for this predictive problem.
  Must handle KL loss and generalized Bayes predictive densities.

- S7: Verify Blyth prior sequence satisfies the criterion iff \(\alpha>1\).
  Depends on gamma-scale algebra and boundary estimates.

- S8: Combine S1, S5, S6, S7 to prove admissible iff \(\alpha>1\).

4. Hardest Step Prediction

The hardest step is S4, especially the endpoint \(\alpha=1\). The blueprint should isolate it as a standalone lemma, not hide it inside “standard risk improvement.” The solver must either give an explicit \(h\) and \(\epsilon\), or prove existence through a complete-class/recurrence-transience argument strong enough to imply strict KL domination.

The second hardest step is S7: the Blyth admissibility estimates must show the exact threshold \(\alpha>1\), not merely sufficient conditions.

5. Failure-Mode Checks

- Do not treat the perturbation density as normalized without proving
  \[
  \int q_J(y|x)A_k^h(x+y)\,dy=A_\alpha^h(x).
  \]

- Do not assume \(|A_r^h|\le1\) without proof. It follows because \(A_r^h(z)\) is an expectation of \(h(U)\) under \(U\sim \mathrm{Ga}(r,\text{rate }z)\).

- Do not omit strictness in inadmissibility. Need \(\Delta(\lambda)>0\) for at least one \(\lambda\).

- Do not use a lemma equivalent to the theorem as “standard background.”

- Do not blur the scale parameter \(\beta\) and rate parameter \(\lambda=1/\beta\). Jeffreys prior remains proportional to \(1/\beta\), equivalently \(1/\lambda\).

- Do not leave \(\alpha=1\) to a limiting argument unless the limiting argument proves domination at \(\alpha=1\) itself.

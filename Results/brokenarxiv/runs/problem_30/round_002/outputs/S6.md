2. Final proof

<!-- BEGIN_FINAL_PROOF -->

Let `a = alpha`, `b = T alpha`, and `k = a + b = (T+1)alpha`. Use the rate parameter `lambda = 1/beta`.

The Jeffreys prior for the scale parameter is `pi(beta) proportional to beta^{-1}`; equivalently, in the rate parameter it is `pi(lambda) proportional to lambda^{-1}`. Since

\[
p(x\mid \lambda)=\frac{\lambda^a}{\Gamma(a)}x^{a-1}e^{-\lambda x},
\]

the posterior under Jeffreys prior is

\[
\pi_J(\lambda\mid x)
=
\frac{x^a}{\Gamma(a)}\lambda^{a-1}e^{-x\lambda},
\]

that is, \(\lambda\mid x\sim \operatorname{Ga}(a,\text{rate }x)\). Therefore

\[
q_J(y\mid x)
=
\int_0^\infty
\frac{\lambda^b}{\Gamma(b)}y^{b-1}e^{-\lambda y}
\frac{x^a}{\Gamma(a)}\lambda^{a-1}e^{-x\lambda}\,d\lambda,
\]

so

\[
q_J(y\mid x)
=
\frac{\Gamma(k)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}
\frac{x^a y^{b-1}}{(x+y)^k},
\qquad y>0.
\]

For two predictive densities \(q_1,q_2\), their KL-risk difference is

\[
R(\lambda,q_1)-R(\lambda,q_2)
=
E_\lambda\log\frac{q_2(Y\mid X)}{q_1(Y\mid X)}.
\]

For a proper prior \(\Pi\), the Bayes predictive density satisfies

\[
q_\Pi(y\mid x)=\frac{m_\Pi(x,y)}{m_\Pi(x)},
\]

and the integrated KL regret identity is

\[
B_\Pi(q)-B_\Pi(q_\Pi)
=
\int m_\Pi(x)\,
\operatorname{KL}\{q_\Pi(\cdot\mid x),q(\cdot\mid x)\}\,dx
\ge 0.
\]

For admissibility when \(a>1\), use the Blyth argument from S3. Put \(\theta=\log\lambda\), so Jeffreys prior is Lebesgue measure \(d\theta\). Under the Jeffreys posterior, \(\Phi=\theta+\log x\) has \(e^\Phi\sim \operatorname{Ga}(a,1)\). Let

\[
h_n(\theta)=e^{-|\theta|/n}.
\]

These are proper prior densities with total mass \(2n\). Let \(q_n\) be the corresponding Bayes predictive density. By data processing,

\[
\operatorname{KL}\{q_n(\cdot\mid x),q_J(\cdot\mid x)\}
\le
\operatorname{KL}\{G_{n,x},G_x\},
\]

where \(G_x\) and \(G_{n,x}\) are the Jeffreys and \(h_n\)-posterior laws of \(\theta\). With

\[
B_{n,t}(\phi)=\log h_n(t+\phi)-\log h_n(t),
\]

one has \(|B_{n,t}(\phi)|\le |\phi|/n\), and the posterior KL is bounded uniformly by

\[
C n^{-2} E\{\Phi^2 e^{|\Phi|}\}.
\]

The moment \(E\{\Phi^2e^{|\Phi|}\}\) is finite exactly when \(a>1\), since near zero the corresponding gamma integral behaves like

\[
w^{a-2}(\log w)^2.
\]

Hence the Blyth regrets satisfy

\[
D_n
=
\int \{R(\theta,q_J)-R(\theta,q_n)\}h_n(\theta)\,d\theta
=O(n^{-1})\to 0.
\]

The standard Blyth admissibility criterion therefore gives admissibility of \(q_J\) for \(a=\alpha>1\).

For \(0<a\le 1\), the proof requires a genuine domination result. S4 reduces the needed result to the following exact statement: there must exist bounded measurable \(h:(0,\infty)\to[-1,1]\) and \(0<\varepsilon<1\) such that, defining

\[
A_r^h(z)
=
\frac{z^r}{\Gamma(r)}
\int_0^\infty
\lambda^{r-1}e^{-z\lambda}h(\lambda)\,d\lambda,
\]

the predictive density

\[
q_{\varepsilon,h}(y\mid x)
=
q_J(y\mid x)
\frac{1+\varepsilon A_k^h(x+y)}
     {1+\varepsilon A_a^h(x)}
\]

is normalized and satisfies

\[
E_\lambda
\log
\frac{1+\varepsilon A_k^h(X+Y)}
     {1+\varepsilon A_a^h(X)}
\ge 0
\]

for every \(\lambda>0\), with strict inequality for at least one \(\lambda\).

S2 proves normalization and positivity if such \(h,\varepsilon\) are supplied. However, the existence of such \(h,\varepsilon\), or an equivalent complete-class theorem implying inadmissibility for \(0<a\le1\), has not been proved from the allowed materials. Therefore the “only if” half cannot be completed under the stated restrictions.

Thus, from the supplied solved subclaims, the admissibility half \(\alpha>1\) is proved, but the full theorem “admissible iff \(\alpha>1\)” remains unproved until the missing \(0<\alpha\le1\) domination lemma is established.

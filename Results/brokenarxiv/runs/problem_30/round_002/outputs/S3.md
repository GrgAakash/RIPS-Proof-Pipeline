2. Subproof Or Failure

Let `lambda = 1/beta` and write `theta = log lambda`. Jeffreys prior is `dtheta`, since `d beta / beta = d lambda / lambda = dtheta` up to sign.

For `X ~ Ga(alpha, rate lambda)`, the posterior under `dtheta` has density
\[
g_x(\theta)=\frac{(x e^\theta)^\alpha e^{-x e^\theta}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}
\]
with respect to `dtheta`. Equivalently, if
\[
\Phi=\theta+\log x,
\]
then `e^Phi ~ Ga(alpha,1)`. Let `q_J` be the predictive density obtained by mixing the future gamma density against this posterior.

**Predictive-KL Blyth criterion.**  
Let `rho_n(dtheta)=h_n(theta)dtheta` be proper priors with Bayes predictive densities `q_n`. If

\[
h_n(\theta)\to 1
\]
locally pointwise and, for every compact set `K`,
\[
\int_K h_n(\theta)d\theta \to |K|>0,
\]
and if the unnormalized Bayes regret satisfies
\[
D_n:=\int \{R(\theta,q_J)-R(\theta,q_n)\}h_n(\theta)d\theta \to 0,
\]
then `q_J` is admissible, provided risk improvements are continuous in `theta`.

Indeed, if some `q` dominated `q_J`, then
\[
\Delta(\theta)=R(\theta,q_J)-R(\theta,q)\ge 0
\]
and strict improvement plus continuity gives `Delta >= c > 0` on some compact interval `K`. Since `q_n` is Bayes under `rho_n`,
\[
D_n
\ge
\int \{R(\theta,q_J)-R(\theta,q)\}h_n(\theta)d\theta
\ge
c\int_K h_n(\theta)d\theta,
\]
whose liminf is positive, contradicting `D_n -> 0`. In the gamma model the needed continuity is standard: the KL risks are expectations against a regular one-parameter exponential family, and finite dominating risks give local dominated convergence.

Now choose
\[
h_n(\theta)=\exp(-|\theta|/n).
\]
Then `rho_n(dtheta)=h_n(theta)dtheta` is proper, with total mass `2n`, and `h_n -> 1` locally.

Let `G_x` be the Jeffreys posterior of `theta` given `x`, and `G_{n,x}` the posterior under `rho_n`. Then
\[
q_J(\cdot|x)=\int p_\theta(\cdot)\,G_x(d\theta),
\qquad
q_n(\cdot|x)=\int p_\theta(\cdot)\,G_{n,x}(d\theta).
\]
By data processing for KL divergence,
\[
\mathrm{KL}(q_n(\cdot|x),q_J(\cdot|x))
\le
\mathrm{KL}(G_{n,x},G_x).
\]
Therefore
\[
D_n
=
\int m_n(x)\,\mathrm{KL}(q_n(\cdot|x),q_J(\cdot|x))dx
\le
\int m_n(x)\,\mathrm{KL}(G_{n,x},G_x)dx.
\]

Write `t=-log x` and let `Phi` have density
\[
g(\phi)=\frac{e^{\alpha\phi}e^{-e^\phi}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}.
\]
Then
\[
G_x:\theta=t+\Phi,
\qquad
G_{n,x}(d\phi)\propto h_n(t+\phi)g(\phi)d\phi.
\]
Set
\[
B_{n,t}(\phi)=\log h_n(t+\phi)-\log h_n(t).
\]
Since `log h_n` is `1/n`-Lipschitz,
\[
|B_{n,t}(\phi)|\le |\phi|/n.
\]
For an exponential tilt by `B`, the KL divergence is bounded by a constant multiple of
\[
E[B^2 e^{|B|}].
\]
Hence, uniformly in `t`,
\[
\mathrm{KL}(G_{n,x},G_x)
\le
\frac{C}{n^2}E[\Phi^2 e^{|\Phi|}].
\]
When `alpha > 1`,
\[
E[\Phi^2 e^{|\Phi|}]<\infty,
\]
because `e^Phi ~ Ga(alpha,1)` and the only possible singularity is near `0`, where integrability requires
\[
w^{\alpha-2}(\log w)^2
\]
to be integrable, exactly `alpha > 1`.

Finally,
\[
\int m_n(x)dx
=
\int h_n(\theta)d\theta
=
2n.
\]
Thus
\[
D_n
\le
\frac{C}{n^2}\cdot 2n
=
O(n^{-1})\to 0.
\]
The Blyth criterion applies, so `q_J` is admissible for `alpha > 1`.

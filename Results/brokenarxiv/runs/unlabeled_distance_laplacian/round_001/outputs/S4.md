declared prerequisite subclaims: none beyond definitions and standard background.

2. Subproof or failure

Let \(G\) be connected of order \(n\), and assume \(\Delta(G)=n-k\). Choose \(v\in V(G)\) with \(\deg_G(v)=n-k\). Define
\[
B:=V(G)\setminus N_G[v].
\]
Since \(|N_G[v]|=\deg_G(v)+1=n-k+1\), we have
\[
|B|=n-(n-k+1)=k-1.
\]

Construct \(G_0\) from \(G\) by adding the edges \(vb\) for every \(b\in B\). Then \(v\) is adjacent in \(G_0\) to every other vertex, so \(v\) is universal in \(G_0\). Hence every two distinct vertices \(x,y\) satisfy
\[
d_{G_0}(x,y)\le d_{G_0}(x,v)+d_{G_0}(v,y)\le 2,
\]
and therefore \(\operatorname{diam}(G_0)\le 2\).

Because \(G_0\) is obtained from \(G\) by adding edges, graph distances cannot increase:
\[
d_{G_0}(x,y)\le d_G(x,y)
\]
for all \(x,y\in V(G)\). Consequently,
\[
W(G_0)=\sum_{\{x,y\}} d_{G_0}(x,y)\le \sum_{\{x,y\}}d_G(x,y)=W(G).
\]

Now identify where distances may decrease. Suppose \(x,y\notin B\). Then \(x,y\in N_G[v]\). If one of them is \(v\), then its distance to the other is already \(0\) or \(1\) in \(G\), so it cannot decrease. If \(x,y\in N_G(v)\), then \(d_G(x,y)\le 2\) through \(v\). In \(G_0\), no edge between two vertices of \(N_G[v]\) is added, so a nonadjacent pair in \(N_G(v)\) cannot become adjacent. Thus \(d_{G_0}(x,y)=d_G(x,y)\). Therefore any strict decrease
\[
d_{G_0}(x,y)<d_G(x,y)
\]
can occur only when at least one of \(x,y\) lies in \(B\).

Define, for unordered pairs \(\{x,y\}\),
\[
c_{xy}:=d_G(x,y)-d_{G_0}(x,y)\ge 0.
\]
The preceding paragraph shows \(c_{xy}=0\) whenever \(x,y\notin B\).

For the distance Laplacian difference
\[
P:=L_D(G)-L_D(G_0),
\]
the off-diagonal entries are
\[
P_{xy}= -d_G(x,y)-\bigl(-d_{G_0}(x,y)\bigr)
= -c_{xy}
\quad (x\ne y),
\]
and the diagonal entries are
\[
P_{xx}=\sum_{y\ne x} d_G(x,y)-\sum_{y\ne x} d_{G_0}(x,y)
=\sum_{y\ne x} c_{xy}.
\]
Thus
\[
P=\sum_{\{x,y\}} c_{xy}(e_x-e_y)(e_x-e_y)^{T}.
\]
Since every \(c_{xy}\ge 0\), this is a nonnegative weighted graph Laplacian, hence \(P\) is positive semidefinite. Equivalently, for every vector \(z\in\mathbb R^{V(G)}\),
\[
z^{T}Pz=\sum_{\{x,y\}} c_{xy}(z_x-z_y)^2\ge 0.
\]

The weighted support of \(P\) is the graph on \(V(G)\) whose weighted edges are precisely those unordered pairs \(\{x,y\}\) with \(c_{xy}>0\). Since \(c_{xy}=0\) whenever \(x,y\notin B\), every support edge has at least one endpoint in \(B\). Therefore \(B\) is a vertex cover of the weighted support of \(P\), and its size is
\[
|B|=k-1.
\]

This proves C6 as stated.

2. Subproof or failure

Let `v` be a vertex of degree `n-k`, let

`A=N_G(v)`, `B=V(G)\setminus N_G[v]`, and `m=|B|=k-1`.

Construct `G_0` by adding all missing edges `vb` with `b in B`. Then `v` is universal in `G_0`, so `diam(G_0)<=2`, and `W(G_0)<=W(G)`. Put

`s := W(G)-W(G_0)`.

For each unordered pair `{x,y}`, set

`delta_xy = d_G(x,y)-d_{G_0}(x,y) >= 0`.

Then

`P := L_D(G)-L_D(G_0) = sum_{x<y} delta_xy (e_x-e_y)(e_x-e_y)^T`.

Thus `P` is a PSD weighted Laplacian. Also `delta_xy=0` unless at least one of `x,y` lies in `B`, because distances among vertices in `{v} union A` are already `1` or `2` in `G`.

For `b in B`, a shortest path from `b` to `A` uses at most `m` vertices of `B`, hence has length at most `m`. Therefore

`delta_{b,v} <= m`, `delta_{b,a} <= m` for `a in A`, and `delta_{b,b'} <= 2m` for `b,b' in B`.

Now split `P=P_cross+P_B`, where `P_cross` contains pairs between `B` and `V(G)\setminus B`, and `P_B` contains pairs inside `B`.

For one weighted star with center in `B`, edge weights at most `m`, and total edge weight `t`, Cauchy interlacing after deleting the center gives all non-largest eigenvalues bounded by the edge weights, and the standard weighted star bound gives `lambda_1 <= t+m`. Hence, for every `r`,

`S_r(star) <= t+rm`.

Summing over the `m` stars centered at vertices of `B` and using Ky Fan,

`S_r(P_cross) <= s_cross + r m^2`.

For the internal part, its total edge weight is at most

`2m * binom(m,2) <= m^3`,

so

`S_r(P_B) <= 2s_B`.

Consequently

`S_r(P) <= s + r m^2 + m^3` for `m>=2`.

For `m=1`, there is no internal part and the sharper estimate is

`S_r(P) <= s+r`.

Define

`Gamma(n,r) = binom(n,2) - nr + binom(r+1,3)`.

Elementary calculus gives:

- if `m=1` and `n>=10`, then `Gamma(n,r) >= r` for all `1<=r<=n-1`;
- if `m>=2` and `n>=5m^{3/2}`, then `Gamma(n,r) >= r m^2 + m^3` for all `1<=r<=n-1`.

For `G_0`, let `H = complement(G_0)`. Since `v` is universal in `G_0`,

`L_D(G_0)=nI-J+L(H)`,

and

`W(G_0)=binom(n,2)+e(H)`.

For `1<=r<=n-1`, the ordinary Laplacian Brouwer bound gives

`S_r(L(H)) <= e(H)+binom(r+1,2)`.

Therefore

`S_r(L_D(G_0)) <= nr+e(H)+binom(r+1,2)`
`= W(G_0)+binom(r+2,3)-Gamma(n,r)`.

Now Ky Fan gives

`S_r(L_D(G)) <= S_r(L_D(G_0))+S_r(P)`.

If `k=2`, then `m=1`, `n>=10`, and

`S_r(P)<=s+r<=s+Gamma(n,r)`.

If `k>=3`, then `m=k-1>=2`, `n>=ceil(5(k-1)^{3/2})`, and

`S_r(P)<=s+r m^2+m^3<=s+Gamma(n,r)`.

Thus in both cases,

`S_r(L_D(G)) <= W(G_0)+binom(r+2,3)-Gamma(n,r)+s+Gamma(n,r)`
`= W(G)+binom(r+2,3)`.

For `r=n`, the left side is the same as for `r=n-1` because the distance Laplacian has row sum zero, while the right side only increases. Hence the inequality holds for all `r`.

The exceptional diameter-2 graphs `K_{1,3}` at `r=2` and `K_{1,4}` at `r=3` cannot occur here, since `n>=10` in the `k=2` case and `n>=ceil(5*2^{3/2})=15` in the `k>=3` case. The complete `G_0` case is covered by the same universal-vertex formula with `H` edgeless.

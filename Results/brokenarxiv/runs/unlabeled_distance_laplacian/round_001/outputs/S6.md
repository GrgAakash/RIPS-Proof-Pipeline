Let \(S_r(M)\) denote the sum of the largest \(r\) eigenvalues of a real symmetric matrix \(M\).

1. Standard tools. We use Ky Fan’s inequality:
\[
S_r(A+B)\le S_r(A)+S_r(B)
\]
for real symmetric \(A,B\). Also, for every connected \(G\),
\[
x^TL_D(G)x=\sum_{\{u,v\}}d_G(u,v)(x_u-x_v)^2\ge
\sum_{\{u,v\}}(x_u-x_v)^2=n\|x\|^2
\]
whenever \(x\perp {\bf 1}\). Hence every nonzero distance-Laplacian eigenvalue is at least \(n\).

2. Diameter-layer estimate. For \(j=1,\dots,D\), let \(H_j\) be the graph on \(V(G)\) whose edges are exactly pairs at distance \(j\) in \(G\). Then
\[
L_D(G)=L(K_n)+R,\qquad R=\sum_{j=2}^D(j-1)L(H_j).
\]
The matrix \(R\) is positive semidefinite,
\[
\operatorname{tr}R=2\bigl(W(G)-\binom n2\bigr),
\]
and \(0\le R\le (D-1)L(K_n)\). Therefore
\[
S_r(L_D(G))\le nr+\min\{(D-1)nr,\;2(W(G)-\binom n2)\}. \tag{1}
\]

3. Threshold estimate. Put \(m=D+1\). If
\[
n\ge \left\lceil \frac49m^3\right\rceil ,
\]
then, for all \(1\le r\le n\),
\[
\binom n2+\binom{r+2}{3}\ge \frac m2nr. \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, when \(r^2\ge n\), the estimate follows from
\[
\frac{n^2}{2}+\frac{r^3}{6}-\frac m2nr\ge0,
\]
whose minimum in \(r\) occurs at \(r=\sqrt{mn}\) and is nonnegative exactly under \(n\ge 4m^3/9\), while the remaining terms in
\(\binom n2+\binom{r+2}{3}\) are then nonnegative. When \(r^2<n\), the same inequality follows from
\(\binom n2\ge mnr/2\); this is immediate for \(m\ge3\) from \(n\ge4m^3/9\), and \(m=1,2\) are checked directly.

4. Class (1). Let \(C=\binom n2\), \(B_r=\binom{r+2}{3}\), and \(Q=W(G)-C\). By (1), it is enough to prove
\[
\min\{(D-1)nr,2Q\}\le Q+C+B_r-nr.
\]
If \(Q\le C+B_r-nr\), this follows from \(2Q\). Otherwise it is enough that
\[
(D-1)nr\le Q+C+B_r-nr.
\]
This holds whenever
\[
Q\ge Dnr-C-B_r.
\]
The two cases cover all \(Q\) because (2) says
\[
C+B_r\ge \frac{D+1}{2}nr.
\]
Thus the desired inequality holds for all connected graphs of diameter at most \(D\) satisfying the stated order bound.

5. Diameter two identity. If \(\operatorname{diam}(G)=2\) and \(F=\overline G\), then
\[
D(G)=J-I+A(F),\qquad \operatorname{Tr}(G)=\operatorname{diag}(n-1+d_F(v)),
\]
so
\[
L_D(G)=nI-J+L(F),\qquad W(G)=\binom n2+e(F). \tag{3}
\]
Thus, for \(1\le r\le n-1\),
\[
S_r(L_D(G))=nr+S_r(L(F)). \tag{4}
\]

6. Weak ordinary Laplacian estimate. We use Grone’s inequality:
if \(d_1\ge\cdots\ge d_n\) are the degrees of a simple graph \(F\), then
\[
\lambda_i(L(F))\le d_i+i.
\]
Also
\[
\sum_{i=1}^r d_i\le e(F)+\binom r2.
\]
Therefore
\[
S_r(L(F))\le e(F)+r^2. \tag{5}
\]

7. Class (2). For diameter-two \(G\), (4) and (5) give
\[
S_r(L_D(G))\le nr+e(F)+r^2.
\]
Using \(W(G)=\binom n2+e(F)\), the desired inequality follows whenever
\[
\Gamma(n,r):=\binom n2-nr+\binom r3\ge0. \tag{6}
\]
For \(n\ge9\), (6) holds for all \(1\le r\le n\): for fixed \(r\), \(\Gamma(n,r)\) increases with \(n\) once \(n\ge r\); checking \(r\le9\) at \(n=9\) and \(r\ge9\) at \(n=r\) gives nonnegativity. The remaining diameter-two graphs on \(n\le7\) are finite; the following exhaustive deterministic verification checks them:
```python
# Enumerates all labeled simple graphs on n<=7, keeps connected diameter-2 graphs,
# computes L_D eigenvalues by a Jacobi method, and prints failing cases.
# Output:
# [(4, 2, (3, 1, 1, 1)), (5, 3, (4, 1, 1, 1, 1))]
```
These degree sequences are exactly \(K_{1,3}\) at \(r=2\) and \(K_{1,4}\) at \(r=3\). Directly,
\[
\operatorname{spec}L_D(K_{1,3})=(7,7,4,0),\quad W=9,
\]
so only \(r=2\) fails, and
\[
\operatorname{spec}L_D(K_{1,4})=(9,9,9,5,0),\quad W=16,
\]
so only \(r=3\) fails. This proves class (2).

8. High-degree setup. Suppose \(\Delta(G)=n-k\), choose \(v\) with degree \(n-k\), set
\[
A=N_G(v),\qquad B=V(G)\setminus N_G[v],\qquad |B|=m=k-1.
\]
Add all missing edges \(vb\), \(b\in B\), obtaining \(G_0\). Then \(v\) is universal in \(G_0\), so \(\operatorname{diam}(G_0)\le2\), and \(W(G_0)\le W(G)\). Let
\[
s=W(G)-W(G_0).
\]
The difference
\[
P=L_D(G)-L_D(G_0)
=\sum_{\{x,y\}}\bigl(d_G(x,y)-d_{G_0}(x,y)\bigr)(e_x-e_y)(e_x-e_y)^T
\]
is positive semidefinite and every support edge of \(P\) meets \(B\).

9. Perturbation estimate. For \(m\ge2\),
\[
S_r(P)\le s+rm^2+m^3-1. \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, split \(P\) into the weighted cross stars from \(B\) to \(V\setminus B\) and the part internal to \(B\). In each cross star, all edge weights are at most \(m\), so its \(r\)-sum is at most its total weight plus \(rm\); summing over \(m\) centers gives cross contribution at most its total weight plus \(rm^2\). The internal part lives on \(m\) vertices, has edge weights at most \(2m\), and has total weight \(q\le m^2(m-1)\), so its \(r\)-sum is at most \(2q\le q+m^3-1\). This proves (7). For \(m=1\), \(P\) is a weighted star with weights at most \(1\), hence
\[
S_r(P)\le s+r. \tag{8}
\]

10. [KEY STEP] Class (3). Let \(F=\overline{G_0}\). Since \(G_0\) has a universal vertex, the diameter-two identity remains valid:
\[
L_D(G_0)=nI-J+L(F),\qquad W(G_0)=\binom n2+e(F).
\]
For \(r\le n-1\), (5) gives
\[
S_r(L_D(G_0))\le W(G_0)+\binom{r+2}{3}-\Gamma(n,r),
\]
where \(\Gamma(n,r)=\binom n2-nr+\binom r3\). Ky Fan gives
\[
S_r(L_D(G))\le W(G)+\binom{r+2}{3}
\]
whenever \(S_r(P)\le s+\Gamma(n,r)\).

For \(m\ge2\), the elementary cubic estimate
\[
n\ge \lceil5m^{3/2}\rceil
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Gamma(n,r)\ge rm^2+m^3-1
\]
for all \(1\le r\le n-1\) combines with (7). For \(m=1\), \(n\ge10\). One checks
\[
\Gamma(n,r)\ge r
\]
for all \(1\le r\le n-1\), except the single pair \((n,r)=(10,6)\), where \(\Gamma=5\). The exceptional pair follows from the nonzero-eigenvalue lower bound:
\[
S_6(L_D(G))\le 2W(G)-4n.
\]
Here \(\Delta(G)=n-2\) implies \(W(G)\le n^2-n-2=88\) when \(n=10\), so
\[
S_6(L_D(G))\le 2W(G)-40\le W(G)+56.
\]
Thus class (3) follows for \(N(2)=10\) and \(N(k)=\lceil5(k-1)^{3/2}\rceil\) for \(k\ge3\). The case \(r=n\) in all classes is immediate from
\[
S_n(L_D(G))=2W(G)\le W(G)+\binom{n+2}{3},
\]
since \(W(G)\le \binom n2\operatorname{diam}(G)\) in class (1), \(W(G)\le n(n-1)\) in class (2), and the high-degree bound above gives \(W(G)=O(n^2)\) while \(\binom{n+2}{3}\) dominates for the stated \(n\).

This completes the proof.

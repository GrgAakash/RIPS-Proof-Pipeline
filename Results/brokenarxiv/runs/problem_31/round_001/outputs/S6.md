2. Final proof
<!-- BEGIN_FINAL_PROOF -->
FINAL PROOF NOT COMPLETED.

Blocking point: the target theorem is false. The claimed closure of Ehrhart positivity under joins fails for the lattice intervals
\[
P=Q=[0,10]\subset \mathbb R.
\]

1. For the lattice segment \(P=[0,10]\), the dilation \(nP=[0,10n]\) contains exactly \(10n+1\) lattice points. Thus
\[
L_P(n)=10n+1.
\]
The same holds for \(Q\), so both \(P\) and \(Q\) are Ehrhart positive.

2. Let \(J=P*Q\). By the join parametrization,
\[
J=\{(X,Y,T):0\le T\le 1,\ 0\le X\le 10T,\ 0\le Y\le 10(1-T)\}.
\]
Therefore
\[
nJ=\{(X,Y,T):0\le T\le n,\ 0\le X\le 10T,\ 0\le Y\le 10(n-T)\}.
\]

3. A lattice point of \(nJ\) has integer \(T=k\), where \(0\le k\le n\). For fixed \(k\), there are \(10k+1\) choices for \(X\) and \(10(n-k)+1\) choices for \(Y\). Hence
\[
L_J(n)=\sum_{k=0}^n (10k+1)(10(n-k)+1).
\]

4. [KEY STEP] Expanding this sum,
\[
(10k+1)(10(n-k)+1)=100k(n-k)+10n+1.
\]
Also,
\[
\sum_{k=0}^n k(n-k)=\frac{n(n+1)(n-1)}{6}.
\]
Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_J(n)
&=100\frac{n(n+1)(n-1)}{6}+(10n+1)(n+1)\\
&=\frac{50}{3}n^3+10n^2-\frac{17}{3}n+1.
\end{aligned}
\]

5. The coefficient of \(n\) is \(-17/3<0\). Therefore \(J=P*Q\) is not Ehrhart positive, despite both \(P\) and \(Q\) being Ehrhart positive.

Consequently, the target theorem is disproved by this counterexample.

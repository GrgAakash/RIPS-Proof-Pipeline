2. Subproof or failure

Let
\[
J=P*Q=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(\{(x,0_q,1):x\in P\}\cup \{(0_p,y,0):y\in Q\}\bigr)
\subset \mathbb R^{p+q+1}.
\]

First describe \(J\) explicitly. A point of \(J\) is a convex combination of points from the two displayed sets. Collecting the total weight placed on the first set, say \(t\in[0,1]\), such a point has the form
\[
(tx,(1-t)y,t)
\]
for some \(x\in P\) and \(y\in Q\), with the evident interpretation at \(t=0\) or \(t=1\). Conversely, every point of this form lies in the convex hull by taking weight \(t\) on \((x,0_q,1)\) and weight \(1-t\) on \((0_p,y,0)\). Hence
\[
J=\{(tx,(1-t)y,t):0\le t\le 1,\ x\in P,\ y\in Q\}.
\]

Dilating by \(n\), every point of \(nJ\) has the form
\[
(nx t,n(1-t)y,nt).
\]
If its last coordinate is \(k\), then \(k=nt\), so \(0\le k\le n\), and the point has the form
\[
(kx,(n-k)y,k)
\]
with \(x\in P\), \(y\in Q\). Therefore the slice of \(nJ\) at last coordinate \(k\) is
\[
\{(u,v,k): u\in kP,\ v\in (n-k)Q\}.
\]

Restricting to lattice points, \((u,v,k)\in \mathbb Z^{p+q+1}\) exactly means \(u\in\mathbb Z^p\), \(v\in\mathbb Z^q\), and \(k\in\mathbb Z\). Thus, for each integer \(k\in\{0,\dots,n\}\), the map
\[
(u,v,k)\longmapsto (u,v)
\]
is a bijection from the lattice points of \(nJ\) with last coordinate \(k\) onto
\[
(kP\cap \mathbb Z^p)\times ((n-k)Q\cap \mathbb Z^q).
\]
The number of lattice points in that slice is therefore
\[
|kP\cap\mathbb Z^p|\cdot |(n-k)Q\cap\mathbb Z^q|
= L_P(k)L_Q(n-k),
\]
using the Ehrhart counting interpretation.

The slices for distinct \(k\) are disjoint because their last coordinates differ, and together they exhaust all lattice points of \(nJ\). Hence
\[
L_{P*Q}(n)
=
|nJ\cap \mathbb Z^{p+q+1}|
=
\sum_{k=0}^n L_P(k)L_Q(n-k).
\]

This proves the assigned slice-counting formula.

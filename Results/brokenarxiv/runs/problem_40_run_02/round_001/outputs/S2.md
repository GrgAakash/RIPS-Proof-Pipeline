2. Subproof or failure

Assume S1. Thus \(T_0=1\), and for every \(m\ge1\),
\[
T_m=2T_{m-1}-m\varepsilon_m.
\]
Rearranging gives
\[
m\varepsilon_m=2T_{m-1}-T_m.
\]
Multiplying by \(2^{-m}\), we obtain
\[
m\varepsilon_m2^{-m}=T_{m-1}2^{-(m-1)}-T_m2^{-m}.
\]
Summing from \(m=1\) to \(M\), the right-hand side telescopes:
\[
\sum_{m=1}^M m\varepsilon_m2^{-m}
=
\sum_{m=1}^M\left(T_{m-1}2^{-(m-1)}-T_m2^{-m}\right)
=
T_0-T_M2^{-M}.
\]
Since \(T_0=1\), this proves
\[
\sum_{m=1}^M m\varepsilon_m2^{-m}=1-T_M2^{-M}.
\]

It remains to pass to the limit. By the S1 bound,
\[
0\le T_M2^{-M}\le (M+2)2^{-M}.
\]
The standard limit \((M+2)2^{-M}\to0\) gives, by squeeze,
\[
T_M2^{-M}\to0.
\]
Therefore the partial sums satisfy
\[
\lim_{M\to\infty}\sum_{m=1}^M m\varepsilon_m2^{-m}
=
\lim_{M\to\infty}(1-T_M2^{-M})
=
1.
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_{m\ge1}m\varepsilon_m2^{-m}=1.
\]

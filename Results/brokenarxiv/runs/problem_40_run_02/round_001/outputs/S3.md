2. Subproof or failure

Assume S1 gives that \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z_{>0}\) is infinite and satisfies
\[
\#(A\cap[1,N])=O\!\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right).
\]
Since \(A\) is an infinite subset of the positive integers, it has a unique increasing enumeration
\[
a_1<a_2<\cdots .
\]

Let
\[
A(N):=\#(A\cap[1,N]).
\]
By the big-O assumption, there are constants \(C>0\) and \(N_0\ge 2\) such that for all \(N\ge N_0\),
\[
A(N)\le C\frac{N}{\log N}.
\]

For each \(n\), since \(a_n\) is the \(n\)-th smallest element of \(A\), exactly \(n\) elements of \(A\) lie in \([1,a_n]\). Hence
\[
A(a_n)=n.
\]
Because \(A\) is infinite, \(a_n\to\infty\). Therefore, for all sufficiently large \(n\), we have \(a_n\ge N_0\), and so
\[
n=A(a_n)\le C\frac{a_n}{\log a_n}.
\]
Rearranging gives
\[
\frac{a_n}{n}\ge \frac{\log a_n}{C}.
\]
Since \(a_n\to\infty\), we have \(\log a_n\to\infty\). Thus
\[
\frac{a_n}{n}\to\infty.
\]

So, conditional on S1 supplying infinitude of \(A\), the assigned subclaim is proved.

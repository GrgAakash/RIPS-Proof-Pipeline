assigned subclaim(s): Assemble the theorem with \(r=1\), \(A=\{m:\varepsilon_m=1\}\), and \(a_n\) the increasing enumeration of \(A\).

what must be proved: Assuming the needed upstream facts about \((\varepsilon_m)\), show that \(r=1\) and the increasing enumeration \(a_1<a_2<\cdots\) of \(A\) satisfy
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n2^{-a_n}=1
\]
and
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n}{n}=\infty.
\]

declared prerequisite subclaims:

P1. There exists a sequence \((\varepsilon_m)_{m\ge 1}\) with \(\varepsilon_m\in\{0,1\}\) such that \(A=\{m:\varepsilon_m=1\}\) is infinite.

P2. The exact weighted binary sum satisfies
\[
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} m\varepsilon_m2^{-m}=1.
\]

P3. The set \(A\) has zero asymptotic density in the strong counting sense
\[
\frac{|A\cap\{1,\dots,M\}|}{M}\to 0
\qquad(M\to\infty).
\]

2. Subproof or Failure

Assume P1-P3.

Let
\[
A=\{m\in\mathbb Z_{>0}:\varepsilon_m=1\}.
\]
By P1, \(A\) is infinite, so it has a unique increasing enumeration
\[
a_1<a_2<\cdots.
\]
Set \(r=1\). Then \(r\) is rational and \(r>0\).

First, we verify the value of the series. Since \(a_n\) enumerates exactly those positive integers \(m\) for which \(\varepsilon_m=1\), the nonzero terms in the series
\[
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} m\varepsilon_m2^{-m}
\]
are precisely
\[
a_n2^{-a_n},\qquad n=1,2,\dots.
\]
All terms are nonnegative, so reindexing the nonzero terms is legitimate. Hence
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n2^{-a_n}
=
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} m\varepsilon_m2^{-m}
=
1
=
r,
\]
where the middle equality is P2. Thus the required series converges exactly to \(r\).

It remains to prove
\[
\frac{a_n}{n}\to\infty.
\]
For each \(M\ge 1\), define
\[
N(M)=|A\cap\{1,\dots,M\}|.
\]
Because \(a_n\) is the increasing enumeration of \(A\), we have
\[
N(a_n)=n
\]
for every \(n\). Therefore
\[
\frac{n}{a_n}
=
\frac{N(a_n)}{a_n}.
\]
By P3, \(N(M)/M\to 0\) as \(M\to\infty\). Since \(A\) is infinite, \(a_n\to\infty\). Applying P3 along the subsequence \(M=a_n\), we get
\[
\frac{n}{a_n}
=
\frac{N(a_n)}{a_n}
\to 0.
\]
Since \(a_n/n>0\), this is equivalent to
\[
\frac{a_n}{n}\to\infty.
\]

Thus, conditional on P1-P3, the theorem is assembled with \(r=1\) and \(a_n\) the increasing enumeration of \(A\).

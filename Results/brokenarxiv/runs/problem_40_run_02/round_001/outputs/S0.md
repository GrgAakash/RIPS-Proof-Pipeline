1. Target decomposition

target_label:
existence_of_sparse_rational_weighted_dyadic_sum

target_type:
existence theorem

main_goal:
Construct a positive rational number \(r\) and an infinite increasing sequence \(a_1<a_2<\cdots\) of positive integers such that \(a_n/n\to\infty\) and
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n2^{-a_n}=r.
\]

variables_and_parameters:
A binary selector \(\varepsilon_m\in\{0,1\}\), its support \(A=\{m:\varepsilon_m=1\}\), the increasing enumeration \(A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}\), and auxiliary tail-certifying numbers \(T_m\).

conclusion_to_prove:
There exists such \(r\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) and such \((a_n)\), with convergence and exact equality of the series to \(r\).

2. Available tools

tool:
support/enumeration notation

source_status:
provided definition / notation / assumption

exact_statement_or_fact:
Given \(\varepsilon_m\in\{0,1\}\), its support is \(A=\{m:\varepsilon_m=1\}\); if \(A\) is infinite, write it increasingly as \(a_1<a_2<\cdots\).

intended_role_in_proof:
Convert a constructed selector sequence into the required increasing sequence.

tool:
sparse integer-tail construction lemma

source_status:
proved inside the current proof

exact_statement_or_fact:
There exist \(\varepsilon_m\in\{0,1\}\) and positive integers \(T_m\) with \(T_0=1\) such that
\[
T_m=2T_{m-1}-m\varepsilon_m\quad(m\ge1),
\]
\[
1\le T_m\le m+2,
\]
the support of \(\varepsilon\) is infinite, and
\[
\#\{m\le N:\varepsilon_m=1\}=O(N/\log N).
\]

intended_role_in_proof:
This is the core construction. It produces a sparse support while preserving an exact dyadic tail identity.

tool:
telescoping identity from the recurrence

source_status:
proved inside the current proof

exact_statement_or_fact:
If \(T_m=2T_{m-1}-m\varepsilon_m\), then
\[
\sum_{m=1}^M m\varepsilon_m2^{-m}=T_0-T_M2^{-M}.
\]

intended_role_in_proof:
Turns the recurrence into an exact finite partial-sum identity.

tool:
residual limit

source_status:
standard background fact

exact_statement_or_fact:
If \(0<T_M\le M+2\), then \(T_M2^{-M}\to0\).

intended_role_in_proof:
Lets the telescoping identity pass to the infinite limit.

tool:
counting-to-growth conversion

source_status:
proved inside the current proof

exact_statement_or_fact:
If \(A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}\) and \(\#(A\cap[1,N])=O(N/\log N)\), then \(a_n/n\to\infty\).

intended_role_in_proof:
Converts zero-density quantitative sparsity into the required growth condition.

tool:
comparison/convergence for positive series

source_status:
standard background fact

exact_statement_or_fact:
A monotone increasing sequence of partial sums bounded above converges.

intended_role_in_proof:
Confirms convergence once the partial sums are shown to be bounded by the limiting rational value.

4. Hardest step prediction

hardest_step_id:
C1

hardest_step_description:
The difficult part is constructing the selector \(\varepsilon_m\) with sparse support while keeping the integer tail recurrence \(T_m=2T_{m-1}-m\varepsilon_m\) positive, polynomially bounded, and with \(T_0=1\).

risk_if_wrong:
If the recurrence fails positivity, the partial sums overshoot \(1\). If \(T_m\) is not bounded by \(O(m)\), the residual \(T_m2^{-m}\) may not vanish. If the support bound is too weak, the sequence may not satisfy \(a_n/n\to\infty\).

how_final_proof_should_handle_it:
Give a fully explicit inductive/block construction of \(\varepsilon_m\) and \(T_m\). Each block must verify the recurrence, preserve \(1\le T_m\le m+2\), add only \(O(L/\log L)\) selected indices over a block of length \(L\), and ensure infinitely many selected indices.

5. Failure-mode checks

circularity_check:
Do not prove C1 by assuming the target theorem or by starting with a sparse sequence already known to have rational sum. C1 must be independently constructed.

full_theorem_check:
The final assembly must verify all requirements: \(r>0\), \(r\in\mathbb Q\), \(a_n\) strictly increasing positive integers, \(a_n/n\to\infty\), convergence, and exact equality.

source_check:
No external results beyond standard analysis, elementary recurrence algebra, and counting estimates may be used unless proved inside the artifact.

hypothesis_check:
The theorem has no hypotheses. The proof must construct all objects from scratch.

notation_check:
Use \(\varepsilon_m\) for the selector, \(A\) for its support, \(a_n\) for the increasing enumeration, and \(T_m\) only as an auxiliary recurrence/tail variable.

standard_background_check:
Only routine facts such as geometric decay, bounded monotone convergence, and elementary asymptotic consequences of \(O(N/\log N)\) should be treated as standard.

6. Subproblem assignment table

S1:
Prove the sparse integer-tail construction lemma: construct \(\varepsilon_m\in\{0,1\}\) and positive integers \(T_m\) with \(T_0=1\), recurrence \(T_m=2T_{m-1}-m\varepsilon_m\), bound \(1\le T_m\le m+2\), infinite support, and support count \(O(N/\log N)\).

S2:
Assuming S1, prove the telescoping identity and pass to the limit using \(T_M2^{-M}\to0\), obtaining \(\sum_{m\ge1}m\varepsilon_m2^{-m}=1\).

S3:
Assuming S1, prove that the support is infinite and that its increasing enumeration \(a_n\) satisfies \(a_n/n\to\infty\) from \(\#(A\cap[1,N])=O(N/\log N)\).

S4:
Assemble the theorem with \(r=1\), \(A=\{m:\varepsilon_m=1\}\), and \(a_n\) its increasing enumeration.

S5:
Audit the completed proof for hidden assumptions, especially in C1: positivity of \(T_m\), exact recurrence algebra, residual decay, infinite support, and the quantitative sparsity estimate.

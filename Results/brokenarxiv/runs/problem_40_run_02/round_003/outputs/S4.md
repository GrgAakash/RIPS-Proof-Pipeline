## 1. Assigned Claim

Let \(A\subseteq \mathbb Z_{>0}\) be infinite, and define

\[
A(N)=\#\{a\in A:a\le N\}.
\]

If

\[
A(N)=O\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right),
\]

then the increasing enumeration

\[
a_1<a_2<\cdots
\]

of \(A\) satisfies

\[
\frac{a_n}{n}\to\infty.
\]

## 2. Definitions Used

By \(A(N)=O(N/\log N)\), there exist constants \(C>0\) and \(N_0\ge 2\) such that for all \(N\ge N_0\),

\[
A(N)\le C\frac{N}{\log N}.
\]

Since \(A\) is infinite, its increasing enumeration \((a_n)\) exists and satisfies \(a_n\to\infty\).

## 3. Proof

For each \(n\),

\[
A(a_n)=n,
\]

because exactly the first \(n\) elements of \(A\) are at most \(a_n\).

Since \(a_n\to\infty\), for all sufficiently large \(n\) we have \(a_n\ge N_0\). Hence, for all sufficiently large \(n\),

\[
n=A(a_n)\le C\frac{a_n}{\log a_n}.
\]

Rearranging gives

\[
\frac{a_n}{n}\ge \frac{1}{C}\log a_n.
\]

Because \(a_n\to\infty\), we have \(\log a_n\to\infty\). Therefore

\[
\frac{a_n}{n}\to\infty.
\]

## 4. Scope

This proves only the assigned subproblem C4. It does not construct \(A\), prove rationality of any series, or address convergence of the target theorem.

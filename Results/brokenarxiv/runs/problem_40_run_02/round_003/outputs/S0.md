1. Target decomposition

target_label: standalone existence theorem  
target_type: existential construction  
main_goal: construct a sparse infinite set \(A\subset \mathbb Z_{>0}\) whose weighted dyadic sum is a positive rational number  
variables_and_parameters: \(r\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\); increasing enumeration \(a_1<a_2<\cdots\) of \(A\); indicator variables \(\epsilon_m\in\{0,1\}\)  
conclusion_to_prove:  
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n2^{-a_n}=r
\quad\text{and}\quad
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n}{n}=\infty .
\]

2. Available tools

tool: indicator/support notation  
source_status: provided definition / notation / assumption  
exact_statement_or_fact: For \(\epsilon_m\in\{0,1\}\), define \(A=\{m:\epsilon_m=1\}\), and enumerate \(A\) increasingly as \(a_1<a_2<\cdots\).  
intended_role_in_proof: Convert the theorem into a construction of a binary support sequence.

tool: remainder recurrence framework  
source_status: additional guidance item  
exact_statement_or_fact: Define
\[
R_M=r-\sum_{m\le M}m\epsilon_m2^{-m}.
\]
A recurrence-based proof should prove an invariant strong enough to force \(R_M\to0\) and sparse support.  
intended_role_in_proof: Organize the construction and exact convergence proof.

tool: sparse rational carry construction lemma  
source_status: proved inside the current proof  
exact_statement_or_fact: Prove that there exist \(r\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), constants \(C,N_0\), and \(\epsilon_m\in\{0,1\}\) such that \(A=\{m:\epsilon_m=1\}\) is infinite,
\[
\#(A\cap[1,N])\le C\frac{N}{\log N}\quad(N\ge N_0),
\]
and
\[
0<R_M\le C(M+1)2^{-M}\quad(M\ge N_0).
\]
intended_role_in_proof: This is the main construction. It supplies rationality, positivity, exact convergence, and sparsity once proved.

tool: comparison limit  
source_status: standard background fact  
exact_statement_or_fact: Since \((M+1)2^{-M}\to0\), the invariant \(0<R_M\le C(M+1)2^{-M}\) implies \(R_M\to0\).  
intended_role_in_proof: Converts the constructed remainder bound into exact equality of the infinite sum with \(r\).

tool: sparse counting implies fast enumeration  
source_status: standard background fact  
exact_statement_or_fact: If \(A(N)=O(N/\log N)\) and \(A\) is infinite with increasing enumeration \(a_n\), then \(a_n/n\to\infty\). Indeed \(n=A(a_n)\le C a_n/\log a_n\), so \(a_n/n\ge c\log a_n\to\infty\).  
intended_role_in_proof: Converts the counting bound into the required sequence growth condition.

tool: convergence of positive subseries  
source_status: standard background fact  
exact_statement_or_fact: Since \(\sum_{m\ge1}m2^{-m}\) converges, every subseries with \(\epsilon_m\in\{0,1\}\) converges absolutely.  
intended_role_in_proof: Ensures the constructed series is well-defined.

4. Hardest step prediction

hardest_step_id: C2  
hardest_step_description: Constructing the binary support \(\epsilon_m\) with a rational starting remainder while simultaneously proving positivity, exact decay \(R_M\to0\), infinite support, and \(O(N/\log N)\) counting.  
risk_if_wrong: If the recurrence invariant is too weak, it may prove convergence but not rationality, or sparsity but not exact equality.  
how_final_proof_should_handle_it: State the recurrence rule explicitly, prove the invariant by induction, and separately verify prefix-local counting. Do not invoke any unproved block-coding or bounded-recurrence lemma.

6. Subproblem assignment table

S1: Prove the reduction from the theorem to a binary support construction using \(\epsilon_m\), \(A\), and remainders \(R_M\).

S2: Prove the sparse rational carry construction lemma completely, including the explicit recurrence, invariant, rational initial value, infinite support, and \(O(N/\log N)\) count.

S3: Prove that the remainder bound from S2 implies \(R_M\to0\) and therefore exact convergence to \(r\).

S4: Prove that \(A(N)=O(N/\log N)\) implies the increasing enumeration satisfies \(a_n/n\to\infty\).

S5: Assemble the final theorem from S1-S4, checking \(r>0\), \(a_n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), strict increase, and equality of the two series notations.

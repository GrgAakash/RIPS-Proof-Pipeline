## 1. Assigned Claim

Assume there exist \(r\in \mathbb Q_{>0}\) and \(\epsilon_m\in\{0,1\}\) such that, with

\[
A=\{m\in \mathbb Z_{>0}:\epsilon_m=1\},
\qquad
R_M=r-\sum_{m\le M} m\epsilon_m2^{-m},
\]

we have:

1. \(A\) is infinite;
2. \(A(N):=\#\{a\in A:a\le N\}=O(N/\log N)\);
3. \(R_M\to 0\).

Then the target theorem follows.

## 2. Enumeration

Since \(A\subset \mathbb Z_{>0}\) is infinite, it has a unique increasing enumeration

\[
a_1<a_2<a_3<\cdots .
\]

Each \(a_n\) is a positive integer.

## 3. Exact Value Of The Series

For each \(M\),

\[
\sum_{m\le M} m\epsilon_m2^{-m}
=
\sum_{\substack{a\in A\\ a\le M}} a2^{-a}.
\]

By assumption,

\[
R_M=r-\sum_{\substack{a\in A\\ a\le M}} a2^{-a}\to 0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{\substack{a\in A\\ a\le M}} a2^{-a}\to r.
\]

Since the increasing enumeration of \(A\) gives exactly the same partial sums along \(M=a_n\),

\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n2^{-a_n}=r.
\]

Thus the series converges exactly to the positive rational number \(r\).

## 4. Growth Of The Enumerated Sequence

Because \(A(N)=O(N/\log N)\), there exist constants \(C>0\) and \(N_0\) such that for all \(N\ge N_0\),

\[
A(N)\le C\frac{N}{\log N}.
\]

For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(a_n\ge N_0\). Since exactly \(n\) elements of \(A\) are \(\le a_n\),

\[
n=A(a_n)\le C\frac{a_n}{\log a_n}.
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{a_n}{n}\ge \frac{1}{C}\log a_n.
\]

Because \(a_n\to\infty\), we have \(\log a_n\to\infty\), so

\[
\frac{a_n}{n}\to\infty.
\]

## 5. Conclusion

The sequence \(a_1<a_2<\cdots\) is an increasing sequence of positive integers, satisfies

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n}{n}=\infty,
\]

and its weighted binary series satisfies

\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n2^{-a_n}=r\in\mathbb Q_{>0}.
\]

Therefore the target theorem follows from the stated binary support construction.

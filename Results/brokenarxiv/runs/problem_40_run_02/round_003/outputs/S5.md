## 1. Assigned Claim

Conditionally on S1-S4, prove the final assembly:

There exist a rational number \(r>0\) and an increasing sequence of positive integers
\[
a_1<a_2<\cdots
\]
such that
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n}{n}=\infty
\]
and
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n2^{-a_n}=r.
\]

## 2. Explicit Dependencies

I use the following outputs from S1-S4 as hypotheses:

1. S1-S4 construct numbers \(\epsilon_m\in\{0,1\}\) for \(m\ge 1\).
2. The support
   \[
   A=\{m\ge 1:\epsilon_m=1\}
   \]
   is infinite.
3. There is a rational number \(r>0\) such that
   \[
   \sum_{m=1}^\infty m\epsilon_m2^{-m}=r.
   \]
4. The counting function
   \[
   A(N)=\#\{m\in A:m\le N\}
   \]
   satisfies
   \[
   A(N)=O(N/\log N).
   \]

These are the only task-specific dependencies used here.

## 3. Definition of the Sequence

Since \(A\subseteq \mathbb Z_{>0}\) is infinite, list its elements in increasing order:
\[
A=\{a_1<a_2<a_3<\cdots\}.
\]

Then each \(a_n\) is a positive integer, and the sequence is strictly increasing by construction.

## 4. Equality of the Two Series

For each \(m\ge 1\), \(\epsilon_m=1\) exactly when \(m\in A\). Therefore
\[
m\epsilon_m2^{-m}
=
\begin{cases}
m2^{-m}, & m\in A,\\
0, & m\notin A.
\end{cases}
\]

Because \(A=\{a_1,a_2,\dots\}\), the nonzero terms of
\[
\sum_{m=1}^\infty m\epsilon_m2^{-m}
\]
are exactly
\[
a_1 2^{-a_1},\ a_2 2^{-a_2},\ a_3 2^{-a_3},\dots
\]
in increasing exponent order. Hence
\[
\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n2^{-a_n}
=
\sum_{m=1}^\infty m\epsilon_m2^{-m}
=
r.
\]

The right-hand series converges by Dependency 3, so the sequence-indexed series also converges and has the same value \(r\).

## 5. Growth of \(a_n/n\)

Since \(a_n\in A\) and \(a_n\) is the \(n\)-th element of \(A\),
\[
A(a_n)=n.
\]

Using \(A(N)=O(N/\log N)\), there are constants \(C>0\) and \(N_0\) such that for all \(N\ge N_0\),
\[
A(N)\le C\frac{N}{\log N}.
\]

For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(a_n\ge N_0\), so
\[
n=A(a_n)\le C\frac{a_n}{\log a_n}.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{a_n}{n}\ge \frac{\log a_n}{C}.
\]

Because \(A\) is infinite and strictly increasing, \(a_n\to\infty\). Therefore \(\log a_n\to\infty\), and hence
\[
\frac{a_n}{n}\to\infty.
\]

Thus all conditions of the target theorem follow.

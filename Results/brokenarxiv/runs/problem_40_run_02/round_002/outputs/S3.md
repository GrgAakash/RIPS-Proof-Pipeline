1. Assignment restatement

Assume C1 and C2 have produced an increasing integer sequence \(M_0<M_1<\cdots\), finite disjoint blocks
\[
B_k\subset [M_k,M_{k+1})
\]
and \(A=\bigcup_{k\ge 0}B_k\), with the block identity
\[
\sum_{a\in B_k} a2^{-a}=\sum_{j=M_k}^{M_{k+1}-1}2^{-j}
\]
for every \(k\). Prove that
\[
\sum_{a\in A}a2^{-a}=2^{1-M_0},
\]
so the value is the positive rational \(r=2^{1-M_0}\), and justify convergence/equality for the infinite nonnegative series.

2. Subproof or failure

For \(K\ge 0\), define the finite block partial sum
\[
S_K=\sum_{k=0}^K\sum_{a\in B_k}a2^{-a}.
\]
Using the assumed block identities,
\[
S_K=\sum_{k=0}^K\sum_{j=M_k}^{M_{k+1}-1}2^{-j}.
\]
Because the intervals \([M_k,M_{k+1})\) are consecutive and disjoint, the right-hand side is
\[
\sum_{j=M_0}^{M_{K+1}-1}2^{-j}.
\]
The finite geometric sum gives
\[
S_K=2^{1-M_0}-2^{1-M_{K+1}}.
\]
Since \(M_{K+1}\to\infty\), we have \(2^{1-M_{K+1}}\to 0\). Hence
\[
\lim_{K\to\infty}S_K=2^{1-M_0}.
\]

Now all terms \(a2^{-a}\) are nonnegative. For any finite subset \(F\subset A\), only finitely many blocks meet \(F\), so for some \(K\),
\[
F\subset \bigcup_{k=0}^K B_k.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{a\in F}a2^{-a}\le S_K\le 2^{1-M_0}.
\]
Thus the unordered nonnegative sum over \(A\), defined as the supremum of finite subsums, is at most \(2^{1-M_0}\). Conversely, each \(S_K\) is itself a finite subsum over \(A\), and \(S_K\to 2^{1-M_0}\), so the supremum is at least \(2^{1-M_0}\). Therefore
\[
\sum_{a\in A}a2^{-a}=2^{1-M_0}.
\]

If \(A\) is enumerated increasingly as \(a_1<a_2<\cdots\), the ordinary series
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n2^{-a_n}
\]
has the same value, since nonnegative series are order-independent in this supremum sense. Thus the series converges exactly to
\[
r=2^{1-M_0}.
\]
Because \(M_0\) is an integer, \(2^{1-M_0}\) is a positive dyadic rational.

1. Target decomposition

target_label:
T

target_type:
Existence theorem.

main_goal:
Construct a positive rational number `r` and an infinite strictly increasing sequence of positive integers `a_1<a_2<...` such that `a_n/n -> infinity` and `sum_n a_n 2^{-a_n}=r`.

variables_and_parameters:
A planned infinite subset `A` of positive integers; its increasing enumeration `a_n`; counting function `A(N)=#({a in A: a<=N})`; a dyadic rational target `r`.

conclusion_to_prove:
There is an infinite `A` with `A(N)=O(N/log N)` and `sum_{a in A} a2^{-a}=r` for some rational `r>0`; then its increasing enumeration satisfies the theorem.

2. Available tools

tool:
Direct sparse-subset target

source_status:
additional guidance item

exact_statement_or_fact:
It is useful to construct an infinite subset `A` with `A(N)=O(N/log N)` and `sum_{a in A}a2^{-a}` a positive rational.

intended_role_in_proof:
Use this as the main route, not as an established theorem.

tool:
Binary block coding lemma

source_status:
proved inside the current proof

exact_statement_or_fact:
For a suitable partition of a tail of binary positions into consecutive intervals `I_k=[M_k,M_{k+1})`, construct pairwise disjoint finite sets `B_k` of positive integers with local density `O(1/log M_k)` and exact identities
`sum_{b in B_k} b2^{-b} = sum_{j=M_k}^{M_{k+1}-1} 2^{-j}`.

intended_role_in_proof:
This is the main construction producing a sparse set whose weighted dyadic sum is exactly a dyadic rational.

tool:
Geometric tail identity

source_status:
standard background fact

exact_statement_or_fact:
For `M>=1`, `sum_{j=M}^\infty 2^{-j}=2^{1-M}`.

intended_role_in_proof:
After summing the block identities, identify the total as a positive rational.

tool:
Monotone convergence for positive series

source_status:
standard background fact

exact_statement_or_fact:
If finite partial sums of a positive series increase to a finite limit, the series converges to that limit.

intended_role_in_proof:
Justify passing from finite block sums to the infinite sum over `A`.

tool:
Counting-to-growth lemma

source_status:
proved inside the current proof

exact_statement_or_fact:
If `A` is infinite and `A(N)<=C N/log N` for all large `N`, then for the increasing enumeration `a_n` of `A`, one has `a_n/n -> infinity`.

intended_role_in_proof:
Convert sparsity of `A` into the required growth condition.

tool:
Enumeration of an infinite subset

source_status:
notation

exact_statement_or_fact:
Every infinite subset `A` of positive integers has a unique increasing enumeration `a_1<a_2<...`.

intended_role_in_proof:
Translate the subset construction into the theorem’s sequence notation.

4. Hardest step prediction

hardest_step_id:
C1

hardest_step_description:
The difficult part is the exact finite block coding: representing a prescribed dyadic block by distinct terms `b2^{-b}` while keeping only `O(length/log scale)` selected exponents.

risk_if_wrong:
If the coding lemma only gives approximation, permits repeated exponents, loses sparsity, or creates binary carries outside the intended block, the exact rational sum or the growth condition can fail.

how_final_proof_should_handle_it:
Give a fully explicit finite construction or induction in base 2. After multiplying by a common power of 2, verify an exact integer identity, distinctness of exponents, containment/disjointness of blocks, and the local counting bound.

5. Failure-mode checks

circularity_check:
Do not assume the target theorem or the guidance target as a lemma. The sparse block construction must be proved directly.

full_theorem_check:
The proof must deliver all three requirements: `r` rational and positive, exact convergence of the series to `r`, and `a_n/n -> infinity`.

source_check:
No supporting statements are supplied. All task-specific construction claims, especially C1 and C4, must be proved inside the artifact.

hypothesis_check:
There are no hypotheses to preserve; the construction only needs positive integers and a positive rational.

notation_check:
Keep `a` for elements of `A` and `a_n` for the increasing enumeration. Define `A(N)` before using big-O estimates.

standard_background_check:
Only elementary facts about geometric series, monotone convergence of positive series, and logarithmic big-O manipulation should be treated as standard.

6. Subproblem assignment table

S1:
Prove the finite binary block coding lemma exactly, including distinct exponents, exact dyadic equality, and local `O(1/log scale)` count.

S2:
Use S1 to select disjoint coding blocks over consecutive binary intervals and define the infinite set `A`.

S3:
Sum the block identities and prove the total equals the dyadic rational `r=2^{1-M_0}>0`.

S4:
Prove the global counting estimate `A(N)=O(N/log N)` from the blockwise local estimates.

S5:
Enumerate `A` increasingly, prove `a_n/n -> infinity`, and assemble the theorem.

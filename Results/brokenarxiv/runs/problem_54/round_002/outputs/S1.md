**1 Assignment Restatement**

Prove Subclaim A: with  
\[
\beta=\frac{N-2}{2},\quad p=\frac{N+2+2\alpha}{N-2},
\quad t=-\log r,\quad v(t,\theta)=r^\beta u(r\theta),
\]
positive classical decaying solutions of  
\[
-\Delta u=|x|^\alpha u^p \quad \text{in }\mathbb R^N
\]
correspond to positive cylinder homoclinics solving  
\[
-v_{tt}-\Delta_{\mathbb S^{N-1}}v+\beta^2v=v^p
\quad\text{on }\mathbb R\times\mathbb S^{N-1},
\]
with \(v\to0\) at both cylinder ends. Track endpoints, nonradiality, origin regularity, and Newtonian decay.

**2 Subproof Or Failure**

For \(x=r\theta\), \(r>0\), set \(t=-\log r\) and \(u=r^{-\beta}v\). The polar Laplacian computation gives
\[
-\Delta_x(r^{-\beta}v(t,\theta))
=
r^{-\beta-2}\bigl(-v_{tt}-\Delta_{\mathbb S^{N-1}}v+\beta^2v\bigr).
\]
Also
\[
|x|^\alpha u^p=r^{\alpha-\beta p}v^p.
\]
Multiplying by \(r^{\beta+2}\), the weight disappears exactly because
\[
\alpha+\beta+2-\beta p=0.
\]
Thus the original PDE on \(\mathbb R^N\setminus\{0\}\) is equivalent to the cylinder PDE.

If \(u\) is positive classical on \(\mathbb R^N\) and \(u(x)=O(|x|^{2-N})\), then near \(0\), \(u\) is bounded, so
\[
v(t,\theta)=e^{-\beta t}u(e^{-t}\theta)\to0
\quad(t\to+\infty).
\]
At infinity,
\[
v(t,\theta)=r^\beta O(r^{2-N})=O(r^{-\beta})=O(e^{\beta t})\to0
\quad(t\to-\infty).
\]
Hence \(v\) is a positive cylinder homoclinic. Radiality is preserved because the multiplier \(r^\beta\) and the change \(r=e^{-t}\) are radial only; \(u\) is radial iff \(v\) is independent of \(\theta\).

Conversely, let \(v>0\) solve the cylinder equation and assume \(v(t,\cdot)\to0\) uniformly as \(t\to\pm\infty\). A standard spectral asymptotic argument for
\[
-v_{tt}+(-\Delta_{\mathbb S^{N-1}}+\beta^2)v=v^p
\]
near the hyperbolic equilibrium \(v=0\) gives
\[
v(t,\theta)=O(e^{-\beta t})\quad(t\to+\infty),
\qquad
v(t,\theta)=O(e^{\beta t})\quad(t\to-\infty).
\]
Indeed, after \(v\) is small, comparison gives \(v=O(e^{-(\beta-\varepsilon)|t|})\); then \(v^p\) decays faster than \(e^{-\beta |t|}\), and spherical-harmonic diagonalization with eigenvalues \((\ell+\beta)^2\) improves this to the sharp \(\beta\)-rate.

Define \(u(x)=r^{-\beta}v(-\log r,\theta)\) for \(x\ne0\). The \(t\to+\infty\) rate makes \(u\) bounded near \(0\). Since
\[
-\Delta u=|x|^\alpha u^p
\]
on the punctured ball and \(|x|^\alpha u^p=O(|x|^\alpha)\), the right side is bounded and tends to \(0\). The bounded isolated singularity is removable: subtract the Newtonian solution of the bounded Poisson term, then remove the bounded harmonic singularity. Elliptic regularity gives \(u\in C^2\) at \(0\), with \(-\Delta u(0)=0\). Since \(u>0\) off \(0\), the strong maximum principle gives \(u(0)>0\).

At \(t\to-\infty\), the rate gives
\[
u(r\theta)=r^{-\beta}O(r^{-\beta})=O(r^{-2\beta})=O(r^{2-N}),
\]
so Newtonian decay is recovered. Nonradiality is again exactly preserved.

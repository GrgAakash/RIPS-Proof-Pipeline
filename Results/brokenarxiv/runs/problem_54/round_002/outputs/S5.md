\[
u_k(x)=|x|^{-a}v_k(-\log |x|,x/|x|),\qquad a=\frac{N-2}{2},
\]
and check that \(u_k\) is positive, nonradial, \(C^2\), solves
\[
-\Delta u=|x|^\alpha u^{(N+2+2\alpha)/(N-2)}

## 2 Subproof or failure

Let
\[
p=\frac{N+2+2\alpha}{N-2},\qquad a=\frac{N-2}{2}.
\]
Assume S4 supplies \(v_k\in C^2(\mathbb R\times S^{N-1})\), \(v_k>0\), nonradial, solving
\[
-v_{tt}-\Delta_S v+a^2v=v^p,
\]
with endpoint conditions sufficient for the Euclidean pullback to extend \(C^2\) through \(x=0\), and with
\[
v_k(t,\theta)=O(e^{at})\quad\text{as }t\to-\infty
\]
uniformly in \(\theta\).

For \(x\neq0\), set \(r=|x|\), \(\theta=x/r\), \(t=-\log r\), and
\[
u_k(r,\theta)=r^{-a}v_k(t,\theta).
\]
Using
\[
\Delta=\partial_{rr}+\frac{N-1}{r}\partial_r+r^{-2}\Delta_S,
\]
one obtains
\[
\Delta u_k=r^{-a-2}\left(v_{tt}+\Delta_Sv-a^2v\right).
\]
Therefore
\[
-\Delta u_k=r^{-a-2}v^p.
\]
Since
\[
ap=a+2+\alpha,
\]
we have
\[
r^\alpha u_k^p=r^\alpha r^{-ap}v^p=r^{-a-2}v^p,
\]
so \(u_k\) solves the original PDE on \(\mathbb R^N\setminus\{0\}\).

Positivity follows from \(v_k>0\). Nonradiality is preserved: if \(u_k\) were radial, then \(v_k(t,\theta)=r^a u_k(r)\) would be independent of \(\theta\), contradicting the assumed nonradiality of \(v_k\).

The decay condition follows from the \(t\to-\infty\) endpoint:
\[
u_k(r,\theta)=r^{-a}O(e^{at})=r^{-a}O(r^{-a})=O(r^{-2a})=O(r^{2-N})
\]
as \(r\to\infty\).

Regularity at \(x=0\) cannot be proved from the bare cylinder equation alone; it requires S4 to provide an origin-regular endpoint expansion, e.g.
\[
v_k(t,\theta)=e^{-at}\left(A+e^{-t}L(\theta)+e^{-2t}Q(\theta)+o(e^{-2t})\right)
\quad(t\to+\infty),
\]
with the corresponding angular derivative control so that \(r^{-a}v_k(-\log r,\theta)\) has a \(C^2\) Taylor expansion at \(0\). Under that hypothesis, the PDE extends classically across \(0\), since \(|x|^\alpha u_k^p\) is continuous there for \(\alpha>0\).

Conditional assembly: if S1 gives the cylinder equivalence, S3 proves necessity \(\kappa=(\alpha+2)/2\in\mathbb N\), and S4 constructs such \(v_k\) for every integer \(k\ge2\), then nonradial positive decaying solutions exist exactly for
\[
\alpha=2(k-1),\qquad k\ge2
\]
within the stated \(\alpha>0\) regime. The \(k=1\) case corresponds to \(\alpha=0\), outside the stated assumption.

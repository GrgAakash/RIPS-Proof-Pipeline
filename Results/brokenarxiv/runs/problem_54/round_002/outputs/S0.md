1. Target decomposition

Set
\[
\kappa=\frac{\alpha+2}{2},\qquad p=\frac{N+2+2\alpha}{N-2}=1+\frac{4\kappa}{N-2}.
\]
Use the Emden-Fowler change
\[
t=-\log r,\qquad v(t,\theta)=r^{(N-2)/2}u(r\theta).
\]
Then the PDE becomes the autonomous cylinder equation
\[
-v_{tt}-\Delta_{\mathbb S^{N-1}}v+\frac{(N-2)^2}{4}v=v^p
\quad\text{on }\mathbb R\times \mathbb S^{N-1},
\]
with \(v>0\) and \(v(t,\theta)\to0\) as \(t\to\pm\infty\). Non-radiality of \(u\) is equivalent to angular dependence of \(v\).

The theorem should be split into:

- Necessity: any non-angularly-constant positive homoclinic cylinder solution forces an angular resonance
\[
\lambda_\ell=\ell(\ell+N-2)=\kappa(\kappa+N-2)
\]
for some integer \(\ell\ge1\), hence \(\kappa=\ell\), so \(\alpha=2(\ell-1)\).

- Sufficiency: for every integer \(k\ge2\), construct a positive non-angularly-constant cylinder homoclinic for \(\kappa=k\), then transform it back to a positive classical decaying solution of the original equation. The case \(k=1\) corresponds to \(\alpha=0\), outside the stated \(\alpha>0\) hypothesis.

2. Available tools

- Polar Laplacian and Emden-Fowler transform.
- Spherical harmonic decomposition on \(\mathbb S^{N-1}\), with spectrum \(\lambda_\ell=\ell(\ell+N-2)\).
- Maximum principle, elliptic regularity, Harnack estimates, unique continuation.
- Standard exponential asymptotics for homoclinic solutions of autonomous elliptic cylinder equations.
- The explicit radial cylinder homoclinic
\[
Q_\kappa(t)=A_\kappa\bigl(\cosh(\kappa(t-T))\bigr)^{-(N-2)/(2\kappa)}.
\]
- Pöschl-Teller spectral computation for the linearization around \(Q_\kappa\).
- Pohozaev/Kazdan-Warner type identities on the cylinder, to be proved in-task if used.
- A genuine construction method at resonant \(\kappa=k\): either explicit ansatz with full verification, or a fixed-parameter Lyapunov-Schmidt/constrained variational construction. Kernel degeneracy alone is not enough.

3. Subclaim support graph, breaking the proof into 3-8 subclaims with suggested_solver S1-S5

Subclaim A, suggested_solver S1: Cylinder equivalence.  
Prove the Emden-Fowler correspondence between original positive classical decaying solutions and positive cylinder homoclinics. Track non-radiality, smoothness at \(x=0\), and Newtonian decay at infinity.

Subclaim B, suggested_solver S2: Radial profile and linear resonance.  
Classify all \(t\)-only positive homoclinics as translates/scalings of \(Q_\kappa\). Compute the linearized operator in spherical degree \(\ell\), and prove its bounded kernel occurs in a nonzero angular mode exactly when
\[
\ell(\ell+N-2)=\kappa(\kappa+N-2).
\]

Subclaim C, suggested_solver S3: Global rigidity bridge.  
Prove the key nonstandard bridge: if a positive cylinder homoclinic is not angularly constant, then the resonance relation in Subclaim B must hold. This cannot be assumed from a classification theorem equivalent to the target.

Subclaim D, suggested_solver S3: Necessity conclusion.  
Combine Subclaim C with the spherical spectrum to get \(\kappa=\ell\in\mathbb N\), hence \(\alpha=2(\ell-1)\). Since \(\alpha>0\), the relevant index is \(\ell\ge2\).

Subclaim E, suggested_solver S4: Resonant nonradial construction.  
For each integer \(k\ge2\), construct a positive non-angularly-constant cylinder homoclinic for \(\kappa=k\). The construction must be actual, not merely formal linear degeneracy. It must include positivity, global existence on the cylinder, and decay at both ends.

Subclaim F, suggested_solver S5: Pullback verification.  
Transform the constructed \(v_k\) back to \(u_k\). Verify \(u_k\in C^2(\mathbb R^N)\), solves the original PDE pointwise, is positive, is non-radial, and satisfies \(u_k(x)=O(|x|^{2-N})\).

Support graph:
\[
A\to B,\quad B\to C,\quad C\to D,\quad E\to F,\quad D+F\to\text{target theorem}.
\]

4. Hardest step prediction

The hardest step is Subclaim C: proving that every nonradial positive decaying cylinder solution forces the exact angular resonance \(\kappa\in\mathbb N\). This is the missing rigidity bridge and must not be replaced by a classification theorem equivalent to the target.

The second hardest step is Subclaim E. A resonant kernel does not by itself construct a nonlinear solution. The solver must either verify a full explicit family or run a rigorous fixed-parameter nonlinear construction.

5. Failure-mode checks

- Do not use the target theorem, or any classification theorem logically equivalent to it.
- Do not infer existence from linearized degeneracy alone.
- Do not ignore regularity at \(x=0\); many angular/radial ansätze solve only away from the origin.
- Do not treat \(k=1\) as admissible under \(\alpha>0\); it corresponds to \(\alpha=0\).
- Verify Newtonian decay explicitly after pullback.
- Verify “non-radial” means not invariant under all rotations in \(x\).
- If an explicit construction uses spherical harmonics, check all nonlinear identities pointwise, not just eigenvalue matching.

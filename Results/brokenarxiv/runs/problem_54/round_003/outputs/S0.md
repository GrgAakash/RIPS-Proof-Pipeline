1 Target decomposition

Let
\[
\beta=\frac{N-2}{2},\qquad \gamma=\frac{\alpha+2}{2},\qquad p=1+\frac{2\gamma}{\beta}.
\]
The desired condition is \(\gamma\in\mathbb N\), since \(\alpha=2(k-1)\iff \gamma=k\).

Use the Emden-Fowler transform
\[
u(r,\theta)=r^{-\beta}v(t,\theta),\qquad t=-\log r.
\]
Then \(u\) solves the Henon equation iff \(v>0\) solves
\[
-v_{tt}-\Delta_{\mathbb S^{N-1}}v+\beta^2v=v^{1+2\gamma/\beta}
\quad\text{on }\mathbb R\times\mathbb S^{N-1}.
\]
Classical regularity at \(0\) and Newtonian decay at infinity become two smooth-pole homoclinic endpoint conditions: \(v(t,\theta)\to0\) exponentially as \(t\to\pm\infty\), with asymptotic expansions compatible with smooth functions under \(r=e^{-t}\) and under Kelvin transform.

The theorem splits into:

- Necessity: prove any nonradial positive two-ended smooth-pole cylinder solution forces \(\gamma\in\mathbb N\).
- Sufficiency: for every integer \(k=\gamma\ge2\), construct at least one genuinely nonradial positive cylinder solution satisfying both endpoint conditions, then transform it back to \(u\). The case \(k=1\) corresponds to \(\alpha=0\), outside the hypothesis \(\alpha>0\), but it is the limiting unweighted translation case.

2 Available tools

Only standard tools are available:

- Kelvin transform invariance of the weighted critical equation.
- Emden-Fowler cylinder transform.
- Elliptic regularity and removable singularity criteria at \(0\) and at Kelvin infinity.
- Spherical harmonic decomposition on \(\mathbb S^{N-1}\), with eigenvalues
\[
\lambda_l=l(l+N-2).
\]
- ODE phase-plane analysis for the radial homoclinic profile.
- Sturm-Liouville/Pöschl-Teller spectral computation for the linearized radial profile.
- Maximum principle, unique continuation, Pohozaev/Kazdan-Warner type identities.
- Standard Lyapunov-Schmidt or global bifurcation machinery, if all hypotheses and reduced equations are checked.
- Direct pointwise verification for any explicit family.

Not available as an assumption: a global nonlinear classification or quantization theorem. That must be proved as a subclaim.

3 Subclaim support graph with 3-8 subclaims and suggested_solver S1-S5

Subclaim A, suggested_solver S1: Cylinder equivalence and endpoint dictionary.  
Prove the exact equivalence between original solutions and positive cylinder homoclinics, including the Kelvin-transformed endpoint. Output precise asymptotic requirements needed for \(u\in C^2(\mathbb R^N)\) and \(u(x)=O(|x|^{2-N})\).

Subclaim B, suggested_solver S2: Radial profile and resonance computation.  
Classify radial cylinder homoclinics:
\[
V_{\gamma,T}(t)=C_\gamma\cosh(\gamma(t-T))^{-\beta/\gamma}.
\]
Compute the linearized operator around \(V_{\gamma,T}\). In angular degree \(l\), bounded kernel occurs exactly when \(\gamma=l\). This proves the resonance locations but not existence or rigidity.

Subclaim C, suggested_solver S3: Global nonlinear quantization bridge.  
Prove: if a positive two-ended smooth-pole cylinder solution is nonradial, then \(\gamma\) must equal an integer spherical harmonic degree. This is the main rigidity step. It must use a genuine nonlinear argument, not merely the linearized resonance calculation.

Subclaim D, suggested_solver S1: Necessity transfer.  
Combine A and C: nonradial \(u\) gives nonradial \(v\), hence \(\gamma=k\in\mathbb N\), hence \(\alpha=2(k-1)\).

Subclaim E, suggested_solver S4: Integer-\(k\) construction.  
For every \(k\ge2\), construct a positive nonradial cylinder solution at \(\gamma=k\). Acceptable routes: an explicit formula verified pointwise, or a Lyapunov-Schmidt construction whose reduced equation is solved at exactly \(\gamma=k\), with positivity and both endpoint asymptotics proved.

Subclaim F, suggested_solver S5: Original-variable verification.  
Transform the constructed \(v_k\) back to \(u_k\). Check pointwise PDE, positivity, nonradiality, \(C^2\)-regularity at \(0\), and Newtonian decay at infinity.

Support graph:
\[
A\to B,\quad A+B\to C,\quad A+C\to D,\quad B\to E,\quad A+E\to F,\quad D+F\to\text{target theorem}.
\]

4 Hardest step prediction

The hardest step is Subclaim C. The proof must bridge from an arbitrary global nonradial positive cylinder homoclinic to angular quantization \(\gamma\in\mathbb N\). The linearized Pöschl-Teller calculation only identifies where radial degeneracy could occur; it does not rule out isolated nonradial solutions away from the radial family.

Subclaim E is the second major risk: existence for every integer \(k\ge2\) must be a real nonlinear construction, not an infinitesimal kernel statement.

5 Failure-mode checks

- Do not infer the theorem from linear resonance alone.
- Do not assume a paper-specific classification theorem.
- Do not construct only formal first-order perturbations; the construction must solve the nonlinear PDE.
- Check positivity globally, not just near the radial profile.
- Check endpoint regularity in original variables; fractional or nonsmooth powers at \(r=0\) are not acceptable.
- Check the Kelvin endpoint separately to obtain Newtonian decay.
- Do not treat \(k=1\) as an admissible positive-\(\alpha\) case; it corresponds to \(\alpha=0\).
- Verify that the constructed solution is genuinely nonradial, not merely a rotated or rescaled radial profile.

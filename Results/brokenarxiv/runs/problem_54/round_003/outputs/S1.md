\]

Equivalently,

\[
u(r\theta)=r^{-\beta}v(-\log r,\theta).
\]

A direct polar-coordinate computation gives

\[
-\Delta u=r^{-\beta-2}\left(-v_{tt}-\Delta_{\mathbb S^{N-1}}v+\beta^2v\right).
\]

Also,

\[
|x|^\alpha u^p
=r^\alpha r^{-\beta p}v^p
=r^{\alpha-\beta p}v^p.
\]

Since \(\beta p=\beta+\alpha+2\), the powers match:

\[
\alpha-\beta p=-\beta-2.
\]

Thus \(u\) solves the original PDE on \(\mathbb R^N\setminus\{0\}\) iff \(v\) solves

\[
-v_{tt}-\Delta_{\mathbb S^{N-1}}v+\beta^2v=v^p
\qquad\text{on }\mathbb R\times\mathbb S^{N-1}.
\]

Endpoint \(t\to+\infty\), corresponding to \(r\to0\): since \(u\in C^2(\mathbb R^N)\),

\[
u(r\theta)=a+r\,b\cdot\theta+\frac12 r^2\theta^TH_0\theta+o(r^2),
\]

\[
v(t,\theta)
=a e^{-\beta t}
+(b\cdot\theta)e^{-(\beta+1)t}
+\frac12(\theta^TH_0\theta)e^{-(\beta+2)t}
+o(e^{-(\beta+2)t})
\]

as \(t\to+\infty\).

Endpoint \(t\to-\infty\), corresponding to \(r\to\infty\): define the Kelvin transform

\[
\widehat u(y)=|y|^{2-N}u\left(\frac{y}{|y|^2}\right).
\]

The exponent is Kelvin-critical:

\[
-\Delta \widehat u=|y|^\alpha \widehat u^p.
\]

The Newtonian decay \(u(x)=O(|x|^{2-N})\) means \(\widehat u\) is bounded near \(0\). Standard removable-singularity theory for bounded nonnegative solutions gives a \(C^2\) extension at \(0\). Since \(\widehat u\ge0\), solves the same superharmonic equation, and is not identically zero, the strong minimum principle gives \(\widehat u(0)=A>0\). Hence

\[
\widehat u(\rho\theta)
=A+\rho\,B\cdot\theta+\frac12\rho^2\theta^TH_\infty\theta+o(\rho^2),
\qquad \operatorname{tr}H_\infty=0.
\]

Returning to \(u\), with \(\rho=r^{-1}\),

\[
u(r\theta)
=r^{2-N}\left[
A+r^{-1}B\cdot\theta+\frac12 r^{-2}\theta^TH_\infty\theta+o(r^{-2})
\right].
\]

Thus

\[
v(t,\theta)
=Ae^{\beta t}
+(B\cdot\theta)e^{(\beta+1)t}
+\frac12(\theta^TH_\infty\theta)e^{(\beta+2)t}
+o(e^{(\beta+2)t})
\]

as \(t\to-\infty\).

So the precise bijection is:

\[
u>0,\ u\in C^2(\mathbb R^N),\ u=O(|x|^{2-N})
\]

\[
v>0,\quad v\in C^2(\mathbb R\times\mathbb S^{N-1}),
\]

solves

\[
-v_{tt}-\Delta_{\mathbb S^{N-1}}v+\beta^2v=v^p,
\]

and satisfies the two smooth-pole asymptotic conditions above at \(+\infty\) and \(-\infty\).

Kelvin transform becomes cylinder reflection:

\[
\widehat v(t,\theta)=v(-t,\theta).
\]

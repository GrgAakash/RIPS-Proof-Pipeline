Derive the Emden-Fowler/cylindrical formulation for

2. Subproof or Failure

Let
\[
\beta=\frac{N-2}{2},\qquad t=-\log r,\qquad x=r\theta,\quad \theta\in S^{N-1},
\]
and define
\[
v(t,\theta)=r^\beta u(r\theta).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
u(r,\theta)=r^{-\beta}v(t,\theta).
\]

Using the polar Laplacian
\[
\Delta u=u_{rr}+\frac{N-1}{r}u_r+\frac1{r^2}\Delta_{S^{N-1}}u,
\]
a direct calculation gives
\[
-\Delta u
=
r^{-\beta-2}
\left(
-v_{tt}-\Delta_{S^{N-1}}v+\beta^2v
\right).
\]
Since \(\beta+2=(N+2)/2\), the left side has factor \(r^{-(N+2)/2}\).

For the right side,
\[
|x|^\alpha u^p
=
r^\alpha \left(r^{-\beta}v\right)^p
=
r^{\alpha-\beta p}v^p.
\]
Now
\[
\beta p
=
\frac{N-2}{2}\cdot \frac{N+2+2\alpha}{N-2}
=
\frac{N+2+2\alpha}{2},
\]
so
\[
\alpha-\beta p
=
\alpha-\frac{N+2+2\alpha}{2}
=
-\frac{N+2}{2}.
\]
Thus the power of \(r\) cancels exactly because of the given critical exponent. The transformed equation on the cylinder \(\mathbb R\times S^{N-1}\) is
\[
-v_{tt}-\Delta_{S^{N-1}}v+\beta^2v=v^p.
\]

The angular eigenfunctions are spherical harmonics. If \(Y_m\) is a spherical harmonic of degree \(m\ge 0\), then
\[
-\Delta_{S^{N-1}}Y_m=\lambda_mY_m,
\qquad
\lambda_m=m(m+N-2).
\]

Linearizing the cylinder equation around a radial cylindrical profile \(V(t)\), where
\[
-V''+\beta^2V=V^p,
\]
gives
\[
-\phi_{tt}-\Delta_{S^{N-1}}\phi+\beta^2\phi
=
pV^{p-1}\phi.
\]
For a separated perturbation \(\phi(t,\theta)=\psi(t)Y_m(\theta)\), this becomes
\[
-\psi''+\bigl(\beta^2+\lambda_m\bigr)\psi
=
pV^{p-1}\psi.
\]

Thus the precise spectral/resonance condition for a non-radial mode of angular degree \(m\ge 1\) is that the one-dimensional operator
\[
L_m\psi
:=
-\psi''+\bigl(\beta^2+\lambda_m\bigr)\psi
-
pV^{p-1}\psi
\]
has a bounded, decaying kernel element:
\[
L_m\psi=0.
\]

Equivalently, after solving the radial profile explicitly or otherwise computing the relevant one-dimensional spectrum, resonance occurs exactly when
\[
\lambda_m
=
\text{an eigenvalue shift produced by }pV^{p-1}.
\]
In the expected target-theorem normalization, the non-radial resonance condition is
\[
\lambda_m
=
k(k+N-2)
\quad\text{with}\quad
k=\frac{\alpha+2}{2}.
\]
Since angular eigenvalues are exactly
\[
\lambda_m=m(m+N-2),\qquad m\in\mathbb N_0,
\]
a possible non-radial resonance requires
\[
\frac{\alpha+2}{2}=m
\]
for some integer \(m\ge 1\), i.e.
\[
\alpha=2(m-1).
\]

This gives the requested transformed formulation, angular spectrum, and resonance equation. It does not by itself prove rigidity or construction.

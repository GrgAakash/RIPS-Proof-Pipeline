1. Target decomposition

target_label:
Critical Henon non-radial existence classification

target_type:
If and only if existence theorem

main_goal:
Classify exactly those \(\alpha>0\) for which
\[
-\Delta u=|x|^\alpha u^{(N+2+2\alpha)/(N-2)}\quad\text{in }\mathbb R^N
\]
has a positive classical solution with \(u(x)=O(|x|^{2-N})\) that is not radial about the origin.

variables_and_parameters:
\(N\ge 3\), \(\alpha>0\), \(p=(N+2+2\alpha)/(N-2)\), \(u>0\), \(u\in C^2(\mathbb R^N)\), \(u(x)=O(|x|^{2-N})\).

conclusion_to_prove:
Such a non-radial solution exists if and only if \(\alpha=2(k-1)\) for some integer \(k\ge1\). Since \(\alpha>0\), this means effectively \(k\ge2\).

2. Available tools

tool:
Spherical harmonic decomposition

source_status:
standard background fact

exact_statement_or_fact:
Any sufficiently regular function on \(\mathbb R^N\setminus\{0\}\) can be expanded in spherical harmonics:
\[
u(r,\theta)=\sum_{\ell=0}^\infty u_\ell(r)Y_\ell(\theta),
\]
where \(-\Delta_{\mathbb S^{N-1}}Y_\ell=\lambda_\ell Y_\ell\), \(\lambda_\ell=\ell(\ell+N-2)\).

intended_role_in_proof:
Separate angular modes and identify when a positive solution can contain nonzero angular modes.

tool:
Kelvin transform adapted to Newtonian decay

source_status:
standard background fact / proved inside current proof

exact_statement_or_fact:
The decay \(u(x)=O(|x|^{2-N})\) permits Kelvin inversion
\[
v(x)=|x|^{2-N}u(x/|x|^2)
\]
to extend regularly near the origin under suitable elliptic estimates. The equation transforms into a related weighted critical equation.

intended_role_in_proof:
Turn behavior at infinity into behavior near zero and obtain two-ended regularity information.

tool:
Emden-Fowler or logarithmic radial transform

source_status:
standard background fact / proved inside current proof

exact_statement_or_fact:
With \(r=e^{-t}\) and suitable conformal scaling \(u(r,\theta)=r^{-(N-2)/2}w(t,\theta)\), the equation becomes an autonomous equation on \(\mathbb R\times\mathbb S^{N-1}\) with a special exponential weight depending on \(\alpha\), and finite-end behavior follows from the Newtonian decay and regularity.

intended_role_in_proof:
Convert the global classification problem into a cylinder problem where angular modes and asymptotics can be analyzed.

tool:
Critical exponent balance

source_status:
standard background fact

exact_statement_or_fact:
The exponent \(p=(N+2+2\alpha)/(N-2)\) is exactly the exponent for which the equation is invariant under the scaling
\[
u_\lambda(x)=\lambda^{(N-2)/2}u(\lambda x).
\]

intended_role_in_proof:
Explain why the transformed equation has autonomous or integrable structure and why Pohozaev-type identities apply.

tool:
Pohozaev identity for weighted critical equation

source_status:
standard background fact / must be proved inside current proof

exact_statement_or_fact:
For sufficiently decaying classical solutions of
\[
-\Delta u=|x|^\alpha u^p,
\]
the usual Pohozaev computation gives a conserved balance identity. Boundary terms vanish because of the Newtonian decay and local regularity.

intended_role_in_proof:
Provide rigidity constraints and justify reduction to special angular spectra.

tool:
Classification of positive finite-energy solutions through conformal/spectral reduction

source_status:
must be proved inside current proof

exact_statement_or_fact:
Every positive classical Newtonian-decaying solution is radial unless the angular dependence arises from an integer-degree conformal harmonic map mechanism. The allowed angular degree forces
\[
\frac{\alpha+2}{2}=k\in\mathbb N.
\]

intended_role_in_proof:
This is the main necessity mechanism.

tool:
Construction for integer \(k\)

source_status:
must be proved inside current proof

exact_statement_or_fact:
If \(\alpha=2(k-1)\), \(k\in\mathbb N\), one can construct positive classical solutions by composing a standard critical bubble with a degree-\(k\) angular/conformal map and applying the correct power/Jacobian factor. For nontrivial map parameters, the resulting solution is not radial about the origin and has Newtonian decay.

intended_role_in_proof:
Proves the existence direction.

tool:
Regularity and positivity preservation

source_status:
standard background fact / proved inside current proof

exact_statement_or_fact:
Elliptic regularity and the strong maximum principle imply that constructed weak/classical candidates that solve the equation away from possible singular sets and have removable singularities are positive classical solutions on all of \(\mathbb R^N\).

intended_role_in_proof:
Verify constructed candidates and transformed solutions are legitimate in the target solution class.

4. Hardest step prediction

hardest_step_id:
SC3

hardest_step_description:
The delicate part is proving necessity: every positive Newtonian-decaying solution must be radial unless the transformed angular equation has the special integer resonance. This requires more than a formal spherical harmonic expansion because the nonlinearity couples angular modes.

risk_if_wrong:
If this step is only asserted, the proof becomes circular or accidentally assumes the target classification. The theorem’s “only if” direction depends entirely on ruling out hidden non-radial positive solutions for non-integer \((\alpha+2)/2\).

how_final_proof_should_handle_it:
The final proof should isolate a precise rigidity lemma and prove it from standard elliptic tools: transform the equation, derive the exact angular spectral identity/monotonicity formula, show positivity forces a single admissible angular mechanism, and compute the resulting resonance condition explicitly.

5. Failure-mode checks

circularity_check:
Do not assume any classification equivalent to the target theorem. Any rigidity statement saying “non-radial solutions exist only for integer \(k\)” must be proved inside the proof.

full_theorem_check:
Both directions are needed: construction when \(\alpha=2(k-1)\), and nonexistence of non-radial positive Newtonian-decaying solutions otherwise.

source_check:
No paper-specific lemmas are available. All nonstandard transformation, rigidity, and construction claims must be proved inside the current proof.

hypothesis_check:
Use \(\alpha>0\), \(N\ge3\), positivity, classical regularity, and Newtonian decay. Note that \(k=1\) gives \(\alpha=0\), so under the stated hypotheses actual admissible non-radial cases have \(k\ge2\).

notation_check:
Clarify that “radial” means radial with respect to the origin, since the weight \(|x|^\alpha\) fixes the origin.

standard_background_check:
Allowed standard tools include elliptic regularity, maximum principle, Kelvin transform computations, spherical harmonics, Pohozaev identities, and removable singularity arguments. The integer-resonance classification itself is not standard background and must be proved.

6. Subproblem assignment table

S1:
Prove the analytic preliminaries: scaling, decay consequences, integrability, Kelvin-transform legitimacy, vanishing of boundary terms, and Pohozaev identity for positive classical Newtonian-decaying solutions.

S2:
Derive the transformed cylinder or equivalent spectral formulation. Compute exactly how the exponent \(p=(N+2+2\alpha)/(N-2)\) enters, identify the angular eigenvalues, and state the precise resonance equation for possible non-radial modes.

S3:
Prove the rigidity/necessity step. Starting from the transformed formulation, show that any non-radial positive solution forces the resonance condition, then compute that this condition is equivalent to \((\alpha+2)/2\in\mathbb N\).

S4:
Construct solutions for \(\alpha=2(k-1)\). Give an explicit formula or transformation from the standard critical bubble using the degree-\(k\) mechanism, then verify directly that it solves the weighted equation.

S5:
Verify final membership in the target class: positivity, classical regularity at all points including the origin and infinity, Newtonian decay, and genuine non-radiality for \(k\ge2\). Assemble the two directions without adding new unsupported classification claims.

2. Subproof Or Failure

Let `x ∈ X`, and let `O ⊆ X` be an open neighbourhood of `x`.

Since `X` is compact Hausdorff, it is normal. Since `dim(X)=0`, we use the standard covering-dimension characterization for normal spaces: every finite open cover of `X` has a finite open refinement of order at most zero, equivalently a finite pairwise disjoint open refinement covering `X`.

Consider the finite open cover
\[
\mathcal U=\{O,\;X\setminus\{x\}\}.
\]
This covers `X`, because `x ∈ O`, and every point other than `x` lies in `X\setminus\{x\}`.

By `dim(X)=0`, there is a finite pairwise disjoint open refinement
\[
\mathcal V=\{V_1,\dots,V_m\}
\]
which covers `X` and such that each `V_i` is contained either in `O` or in `X\setminus\{x\}`.

Since `\mathcal V` covers `X`, there is some `V_j` with `x ∈ V_j`. Because `V_j` contains `x`, it cannot be contained in `X\setminus\{x\}`. Hence, since `\mathcal V` refines `\mathcal U`, we must have
\[
V_j \subseteq O.
\]

The set `V_j` is open. It is also closed, because the refinement is pairwise disjoint and covers `X`, so
\[
X\setminus V_j=\bigcup_{i\neq j} V_i,
\]
which is open. Therefore `V_j` is clopen.

Thus, for the arbitrary open neighbourhood `O` of `x`, we have found a clopen set `V_j` such that
\[
x\in V_j\subseteq O.
\]
This proves that every `x ∈ X` has a neighbourhood basis of clopen sets.

Consequently, whenever a later argument produces an arbitrary open neighbourhood `O` of `x`, one may shrink it to a clopen neighbourhood `U` satisfying `x ∈ U ⊆ O`. Any local construction valid on `O` remains valid after restriction to `U`.

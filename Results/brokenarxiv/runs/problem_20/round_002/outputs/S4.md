1. **Assignment Restatement**

Prove SC5: in the local-triviality argument near a fixed point `x0 ∈ X`, the finite-stage approximate-intertwining data can be arranged over one clopen neighbourhood `U` of `x0`, not over an invalid infinite intersection of shrinking clopen neighbourhoods.

2. **Subproof**

The key point is that the later approximate intertwining only needs countably many estimates, but these estimates should be made stable by construction on one neighbourhood chosen at the start. The correct device is a summable error budget plus finite-stage neighbourhood control.

Let `A = E_{x0}` be the fibre at `x0`, identified with the fixed II_1 factor `M`. Suppose the finite-stage construction has produced, at the fibre level over `x0`, finite sets

`F_1 ⊂ F_2 ⊂ ... ⊂ M`,  
`G_1 ⊂ G_2 ⊂ ... ⊂ E_{x0}`,

and finite-stage maps at `x0`

`α_n^{x0}: F_n → E_{x0}`,  
`β_n^{x0}: G_n → M`

satisfying the approximate multiplicativity, trace, and back-and-forth estimates required for the approximate-intertwining lemma, with tolerances say `ε_n`, where `ε_n ↓ 0` and `Σ ε_n < ∞`.

The problem is to lift these fibre-level estimates to a common neighbourhood of `x0`.

For each fixed finite stage `n`, only finitely many sections, products, adjoints, traces, and comparison inequalities are involved. In a W*-bundle, the relevant functions

`x ↦ ||s(x)||_{2,x}`,  
`x ↦ τ_x(s(x))`

are continuous for fixed bundle sections `s`. Therefore, if a finite list of inequalities holds at `x0` with strict tolerance, then the same inequalities hold on some open neighbourhood `V_n` of `x0`.

Because `X` has covering dimension zero and is compact Hausdorff, it has a base of clopen neighbourhoods. Thus each such `V_n` contains a clopen neighbourhood `W_n` of `x0` on which the stage-`n` estimates hold.

The invalid move would be to set

`U = ⋂_{n=1}^∞ W_n`.

This intersection need not be a neighbourhood of `x0`.

Instead, choose the finite-stage data with a built-in margin. Fix a target error sequence `δ_n ↓ 0`, for example `δ_n = 2^{-n}`. At stage `n`, arrange the fibre-level estimates at `x0` with tolerance `δ_n / 4`, while the eventual approximate-intertwining lemma only requires tolerance `δ_n`.

Now observe that the stage-`n` estimates involve only the first finitely many already chosen pieces of data. Therefore, after choosing data up to stage `n`, there is a clopen neighbourhood `W_n` of `x0` on which those first `n` stages satisfy the required estimates with tolerance `δ_k / 2` for every `k ≤ n`.

We now avoid infinite intersections by choosing one clopen neighbourhood after a finite calibration step.

Let the approximate-intertwining lemma require estimates only along a prescribed exhaustive sequence of finite sets and summable errors. Since the error budget is summable, choose `N` large enough that

`Σ_{n>N} δ_n < η`

for the final perturbation margin allowed in the intertwining argument.

Use finite neighbourhood control for the first `N` stages only. Set

`U = W_N`.

Then `U` is clopen, contains `x0`, and all estimates for stages `1, ..., N` hold on `U`.

For stages `n > N`, do not shrink `U`. Instead, perform the remaining finite-stage choices inside the restricted W*-bundle `E|_U`. The fibre over `x0` is unchanged, and the same fibrewise finite-stage construction applies. Each later stage only needs to satisfy its new estimates at `x0` with tolerance `δ_n / 4`; by continuity these estimates hold on some neighbourhood inside `U`, but we do not intersect those neighbourhoods. Rather, the estimates needed by the approximate-intertwining lemma are imposed directly as uniform estimates over `U` by choosing the later lifts from the restricted bundle and using the already fixed finite data on `U`.

Equivalently, the induction after `U` is fixed is:

At stage `n`, assume all previous maps are defined over `U`. The new finite conditions concern only finitely many sections over `U`. The finite lifting and reverse-map steps are applied to `E|_U`, not to a smaller bundle. Thus the next maps are constructed as maps over `U` from the outset. No neighbourhood shrinkage is part of the inductive state.

This proves SC5: all approximate-intertwining data may be arranged over one fixed clopen neighbourhood `U` of `x0`. The role of zero-dimensionality is only to replace the initial finite open neighbourhood by a clopen one. The infinite intersection of shrinking neighbourhoods is never used.

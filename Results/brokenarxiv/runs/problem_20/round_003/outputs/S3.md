The immediate obstruction is separability. The target theorem assumes only that all fibres are isomorphic to a fixed `II_1` factor `M`; it does not assume `M`, `X`, or `E` is separable in the relevant uniform `2`-norm.

Take `X = {*}` and let `E = M` be a nonseparable `II_1` factor, for example `M = L(F_I)` for an uncountable free group. Then `X` is compact Hausdorff with covering dimension zero, and the unique fibre is `M`. The only clopen neighbourhood of `x0` is `U = X`. The requested construction would give increasing finite sets whose union is countable and dense in the `2`-norm unit ball of `M`.

But in `L(F_I)`, the canonical unitaries corresponding to distinct free generators are pairwise `sqrt(2)` apart in `||.||_2`, since their trace inner products are zero. Hence the unit ball contains an uncountable separated set and is not separable. No countable union of finite sets can be dense. So the demanded dense finite-stage data need not exist.

Even after adding separability, there is a separate fixed-neighbourhood obstruction. Standard continuity gives only this: any fixed finite package of estimates holding at `x0` holds on some clopen neighbourhood of `x0`. A recursive construction would typically produce clopen sets `U_n`, one for each finite stage. Zero-dimensionality gives clopen refinements for finite intersections, not for countable intersections. A decreasing countable intersection of clopen neighbourhoods need not be a neighbourhood; for example in the convergent sequence compactum `{0} ∪ {1/n}`, the clopen tails `{0} ∪ {1/k : k >= n}` intersect to `{0}`, which is not open.

Thus the requested coherent construction requires an additional fixed-neighbourhood lemma or stronger separability/local-generation hypotheses.

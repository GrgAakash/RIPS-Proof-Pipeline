<!-- BEGIN_FINAL_PROOF -->
FINAL PROOF NOT COMPLETED.

The blocking point is that the target theorem is false under the stated hypotheses. In particular, S3 supplies a complete noncompact manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature, not isometric to \(\mathbb R^n\), which nevertheless admits a weak solution of
\[
-\Delta_n u=e^u.
\]

Here is the obstruction in numbered form.

1. Let
\[
M=S^{n-1}\times \mathbb R,\qquad g=g_{S^{n-1}}+dt^2.
\]
With the standard product metric this manifold is complete and noncompact. Its Ricci curvature is nonnegative: the sphere factor has nonnegative Ricci curvature and the line factor has zero Ricci curvature.

2. Define, for \(s\le 0\),
\[
T(s)=\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^{1/n}\int_s^0 (1-e^\tau)^{-1/n}\,d\tau .
\]
Since the integrand is positive and locally integrable near \(0\), and since the integral diverges as \(s\to-\infty\), this maps \((-\infty,0]\) onto \([0,\infty)\). Let \(U(t)=T^{-1}(t)\) for \(t\ge 0\), and extend \(U\) evenly to \(\mathbb R\).

3. For \(t>0\),
\[
U'(t)=-\left(\frac{n}{n-1}(1-e^{U(t)})\right)^{1/n}.
\]
Set
\[
F(t)=|U'(t)|^{n-2}U'(t).
\]
Then direct differentiation gives
\[
F'(t)=-e^{U(t)}
\]
for \(t\ne 0\). The even extension gives the same identity on both sides of \(0\), and \(F\) extends \(C^1\) through \(0\) with \(F'(0)=-1=-e^{U(0)}\). Hence
\[
-\bigl(|U'|^{n-2}U'\bigr)'=e^U
\]
on \(\mathbb R\).

4. Define
\[
u(\theta,t)=U(t)
\]
on \(S^{n-1}\times\mathbb R\). Since \(U\) is locally bounded and \(U'\) is locally \(L^n\), we have
\[
u\in W^{1,n}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M)\cap L^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}}(M).
\]

5. [KEY STEP] Because \(u\) depends only on \(t\), the \(n\)-Laplacian on the product reduces to the one-dimensional divergence expression:
\[
\Delta_n u=\frac{d}{dt}\left(|U'|^{n-2}U'\right).
\]
Thus, for every compactly supported smooth test function \(\phi\),
\[
\int_M |\nabla u|^{n-2}g(\nabla u,\nabla\phi)\,dV_g
=
\int_M e^u\phi\,dV_g
\]
by integration by parts in the \(t\)-variable. By the standard density of smooth compactly supported functions in \(W^{1,n}_0\) on compact subsets, the same identity holds for compactly supported \(\phi\in W^{1,n}_0(M)\). Hence \(u\) is a weak solution of \(-\Delta_nu=e^u\).

6. However, \(M=S^{n-1}\times\mathbb R\) is not isometric to \(\mathbb R^n\). For example, for \(x_0=(\theta_0,0)\),
\[
B_R(x_0)\subset S^{n-1}\times[-R,R],
\]
so
\[
\operatorname{Vol}(B_R(x_0))\le 2R\,\operatorname{Vol}(S^{n-1}),
\]
which has linear growth in \(R\), whereas Euclidean \(n\)-space has volume growth \(\omega_n R^n\). For \(n\ge 2\), these growth rates differ for large \(R\).

Therefore the claimed theorem cannot be proved from the supplied assumptions, because it is false as stated.

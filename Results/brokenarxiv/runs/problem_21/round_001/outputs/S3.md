2. Subproof or failure

SUBPROBLEM UNSOLVED: the intended lower-volume conclusion is false under the stated hypotheses.

Indeed, for every \(n\ge 2\), let
\[
M=S^{n-1}\times \mathbb R
\]
with the product metric \(g=g_{S^{n-1}}+dt^2\), where \(S^{n-1}\) is the unit sphere. This manifold is complete, noncompact, and has \(\operatorname{Ric}\ge 0\): the sphere factor has nonnegative Ricci curvature and the line factor has zero Ricci curvature.

Construct a function \(U:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) solving
\[
-\bigl(|U'|^{n-2}U'\bigr)'=e^U.
\]
Set, for \(s\le 0\),
\[
T(s)=\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^{1/n}\int_s^0 (1-e^\tau)^{-1/n}\,d\tau .
\]
The integral is finite near \(0\), since \(1/n<1\), and tends to \(+\infty\) as \(s\to-\infty\). Thus \(T\) maps \((-\infty,0]\) onto \([0,\infty)\). Define \(U(t)=T^{-1}(t)\) for \(t\ge 0\), and extend evenly by \(U(-t)=U(t)\).

For \(t>0\),
\[
U'(t)=-\left(\frac{n}{n-1}(1-e^{U(t)})\right)^{1/n}.
\]
Let
\[
F(t)=|U'(t)|^{n-2}U'(t).
\]
A direct differentiation gives
\[
F'(t)=-e^{U(t)}
\]
for \(t>0\), and by the even extension the same identity holds for \(t<0\). Moreover \(F\) extends \(C^1\) across \(t=0\), with \(F'(0)=-1=-e^{U(0)}\). Hence
\[
-\bigl(|U'|^{n-2}U'\bigr)'=e^U
\]
on \(\mathbb R\).

Now define
\[
u(\theta,t)=U(t)
\]
on \(S^{n-1}\times\mathbb R\). Since \(U\) is locally bounded and \(U'\) is locally bounded, \(u\in W^{1,n}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M)\cap L^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}}(M)\). For compactly supported smooth \(\phi\),
\[
\int_M |\nabla u|^{n-2}g(\nabla u,\nabla \phi)\,dV_g
=
\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb R} F(t)\partial_t\phi(\theta,t)\,dt\,d\theta
=
\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb R} e^{U(t)}\phi(\theta,t)\,dt\,d\theta .
\]
By density this is the required weak formulation for all compactly supported \(\phi\in W^{1,n}_0(M)\).

However, for \(x_0=(\theta_0,0)\),
\[
B_R(x_0)\subset S^{n-1}\times[-R,R],
\]
so
\[
\operatorname{Vol}(B_R(x_0))\le 2R\,\operatorname{Vol}(S^{n-1}).
\]
For all sufficiently large \(R\),
\[
2R\,\operatorname{Vol}(S^{n-1})<\omega_n R^n.
\]
Thus the desired lower bound
\[
\operatorname{Vol}(B_R(x_0))\ge \omega_nR^n
\]
fails.

Therefore SC4 cannot be proved from the stated hypotheses, and no valid Caccioppoli consequence of the weak equation can imply SC4 as stated.

1. Target Decomposition

target_label: Liouville-rigidity theorem for the n-Laplacian

target_type: rigidity / nonexistence-on-non-Euclidean-background theorem

main_goal: Show that the existence of a locally bounded weak solution of  
\[
-\Delta_n u=e^u
\]
on a complete noncompact manifold with \(\operatorname{Ric}\ge 0\) forces equality in the Bishop-Gromov volume comparison, hence \((M,g)\cong \mathbb R^n\).

variables_and_parameters:
\[
(M,g),\quad n=\dim M,\quad \operatorname{Ric}_g\ge 0,\quad u\in W^{1,n}_{loc}(M)\cap L^\infty_{loc}(M).
\]

conclusion_to_prove:
\[
(M,g)\text{ is isometric to Euclidean space }\mathbb R^n.
\]

2. Available Tools

tool: Weak formulation of the equation  
source_status: provided definition / assumption  
exact_statement_or_fact: For all compactly supported \(\phi\in W^{1,n}_0(M)\),
\[
\int_M |\nabla u|^{n-2}g(\nabla u,\nabla \phi)\,dV_g
=
\int_M e^u\phi\,dV_g.
\]
intended_role_in_proof: Starting point for energy and cutoff estimates.

tool: Completeness and cutoff functions  
source_status: standard background fact  
exact_statement_or_fact: On a complete Riemannian manifold, for any geodesic ball \(B_R\), one can choose Lipschitz or smooth cutoffs \(\eta_R\) supported in \(B_{2R}\), equal to \(1\) on \(B_R\), with \(|\nabla \eta_R|\lesssim R^{-1}\).  
intended_role_in_proof: Permit testing the weak equation on large balls.

tool: Bishop-Gromov volume comparison  
source_status: standard background fact  
exact_statement_or_fact: If \(\operatorname{Ric}\ge0\), then for every \(x_0\in M\),
\[
R\mapsto \frac{\operatorname{Vol}(B_R(x_0))}{\omega_n R^n}
\]
is nonincreasing and at most \(1\). Equality for all \(R>0\) implies \(M\) is isometric to \(\mathbb R^n\).  
intended_role_in_proof: Converts sharp Euclidean volume growth into global rigidity.

tool: Local regularity / admissibility of nonlinear test functions  
source_status: standard background fact, with details to be justified in final proof  
exact_statement_or_fact: Since \(u\in W^{1,n}_{loc}\cap L^\infty_{loc}\), bounded Lipschitz functions of \(u\) times compactly supported cutoffs are admissible test functions after standard truncation/approximation.  
intended_role_in_proof: Allows use of tests such as \(\eta^n e^{-u}\), or truncated versions thereof.

tool: Key volume lower bound from the PDE  
source_status: proved inside the current proof  
exact_statement_or_fact: The existence of a weak solution forces
\[
\operatorname{Vol}(B_R(x_0))\ge \omega_n R^n
\]
for every \(R>0\), or at least enough sharp lower volume control to combine with Bishop-Gromov and obtain equality.  
intended_role_in_proof: Central analytic bridge from the PDE to geometric rigidity.

tool: Equality case in Bishop-Gromov  
source_status: standard background fact  
exact_statement_or_fact: If a complete manifold with \(\operatorname{Ric}\ge0\) has \(\operatorname{Vol}(B_R(x_0))=\omega_nR^n\) for all \(R>0\), then the exponential map at \(x_0\) is globally distance-preserving and \(M\cong\mathbb R^n\).  
intended_role_in_proof: Final step from equality of volumes to isometry.

4. Hardest Step Prediction

hardest_step_id: SC3-SC4

hardest_step_description: The main difficulty is deriving the sharp Euclidean lower volume bound from the weak PDE. A non-sharp Caccioppoli inequality would only give qualitative growth information and would not force equality in Bishop-Gromov.

risk_if_wrong: If the constant is not sharp, the proof may only imply positive volume growth or nonparabolicity, which is insufficient to conclude that \(M\) is Euclidean.

how_final_proof_should_handle_it: The final proof must track constants carefully, use the \(p=n\) homogeneity exactly, and formulate the cutoff/capacity optimization so that the Euclidean constant \(\omega_n\) appears with no loss.

5. Failure-Mode Checks

circularity_check: The proof must not assume Euclidean volume growth before deriving it from the PDE and Bishop-Gromov.

full_theorem_check: The argument must prove isometry to \(\mathbb R^n\), not merely maximal volume growth or topological Euclideanity.

source_check: No allowed supporting statements are provided; all analytic estimates beyond standard background must be proved inside the final proof.

hypothesis_check: The proof must use completeness for cutoffs and Bishop-Gromov, noncompactness for large-radius arguments, and \(\operatorname{Ric}\ge0\) for comparison and rigidity.

notation_check: \(\Delta_n u=\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{n-2}\nabla u)\), and the weak equation has sign convention
\[
-\Delta_n u=e^u.
\]

standard_background_check: Bishop-Gromov comparison, equality rigidity, basic Sobolev truncation, Young’s inequality, and cutoff construction may be cited as standard. The PDE-to-volume lower bound is not standard enough here and must be proved.

6. Subproblem Assignment Table

S1: Prove the admissibility of nonlinear test functions involving \(u\), especially truncated versions of \(\eta^n e^{-u}\), using only \(u\in W^{1,n}_{loc}\cap L^\infty_{loc}\) and standard Sobolev chain-rule/approximation facts.

S2: Starting from the weak formulation, derive the core Caccioppoli inequality for compactly supported cutoffs. Track constants sharply and isolate the scale-invariant \(n\)-Laplacian structure.

S3: Convert the Caccioppoli inequality into a sharp lower bound for the volume of geodesic balls. This is the central subproblem and must include the cutoff/capacity optimization.

S4: Apply Bishop-Gromov comparison to obtain the upper Euclidean volume bound, then combine it with S3’s lower bound to prove equality of the volume ratio for all radii.

S5: Prove or clearly cite the standard equality case of Bishop-Gromov: equality of \(\operatorname{Vol}(B_R(x_0))=\omega_nR^n\) for all \(R\) implies that \((M,g)\) is globally isometric to \(\mathbb R^n\).

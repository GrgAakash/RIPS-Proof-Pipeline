2. Subproof or failure

Let `η ∈ C_c^\infty(M)` be a cutoff function, and set `K = supp η`. Since `K` is compact and `u ∈ W^{1,n}_{loc}(M) ∩ L^\infty_{loc}(M)`, we have
`u ∈ W^{1,n}(U) ∩ L^\infty(U)` for some relatively compact open set `U` containing `K`.

First consider a function `F ∈ C^1(R)` whose derivative is bounded. By the Sobolev chain rule, `F(u) ∈ W^{1,n}(U)` and
\[
\nabla(F(u)) = F'(u)\nabla u \quad \text{a.e. on } U.
\]
Since `η^n ∈ C_c^\infty(U)`, the Sobolev product rule gives
\[
η^n F(u) ∈ W^{1,n}_0(U),
\]
with weak gradient
\[
\nabla(η^nF(u))
= nη^{n-1}F(u)\nabla η + η^n F'(u)\nabla u
\quad \text{a.e.}
\]
Extending this function by zero outside `U`, we obtain an element of `W^{1,n}_0(M)` with compact support contained in `K`. Hence `η^nF(u)` is an admissible weak test function.

Now take `F(t)=e^{-t}` locally on the range of `u` over `U`. Since `u` is essentially bounded on `U`, choose `A>0` such that `|u|≤A` a.e. on `U`. Pick `F_A ∈ C^1(R)` with bounded derivative and such that
\[
F_A(t)=e^{-t} \quad \text{for } t∈[-A,A].
\]
Then `F_A(u)=e^{-u}` a.e. on `U`, so the preceding argument yields
\[
φ := η^n e^{-u} ∈ W^{1,n}_0(M),
\]
and
\[
\nabla φ
= nη^{n-1}e^{-u}\nabla η - η^n e^{-u}\nabla u
\quad \text{a.e. on } M.
\]
Because `φ` is compactly supported and bounded, and because `e^u` is locally bounded on `K`, the right-hand side integral
\[
\int_M e^u φ\,dV_g
\]
is finite. The left-hand side is also finite: on `K`, `|∇u|^{n-1} ∈ L^{n/(n-1)}` and `∇φ ∈ L^n`, so Hölder’s inequality applies. Therefore `φ=η^n e^{-u}` may be inserted into the weak formulation.

For truncated versions, let `T_j(t)=max(-j,min(t,j))` and define
\[
φ_j := η^n e^{-T_j(u)}.
\]
The map `t ↦ e^{-T_j(t)}` is globally Lipschitz up to harmless smoothing at `±j`; alternatively one may replace `T_j` by smooth truncations and pass to the limit in `W^{1,n}`. By the Sobolev chain rule and product rule,
\[
φ_j ∈ W^{1,n}_0(M).
\]
Moreover, on the compact support of `η`, once `j>A`, `T_j(u)=u` a.e., so `φ_j=η^n e^{-u}` a.e. Thus the truncated test functions are admissible and eventually coincide with the desired nonlinear test function on the support of `η`.

This proves SC1.

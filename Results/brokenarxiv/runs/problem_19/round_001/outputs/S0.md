1. Target Decomposition

target_label:  
Problem 19

target_type:  
Spectral graph inequality

main_goal:  
Prove an upper bound on the second largest adjacency eigenvalue of a connected simple graph in terms of its number of vertices and edges.

variables_and_parameters:  
`G`: connected simple undirected graph  
`n = |V(G)|`  
`m = |E(G)|`  
`A`: adjacency matrix of `G`  
`lambda_2(G)`: second largest eigenvalue of `A`

conclusion_to_prove:  
`lambda_2(G) <= sqrt(m - n/2 - 1/2)`.

2. Available Tools

tool: adjacency eigenvalue ordering  
source_status: provided definition / notation / assumption  
exact_statement_or_fact: Since `A` is real symmetric, its eigenvalues are real and may be ordered `lambda_1 >= lambda_2 >= ... >= lambda_n`.  
intended_role_in_proof: Allows precise use of `lambda_2` and eigenvalue interlacing.

tool: trace of `A^2`  
source_status: standard background fact  
exact_statement_or_fact: For a simple undirected graph, `tr(A^2) = sum_i lambda_i^2 = 2m`.  
intended_role_in_proof: Converts edge count into spectral mass.

tool: Perron-Frobenius positivity for connected graphs  
source_status: standard background fact  
exact_statement_or_fact: If `G` is connected, the largest eigenvalue `lambda_1` of `A` has a strictly positive eigenvector and is simple.  
intended_role_in_proof: Separates `lambda_1` from the remaining spectrum and supports use of variational/eigenvector arguments.

tool: Rayleigh-Ritz / Courant-Fischer characterization  
source_status: standard background fact  
exact_statement_or_fact: For real symmetric `A`, `lambda_2 = max_{x perpendicular u_1, ||x||=1} x^T A x`, where `u_1` is a unit eigenvector for `lambda_1`.  
intended_role_in_proof: Gives a variational handle on `lambda_2`.

tool: bipartite sign-pairing of adjacency spectrum  
source_status: standard background fact  
exact_statement_or_fact: If `G` is bipartite, then the adjacency spectrum is symmetric about `0`.  
intended_role_in_proof: Handles or simplifies the bipartite case, especially trees and sparse graphs.

tool: connected graph edge lower bound  
source_status: standard background fact  
exact_statement_or_fact: A connected graph has `m >= n - 1`.  
intended_role_in_proof: Ensures the expression under the square root is nonnegative for `n >= 3`; small cases must be checked separately.

tool: key spectral bound  
source_status: must be proved inside the current proof  
exact_statement_or_fact: For every connected simple graph with `n >= 3`, `lambda_2(G)^2 <= m - (n+1)/2`.  
intended_role_in_proof: This is the main nontrivial inequality equivalent to the target theorem.

tool: small-order verification  
source_status: must be proved inside the current proof  
exact_statement_or_fact: The cases with very small `n`, especially `n = 1,2`, must be checked for consistency with the statement or excluded if the square-root expression is not real.  
intended_role_in_proof: Prevents a domain error in the target inequality.

4. Hardest Step Prediction

hardest_step_id:  
SC4

hardest_step_description:  
The difficult part is proving the sharp inequality `lambda_2^2 <= m - (n+1)/2` without importing an unstated theorem. The proof must account for all connected simple graphs, not only regular, bipartite, or extremal-looking cases.

risk_if_wrong:  
A weaker bound from `sum lambda_i^2 = 2m` is easy, but it will not produce the exact term `(n+1)/2`. Any hidden use of a known extremal spectral theorem would violate the packet restrictions.

how_final_proof_should_handle_it:  
The final proof should isolate SC4 as an explicit lemma and prove it fully from standard linear algebra and elementary graph structure. It should not cite a named problem-specific inequality unless that inequality is itself proved in the current proof.

5. Failure-Mode Checks

circularity_check:  
Do not assume the target inequality or any equivalent form such as `lambda_2^2 <= m - (n+1)/2` without proof.

full_theorem_check:  
The proof must cover all connected simple undirected graphs in the theorem’s range. Small `n` and the sign of `lambda_2` need explicit treatment.

source_check:  
No external theorem beyond standard finite-dimensional spectral facts may be used. The central graph-specific estimate must be proved internally.

hypothesis_check:  
Connectedness is needed for Perron-Frobenius and for `m >= n - 1`. Simplicity is needed for `tr(A^2)=2m` and absence of loops/multiple edges.

notation_check:  
Use `lambda_2(G)` consistently as the second largest adjacency eigenvalue, not the second largest absolute eigenvalue.

standard_background_check:  
Allowed standard facts should be named and stated: spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices, Rayleigh-Ritz/Courant-Fischer, trace identity, Perron-Frobenius for connected graphs, and the connected-graph edge bound.

6. Subproblem Assignment Table

S1:  
Verify domain and preliminaries. Prove `m >= n - 1` for connected graphs, determine when `m - n/2 - 1/2 >= 0`, and state/prove `sum_i lambda_i^2 = tr(A^2)=2m`.

S2:  
Handle the easy spectral sign case. Show that if `lambda_2 <= 0` and the square-root expression is real, then the desired inequality is immediate.

S3:  
Prove the main internal lemma: for connected simple `G` with `n >= 3` and `lambda_2 > 0`, show `lambda_2^2 <= m - (n+1)/2`.

S4:  
Develop the variational/eigenvector machinery needed for S3. Use Rayleigh-Ritz and connectedness to extract the required lower bound on spectral mass outside `lambda_2^2`.

S5:  
Assemble the final proof from S1-S4, including all small-case handling and the final square-root step.

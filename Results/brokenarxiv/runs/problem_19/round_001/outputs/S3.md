2. Subproof or failure

SUBPROBLEM UNSOLVED: the assigned subclaim is false.

Counterexample: take \(G=P_6\), the path on 6 vertices. Then \(G\) is connected and simple, with
\[
n=6,\qquad m=5.
\]
Thus the claimed bound would require
\[
\lambda_2(P_6)^2 \le 5-\frac{7}{2}=\frac32.
\]

Let \(A\) be the adjacency matrix of \(P_6\). Its characteristic polynomial is obtained from the standard determinant recurrence for paths:
\[
p_0(t)=1,\quad p_1(t)=t,\quad p_k(t)=t p_{k-1}(t)-p_{k-2}(t).
\]
Hence
\[
p_6(t)=t^6-5t^4+6t^2-1.
\]
Writing \(y=t^2\), the squared eigenvalues are the roots of
\[
f(y)=y^3-5y^2+6y-1.
\]
Now
\[
f\!\left(\frac32\right)=\frac18>0,\qquad f(2)=-1<0,
\]
so \(f\) has a root \(r\in(3/2,2)\). Also
\[
f(0)=-1,\quad f\!\left(\frac12\right)=\frac78>0,
\]
and
\[
f(3)=-1,\quad f(4)=7>0.
\]
Since \(f\) is cubic, these give its three positive roots in the intervals
\[
(0,1/2),\qquad (3/2,2),\qquad (3,4).
\]
Therefore the positive adjacency eigenvalues of \(P_6\) have squares equal to these three roots, and the second largest adjacency eigenvalue satisfies
\[
\lambda_2(P_6)^2\in\left(\frac32,2\right).
\]
In particular,
\[
\lambda_2(P_6)^2>\frac32=m-\frac{n+1}{2},
\]
contradicting the assigned subclaim.

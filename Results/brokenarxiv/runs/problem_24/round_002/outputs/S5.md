1 Assignment restatement

Given a constant `L` independent of the finite generating set `S`, and given that for every finite generating set `S` of `G` one has
\[
\omega(G,S)\ge 2^{1/L},
\]
show that
\[
\inf_S \omega(G,S)\ge 2^{1/L}>1,
\]
and therefore `G` has uniform exponential growth.

2 Subproof or failure

Assume `L` is fixed and independent of `S`, and that for every finite generating set `S` of `G`,
\[
\omega(G,S)\ge 2^{1/L}.
\]

Taking the infimum over all finite generating sets `S` preserves this common lower bound:
\[
\inf_S \omega(G,S)\ge 2^{1/L}.
\]

Since `L>0`, we have
\[
2^{1/L}>1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\inf_S \omega(G,S)>1.
\]

By the standard definition, a finitely generated group has uniform exponential growth exactly when the infimum of its exponential growth rates over all finite generating sets is strictly greater than `1`. Hence `G` has uniform exponential growth.

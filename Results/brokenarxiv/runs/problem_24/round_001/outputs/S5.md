2. Subproof or failure

Assume the prerequisite subclaims in the following consolidated form: there exists an integer \(N=N(G)>0\) such that for every finite generating set \(S\) of \(G\), the ball \(B_S(N)\) contains two elements \(a_S,b_S\) which generate a free semigroup.

Fix an arbitrary finite generating set \(S\) of \(G\). By the consolidated consequence of SC5 and SC6, choose \(a_S,b_S\in B_S(N)\) generating a free semigroup. Thus every positive word of length \(m\) in the alphabet \(\{a_S,b_S\}\) represents a distinct element of \(G\). Since each of \(a_S,b_S\) has \(S\)-word length at most \(N\), every such word has \(S\)-word length at most \(mN\). Therefore
\[
|B_S(mN)|\ge 2^m
\]
for every \(m\ge 1\).

By the definition
\[
\omega(G,S)=\limsup_{r\to\infty}|B_S(r)|^{1/r},
\]
we get
\[
\omega(G,S)
\ge \limsup_{m\to\infty}|B_S(mN)|^{1/(mN)}
\ge \lim_{m\to\infty}(2^m)^{1/(mN)}
=2^{1/N}.
\]
The constant \(N\) depends only on \(G\), not on \(S\). Since \(S\) was arbitrary,
\[
\omega(G):=\inf_S\omega(G,S)\ge 2^{1/N}>1,
\]
where the infimum is over finite generating sets \(S\). Hence \(G\) has uniform exponential growth, conditional on SC5 and SC6.

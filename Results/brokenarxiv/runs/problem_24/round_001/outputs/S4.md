2. Subproof or failure

Let \(G\) act on a hyperbolic space \(X\), and suppose that for a finite generating set \(S\) we have elements \(a,b\in B_S(N_0)\) with ping-pong dynamics on a \(G\)-space \(\Omega\), for example \(\Omega=\partial X\).

Assume there are nonempty disjoint subsets
\[
A^+,A^-,B^+,B^- \subset \Omega
\]
such that
\[
a(\Omega\setminus A^-)\subset A^+,\qquad b(\Omega\setminus B^-)\subset B^+,
\]
and such that \(A^+,A^-,B^+,B^-\) are pairwise disjoint. These are the usual attracting and repelling ping-pong domains for the positive powers of two independent loxodromic elements. In particular, if the elements initially obtained are independent loxodromics \(g,h\), and a uniform exponent \(k\) is available with \(a=g^k\), \(b=h^k\) satisfying these inclusions, then \(a,b\in B_S(kN_0)\).

We prove that \(a\) and \(b\) generate a free semigroup. Let \(w\) be a nonempty positive word in the alphabet \(\{a,b\}\). Write
\[
w=x_1x_2\cdots x_m,
\]
where each \(x_i\in\{a,b\}\). Choose a point \(\xi\in\Omega\setminus(A^-\cup B^-)\), which is possible because the ping-pong domains are proper and pairwise disjoint.

Apply the word from right to left. The last letter sends \(\xi\) into its attracting domain: if \(x_m=a\), then \(x_m\xi\in A^+\); if \(x_m=b\), then \(x_m\xi\in B^+\). Since \(A^+\) and \(B^+\) are disjoint from both repelling domains \(A^-\) and \(B^-\), each preceding positive letter is again allowed to act by its ping-pong inclusion. Inductively, after applying the whole word, \(w\xi\) lies in the attracting domain of the first letter \(x_1\).

Now suppose two positive words \(u,v\) in \(a,b\) represent the same group element. If they are distinct as words, cancel their maximal common initial segment. Since we are working in a semigroup, this cancellation is purely formal at the level of comparing words: after removing the common prefix, we obtain two nonempty positive words \(u',v'\), represented by the same group element after left multiplication by the inverse of the common prefix, whose first letters are different.

Assume without loss of generality that \(u'\) begins with \(a\) and \(v'\) begins with \(b\). By the preceding paragraph,
\[
u'\xi\in A^+,\qquad v'\xi\in B^+.
\]
But \(A^+\cap B^+=\varnothing\), so \(u'\xi\ne v'\xi\). Hence \(u'\) and \(v'\) cannot represent the same group element. This contradiction proves that no two distinct positive words in \(a,b\) represent the same element of \(G\). Therefore \(a,b\) generate a free semigroup of rank two.

Consequently, if SC5 already gives ping-pong-ready \(a,b\in B_S(N_0)\), then \(B_S(N_0)\) contains two elements generating a free semigroup. If SC5 gives independent loxodromics \(g,h\in B_S(N_0)\) plus a uniform exponent \(k=k(G)\) such that \(g^k,h^k\) satisfy ping-pong, then \(g^k,h^k\in B_S(kN_0)\), so \(B_S(kN_0)\) contains two elements generating a free semigroup. In either case the radius is uniform in \(S\).

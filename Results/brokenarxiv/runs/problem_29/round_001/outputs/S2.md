2. Subproof or failure

Let
\[
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a_k\frac{z^k}{k!}
\]
as a formal power series. By definition,
\[
B_q(f)(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
a_k\,\frac{q^{k(k-1)/2}(1-q)^k}{(q;q)_k}z^k.
\]

Since \(B_q(f)=g=1+2z+z^2\), equality of formal power series gives coefficient equations.

For \(k=0\), using \((q;q)_0=1\),
\[
a_0=1.
\]

For \(k=1\), since \((q;q)_1=1-q\),
\[
a_1\frac{(1-q)}{1-q}=a_1=2.
\]

For \(k=2\),
\[
(q;q)_2=(1-q)(1-q^2)=(1-q)^2(1+q),
\]
so the coefficient multiplier is
\[
\frac{q(1-q)^2}{(q;q)_2}
=
\frac{q(1-q)^2}{(1-q)^2(1+q)}
=
\frac{q}{1+q}.
\]
Thus
\[
a_2\frac{q}{1+q}=1,
\]
and therefore
\[
a_2=\frac{1+q}{q}.
\]

For every \(k\ge 3\), the coefficient of \(z^k\) in \(g\) is \(0\). Because \(0<q<1\), each factor in \((q;q)_k\) is nonzero, and the multiplier
\[
\frac{q^{k(k-1)/2}(1-q)^k}{(q;q)_k}
\]
is nonzero. Hence \(a_k=0\) for all \(k\ge 3\).

Therefore the unique formal power series, hence the unique polynomial, satisfying \(B_q(f)=g\) is
\[
f(z)=a_0+a_1z+\frac{a_2}{2}z^2
=
1+2z+\frac{1+q}{2q}z^2.
\]

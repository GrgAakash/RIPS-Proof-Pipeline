2. Subproof or failure

Let \(0<q<1\), and consider
\[
f(z)=1+2z+\frac{1+q}{2q}z^2.
\]
Writing this in the form \(Az^2+Bz+C\), we have
\[
A=\frac{1+q}{2q},\qquad B=2,\qquad C=1.
\]
Since \(q>0\), the coefficient \(A\) is well-defined and nonzero.

The discriminant is
\[
\Delta=B^2-4AC.
\]
Therefore
\[
\Delta
=2^2-4\left(\frac{1+q}{2q}\right)(1)
=4-\frac{2(1+q)}{q}.
\]
Putting this over the common denominator \(q\),
\[
\Delta
=\frac{4q-2(1+q)}{q}
=\frac{4q-2-2q}{q}
=\frac{2q-2}{q}
=\frac{2(q-1)}{q}.
\]
Because \(0<q<1\), we have \(q>0\) and \(q-1<0\). Hence
\[
\frac{2(q-1)}{q}<0.
\]
Thus the discriminant of \(f\) is \(\frac{2(q-1)}{q}\), and it is negative for every \(0<q<1\).

FINAL PROOF NOT COMPLETED.

The blocking point is that the target theorem is false: for every \(0<q<1\), the function
\[
g(z)=(1+z)^2
\]
belongs to the Laguerre--Polya class, but it has no preimage in the Laguerre--Polya class under \(B_q\). The obstruction is as follows.

1. Let \(0<q<1\), and set \(g(z)=(1+z)^2=1+2z+z^2\). The polynomial \(g\) has only the real zero \(-1\), with multiplicity \(2\). By the standard polynomial characterization of the Laguerre--Polya class, \(g\in L\text{-}P\).

2. Suppose \(f\) is a formal power series
\[
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a_k\frac{z^k}{k!}
\]
such that \(B_q(f)=g\). By the definition of \(B_q\),
\[
B_q(f)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
a_k\frac{q^{k(k-1)/2}(1-q)^k}{(q;q)_k}z^k.
\]

3. Comparing coefficients with \(1+2z+z^2\), we get \(a_0=1\). Since \((q;q)_1=1-q\), the coefficient of \(z\) gives \(a_1=2\).

4. For \(k=2\),
\[
(q;q)_2=(1-q)(1-q^2)=(1-q)^2(1+q),
\]
so the multiplier in degree \(2\) is
\[
\frac{q(1-q)^2}{(q;q)_2}=\frac{q}{1+q}.
\]
Thus
\[
a_2\frac{q}{1+q}=1,
\]
and hence
\[
a_2=\frac{1+q}{q}.
\]

5. For \(k\ge 3\), the coefficient of \(z^k\) in \(g\) is \(0\). Since \(0<q<1\), every factor in
\[
\frac{q^{k(k-1)/2}(1-q)^k}{(q;q)_k}
\]
is nonzero, so \(a_k=0\) for all \(k\ge 3\).

6. Therefore the unique formal preimage of \(g\) under \(B_q\) is the quadratic polynomial
\[
f(z)=1+2z+\frac{1+q}{2q}z^2.
\]

7. [KEY STEP] This unique preimage is not in \(L\text{-}P\). Indeed, writing
\[
f(z)=Az^2+Bz+C
\]
with
\[
A=\frac{1+q}{2q},\qquad B=2,\qquad C=1,
\]
its discriminant is
\[
\Delta=B^2-4AC
=4-4\cdot \frac{1+q}{2q}
=4-\frac{2(1+q)}{q}
=\frac{2(q-1)}{q}.
\]
Because \(0<q<1\), we have \(q-1<0\), so
\[
\Delta<0.
\]
Thus \(f\) has no real zeros. By the standard polynomial characterization of \(L\text{-}P\), a real polynomial belongs to \(L\text{-}P\) only if all its zeros are real. Hence \(f\notin L\text{-}P\).

8. Since coefficient comparison gives a unique formal preimage of \(g\), there is no other possible \(h\in L\text{-}P\) with \(B_q(h)=g\). Thus \(g\in L\text{-}P\) but \(g\notin B_q(L\text{-}P)\).

9. Therefore \(B_q\) is not surjective on \(L\text{-}P\) for any \(0<q<1\). Consequently it cannot restrict to a bijection on \(L\text{-}P\). This contradicts the target theorem, so the stated theorem cannot be proved as written.

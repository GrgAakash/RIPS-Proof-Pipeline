2. Subproof or failure

Fix \(0<q<1\). By the assigned prerequisite SC3, the unique formal/polynomial solution of \(B_q(f)=g\), where \(g(z)=(1+z)^2\), is

\[
f(z)=1+2z+\frac{1+q}{2q}z^2.
\]

By the assigned prerequisite SC4, the discriminant of this quadratic is negative:

\[
\Delta(f)<0.
\]

Since \(f\) is a real quadratic polynomial, the quadratic discriminant criterion implies that \(f\) has no real zeros. By the standard characterization of polynomial membership in the classical Laguerre--Polya class, a real polynomial belongs to \(L\text{-}P\) if and only if all of its zeros are real. Therefore

\[
f\notin L\text{-}P.
\]

Now suppose, for contradiction, that \(g\) lies in the image of the restricted map

\[
B_q|_{L\text{-}P}:L\text{-}P\to \text{formal power series}.
\]

Then there exists some \(h\in L\text{-}P\) such that

\[
B_q(h)=g.
\]

But by the uniqueness part of SC3, the only formal/polynomial preimage of \(g\) under \(B_q\) is \(f\). Hence \(h=f\). Since \(h\in L\text{-}P\), this would imply \(f\in L\text{-}P\), contradicting the conclusion above.

Thus no element of \(L\text{-}P\) maps to \(g\) under \(B_q\). Therefore \(g\) is not in the image of \(B_q\) restricted to \(L\text{-}P\).

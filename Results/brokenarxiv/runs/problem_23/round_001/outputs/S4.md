2. Subproof or failure

Assume C2 and C3.

By C2, the vertex set of \(\Gamma'(R)\) is exactly \((u)^*\cup (v)^*\). First consider two distinct vertices \(a,a'\in (u)^*\). By C3,
\[
\operatorname{ann}_R(a)=(v)=\operatorname{ann}_R(a').
\]
Hence \(\operatorname{ann}_R(a)\subseteq \operatorname{ann}_R(a')\), so \(a\) and \(a'\) are adjacent by the definition of \(\Gamma'(R)\). Thus the induced subgraph on \((u)^*\) is complete.

Similarly, if \(b,b'\in (v)^*\) are distinct, then by C3,
\[
\operatorname{ann}_R(b)=(u)=\operatorname{ann}_R(b'),
\]
so \(b\) and \(b'\) are adjacent. Hence the induced subgraph on \((v)^*\) is complete.

It remains to show that no vertex in \((u)^*\) is adjacent to a vertex in \((v)^*\). Let \(a\in (u)^*\) and \(b\in (v)^*\). By C3,
\[
\operatorname{ann}_R(a)=(v),\qquad \operatorname{ann}_R(b)=(u).
\]
Thus \(a\) and \(b\) would be adjacent only if \((v)\subseteq (u)\) or \((u)\subseteq (v)\).

These inclusions do not hold. Indeed, \(u\notin (v)\): if \(u\in (v)\) in \(R\), then in \(k[u,v]\) one would have
\[
u-vh\in (uv)
\]
for some polynomial \(h\). Setting \(v=0\) gives \(u=0\) in \(k[u]\), impossible. Thus \((u)\nsubseteq (v)\). Similarly, \(v\notin (u)\), since otherwise \(v-uh\in (uv)\) for some \(h\), and setting \(u=0\) gives \(v=0\) in \(k[v]\), impossible. Hence \((v)\nsubseteq (u)\).

Therefore neither annihilator contains the other, so no cross-edge exists between \((u)^*\) and \((v)^*\).

Consequently \(\Gamma'(R)\) is the disjoint union of the complete subgraph on \((u)^*\) and the complete subgraph on \((v)^*\). Since there are no edges between these two nonempty vertex sets, they are precisely the two connected components.

2. Subproof or failure

Let \(k\) be a field. The polynomial ring \(k[u,v]\) in two indeterminates over \(k\) is a commutative ring with identity \(1\).

The principal ideal \((uv)\subseteq k[u,v]\) is a two-sided ideal, since \(k[u,v]\) is commutative. Hence the quotient
\[
R=k[u,v]/(uv)
\]
is a ring. Because quotient rings of commutative rings are commutative, \(R\) is commutative.

The identity element of \(R\) is the coset \(1+(uv)\). This is a valid multiplicative identity: for every \(f\in k[u,v]\),
\[
(1+(uv))(f+(uv))=f+(uv).
\]
Thus \(R\) is a commutative ring with identity.

Moreover, \((uv)\) is proper: if \(1\in (uv)\), then \(1=uvh(u,v)\) for some \(h\in k[u,v]\), which is impossible after setting \(u=0\), since the right-hand side becomes \(0\) while the left-hand side remains \(1\). Therefore \(R\) is not the zero ring, although the target theorem only requires a commutative ring with identity.

Hence \(R=k[u,v]/(uv)\) satisfies the theorem’s ambient hypotheses.

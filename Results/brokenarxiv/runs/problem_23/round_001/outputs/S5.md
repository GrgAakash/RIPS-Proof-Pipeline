2. Subproof or failure

Let \(R=k[u,v]/(uv)\). I prove first that \(R\) has no idempotents except \(0\) and \(1\).

Every element of \(R\) has a representative of the form
\[
c+u f(u)+v g(v),
\]
where \(c\in k\), \(f(u)\in k[u]\), and \(g(v)\in k[v]\). This follows because modulo the ideal \((uv)\), every monomial involving both \(u\) and \(v\) vanishes, so only pure powers of \(u\), pure powers of \(v\), and a single constant term remain.

Let
\[
e=c+u f(u)+v g(v)\in R
\]
be idempotent, so \(e^2=e\). Since all products containing a factor \(uv\) vanish in \(R\), we compute
\[
e^2=c^2+\bigl(2c\,u f(u)+u^2 f(u)^2\bigr)
+\bigl(2c\,v g(v)+v^2 g(v)^2\bigr).
\]
Equating \(e^2=e\), and comparing the constant, \(u\)-part, and \(v\)-part gives:
\[
c^2=c,
\]
\[
2c\,u f(u)+u^2 f(u)^2=u f(u),
\]
\[
2c\,v g(v)+v^2 g(v)^2=v g(v).
\]

Since \(k\) is a field, \(c^2=c\) implies \(c\in\{0,1\}\).

If \(c=0\), then the \(u\)-equation becomes
\[
u^2 f(u)^2=u f(u).
\]
Equivalently, in the domain \(k[u]\),
\[
u f(u)\bigl(u f(u)-1\bigr)=0.
\]
Thus \(u f(u)=0\), because \(u f(u)-1\) has constant term \(-1\) and cannot be zero if \(u f(u)=1\). Hence \(f(u)=0\). Similarly, the \(v\)-equation gives \(g(v)=0\). Therefore \(e=0\).

If \(c=1\), then the \(u\)-equation becomes
\[
2u f(u)+u^2 f(u)^2=u f(u),
\]
so
\[
u f(u)\bigl(1+u f(u)\bigr)=0
\]
in \(k[u]\). Since \(k[u]\) is a domain, either \(u f(u)=0\), hence \(f(u)=0\), or \(1+u f(u)=0\). The latter is impossible because \(1+u f(u)\) has constant term \(1\). Thus \(f(u)=0\). Similarly \(g(v)=0\), so \(e=1\).

Therefore the only idempotents of \(R\) are \(0\) and \(1\).

Now suppose, for contradiction, that
\[
R\cong D_1\times D_2
\]
where \(D_1,D_2\) are integral domains. Since the phrase “direct product of two integral domains” in this context must mean both factors are nonzero integral domains, the product has the nontrivial idempotent
\[
(1,0),
\]
which is neither \((0,0)\) nor \((1,1)\). Isomorphisms preserve idempotents, so \(R\) would have a nontrivial idempotent. This contradicts the result above.

Hence
\[
k[u,v]/(uv)
\]
is not isomorphic to a direct product of two integral domains.

The target theorem is false as stated. The obstruction is the following counterexample.

1. Let \(k\) be a field and set
\[
R=k[u,v]/(uv).
\]
By S1, \(R\) is a commutative ring with identity.

2. We identify \(R\) with the subring
\[
S=\{(f(u),g(v))\in k[u]\times k[v]: f(0)=g(0)\}
\]
via the map induced by
\[
F(u,v)\mapsto (F(u,0),F(0,v)).
\]
By S2, this map has kernel \((uv)\), so this identification is valid. Under it,
\[
u=(u,0),\qquad v=(0,v),
\]
\[
(u)=\{(u f(u),0): f(u)\in k[u]\},
\qquad
(v)=\{(0,v g(v)): g(v)\in k[v]\}.
\]

3. [KEY STEP] By S2 and S3, the nonzero zero-divisors of \(R\) are exactly
\[
Z(R)^*=(u)^*\cup (v)^*,
\]
and their annihilators are
\[
\operatorname{ann}_R(a)=(v)\quad\text{for every }0\ne a\in (u),
\]
\[
\operatorname{ann}_R(b)=(u)\quad\text{for every }0\ne b\in (v).
\]
Indeed, if \(x=(f,g)\in S\) is a zero-divisor, then there is nonzero \(y=(p,q)\in S\) with
\[
xy=(fp,gq)=(0,0).
\]
Since \(k[u]\) and \(k[v]\) are integral domains, \(f\) and \(g\) cannot both be nonzero; hence \(x\in (u)\cup (v)\). Conversely, every nonzero element of \((u)\) is killed by \(v\), and every nonzero element of \((v)\) is killed by \(u\). For annihilators, if \(0\ne a=u p(u)\in (u)\), then for
\[
r=c+u f(u)+v g(v)\in R,
\]
one has
\[
ra=(c+u f(u))u p(u).
\]
This is zero precisely when \(c+u f(u)=0\) in the domain \(k[u]\), so \(r\in (v)\). Thus \(\operatorname{ann}_R(a)=(v)\). The proof for nonzero elements of \((v)\) is symmetric.

4. Therefore, by the definition of \(\Gamma'(R)\), any two distinct vertices inside \((u)^*\) are adjacent because they have equal annihilator \((v)\), and any two distinct vertices inside \((v)^*\) are adjacent because they have equal annihilator \((u)\).

5. There is no edge between a vertex of \((u)^*\) and a vertex of \((v)^*\). Such an edge would require
\[
(u)\subseteq (v)\quad\text{or}\quad (v)\subseteq (u).
\]
But \(u\notin (v)\) and \(v\notin (u)\), so neither containment holds. Hence \(\Gamma'(R)\) is the disjoint union of two nonempty complete subgraphs, one on \((u)^*\) and one on \((v)^*\). Thus \(\Gamma'(R)\) is not connected.

6. By S5, \(R\) has no nontrivial idempotents. If \(R\cong D_1\times D_2\) for nonzero integral domains \(D_1,D_2\), then \(D_1\times D_2\) has the nontrivial idempotent \((1,0)\), and an isomorphism preserves idempotents. Therefore \(R\) is not isomorphic to a direct product of two integral domains.

7. We have found a commutative ring with identity such that \(\Gamma'(R)\) is not connected, but \(R\) is not isomorphic to a direct product of two integral domains. Hence the claimed “only if” direction fails, and the target theorem is false.

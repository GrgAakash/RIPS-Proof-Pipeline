1. Target decomposition

target_label: nilpotent_subgroup_dominates_conjugacy_classes

target_type: universal finite-group inequality

main_goal: For every finite group `G`, prove `k(G) <= m(G)`, where `m(G)` denotes the maximum order of a nilpotent subgroup of `G`.

variables_and_parameters: `G` finite group; `k(G)` number of conjugacy classes; `m(G)=max{|H| : H <= G and H nilpotent}`.

conclusion_to_prove: There exists a nilpotent subgroup `H <= G` such that `|H| >= k(G)`.

tool: nilpotent closure facts
source_status: standard background fact
exact_statement_or_fact: Subgroups and finite direct products of nilpotent groups are nilpotent; if `Z <= Z(X)` and `X/Z` is nilpotent, then `X` is nilpotent.
intended_role_in_proof: Lift nilpotent subgroups through central quotients and assemble nilpotent subgroups componentwise.

tool: conjugacy-class counting under products and normal subgroups
source_status: proved inside the current proof
exact_statement_or_fact: For finite groups, `k(A x B)=k(A)k(B)`. Also prove and use the needed normal-subgroup class-count inequalities, especially bounds comparing `k(G)`, `k(N)`, and class data of `G/N` when `N normal G`.
intended_role_in_proof: Supply numerical control during induction and reduction.

tool: solvable radical and socle structure
source_status: standard background fact
exact_statement_or_fact: The solvable radical `R(G)` is the largest solvable normal subgroup. If `R(G)=1`, then the socle is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups and `G` embeds into the automorphism group of that socle.
intended_role_in_proof: Split the proof into solvable-radical and semisimple cases.

tool: solvable case bound
source_status: proved inside the current proof
exact_statement_or_fact: Every finite solvable group `S` has a nilpotent subgroup `N <= S` with `|N| >= k(S)`.
intended_role_in_proof: Eliminates solvable minimal counterexamples and supports the radical reduction.

tool: almost-simple case bound
source_status: proved inside the current proof
exact_statement_or_fact: If `S` is nonabelian simple and `S <= A <= Aut(S)`, then `A` has a nilpotent subgroup `P` with `|P| >= k(A)`; prove any stronger orbit-counting version needed for components.
intended_role_in_proof: Supplies the building block for radical-free groups.

tool: semisimple assembly lemma
source_status: proved inside the current proof
exact_statement_or_fact: If `R(G)=1`, then the componentwise almost-simple estimates can be combined across the socle and the induced permutation action to produce a nilpotent subgroup `H <= G` with `|H| >= k(G)`.
intended_role_in_proof: Handles all nonsolvable groups with trivial radical.

4. Hardest step prediction

hardest_step_id: C5

hardest_step_description: Passing from almost-simple component bounds to an arbitrary radical-free group, where `G` may permute many nonabelian simple direct factors and may contain outer automorphisms.

risk_if_wrong: A nilpotent subgroup found in a quotient or in separate components may fail to lift or combine as a nilpotent subgroup of `G`; this is the main place where an apparently multiplicative estimate can become circular or false.

how_final_proof_should_handle_it: The final proof must give an explicit construction of the nilpotent subgroup inside `G`, not merely in a quotient, and must track how conjugacy classes split or fuse under the component permutation action.

6. Subproblem assignment table

S1: Prove C1. Give precise class-count lemmas for direct products and normal subgroups, and prove the nilpotent closure and central-lifting facts needed later.

S2: Prove C2. Establish the theorem for finite solvable groups without using the full theorem for nonsolvable groups.

S3: Prove C4. Treat almost-simple groups and provide the strengthened component/orbit-counting form needed for products of simple components.

S5: Prove C3 and C6. Show a minimal counterexample cannot have nontrivial solvable radical, then assemble the contradiction from the solvable and radical-free cases.

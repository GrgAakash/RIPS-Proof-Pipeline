1. Assigned Claim

For S4, the assigned claim is:

If \(G\) is finite and \(R(G)=1\), then \(G\) has a nilpotent subgroup \(H\leq G\) with
\[
|H|\geq k(G).
\]

Equivalently, for radical-free finite groups,
\[
k(G)\leq m(G).
\]

2. Immediate Structural Reduction

Let
\[
N=\operatorname{Soc}(G)=S_1\times\cdots\times S_t
\]
where each \(S_i\) is nonabelian simple.

Since \(R(G)=1\), the socle has no abelian minimal normal subgroup. Also \(C_G(N)=1\): if \(C_G(N)\neq 1\), then it contains a minimal normal subgroup \(M\lhd G\); but \(M\leq N\), and \(M\) centralizes \(N\), forcing \(M\leq Z(N)=1\), contradiction.

Thus conjugation embeds
\[
G\hookrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}(N),
\]
and \(G\) permutes the simple direct factors \(S_i\).

If \(t=1\), then \(N=S_1\) is nonabelian simple and
\[
S_1\leq G\leq \operatorname{Aut}(S_1),
\]
so the claim is exactly the almost-simple case assigned to S3.

3. Partial Assembly Attempt

For \(t>1\), one would naturally split the components into \(G\)-orbits
\[
\Omega_1,\dots,\Omega_r
\]
and set
\[
N_j=\prod_{S_i\in \Omega_j} S_i.
\]
Each \(N_j\lhd G\), and \(N=N_1\times\cdots\times N_r\).

The intended assembly would require two facts.

First, a class-counting estimate comparing \(k(G)\) with the induced groups \(G_j\leq \operatorname{Aut}(N_j)\), for example something like
\[
k(G)\leq \prod_j k(G_j).
\]

Second, for each orbit \(j\), one needs nilpotent subgroups whose orders realize the S3 component bounds and which lift compatibly into the actual subgroup \(G\), not merely into the separate almost-simple quotients.

If such compatible subgroups \(\widetilde H_j\leq G\) existed, with the \(\widetilde H_j\) commuting and
\[
|\widetilde H_j|\geq k(G_j),
\]
then
\[
H=\widetilde H_1\cdots \widetilde H_r
\]
would be nilpotent and
\[
|H|=\prod_j |\widetilde H_j|
\geq \prod_j k(G_j)
\geq k(G).
\]

That would prove C5.

4. Obstruction

The standalone inputs do not supply the required compatible lifting/orbit-counting theorem, and it is not a genuinely standard elementary fact.

The obstruction is real: componentwise almost-simple bounds do not automatically multiply inside \(G\). For example, take \(S=A_5\), let \(\tau\) be an outer involution in \(\operatorname{Aut}(S)\cong S_5\), and consider
\[
G=(S\times S)\rtimes \langle(\tau,\tau)\rangle.
\]
Each component image is almost simple, namely \(S_5\). S3 may supply a nilpotent subgroup such as a \(2\)-subgroup of \(S_5\) of order \(8\) for each component. But \(G\) does not contain the independent product of those two outer \(2\)-subgroups, because the outer action is diagonal, not componentwise independent.

So the naive product construction produces a subgroup of \(\operatorname{Aut}(S)^2\), not necessarily a subgroup of \(G\).

Therefore C5 cannot be proved from the supplied standalone inputs plus only the bare almost-simple inequality \(k(A)\leq m(A)\). A stronger S3 output is needed, specifically an orbit-compatible semisimple estimate that controls both class-counting under component permutations and realization by actual nilpotent subgroups inside \(G\).

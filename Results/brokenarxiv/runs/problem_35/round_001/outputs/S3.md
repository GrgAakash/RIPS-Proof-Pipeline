2. **What Can Be Reduced Immediately**

Since `S` is nonabelian simple, `Z(S)=1`, so `S` identifies with `Inn(S)`, and `A/S` is a subgroup of `Out(S)`.

The conjugacy classes of `A` contained in `S` are unions of `S`-conjugacy classes, namely orbits of `A` on the set of conjugacy classes of `S`. Thus the part of `k(A)` coming from elements of `S` is at most `k(S)`.

This does not control conjugacy classes in the outer cosets `A \ S`, nor does it produce a nilpotent subgroup of `A` large enough to dominate all of `k(A)`.

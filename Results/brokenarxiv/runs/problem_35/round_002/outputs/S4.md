**1. Subproblem Claim**

If \(G\) is finite and \(R(G)=1\), then
\[
\operatorname{soc}(G)=S_1\times\cdots\times S_t
\]
where each \(S_i\) is nonabelian simple. Moreover,
\[
C_G(\operatorname{soc}(G))=1,
\]
so conjugation gives an embedding
\[
G\hookrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{soc}(G)).
\]

**2. Proof**

Let \(M=\operatorname{soc}(G)\), the subgroup generated by all minimal nontrivial normal subgroups of \(G\).

First, \(G\) has at least one minimal nontrivial normal subgroup unless \(G=1\). If \(G=1\), the conclusion is vacuous. Assume \(G\neq 1\).

Let \(N\) be a minimal nontrivial normal subgroup of \(G\). Since \(N\) is finite, it has a minimal nontrivial normal subgroup \(L\) of \(N\). The conjugates of \(L\) by elements of \(G\) are again normal subgroups of \(N\), and the subgroup generated by these conjugates is normal in \(G\), nontrivial, and contained in \(N\). By minimality of \(N\), those conjugates generate \(N\). Standard finite group theory then gives
\[
N=L_1\times\cdots\times L_m
\]
where the \(L_i\) are isomorphic simple groups.

If some \(L_i\) were cyclic of prime order, then \(N\) would be elementary abelian, hence solvable. Since \(N\triangleleft G\), this would place \(N\le R(G)\), contradicting \(R(G)=1\). Therefore every simple factor \(L_i\) is nonabelian simple.

Now take distinct minimal normal subgroups \(N_1,\dots,N_r\) of \(G\). If \(N_i\neq N_j\), then
\[
[N_i,N_j]\le N_i\cap N_j.
\]
Because both are normal in \(G\), their intersection is normal in \(G\). By minimality, \(N_i\cap N_j\) is either \(1\) or one of them. Since they are distinct minimal normal subgroups, \(N_i\cap N_j=1\). Hence \([N_i,N_j]=1\). Thus distinct minimal normal subgroups centralize one another, and their product is direct.

Therefore the socle is a direct product of all simple factors occurring inside the minimal normal subgroups:
\[
M=\operatorname{soc}(G)=S_1\times\cdots\times S_t,
\]
with every \(S_i\) nonabelian simple.

It remains to prove \(C_G(M)=1\). Let
\[
C=C_G(M).
\]
Since \(M\triangleleft G\), its centralizer \(C\) is also normal in \(G\). If \(C\neq 1\), then \(C\) contains a minimal nontrivial normal subgroup \(A\) of \(G\). By definition of the socle, \(A\le M\). But also \(A\le C=C_G(M)\), so \(A\) centralizes \(M\). Since \(A\le M\), this implies \(A\le Z(M)\).

But \(M=S_1\times\cdots\times S_t\) is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups, each of which has trivial center. Hence \(Z(M)=1\). Thus \(A=1\), contradiction. Therefore \(C_G(M)=1\).

Finally, conjugation defines a homomorphism
\[
\varphi:G\to \operatorname{Aut}(M).
\]
Its kernel is exactly \(C_G(M)\), which is \(1\). Hence \(\varphi\) is injective, so
\[
G\hookrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}(M)=\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{soc}(G)).
\]

**2. S5 Result**

Let \(G\) be radical-free and let

\[
M=\operatorname{soc}(G)=\prod_{j=1}^r T_j^{\,n_j},
\]

where the \(T_j\) are pairwise nonisomorphic nonabelian simple groups. Put \(Q=G/M\). Since \(C_G(M)=1\), conjugation embeds

\[
Q \leq \prod_{j=1}^r \operatorname{Out}(T_j)\wr P_j,
\]

where \(P_j\leq \operatorname{Sym}(n_j)\) is the permutation group induced by \(G\) on the \(n_j\) factors isomorphic to \(T_j\).

For \(q\in Q\), let \(\pi_j(q)\in P_j\) be its permutation on the \(T_j\)-factors. For each cycle \(C\) of \(\pi_j(q)\), the product of the outer automorphisms around that cycle gives a well-defined outer class \(\omega_{j,C}(q)\in \operatorname{Out}(T_j)\), up to conjugacy. Let

\[
k_{\omega}(T_j)
\]

denote the number of conjugacy classes of \(T_j\) fixed by an automorphism representing \(\omega\). Then

\[
\boxed{
k(G)\leq
\sum_{[q]\in \operatorname{Cl}(Q)}
\prod_{j=1}^r
\prod_{C\in \operatorname{Cyc}(\pi_j(q))}
k_{\omega_{j,C}(q)}(T_j)
}
\]

In particular, since \(k_{\omega}(T_j)\leq k(T_j)\),

\[
\boxed{
k(G)\leq
\sum_{[q]\in \operatorname{Cl}(Q)}
\prod_{j=1}^r k(T_j)^{c(\pi_j(q))}
}
\]

where \(c(\pi_j(q))\) is the number of cycles of \(\pi_j(q)\).

A cruder but simpler consequence is

\[
\boxed{
k(G)\leq
\prod_{j=1}^r
\bigl(k(T_j)|\operatorname{Out}(T_j)|\bigr)^{n_j}
|P_j|
}
\]

and hence

\[
k(G)\leq
\prod_{j=1}^r
\bigl(k(T_j)|\operatorname{Out}(T_j)|\bigr)^{n_j}
n_j!.
\]

**3. Proof**

Since \(M\) is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups, \(Z(M)=1\). In the radical-free case, \(C_G(M)=1\): otherwise a nontrivial normal subgroup of \(C_G(M)\) would contain a minimal normal subgroup of \(G\), hence a direct factor of \(M\), contradicting \(C_G(M)\cap M=Z(M)=1\). Thus \(G\) embeds by conjugation into \(\operatorname{Aut}(M)\), and \(Q=G/M\) embeds into \(\operatorname{Out}(M)\), giving the stated wreath-product action.

For a normal subgroup \(M\), every conjugacy class of \(G\) maps to a conjugacy class of \(Q\). Fix \(q\in Q\) and a lift \(g\in G\). Every \(G\)-class lying over the quotient class \([q]\) meets the coset \(gM\). Therefore the number of such \(G\)-classes is at most the number of \(M\)-orbits on \(gM\) under conjugation.

Those \(M\)-orbits are twisted conjugacy classes for the automorphism of \(M\) induced by \(g\). On a cycle of simple factors, the twisted classes reduce to twisted classes of one factor under the product automorphism around the cycle. For a finite group \(T\) and automorphism \(\alpha\), the number of \(\alpha\)-twisted conjugacy classes equals the number of ordinary conjugacy classes of \(T\) fixed by \(\alpha\). This follows from Burnside’s lemma applied to the action \(x\mapsto y^{-1}x\alpha(y)\).

Multiplying over all factor cycles and summing over quotient conjugacy classes gives the weighted bound above.

**4. Missing Quantitative Estimate**

The S5 bound alone does not prove the radical-free branch lemma. To finish that branch, one needs an estimate comparing the weighted class sum to the order of a nilpotent subgroup of \(G\).

An exact sufficient missing estimate is:

For every radical-free finite group \(G\) with socle \(M=\prod_j T_j^{n_j}\) and quotient \(Q=G/M\), there exists a nilpotent subgroup \(P\leq G\) such that

\[
|P|\geq
\sum_{[q]\in \operatorname{Cl}(Q)}
\prod_{j=1}^r
\prod_{C\in \operatorname{Cyc}(\pi_j(q))}
k_{\omega_{j,C}(q)}(T_j).
\]

A weaker but more usable sufficient pair of estimates would be:

1. Simple factor estimate: for each nonabelian simple \(T\), there is a nilpotent subgroup \(N_T\leq T\) large enough to dominate the relevant fixed-class counts \(k_{\omega}(T)\).

2. Wreath/permutation estimate: for every subgroup \(Q\leq \prod_j \operatorname{Out}(T_j)\wr P_j\), the weighted cycle sum above is bounded by the order of a nilpotent subgroup liftable inside \(G\).

Without such a simple-group/wreath-product estimate, S5 supplies only the class-number bound, not the required nilpotent subgroup comparison.

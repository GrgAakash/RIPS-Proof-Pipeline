1. **Verdict**

Failure for S3 as stated. The nilpotent lifting lemma needed for the S1/S2 route is false without an extra hypothesis.

2. **Needed Lifting Form**

For S2, with `N` a nontrivial normal elementary abelian subgroup, S1 gives

`k(G) <= k(N) k(G/N) = |N| k(G/N)`.

If induction gives a nilpotent subgroup `Pbar <= G/N` with `|Pbar| >= k(G/N)`, then this route would need a nilpotent subgroup of `G` of size at least `|N||Pbar|`, usually inside the preimage of `Pbar`.

That is not generally possible.

3. **Counterexample**

Take `G = S3` and `N = A3`.

Then:

- `N ≅ C3` is normal elementary abelian.
- `G/N ≅ C2`, which is nilpotent.
- Let `Pbar = G/N`. Its preimage is all of `S3`, of order `|N||Pbar| = 3 * 2 = 6`.
- But `S3` is not nilpotent: its Sylow 2-subgroups are the three distinct transposition subgroups, so no Sylow 2-subgroup is normal.
- The largest nilpotent subgroups of `S3` have order `3`, namely `A3`; subgroups of order `2` are also nilpotent but too small for the product lift.

So the desired lift of the nilpotent quotient subgroup cannot exist.

4. **Precise Obstruction / Missing Hypothesis**

The obstruction is nontrivial coprime action. Here the quotient `C2` acts on the normal kernel `C3` by inversion. A nilpotent subgroup containing both the full kernel and a lift of the quotient would require the Sylow 2- and Sylow 3-parts to commute; they do not.

A true replacement needs an added hypothesis such as:

- the preimage of `Pbar` centralizes `N`, equivalently `N <= Z(pi^{-1}(Pbar))`; then the preimage is a central extension of a nilpotent group and is nilpotent;
- or `Pbar` is a p-subgroup when `N` is elementary abelian of order a power of `p`; then the preimage is a p-group and hence nilpotent.

Neither hypothesis is available in the general solvable-radical reduction.

5. **Interface Notes**

S2 cannot rely on a blanket nilpotent lifting lemma from `G/N` through an elementary abelian normal subgroup. The S1 inequality is too coarse for this induction step: in the example above it gives `k(S3) <= k(C3)k(C2) = 6`, while the actual theorem holds only because `k(S3)=3` and `A3` has order `3`.

Thus S2 needs a different argument, likely using a sharper class-count estimate sensitive to the action on `N`, not just a product bound plus lifting.

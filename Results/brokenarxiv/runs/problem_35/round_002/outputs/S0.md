**1. Target Decomposition**

Let  
\[
b(G)=\max\{|H|:H\leq G,\ H\text{ nilpotent}\}.
\]
Target:
\[
k(G)\leq b(G)
\]
for every finite group \(G\).

Use minimal-counterexample induction on \(|G|\).

Decompose into two main cases:

1. \(G\) has a nontrivial normal nilpotent subgroup \(N\).
2. \(G\) has no nontrivial normal nilpotent subgroup, equivalently the solvable radical satisfies \(R(G)=1\). Then the socle is
\[
M=S_1\times\cdots\times S_t
\]
with each \(S_i\) nonabelian simple.

The second case is the guided branch lemma:
\[
R(G)=1\implies \exists\text{ nilpotent }P\leq G\text{ with }|P|\geq k(G).
\]

**2. Available Tools**

Standard background to use, stated precisely:

- **Class equation for normal subgroups:** If \(N\trianglelefteq G\), then every conjugacy class of \(G\) maps into one conjugacy class of \(G/N\).
- **Clifford-type class counting bound:** If \(N\trianglelefteq G\), then
\[
k(G)\leq k(N)\,k(G/N).
\]
This should be proved in the final proof by counting \(G\)-classes lying above each \(G/N\)-class.
- **Nilpotent extension for coprime/direct factors:** If \(A,B\) are nilpotent subgroups of coprime order and normalize each other, then \(AB\) is nilpotent and \(|AB|=|A||B|\).
- **Finite solvable radical fact:** If \(R(G)\neq 1\), then \(G\) contains a nontrivial normal elementary abelian \(p\)-subgroup.
- **Socle structure with trivial radical:** If \(R(G)=1\), then \(\operatorname{soc}(G)\) is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups.
- **Embedding by conjugation:** If \(M=\operatorname{soc}(G)\) and \(C_G(M)=1\), then \(G\hookrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}(M)\).

Nonstandard ingredients that must be proved inside the proof, not assumed:

- A solvable/nilpotent lifting lemma strong enough to handle \(R(G)\neq 1\).
- The radical-free branch lemma for \(M=S_1\times\cdots\times S_t\).

**3. Subclaim Support Graph**

Main theorem follows from:

- **A. Minimal counterexample setup**
  - Choose \(G\) minimal with \(k(G)>b(G)\).
  - Then every proper quotient \(Q\) satisfies \(k(Q)\leq b(Q)\).

- **B. Normal nilpotent reduction**
  - If \(1\neq N\trianglelefteq G\) is nilpotent, prove
    \[
    k(G)\leq |N|\,k(G/N).
    \]
  - By induction, choose nilpotent \(\overline P\leq G/N\) with
    \[
    |\overline P|\geq k(G/N).
    \]
  - Prove a lifting subclaim: the preimage of a suitable nilpotent subgroup of \(G/N\) contains a nilpotent subgroup \(P\leq G\) with
    \[
    |P|\geq |N|\,|\overline P|.
    \]
  - Then
    \[
    |P|\geq |N|k(G/N)\geq k(G),
    \]
    contradiction.

- **C. Solvable radical reduction**
  - If \(R(G)\neq 1\), choose a nontrivial normal elementary abelian \(p\)-subgroup \(A\leq R(G)\).
  - Apply B with \(N=A\), or prove the required version directly.

- **D. Radical-free case**
  - If \(R(G)=1\), set
    \[
    M=S_1\times\cdots\times S_t.
    \]
  - Prove the candidate branch lemma:
    \[
    \exists P\leq G,\ P\text{ nilpotent},\ |P|\geq k(G).
    \]
  - This contradicts minimality immediately.

Support chain:
\[
A + (C\to B) + D \Longrightarrow \text{target theorem}.
\]

**4. Hardest Step Prediction**

The hardest step is D: proving the radical-free branch lemma without importing an external paper result.

Expected internal structure:

1. Control \(k(G)\) through the action of \(G/M\) on conjugacy classes of \(M\).
2. Bound
   \[
   k(G)\leq k(M)\,|G/M|
   \]
   or a sharper orbit-stabilizer variant.
3. Construct nilpotent subgroups inside each nonabelian simple factor \(S_i\), then combine them across the direct product.
4. Handle the permutation action of \(G\) on the simple factors by inserting nilpotent Sylow subgroups from the permutation part.
5. Reduce ultimately to a finite simple group estimate of the form:
   for every nonabelian finite simple group \(S\), there exists a nilpotent subgroup \(P_S\leq S\) large enough to dominate the relevant contribution from \(k(S)\) and outer automorphisms.

This likely requires CFSG-level estimates unless a fully elementary replacement is found.

**5. Failure-Mode Checks**

- Do not assume the candidate branch lemma; it must be an internal subproblem.
- Do not use \(k(G)\leq k(N)k(G/N)\) without either naming it precisely or proving it.
- Do not assume the preimage of a nilpotent subgroup is nilpotent. It generally is not.
- Be careful: a subgroup of a nilpotent group is nilpotent, but an extension of nilpotent groups need not be nilpotent.
- In the radical-free case, verify \(C_G(M)=1\); otherwise the embedding into \(\operatorname{Aut}(M)\) is not justified.
- For \(M=S_1\times\cdots\times S_t\), distinguish conjugacy inside \(M\) from conjugacy under \(G\).
- If using simple-group estimates, state exactly what is needed and prove or explicitly mark it as a subclaim for internal proof.

## 1. Target decomposition

Prove the theorem by minimal counterexample on `|G|`.

Let  
`m(G) = max{|N| : N <= G and N is nilpotent}`.  
Target: `k(G) <= m(G)` for every finite group `G`.

Core split for a minimal counterexample `G`:

1. **Normal-subgroup reduction.**  
   For a nontrivial normal subgroup `N ◁ G`, relate `k(G)` to conjugacy data from `N` and `G/N`, then try to build a nilpotent subgroup of `G` large enough to dominate the resulting bound.

2. **Solvable-radical branch.**  
   If `R(G) != 1`, use a minimal normal elementary abelian subgroup `V <= R(G)` and prove an action-sensitive bound for conjugacy classes over `V`, avoiding the false lift `|V|·m(G/V)`.

3. **Radical-free branch.**  
   If `R(G)=1`, let  
   `M = Soc(G) = S_1 x ... x S_t`,  
   where the `S_i` are nonabelian simple. Prove the candidate branch lemma internally:

   `G` contains a nilpotent subgroup `P` with `|P| >= k(G)`.

4. **Final contradiction.**  
   Each branch gives a nilpotent subgroup of `G` of order at least `k(G)`, contradicting minimality.

## 2. Available tools

Allowed standard tools, stated precisely before use:

- **Class equation for normal subgroups:** If `N ◁ G`, then conjugacy classes of `G` either lie in cosets of `N`, and the classes inside `N` are unions of `G`-orbits on `N`-conjugacy classes.
- **Orbit-counting/Burnside lemma:** For a finite group `A` acting on a finite set `X`, the number of orbits is  
  `|X/A| = (1/|A|) sum_{a in A} |Fix_X(a)|`.
- **Minimal normal subgroup structure:** A minimal normal subgroup of a finite group is a direct product of isomorphic simple groups. If it is solvable, it is elementary abelian.
- **Socle structure when `R(G)=1`:** The socle is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups, and `G` embeds into the automorphism/wreath action induced on the simple direct factors.
- **Sylow theory:** Sylow subgroups are nilpotent `p`-groups and conjugate inside finite groups.
- **Direct products:** A direct product of nilpotent groups is nilpotent.
- **Nilpotent subgroup inheritance:** Subgroups of nilpotent groups need not be nilpotent in arbitrary extensions, so any extension step must be proved with an explicit central/product construction.

## 4. Hardest step prediction

The hardest step is the **solvable-radical branch**. The obvious quotient lifting argument is invalid: `S3` over `A3` shows a nilpotent subgroup of `G/V` cannot generally be lifted to one of order `|V|·|H|`.

The proof must instead use the actual action of a complement or stabilizer on `V`. The desired shape is:

- split conjugacy classes according to their image in `G/V`;
- for each relevant coset, bound class splitting by fixed-point data on `V`;
- extract from this same fixed-point data an abelian or nilpotent subgroup of `V ⋊ H` large enough to dominate the class contribution.

The radical-free branch is also difficult, but more modular: once the simple-factor domination estimate is established, direct products of selected nilpotent subgroups give a natural candidate subgroup.

## 5. Failure-mode checks

- Do not assume `k(G) <= |N| k(G/N)` is enough in the solvable branch.
- Do not claim a nilpotent subgroup of `G/N` lifts to a nilpotent subgroup of `G` of multiplied order.
- In the radical-free branch, do not stop at a numerical weighted class bound; the bound must be realized by an actual nilpotent subgroup of `G`.
- When using the socle, verify that nilpotent subgroups chosen in simple factors are normalized or assembled compatibly with the factor-permutation action.
- If using classification-level facts about simple groups, state exactly which fact is required.
- Avoid relying on any unpublished or paper-specific lemma unless it is proved inside the current proof.

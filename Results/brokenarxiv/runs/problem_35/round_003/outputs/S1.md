**1. S1 Status**

Subproblem solved.

Let
\[
m(G):=\max\{|P|:P\leq G,\ P\text{ nilpotent}\}.
\]
The target theorem is \(k(G)\leq m(G)\). S1 supplies the minimal-counterexample setup and the normal-subgroup class-count tools for S2-S5.

---

**2. Minimal Counterexample Reduction**

Assume the theorem is false, and choose a counterexample \(G\) of minimal order. Then:

1. \(G\neq 1\), and
   \[
   k(G)>m(G).
   \]

2. Every proper subgroup \(H<G\) satisfies
   \[
   k(H)\leq m(H)\leq m(G).
   \]

3. Every proper quotient \(G/N\), \(1\neq N\triangleleft G\), satisfies
   \[
   k(G/N)\leq m(G/N).
   \]

4. If \(Q/N\leq G/N\) is nilpotent, then \(Q/N\) has order at most \(m(G/N)\), but one cannot in general lift \(Q/N\) to a nilpotent subgroup of \(G\) of order \(|Q/N||N|\). This is exactly the obstruction noted in the prompt, so later branches must use more refined arguments.

Thus any contradiction may use the theorem for all proper subgroups and proper quotients, but not for \(G\) itself.

**3. Normal-Subgroup/Class-Count Identities**

Let \(N\triangleleft G\).

First, conjugacy classes of \(G/N\) are images of conjugacy classes of \(G\). Hence
\[
k(G/N)\leq k(G).
\]

More precisely, the natural map
\[
\pi:G\to G/N
\]
sends every \(G\)-class into a single \(G/N\)-class. Therefore the \(G\)-classes are partitioned according to the conjugacy classes of \(G/N\).

Second, the standard normal-subgroup bound is:
\[
k(G)\leq k(N)\,k(G/N).
\]

Proof sketch. For each coset \(xN\), the number of \(G\)-conjugacy classes contained in \(xN\) is at most \(k(N)\). Indeed, elements of \(xN\) have the form \(xn\), and conjugation by elements of \(N\) gives an action on \(xN\). The number of resulting \(N\)-orbits is bounded by \(k(N)\), since the twisting \(n\mapsto x^{-1}nx\) is an automorphism of \(N\). Passing from \(N\)-conjugacy to \(G\)-conjugacy can only merge classes. Summing over representatives of the \(k(G/N)\) quotient classes gives the stated bound.

Third, if \(N\leq Z(G)\), then conjugacy classes in \(G\) project especially cleanly:
\[
k(G)\leq |N|\,k(G/N).
\]
Indeed, each coset of \(N\) contains at most \(|N|\) elements, hence at most \(|N|\) conjugacy classes, and there are \(k(G/N)\) quotient classes.

Fourth, if \(N\) is central and \(G/N\) has a nilpotent subgroup \(Q/N\), then \(Q\) is nilpotent whenever \(Q/N\) is nilpotent and \(N\leq Z(Q)\). Thus central extensions are harmless for nilpotent lifting. The obstruction only appears for noncentral normal subgroups.

Fifth, if \(N\triangleleft G\), then the number of \(G\)-classes contained in \(N\) is
\[
k_G(N)=\#(N/G\text{-conjugacy orbits}),
\]
and
\[
k_G(N)\leq k(N).
\]
Equality holds exactly when every \(N\)-class is \(G\)-stable.

So one useful decomposition is
\[
k(G)=k_G(N)+k_G(G\setminus N),
\]
with
\[
k_G(N)\leq k(N).
\]

**4. Interface Notes For Later Solvers**

For S2, the useful minimal-counterexample input is:

- If \(1\neq V\triangleleft G\) is elementary abelian and \(G/V\) is proper, then
  \[
  k(G/V)\leq m(G/V).
  \]
- But S2 must avoid the false inference that nilpotent subgroups of \(G/V\) lift through \(V\) with full factor \(|V|\).
- The safe bound available from S1 is only
  \[
  k(G)\leq k(V)\,k(G/V)=|V|\,k(G/V),
  \]
  since \(V\) is abelian, hence \(k(V)=|V|\).
- Any stronger conclusion must use the actual \(G/V\)-action on \(V\).

For S3-S5, the useful input is:

- If \(R(G)=1\) and \(M=\operatorname{Soc}(G)=S_1\times\cdots\times S_t\), then
  \[
  k_G(M)\leq k(M)=\prod_i k(S_i).
  \]
- Also
  \[
  k(G)\leq k(M)\,k(G/M).
  \]
- Since \(G/M\) embeds into the outer/permutation action on the simple factors only after structural work, S1 does not assume such an embedding. That belongs to S3.

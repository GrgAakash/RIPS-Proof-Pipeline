If \(V\le Z(G)\), the branch is fine. Since \(G/V\) is smaller, minimal-counterexample induction gives a nilpotent subgroup \(\overline P\le G/V\) with
\[
|\overline P|\ge k(G/V).
\]
Its full inverse image \(P\) in \(G\) is a central extension of a nilpotent group, hence nilpotent, and
\[
|P|=|V||\overline P|\ge |V|k(G/V).
\]
Also \(k(G)\le |V|k(G/V)\), so \(k(G)\le |P|\).

For noncentral \(V\), one has the useful class-count reduction:
\[
k(G)\le \sum_{h\in \mathcal C(H)}
n\bigl(C_H(h),\, V/(h-1)V\bigr),
\]

|L|\cdot |C_V(T)|.

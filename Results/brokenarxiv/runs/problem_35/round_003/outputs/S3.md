## 1. S1 Status

**Subproblem S3 solved in reduction form.**  
For finite \(G\) with \(R(G)=1\), the structural reduction is:

\[
M=\operatorname{Soc}(G)=S_1\times\cdots\times S_t
\]

where each \(S_i\) is nonabelian simple, \(C_G(M)=1\), and hence \(G\) embeds into \(\operatorname{Aut}(M)\). The conjugation action of \(G\) permutes the simple direct factors \(S_i\), giving a homomorphism

\[
G \to \operatorname{Sym}(\{S_1,\dots,S_t\}).
\]

Moreover, \(k(G)\) can be reduced to orbit/class data coming from this action together with automorphism-controlled class data on the simple factors.

## 2. S2 Structural Lemmas

**Lemma 1: Minimal normal subgroups.**  
If \(R(G)=1\), then every minimal normal subgroup of \(G\) is a direct product of isomorphic nonabelian simple groups.

Reason: a minimal normal subgroup is characteristically simple, hence a direct product of isomorphic simple groups. If the simple factor were abelian, the subgroup would be elementary abelian and therefore solvable normal, contradicting \(R(G)=1\).

**Lemma 2: Socle form.**  
Thus

\[
M=\operatorname{Soc}(G)=S_1\times\cdots\times S_t
\]

with each \(S_i\) nonabelian simple. The factors may be grouped into \(G\)-orbits, and each orbit-product is a minimal normal subgroup of \(G\).

**Lemma 3: Faithful action on the socle.**  
Since \(C_G(M)\) is normal in \(G\), and since \(C_G(M)\cap M=Z(M)=1\), the subgroup \(C_G(M)\) contains no nontrivial minimal normal subgroup of \(G\). Hence \(C_G(M)=1\). Therefore conjugation gives an embedding

\[
G \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}(M).
\]

## 3. S3 Induced Action On Factors

Because \(M=S_1\times\cdots\times S_t\) is a direct product of simple factors, conjugation by any \(g\in G\) sends each factor \(S_i\) to another factor \(S_j\). Thus \(G\) induces a permutation action on the index set \(\{1,\dots,t\}\).

Let

\[
\pi:G\to \operatorname{Sym}(t)
\]

be this action. The kernel

\[
K=\ker \pi
\]

normalizes every \(S_i\), so \(K\) acts on each \(S_i\) by automorphisms. Hence

\[
K \leq \prod_{i=1}^t \operatorname{Aut}(S_i),
\]

while the image \(\pi(G)\) records the permutation action on the factors. Equivalently,

\[
G \leq \operatorname{Aut}(M)
\cong
\left(\prod_{i=1}^t \operatorname{Aut}(S_i)\right)\rtimes \operatorname{Sym}_{\text{type}},
\]

where the permutation part only permutes isomorphic simple factors.

## 4. S4 Reduction Of \(k(G)\)

Let \(M=\operatorname{Soc}(G)\). Conjugacy in \(G\) refines into two pieces:

1. conjugacy/orbit data inside \(M\), controlled by the action of \(G\) on \(M\);
2. conjugacy data in \(G/M\), controlled by the induced outer automorphism and factor-permutation action.

A standard class-count reduction is:

\[
k(G)\leq \sum_{x\in \mathcal R} k(C_G(x)/C_M(x)),
\]

where \(\mathcal R\) is a set of representatives for the \(G\)-orbits on conjugacy classes of \(M\). In particular, \(k(G)\) is bounded by data determined by:

\[
\text{\(G\)-orbits on } \operatorname{Cl}(S_1)\times\cdots\times \operatorname{Cl}(S_t),
\]

together with stabilizer class counts coming from the induced automorphism groups of the simple factors and the permutation action on equal-type factors.

Thus the radical-free case reduces to bounding orbit/class data for products of nonabelian simple groups under subgroups of their automorphism wreath products.

2. Subproof or failure

Let \(d=d_p(G)\). By definition of \(d_p(G)\), for every nonempty \(U\subseteq V(G)\),
\[
e(G[U])\le d|U|^p.
\]
For \(U=\varnothing\), the same inequality is trivial.

Let \(A,B\subseteq V(G)\). If \(a=0\), then one of \(A,B\) is empty, so \(e(A,B)=0\), and the desired bound is immediate. Hence assume \(a\ge 1\).

Since the graph is undirected, the ordered count satisfies \(e(A,B)=e(B,A)\). Thus we may relabel so that \(|A|=a\le b=|B|\).

Choose uniformly at random an \(a\)-element subset \(T\subseteq B\). For each ordered adjacent pair \((u,v)\in A\times B\), the probability that \(v\in T\) is \(a/b\). Therefore, by linearity of expectation,
\[
\mathbb E\, e(A,T)=\frac ab e(A,B).
\]

For every fixed \(T\), every ordered adjacent pair counted by \(e(A,T)\) has both endpoints in \(A\cup T\). Each unordered edge of \(G[A\cup T]\) contributes at most two ordered pairs to \(e(A,T)\). Hence
\[
e(A,T)\le 2e(G[A\cup T]).
\]
Using the hereditary bound,
\[
e(A,T)\le 2d|A\cup T|^p\le 2d(|A|+|T|)^p=2d(2a)^p=2^{p+1}da^p.
\]
Taking expectations gives
\[
\frac ab e(A,B)\le 2^{p+1}da^p.
\]
Since \(a>0\),
\[
e(A,B)\le 2^{p+1}d\, b\, a^{p-1}.
\]
Thus SC2 holds with
\[
K_p=2^{p+1}.
\]

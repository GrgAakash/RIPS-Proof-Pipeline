1. Target decomposition

target_label:
Spectral radius bound from hereditary \(p\)-edge density.

target_type:
Asymptotic extremal spectral graph inequality.

main_goal:
Prove an upper bound on \(\lambda(G)\) in terms of \(d_p(G)\) and \(n\), uniformly over all \(n\)-vertex graphs with at least one edge.

variables_and_parameters:
\(1<p\le 2\); \(n\to\infty\); \(G\) an \(n\)-vertex graph with at least one edge; \(\lambda(G)\) the adjacency spectral radius; \(d_p(G)=\max_{\emptyset\ne S\subseteq V(G)} e(G[S])/|S|^p\).

conclusion_to_prove:
There is a constant \(C_p>0\), depending only on \(p\), such that
\[
\lambda(G)\le (C_p+o(1))d_p(G)n^{\max\{1/2,p-1\}}
\]
as \(n\to\infty\).

2. Available tools

tool:
Hereditary edge bound from the definition of \(d_p(G)\).

source_status:
provided definition / notation / assumption.

exact_statement_or_fact:
For every nonempty \(S\subseteq V(G)\),
\[
e(G[S])\le d_p(G)|S|^p.
\]

intended_role_in_proof:
This is the only graph-specific density input. It will be converted into cut bounds and then into a weighted Rayleigh quotient bound.

tool:
Variational characterization of spectral radius.

source_status:
standard background fact.

exact_statement_or_fact:
For a symmetric adjacency matrix \(A\),
\[
\lambda(G)=\max_{\|x\|_2=1}x^TAx.
\]
Since \(A\) is nonnegative, the maximizing vector may be taken entrywise nonnegative.

intended_role_in_proof:
Reduce the theorem to bounding
\[
2\sum_{uv\in E(G)}x_ux_v
\]
for arbitrary nonnegative \(x\) with \(\sum_v x_v^2=1\).

tool:
Layer-cake representation for nonnegative products.

source_status:
standard background fact.

exact_statement_or_fact:
For nonnegative \(a,b\),
\[
ab=\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty \mathbf 1_{s<a}\mathbf 1_{t<b}\,ds\,dt.
\]

intended_role_in_proof:
Express the weighted edge sum through edge counts between level sets
\[
L_t=\{v:x_v\ge t\}.
\]

tool:
Level-set size bound from \(\ell_2\)-normalization.

source_status:
standard background fact.

exact_statement_or_fact:
If \(x\ge0\) and \(\|x\|_2=1\), then
\[
|L_t|\le \min\{n,t^{-2}\}
\]
for every \(t>0\).

intended_role_in_proof:
Turns the layer-cake expression into a one-variable analytic estimate depending only on \(n,p,d_p(G)\).

tool:
Cut bound derived from hereditary \(p\)-density.

source_status:
proved inside the current proof.

exact_statement_or_fact:
There is \(K_p>0\) such that for all \(A,B\subseteq V(G)\),
\[
e(A,B)\le K_p d_p(G)\max\{|A|,|B|\}\min\{|A|,|B|\}^{p-1},
\]
where \(e(A,B)\) counts unordered crossing edges, with harmless adjustment when \(A\cap B\ne\emptyset\).

intended_role_in_proof:
Controls edges between two level sets more sharply than the crude induced bound on \(A\cup B\), especially for star-like configurations.

tool:
Analytic level-set integral estimate.

source_status:
proved inside the current proof.

exact_statement_or_fact:
For every nonnegative \(x\in\mathbb R^n\) with \(\|x\|_2=1\), the level-set integral produced by the previous two tools is bounded by
\[
O_p\bigl(n^{\max\{1/2,p-1\}}\bigr).
\]

intended_role_in_proof:
Completes the Rayleigh quotient estimate:
\[
x^TAx\le C_p d_p(G)n^{\max\{1/2,p-1\}}.
\]

tool:
Lower bound on \(d_p(G)\) for graphs with an edge.

source_status:
proved inside the current proof.

exact_statement_or_fact:
Since \(G\) has an edge, taking its two endpoints gives
\[
d_p(G)\ge 2^{-p}.
\]

intended_role_in_proof:
Allows constants involving \(d_p(G)^{1/2}\) or additive harmless terms to be absorbed into \(C_p d_p(G)\), if needed.

4. Hardest step prediction

hardest_step_id:
SC5.

hardest_step_description:
The delicate part is proving the level-set integral has the exact exponent \(n^{\max\{1/2,p-1\}}\), especially near the endpoint \(p=3/2\), where crude dyadic summation may introduce an unwanted logarithm.

risk_if_wrong:
A logarithmic loss would not prove the theorem as stated, because \(\log n\) cannot be absorbed into \(C_p+o(1)\).

how_final_proof_should_handle_it:
The final proof should use a sharp integral or summation argument, not a loose dyadic bound. If a borderline logarithm appears, the proof must refine the decomposition, use the stronger cut estimate from SC2 more efficiently, or split the vector into heavy/light parts to eliminate the endpoint loss.

5. Failure-mode checks

circularity_check:
No subclaim should assume the target spectral bound. SC2 must be proved only from the definition of \(d_p(G)\), not from spectral information.

full_theorem_check:
The proof must cover all \(1<p\le2\), including \(p=3/2\) and \(p=2\), and every graph with at least one edge.

source_check:
No external extremal graph theorem is available. Any cut estimate, integral estimate, or weighted inequality must be proved inside the current proof unless it is explicitly standard background.

hypothesis_check:
The assumption “with at least one edge” is used to ensure \(d_p(G)>0\). Empty graphs are excluded and need not be handled.

notation_check:
Clarify whether \(e(A,B)\) counts unordered crossing edges or ordered incidences. Constants can absorb factors of \(2\), but the convention must stay consistent.

standard_background_check:
Allowed standard facts are limited to Perron-Frobenius/variational characterization for symmetric nonnegative matrices, layer-cake identities, and elementary inequalities such as Markov/Cauchy estimates for level-set sizes.

6. Subproblem assignment table

S1:
Prove SC1 and SC4. Show directly from the definition that every induced subgraph satisfies \(e(G[S])\le d_p(G)|S|^p\), and because \(G\) has an edge, \(d_p(G)\ge2^{-p}\). Also prove \(|L_t|\le t^{-2}\) from \(\sum_vx_v^2=1\), and include the trivial bound \(|L_t|\le n\).

S2:
Prove SC2. Starting from arbitrary \(A,B\), let \(a=\min\{|A|,|B|\}\), \(b=\max\{|A|,|B|\}\). Use averaging over subsets of the larger side together with the hereditary induced-edge bound to prove
\[
e(A,B)\le K_p d_p(G)b a^{p-1}.
\]
Handle overlaps and small-set edge cases by changing \(K_p\).

S3:
Prove SC3. Use the layer-cake identity
\[
x_ux_v=\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty \mathbf 1_{s<x_u}\mathbf 1_{t<x_v}\,ds\,dt
\]
and sum over edges. Then apply SC2 to the level sets \(L_s,L_t\).

S4:
Prove SC5. Bound the resulting double integral using \(|L_t|\le \min\{n,t^{-2}\}\). Treat separately \(1<p<3/2\), \(p=3/2\), and \(3/2<p\le2\). The endpoint \(p=3/2\) must be handled sharply enough to avoid logarithmic loss.

S5:
Assemble SC6 and SC7. Apply the variational characterization to a nonnegative unit eigenvector or arbitrary nonnegative unit vector. Combine SC3 and SC5, absorb absolute constants into \(C_p\), and conclude the stated \((C_p+o(1))\) asymptotic form.

1. Assignment restatement

S-ID: S4

assigned subclaim(s): SC5

what must be proved: For the level-set integral produced after applying the cut bound to layer-cake level sets, prove an analytic bound using only
\[
|L_t|\le \min\{n,t^{-2}\}.
\]
Specifically, for \(1<p\le 2\), show the double integral is \(O_p(n^{\max\{1/2,p-1\}})\), with the endpoint \(p=3/2\) handled without logarithmic loss.

declared prerequisite subclaims: SC2 and SC3 are prerequisites for knowing the exact integral form. SC1/SC4 are prerequisites for the level-set bound \(|L_t|\le \min\{n,t^{-2}\}\).

2. Subproof or failure

Let \(x=(x_v)_{v\in V(G)}\) be a nonnegative vector with \(\sum_v x_v^2=1\), and define
\[
L_t=\{v:x_v>t\},\qquad m(t)=|L_t|.
\]
From the assigned input, we may use
\[
m(t)\le f(t):=\min\{n,t^{-2}\}.
\]
Since \(x_v\le 1\) for every \(v\), \(L_t=\varnothing\) for \(t\ge 1\), so all level-set integrals may be restricted to \(0<t<1\).

The integral to be bounded after SC3 and SC2 has the analytic form
\[
I_p:=\int_0^1\int_0^1 \max\{m(s),m(t)\}\min\{m(s),m(t)\}^{p-1}\,ds\,dt.
\]
Because \(m\) is nonincreasing, on the region \(0<s\le t<1\) we have \(m(s)\ge m(t)\), hence
\[
I_p\le 2\int_0^1\int_s^1 m(s)m(t)^{p-1}\,dt\,ds.
\]
Using \(m\le f\),
\[
I_p\le 2\int_0^1 f(s)\left(\int_s^1 f(t)^{p-1}\,dt\right)ds.
\]

Put \(\alpha=n^{-1/2}\). Then
\[
f(t)=
\begin{cases}
n,&0<t\le \alpha,\\
t^{-2},&\alpha<t\le 1.
\end{cases}
\]

We split
\[
I_p\le 2(A+B),
\]
where
\[
A=\int_0^\alpha n\left(\int_s^1 f(t)^{p-1}\,dt\right)ds,
\qquad
B=\int_\alpha^1 s^{-2}\left(\int_s^1 t^{-2(p-1)}\,dt\right)ds.
\]

For \(A\), since \(s\le \alpha\),
\[
\int_s^1 f(t)^{p-1}\,dt
\le \int_0^\alpha n^{p-1}\,dt+\int_\alpha^1 t^{-2(p-1)}\,dt.
\]
If \(1<p<3/2\), then
\[
\int_\alpha^1 t^{-2(p-1)}\,dt=O_p(1),
\]
so
\[
A\le n\alpha\left(\alpha n^{p-1}+O_p(1)\right)
=O_p(n^{p-1})+O_p(n^{1/2})
=O_p(n^{1/2}).
\]
If \(p=3/2\), then
\[
\int_\alpha^1 t^{-1}\,dt=\log(1/\alpha)=\tfrac12\log n,
\]
which would give a logarithm from this crude estimate. To avoid this, use the sharper identity
\[
A=n\int_0^\alpha\int_s^1 f(t)^{1/2}\,dt\,ds
=n\int_0^1 f(t)^{1/2}\min\{\alpha,t\}\,dt.
\]
Now \(f(t)^{1/2}=n^{1/2}\) for \(t\le\alpha\), and \(f(t)^{1/2}=t^{-1}\) for \(t\ge\alpha\). Hence
\[
A
=n\left(\int_0^\alpha n^{1/2}t\,dt+\int_\alpha^1 t^{-1}\alpha\,dt\right)
=\frac12 n^{1/2}+n^{1/2}\log(1/\alpha).
\]
This still has a logarithm. Therefore the proposed SC5 statement, in the form assigned here and using only \(|L_t|\le\min\{n,t^{-2}\}\), cannot be proved without logarithmic loss at \(p=3/2\).

Indeed, the obstruction is real. Let
\[
m(t)=\min\{n,t^{-2}\}\quad\text{for }0<t\le 1.
\]
This function satisfies exactly the permitted level-set upper bound. For \(p=3/2\), the model integral becomes
\[
I_{3/2}^{\mathrm{model}}
=2\int_0^1 f(s)\left(\int_s^1 f(t)^{1/2}\,dt\right)ds.
\]
Restricting to \(0<s<\alpha<t<1\), we get the lower bound
\[
I_{3/2}^{\mathrm{model}}
\ge 2\int_0^\alpha n\left(\int_\alpha^1 t^{-1}\,dt\right)ds
=2n\alpha\log(1/\alpha)
=n^{1/2}\log n.
\]
Thus the pointwise estimate \(|L_t|\le\min\{n,t^{-2}\}\) alone permits a logarithmic contribution at \(p=3/2\). Consequently, the assigned endpoint claim cannot be derived from the stated allowed materials.

SUBPROBLEM UNSOLVED: The endpoint \(p=3/2\) requires an additional structural input beyond the pointwise level-set bound \(|L_t|\le\min\{n,t^{-2}\}\), or a different integral form from SC3/SC2 that is smaller than the stated max-min integral.

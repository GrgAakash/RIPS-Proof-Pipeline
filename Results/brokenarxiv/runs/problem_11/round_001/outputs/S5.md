2. Subproof or Failure

Let \(G\) be an \(n\)-vertex graph with at least one edge, and fix \(1<p\le 2\). Put
\[
\alpha=\max\{1/2,p-1\}.
\]

We declare the required prerequisite estimate in the following assembled form, which is exactly the kind of output SC3 and SC5 are intended to provide:

For every nonnegative vector \(x=(x_v)_{v\in V(G)}\) with \(\sum_v x_v^2=1\),
\[
2\sum_{uv\in E(G)}x_u x_v
\le A_p\, d_p(G)\, n^\alpha
\]
for some constant \(A_p>0\) depending only on \(p\).

Assuming this prerequisite estimate, we prove the spectral conclusion.

By the variational characterization of the largest eigenvalue of a real symmetric matrix,
\[
\lambda(G)=\max_{\|x\|_2=1} x^\top A_G x,
\]
where \(A_G\) is the adjacency matrix of \(G\). Since \(A_G\) is entrywise nonnegative and symmetric, the maximum may be taken over a nonnegative unit vector. Indeed, for any real \(x\),
\[
|x|^\top A_G |x|
=
2\sum_{uv\in E(G)} |x_u||x_v|
\ge
2\sum_{uv\in E(G)} x_u x_v
=
x^\top A_Gx.
\]
Thus a maximizing vector can be replaced by its coordinatewise absolute value without decreasing the Rayleigh quotient.

Let \(x\ge 0\) be a unit vector attaining the maximum. Then
\[
\lambda(G)
=
x^\top A_Gx
=
2\sum_{uv\in E(G)}x_u x_v.
\]
Applying the prerequisite SC3+SC5 estimate to this \(x\), we get
\[
\lambda(G)
\le
A_p\, d_p(G)\, n^\alpha.
\]
Set \(C_p=A_p\). Since an exact bound with constant \(C_p\) implies the asymptotic bound with coefficient \(C_p+o(1)\), we obtain
\[
\lambda(G)\le (C_p+o(1))d_p(G)n^{\max\{1/2,p-1\}}.
\]

Therefore SC6 and SC7 are established conditionally on the declared SC3 and SC5 prerequisite estimate.

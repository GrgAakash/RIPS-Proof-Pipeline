1. **Target Decomposition**

Prove the theorem by reducing the spectral-radius estimate to a weighted edge-sum bound.

Let  
\[
D=d_p(G).
\]
Then every nonempty induced subgraph satisfies
\[
e(G[S])\le D|S|^p.
\]
Take a nonnegative unit Perron eigenvector \(x\), so
\[
\lambda(G)=2\sum_{uv\in E(G)}x_ux_v,\qquad \sum_v x_v^2=1.
\]

The proof should split into regimes:

- **Regime A: \(1<p<3/2\).** Show directly that
\[
\lambda(G)\le C_pD\sqrt n.
\]

- **Regime B: \(p=3/2\).** Prove the endpoint estimate
\[
\lambda(G)\le K D\sqrt n
\]
without logarithmic loss. This is the delicate part flagged by the guidance.

- **Regime C: \(3/2<p\le 2\).** Show
\[
\lambda(G)\le C_pD n^{p-1}.
\]

The final theorem follows because \(\max\{1/2,p-1\}=1/2\) for \(p\le3/2\), and \(p-1\) for \(p\ge3/2\). The stated \(o(1)\) is harmless if the proof gives a uniform \(C_p\) bound.

2. **Available Tools**

Genuinely standard background facts to state explicitly:

- **Rayleigh quotient / Perron-Frobenius:** For a graph adjacency matrix \(A\), there is a nonnegative unit eigenvector \(x\) for \(\lambda(G)\), and
\[
\lambda(G)=x^TAx=2\sum_{uv\in E(G)}x_ux_v.
\]

- **Layer-cake representation:** For nonnegative \(x_u,x_v\),
\[
x_ux_v=\int_0^{x_u}\int_0^{x_v} ds\,dt.
\]
Thus
\[
\sum_{uv\in E}x_ux_v
=\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty e(L_s,L_t)\,ds\,dt,
\]
where \(L_t=\{v:x_v\ge t\}\), with an appropriate convention for ordered/unordered edge counting.

- **Level-set size bound from \(\ell_2\)-normalization:**
\[
|L_t|\le \min(n,t^{-2}).
\]

- **Hereditary edge bound:**
\[
e(G[S])\le D|S|^p
\]
for every nonempty \(S\subseteq V(G)\).

- **Bipartite edge reduction:** For two sets \(A,B\),
\[
e(A,B)\le e(G[A\cup B])\le D(|A|+|B|)^p.
\]
Sharper variants may be needed at the endpoint.

- **Dyadic decomposition:** Sort vertices or group them by dyadic magnitudes of \(x_v\). Estimate contributions between classes using hereditary edge bounds and \(\ell_2\)-mass constraints.

3. **Subclaim Support Graph With 3-8 Subclaims and Suggested Solvers S1-S5**

**Subclaim 1, S1: Rayleigh-to-level-set reduction.**  
For a nonnegative unit vector \(x\),
\[
2\sum_{uv\in E}x_ux_v
\]
can be bounded by a dyadic or integral expression involving only level sets \(L_t=\{v:x_v\ge t\}\), their sizes, and edge counts between them.

Depends on: standard Rayleigh quotient and layer-cake.

**Subclaim 2, S1: Basic density consequences.**  
From \(D=d_p(G)\), prove:
\[
e(A,B)\le C_pD(|A|+|B|)^p
\]
and, when useful,
\[
e(A,B)\le C_pD\min\{|A|^p+|B|^p,\ (|A|+|B|)^p\}.
\]

Depends on: definition of \(D\).

**Subclaim 3, S2: Easy subcritical estimate \(1<p<3/2\).**  
Using dyadic level sets and \(|L_t|\le\min(n,t^{-2})\), prove
\[
\lambda(G)\le C_pD\sqrt n
\]
for \(1<p<3/2\). Constants may blow up as \(p\uparrow3/2\).

Depends on: Subclaims 1 and 2.

**Subclaim 4, S3: Supercritical estimate \(3/2<p\le2\).**  
Using either level sets or vertex ordering by decreasing \(x_v\), prove
\[
\lambda(G)\le C_pD n^{p-1}.
\]
A promising route: order vertices by decreasing \(x_v\), bound the number of edges incident backward into the first \(k\) vertices by \(O(Dk^{p-1})\), then sum against \(x_v^2\) or adjacent dyadic masses.

Depends on: Subclaims 1 and 2.

**Subclaim 5, S4: Endpoint structural inequality.**  
Prove the key no-log endpoint statement:

If every induced subgraph satisfies
\[
e(G[S])\le D|S|^{3/2},
\]
then for every nonnegative unit vector \(x\),
\[
2\sum_{uv\in E}x_ux_v\le K D\sqrt n.
\]

This must not rely only on \(|L_t|\le\min(n,t^{-2})\) in the naive max-min integral form. It should use a sharper dyadic bilinear estimate or a structural coupling between different level sets.

Depends on: Subclaims 1 and 2.

**Subclaim 6, S5: Assemble constants and asymptotics.**  
Combine the three regimes into a single constant \(C_p\), depending only on \(p\), and observe that a stronger non-asymptotic estimate implies the stated
\[
(C_p+o(1))D n^{\max\{1/2,p-1\}}.
\]

Depends on: Subclaims 3, 4, 5.

Support graph:

\[
\text{S1: reductions} \to \text{S2 subcritical},\ \text{S3 supercritical},\ \text{S4 endpoint}
\to \text{S5 assembly}.

4. **Hardest Step Prediction**

The endpoint \(p=3/2\) is the hardest step.

The obvious estimate
\[
e(L_s,L_t)\lesssim D\min\{|L_s|+|L_t|\}^{3/2}
\]
combined only with
\[
|L_t|\le\min(n,t^{-2})
\]
risks a logarithmic divergence. The guidance explicitly says the monotone Hardy-style replacement is false, so the endpoint solver must avoid reducing the problem to that invalid inequality.

A plausible successful endpoint strategy is dyadic:

- Partition vertices by \(x_v\in(2^{-(j+1)},2^{-j}]\).
- Let \(B_j\) be each block and \(m_j=|B_j|\).
- Use \(\sum_j 2^{-2j}m_j\le1\).
- Bound edges between \(B_i,B_j\) not independently by the worst level-set estimate, but through cumulative sets or a summation-by-parts argument that couples many dyadic blocks at once.
- Show the total weighted edge contribution is \(O(D\sqrt n)\), with cancellation or summability replacing the false endpoint Hardy bound.

5. **Failure-Mode Checks**

- Do not prove the endpoint from only
\[
|L_t|\le\min(n,t^{-2})
\]
and the naive max-min integral; that route gives or hides a logarithm.

- Do not use the false Hardy estimate mentioned in the prompt.

- Do not assume maximum degree is bounded by \(O(Dn^{p-1})\) and then conclude the whole theorem for \(p<3/2\); that gives the wrong exponent.

- Do not use non-induced subgraph density unless derived from the induced condition.

- Ensure edge-count conventions are consistent: unordered edges in \(e(G[S])\), while Rayleigh has the factor \(2\).

- Constants may depend on \(p\), but not on \(n\), \(G\), or \(D\).

- Since \(G\) has at least one edge, \(D>0\); no division-by-zero issue occurs.

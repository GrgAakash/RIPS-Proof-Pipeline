**S1 Result**

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be finite, simple, undirected. Use these edge-count conventions:

- \(e(S)\): unordered edges inside \(S\), counted once.
- \(e_\times(A,B)\): for disjoint \(A,B\), unordered crossing edges, counted once.
- \(\vec e(A,B)=\sum_{u\in A,v\in B}\mathbf 1_{\{uv\in E\}}\): ordered adjacency count. Thus \(\vec e(S,S)=2e(S)\), and if \(A,B\) are disjoint then \(\vec e(A,B)=e_\times(A,B)\).

Let \(D=d_p(G)\). Then for every \(S\subseteq V\),
\[
e(S)\le D|S|^p,
\]
with the empty case trivial. If \(G\) has an edge, then \(D\ge 2^{-p}>0\).

**Rayleigh/Perron Setup**

Let \(A\) be the adjacency matrix. By Rayleigh-Ritz and \(|z|^TA|z|\ge z^TAz\), there is a nonnegative unit vector \(x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^V\), \(\|x\|_2=1\), such that
\[
\lambda(G)=x^TAx=\sum_{u,v}A_{uv}x_ux_v=2\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E}x_ux_v.
\]
Zeros may occur if \(G\) is disconnected; they do not affect any formula below.

**Layer-Cake Identities**

For \(t\ge0\), define
\[
L_t=\{v:x_v\ge t\},\qquad a(t)=|L_t|.
\]
Then \(a(t)\) is nonincreasing and
\[
\int_0^\infty 2t\,a(t)\,dt=\sum_v x_v^2=1,\qquad
\int_0^\infty a(t)\,dt=\sum_v x_v\le \sqrt n.
\]
Also
\[
a(t)\le n,\qquad a(t)\le t^{-2}\ \text{for }t>0,\qquad L_t=\varnothing\ \text{for }t>1.
\]

Define \(F(s,t)=\vec e(L_s,L_t)\). Tonelli gives the exact Rayleigh layer-cake formula
\[
\lambda(G)=\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty F(s,t)\,ds\,dt.
\]
Indeed each ordered adjacent pair \((u,v)\) contributes
\[
\int_0^{x_u}\int_0^{x_v}ds\,dt=x_ux_v.
\]

A useful one-parameter identity is
\[
\int_0^\infty 2t\,e(L_t)\,dt
=\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E}\min(x_u,x_v)^2.
\]

**Density Consequences**

For disjoint \(A,B\),
\[
e_\times(A,B)\le e(A\cup B)\le D(|A|+|B|)^p.
\]
For arbitrary \(A,B\),
\[
\vec e(A,B)\le 2e(A\cup B)\le 2D|A\cup B|^p\le 2D(|A|+|B|)^p.
\]
So the requested bound holds with \(C_p=1\) for disjoint unordered crossing counts and \(C_p=2\) for ordered counts.

For level sets, since \(L_s,L_t\) are nested,
\[
F(s,t)=\vec e(L_s,L_t)
\le 2D|L_s\cup L_t|^p
=2D\,a(\min\{s,t\})^p.
\]
Hence the general level-set reduction
\[
\lambda(G)
\le 2D\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty a(\min\{s,t\})^p\,ds\,dt.
\]
Equivalently, if \(M=\|x\|_\infty\le1\),
\[
\lambda(G)\le 4D\int_0^M (M-t)a(t)^p\,dt.
\]

**Dyadic Reductions**

Let
\[
B_k=\{v:2^k<x_v\le 2^{k+1}\},\qquad b_k=|B_k|.
\]
Only finitely many \(B_k\) are nonempty. Then
\[
\frac14\le \sum_k 2^{2k}b_k\le 1,
\]
and, with \(m_{ij}=\vec e(B_i,B_j)\),
\[
\sum_{i,j}2^{i+j}m_{ij}\le \lambda(G)
\le 4\sum_{i,j}2^{i+j}m_{ij}.
\]
The block edge matrix satisfies, for all index sets \(I,J\),
\[
\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}m_{ij}
=\vec e\Bigl(\bigcup_{i\in I}B_i,\bigcup_{j\in J}B_j\Bigr)
\le 2D\Bigl(\sum_{i\in I}b_i+\sum_{j\in J}b_j\Bigr)^p.
\]

For nested dyadic level sets \(U_k=L_{2^k}\), define
\[
R_U=\sum_{k,\ell}2^{k+\ell}\vec e(U_k,U_\ell).
\]
Then dyadic rounding gives
\[
\lambda(G)\le R_U\le 4\lambda(G),
\]
and the density bound gives
\[
R_U\le 2D\sum_{k,\ell}2^{k+\ell}|U_{\min\{k,\ell\}}|^p.
\]

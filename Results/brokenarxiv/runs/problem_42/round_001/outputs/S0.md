1. Target Decomposition

target_label: FFD iff IDF + almost atomic

target_type: equivalence theorem

main_goal: Prove that an integral domain `R` is a finite factorization domain exactly when it has the IDF property and is almost atomic.

variables_and_parameters: Integral domain `R`; nonzero elements `r, x`; atoms/irreducibles of `R`; units `R^x`; factorizations up to order and associates.

conclusion_to_prove:  
`R` is an FFD if and only if:
1. every nonzero element is divisible by only finitely many atoms up to associates, and
2. for every nonzero `r in R`, there is an atomic `s in R` such that `rs` is atomic.

4. Hardest Step Prediction

hardest_step_id: SC5

hardest_step_description: Showing that almost atomicity plus IDF forces `r` itself to be atomic. From `s` atomic and `rs` atomic, one must carefully justify that `r` factors into atoms, rather than merely that a multiple of `r` does.

risk_if_wrong: The reverse implication may silently assume cancellation of atom factorizations or divisor-chain behavior that is not automatic in an arbitrary integral domain.

how_final_proof_should_handle_it: Fix atom factorizations of `s` and `rs`. Use divisibility in the integral domain and induction on the length of a factorization of `s` or `rs`, peeling off atoms only via the irreducibility condition and valid cancellation. Avoid assuming unique factorization.

5. Failure-Mode Checks

circularity_check: Do not assume that IDF plus almost atomic already implies atomicity; that is SC5 and must be proved.

full_theorem_check: Both directions are required: FFD implies IDF and almost atomic, and IDF plus almost atomic implies FFD.

source_check: No external lemmas, web sources, or prior results are available. Any nontrivial auxiliary claim must be proved inside the final proof.

hypothesis_check: The proof must use that `R` is an integral domain, especially for cancellation and nonzero product behavior.

notation_check: `R^x` denotes units. Factorizations are considered up to order and associates.

standard_background_check: Only elementary facts about integral domains, units, divisibility, irreducibles, finite sets, and finite multisets should be treated as standard.

6. Subproblem Assignment Table

S1: Prove the forward implication. Show FFD implies every nonzero element is atomic, hence almost atomic by taking multiplier `1`; then prove IDF by showing every atom divisor of a nonzero element appears in one of finitely many factorizations.

S2: Prove that in an IDF domain, every atomic element has only finitely many factorizations. Use the finite set of atom divisors of the element and a finite-multiset counting argument.

S3: Under IDF and almost atomicity, fix arbitrary nonzero `r` and choose atomic `s` with `rs` atomic. Establish that `s` and `rs` have finitely many atom factorizations using S2.

S4: Prove that the same arbitrary `r` is atomic. This is the delicate cancellation/divisibility step and must not use unique factorization.

S5: Prove that `r` has only finitely many factorizations by mapping each factorization of `r` to a factorization of `rs` after multiplying by a fixed factorization of `s`; then assemble the reverse implication and final equivalence.

1. Target decomposition

target_label:
FFD iff IDF and almost atomic

target_type:
Equivalence theorem for integral domains and factorization properties.

main_goal:
Prove that an integral domain `R` is a finite factorization domain exactly when `R` has the IDF property and is almost atomic.

variables_and_parameters:
`R` integral domain; `R^x` unit group; atoms/irreducibles as defined; nonzero elements `r, a, s`.

conclusion_to_prove:
`R` is an FFD if and only if every nonzero element has finitely many atom divisors up to associates and for every nonzero `r` there is atomic `s` with `rs` atomic.

4. Hardest step prediction

hardest_step_id:
SC3

hardest_step_description:
The hard part is proving that IDF plus almost atomicity forces each nonzero `r` to be atomic. The proof must not argue that an atom dividing a product of atoms is associate to one of those atoms. Instead, it needs a descent or minimality argument using an atomic multiplier `s` of minimal factorization length and the IDF finiteness of possible irreducible divisors.

risk_if_wrong:
A proof that treats irreducibles as prime would be invalid in a general integral domain.

how_final_proof_should_handle_it:
The final proof should explicitly avoid prime-style cancellation. It should isolate a minimal atomic multiplier for a hypothetical non-atomic element and prove contradiction using only divisibility, irreducibility, associate classes, finite atom-divisor sets from IDF, and well-founded descent on the chosen atomic multiplier length.

5. Failure-mode checks

circularity_check:
Do not assume atomicity in the reverse implication before SC3 is proved.

full_theorem_check:
Forward direction must prove both IDF and almost atomic. Reverse direction must prove both existence and finiteness of factorizations for every nonzero nonunit.

source_check:
No prior theorem statements are available. Only definitions, standard background, and subclaims proved inside this proof may be used.

hypothesis_check:
The domain hypothesis is needed for cancellation of nonzero factors and to keep divisibility/factorization behavior well-defined. Do not silently use primality of atoms.

notation_check:
`R^x` denotes units. “Up to associates” means modulo multiplication by units. Factorizations are counted up to order and associates.

standard_background_check:
Allowed standard facts should be limited to elementary divisibility facts in integral domains, cancellation by nonzero elements, finite-union finiteness, and induction/well-ordering on natural numbers.

6. Subproblem assignment table

S1:
Prove the forward implication. Show FFD implies atomicity, almost atomicity via `s = 1`, and IDF by observing that every atom divisor of a nonzero element appears in some factorization of that element after factoring the complementary factor.

S2:
Prove SC3. Assume IDF and almost atomic. Give a self-contained proof that every nonzero element is atomic. The proof must use minimality/descent and must not use “irreducible implies prime.”

S3:
Prove SC4. Assuming IDF and atomicity, prove every nonzero nonunit has finitely many atom factorizations. Use finite branching from IDF and induction/descent through proper co-divisors in factorizations.

S4:
Assemble the reverse implication from SC3 and SC4, checking directly against the FFD definition.

S5:
Audit the full proof for forbidden prime-style reasoning, circular use of atomicity, and correct treatment of units, associates, zero exclusions, and finite factorization counting.

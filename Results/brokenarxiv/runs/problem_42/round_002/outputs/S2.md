## 1 Assignment Restatement

Prove SC3: assuming `R` is IDF and almost atomic, show every nonzero element of `R` is atomic, using a minimality/descent argument and without treating irreducibles as primes.

## 2 Subproof Or Failure

SUBPROBLEM UNSOLVED.

The standard minimal-multiplier setup reaches the key obstruction but does not close it from the supplied hypotheses.

Let `0 != r in R`. By almost atomicity, choose an atomic `s` such that `rs` is atomic. Choose such an `s` with minimal factorization length. If `s` is a unit, then `r` is atomic.

Suppose `s` is not a unit. Write `s = pt`, where `p` is an atom and `t` is atomic. By minimality, `rt` is not atomic; otherwise `t` would be a shorter atomic multiplier for `r`. But

`(rt)p = rs`

is atomic. Thus the whole problem reduces to the length-one cancellation lemma:

> If `R` is IDF, `p` is an atom, and `hp` is atomic, must `h` be atomic?

I cannot prove this lemma from IDF plus almost atomicity without an additional argument. The tempting proof would factor `hp` into atoms and cancel `p`, but this is exactly the forbidden prime-style move: an atom divisor `p` of a product of atoms need not be associate to one of those atoms.

IDF only says that `hp` has finitely many atom divisors up to associates. It does not, by itself, force the atom divisor `p` to occur in any factorization of `hp`, nor does it force the quotient `(hp)/p = h` to be atomic.

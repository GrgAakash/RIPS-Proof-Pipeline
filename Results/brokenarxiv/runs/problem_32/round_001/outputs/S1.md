1. Assignment restatement

S-ID: S1

assigned subclaim(s): Cyclic p-group orbit facts.

what must be proved: For a cyclic group \(G=\langle g\rangle\) of order \(p^n\), describe \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), identify its orbits as the \(p\)-adic layers
\[
L_i=\{g^{p^i u}: u\in(\mathbb Z/p^{n-i}\mathbb Z)^\times\},\quad 0\le i<n,
\]
together with \(\{1_G\}\), and prove the generator-layer test:
\[
H.g \text{ is singleton } \Longleftrightarrow H=\{1\},
\]
\[
H.g=L_0 \Longleftrightarrow H=\operatorname{Aut}(G),
\]
so if \(\{1\}<H<\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), then \(H.g\) is a non-singleton proper subset of \(L_0\).

declared prerequisite subclaims: none.

2. Subproof or failure

Fix a generator \(g\) of \(G\). Every endomorphism of \(G\) is determined by the image of \(g\). Since \(g\) has order \(p^n\), the element \(g^a\) has order \(p^n/\gcd(p^n,a)\). Thus \(g^a\) is again a generator exactly when \(\gcd(a,p)=1\), equivalently when the residue class of \(a\) is a unit modulo \(p^n\). Therefore every automorphism of \(G\) has the form
\[
\varphi_u(g)=g^u,\qquad u\in(\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z)^\times,
\]
and composition satisfies \(\varphi_u\varphi_v=\varphi_{uv}\). Hence
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(G)\cong(\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z)^\times.
\]

Now let \(x\in G\). If \(x=1_G\), then every automorphism fixes \(x\). If \(x\ne 1_G\), write
\[
x=g^k,\qquad k=p^i a,
\]
where \(0\le i<n\) and \(p\nmid a\). The integer \(i\) is the exact \(p\)-adic valuation of the exponent \(k\) modulo \(p^n\). For any automorphism \(\varphi_u\),
\[
\varphi_u(x)=g^{uk}=g^{p^i(ua)}.
\]
Since \(u\) is prime to \(p\), the factor \(ua\) is also prime to \(p\). Thus automorphisms preserve the layer
\[
L_i=\{g^{p^i a}: a\in(\mathbb Z/p^{n-i}\mathbb Z)^\times\}.
\]

Conversely, take two elements of the same layer:
\[
x=g^{p^i a},\qquad y=g^{p^i b},
\]
with \(a,b\) units modulo \(p^{n-i}\). Since \(a\) is invertible modulo \(p^{n-i}\), choose \(u\) satisfying
\[
ua\equiv b\pmod{p^{n-i}}.
\]
This \(u\) is prime to \(p\), so it may be regarded as a unit modulo \(p^n\). Then
\[
\varphi_u(x)=g^{p^iua}=g^{p^ib}=y.
\]
Therefore the \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\)-orbits are exactly the layers \(L_i\) for \(0\le i<n\), together with \(\{1_G\}\). Since \(H\le\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), every \(H\)-orbit is contained in an \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\)-orbit, so the \(H\)-orbits refine these layers.

Finally consider the generator layer
\[
L_0=\{g^a:\gcd(a,p)=1\}.
\]
The map
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(G)\to L_0,\qquad \varphi\mapsto \varphi(g)
\]
is bijective: every generator is the image of \(g\) under a unique automorphism. Hence \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) acts regularly on \(L_0\). In particular, the stabilizer of \(g\) is trivial.

For \(H\le\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), the orbit \(H.g\) is singleton exactly when every element of \(H\) fixes \(g\), which by triviality of the stabilizer is equivalent to \(H=\{1\}\). Also \(H.g=L_0\) exactly when the restricted map \(H\to L_0\), \(\varphi\mapsto\varphi(g)\), has image all of \(L_0\). Since the full map \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\to L_0\) is bijective, this happens exactly when \(H=\operatorname{Aut}(G)\). Therefore, if \(\{1\}<H<\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), then \(H.g\) has more than one element but is not all of \(L_0\).

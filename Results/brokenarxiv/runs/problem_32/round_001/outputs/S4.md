2. Subproof or failure

SUBPROBLEM UNSOLVED. Obstacle: the assigned subclaim is false.

Take \(G=\langle g\rangle\) of order \(9\), and identify \(g^r\) with \(r\in \mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(G)\cong (\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z)^\times=\{1,2,4,5,7,8\}.
\]
Let
\[
H=\{1,4,7\}.
\]
This is a nontrivial proper subgroup of \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\). Its orbits on \(G\), in exponent notation, are
\[
O_0=\{0\},\quad A=\{1,4,7\},\quad B=\{2,5,8\},\quad C=\{3\},\quad D=\{6\}.
\]

For an orbit \(X\), let \(A_X e_r=\sum_{x\in X}e_{r+x}\), and let \(E_X^*\) be projection onto the coordinates indexed by \(X\). The Terwilliger algebra is generated by these \(A_X\)'s and \(E_X^*\)'s.

Let
\[
P=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_{O_0},\mathbf 1_A,\mathbf 1_B,\mathbf 1_C,\mathbf 1_D\}
\]
be the primary module, and define
\[
W_A=\left\{\sum_{a\in A}c_a e_a:\sum_{a\in A}c_a=0\right\},\qquad
W_B=\left\{\sum_{b\in B}c_b e_b:\sum_{b\in B}c_b=0\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb C^G=P\oplus W_A\oplus W_B.
\]

For \(f\in W_A\), the relations \(C=\{3\}\) and \(D=\{6\}\) act on \(A=\{1,4,7\}\) by the two cyclic shifts
\[
e_1\mapsto e_4\mapsto e_7\mapsto e_1
\]
and its inverse. The relation \(O_0\) acts as the identity. The relation \(A\) sends every source point of \(A\) to every point of \(B\), so each output coordinate is \(\sum_{a\in A} f(a)=0\). The relation \(B\) sends \(A\) only to the singleton fibers \(O_0,C,D\), again with each resulting coordinate equal to \(\sum_{a\in A}f(a)=0\). Thus \(W_A\) is \(T\)-invariant. The same argument shows \(W_B\) is \(T\)-invariant.

Let \(\zeta\) be a primitive third root of unity. Then
\[
u_A=e_1+\zeta e_4+\zeta^2 e_7,\qquad
v_A=e_1+\zeta^2 e_4+\zeta e_7
\]
span \(W_A\), and each is an eigenvector for the cyclic shifts. Since every other generator of \(T\) acts on these lines by either \(0\) or a scalar, both \(\mathbb C u_A\) and \(\mathbb C v_A\) are one-dimensional \(T\)-modules. Similarly, \(W_B\) splits into two one-dimensional \(T\)-modules.

Finally, \(P\) is irreducible: if a nonzero \(T\)-submodule of \(P\) contains some nonzero multiple of \(\mathbf 1_X\), then applying \(E_{O_0}^*A_{-X}\) gives a nonzero multiple of \(e_0\); from \(e_0\), applying the adjacency matrices \(A_X\) gives every \(\mathbf 1_X\). Hence the submodule is all of \(P\).

So the standard module decomposes as the primary module \(P\) plus four one-dimensional nonprimary modules. There is no nonprimary irreducible module of dimension at least \(2\). This contradicts SC5.

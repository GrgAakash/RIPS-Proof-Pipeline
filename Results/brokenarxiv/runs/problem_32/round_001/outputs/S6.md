\[
\operatorname{Aut}(G)\cong (\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z)^\times=\{1,2,4,5,7,8\}.
\]
Take
\[
H=\{1,4,7\}.
\]
This is a nontrivial proper subgroup of \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\). Its orbits on \(G\), in exponent notation, are
\[
O_0=\{0\},\qquad A=\{1,4,7\},\qquad B=\{2,5,8\},\qquad C=\{3\},\qquad D=\{6\}.
\]

4. For an orbit \(X\), let \(A_X e_r=\sum_{x\in X}e_{r+x}\), and let \(E_X^*\) be projection onto the coordinates indexed by \(X\). Let
\[
P=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_{O_0},\mathbf 1_A,\mathbf 1_B,\mathbf 1_C,\mathbf 1_D\},
\]
\[
W_A=\left\{\sum_{a\in A}c_a e_a:\sum_{a\in A}c_a=0\right\},\qquad
W_B=\left\{\sum_{b\in B}c_b e_b:\sum_{b\in B}c_b=0\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb C^G=P\oplus W_A\oplus W_B.
\]

5. The subspaces \(W_A\) and \(W_B\) are invariant under all generators \(A_X,E_X^*\) of the Terwilliger algebra. Indeed, \(E_A^*\) acts as the identity on \(W_A\), while the other dual idempotents kill \(W_A\); similarly for \(W_B\). On \(W_A\), the singleton orbits \(C=\{3\}\) and \(D=\{6\}\) act by the two cyclic shifts of \(A=\{1,4,7\}\), while \(O_0\) acts as the identity. The orbits \(A\) and \(B\) send a vector in \(W_A\) to coordinates equal to its total coordinate sum, hence to zero. The same argument applies to \(W_B\).

6. Let \(\zeta\) be a primitive third root of unity. Then
\[
u_A=e_1+\zeta e_4+\zeta^2e_7,\qquad
v_A=e_1+\zeta^2e_4+\zeta e_7
\]
span \(W_A\), and each is a simultaneous eigenvector for every generator of the Terwilliger algebra. Hence \(W_A\) splits as two one-dimensional \(T\)-modules. The same construction splits \(W_B\) as two one-dimensional \(T\)-modules.

7. The primary module is
\[
P=\operatorname{span}\{E_X^*\mathbf 1:X\in\{O_0,A,B,C,D\}\}.
\]
It is irreducible: if a nonzero \(T\)-submodule of \(P\) contains a vector with nonzero \(X\)-component, applying \(E_X^*\) gives a nonzero multiple of \(\mathbf 1_X\). Applying \(E_{O_0}^*A_{-X}\) gives a nonzero multiple of \(e_0=\mathbf 1_{O_0}\), and then \(A_Ye_0=\mathbf 1_Y\) for every orbit \(Y\). Thus the submodule is all of \(P\).

8. Therefore \(\mathbb C^G\) decomposes as the primary irreducible module \(P\) plus four one-dimensional nonprimary irreducible modules. By the criterion supplied in S2, the Terwilliger algebra is almost commutative.

9. But \(H=\{1,4,7\}\) is neither trivial nor all of \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\). Hence the target theorem is false as stated, so no complete proof of the target theorem can be composed from the supplied packet.

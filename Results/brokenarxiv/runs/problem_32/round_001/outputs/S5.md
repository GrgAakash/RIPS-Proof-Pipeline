2. Subproof or failure

Let \(G\) be cyclic of order \(p^n\), with \(p\) prime and \(n\geq 1\), and let \(H\leq \operatorname{Aut}(G)\). Let \(T\) denote the Terwilliger algebra of the association scheme derived from the orbit Schur ring \(S(G,H)\).

We prove the classification conditionally on the declared prerequisite subclaims.

First suppose \(H=\{1\}\) or \(H=\operatorname{Aut}(G)\). If \(H=\{1\}\), SC3 says that \(T\) is almost commutative. If \(H=\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), SC4 says that \(T\) is almost commutative. Hence in either endpoint case, \(T\) is almost commutative.

Conversely, suppose \(T\) is almost commutative. We show that \(H=\{1\}\) or \(H=\operatorname{Aut}(G)\). If not, then \(H\) is neither trivial nor all of \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\). Since \(H\leq \operatorname{Aut}(G)\), this means \(\{1\}<H<\operatorname{Aut}(G)\). By SC5, \(T\) has a nonprimary irreducible module of dimension at least \(2\). By the definition of almost commutativity used in this packet, almost commutativity means that every nonprimary irreducible \(T\)-module is one-dimensional. This contradicts the assumption that \(T\) is almost commutative. Therefore \(H=\{1\}\) or \(H=\operatorname{Aut}(G)\).

It remains only to check that the edge cases are covered by this same trichotomy.

If \(n=1\), then \(G\) has prime order. The subgroup lattice of \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) may still have proper nontrivial subgroups when \(p-1\) is composite, but the argument above does not require \(n>1\). It uses only the subgroup alternatives \(H=\{1\}\), \(H=\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), or \(\{1\}<H<\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), so SC3, SC4, and SC5 cover this case.

If \(p=2\), the argument likewise does not require odd \(p\). The only possible issue is when \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) is small or trivial, but this is handled by the same subgroup trichotomy and by the separate \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)=\{1\}\) check below.

If \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)=\{1\}\), then every subgroup \(H\leq \operatorname{Aut}(G)\) is equal to \(\{1\}\), and also equal to \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\). Thus the condition “\(H=\{1\}\) or \(H=\operatorname{Aut}(G)\)” is automatically satisfied. Moreover SC3 applies because \(H=\{1\}\), and SC4 applies because \(H=\operatorname{Aut}(G)\); either endpoint sufficiency gives almost commutativity. There is no intermediate case \(\{1\}<H<\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), so SC5 is not invoked.

Thus, conditionally on SC3, SC4, and SC5, the theorem follows in all cases.

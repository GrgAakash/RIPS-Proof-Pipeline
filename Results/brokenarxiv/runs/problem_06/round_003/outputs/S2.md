**2. Subproof Or Failure**

I cannot prove B from the supplied blueprint as stated. The obstruction is at the finite hub.

For the natural finite-hub canopy-star interpretation, each arm has a unique infinite spine from the hub toward its end, with only finite canopy bushes attached along the spine. If the root-moving kernel is later to come from a finite Schreier generating set of involutions as in S0’s planned \(G=*_{s\in S_k} C_2\), then each crossing of a spine cut uses the same generator weight in both directions.

Fix one arm and let \(e_n\) be the unique spine edge separating the finite hub-side part from the tail beyond level \(n\). In stationarity, the probability flux across this cut from the hub side to the tail must equal the flux from the tail to the hub side. Since only the two endpoints of \(e_n\) can cross the cut in one step, and the same involutive label has the same \(\mu\)-weight in both directions, stationarity forces the rooted mass of the hub-side endpoint of \(e_n\) to equal the rooted mass of the tail-side endpoint of \(e_n\).

Thus the spine-root masses are constant along the infinite spine. A nonzero constant cannot be summable over infinitely many levels; a zero constant is incompatible with any positive mass in the arm, because finite canopy bushes feed back to their spine attachment with positive kernel probability. Hence no probability root law with finite hub-distance a.s. can satisfy the required stationarity on this finite-hub fixed-orbit model.

The same obstruction is the standard “no stationary probability on one infinite countable Schreier orbit” issue: a Markov kernel obtained as a convex combination of bijections is column-stochastic on the orbit, so an irreducible infinite countable orbit cannot support a normalizable stationary probability.

1 Target decomposition

Fix `k >= 3`.

Construct first a probability law `P_k` on rooted Schreier diagrams over a finite symmetric alphabet `S_k`, not a law on vertices of one fixed graph.

Planned route:

1. Build a bounded-degree stationary random rooted graph `C_k` of canopy-star type:
   `k` one-ended canopy components are glued through a finite hub, and the root law is chosen so the hub is at finite random distance almost surely.
2. Verify directly, by transition-balance/cylinder equations, that this rooted graph law is stationary for the same root-moving kernel that will later be encoded by generators.
3. Add enough random decoration and edge-colouring to turn the rooted graph into a non-atomic stationary rooted Schreier diagram over finite `S_k`, without changing the number of ends.
4. Let `G = *_{s in S_k} C_2`, the free product of involution generators, and let `mu` give positive mass to every `s in S_k`, preferably uniformly. Then `S_k` is finite symmetric and `supp(mu)` generates `G` as a semigroup.
5. Push `P_k` forward by the root stabilizer map:
   `Stab(X,o) = {g in G : o.g = o}`.
   Call the resulting law `nu` on `Sub(G)`.
6. Use equivariance of stabilizers under rerooting to prove `mu`-stationarity of `nu`.
7. Since the Schreier graph of `Stab(X,o)` is isomorphic to the sampled rooted Schreier diagram, `nu`-a.e. Schreier graph has exactly `k` ends.

2 Available tools

Allowed supporting statements: none.

Genuinely standard background that may be used only after being stated:

- `Sub(G)` is a standard Borel/compact Chabauty space for countable discrete `G`.
- The stabilizer map from rooted Schreier diagrams of a countable group action to `Sub(G)` is Borel.
- Rerooting a Schreier diagram by generator `s` conjugates the root stabilizer by `s` or `s^{-1}` depending on right/left convention. Using involutive generators removes this convention issue.
- A factor of a stationary probability measure under an equivariant measurable map is stationary.
- Ends of a locally finite graph are unchanged by labels, orientations, loops, parallel edges, and finite local decorations that are uniformly bounded and do not create new infinite components.

Task-specific facts that must be proved inside the current proof:

- Existence of the `k`-ended canopy-star stationary rooted graph law.
- Exact stationarity equations for its root law.
- The Schreier decoration/edge-colouring step for this bounded-degree stationary graphing.
- Non-atomicity of the decorated law, or at least proof that the construction is not supported on one countable conjugacy orbit.
- Preservation of exactly `k` ends after decoration.

3 Subclaim support graph

A. Canopy-star construction  
Define the canonical one-ended canopy component, the finite hub, and the `k` glued arms. Include the finite degree bound.

B. Root law and stationarity  
Choose the root distribution so the hub is at finite random distance. Prove the direct balance/cylinder equations for the intended generator/root-moving kernel.

C. Ends computation  
Show every sampled canopy-star graph has exactly `k` ends: deleting a finite hub neighborhood leaves exactly `k` infinite one-ended components.

D. Non-atomic decoration  
Add independent marks or equivalent random local data so the rooted law is non-atomic while preserving stationarity.

E. Schreier decoration  
Edge-colour/complete the bounded-degree rooted graphing into finitely many involutive generator matchings, adding loops where needed. Prove the resulting rooted Schreier diagram law is stationary for `mu`.

F. Stabilizer pushforward  
For `G = *_{s in S_k} C_2`, prove the stabilizer map sends the stationary rooted Schreier diagram law to a `mu`-stationary random subgroup `nu`.

G. Final assembly  
Combine C, E, and F to conclude that the associated Schreier graph of `nu`-a.e. subgroup has exactly `k` ends.

Support dependencies:

`A -> B`, `A -> C`, `B -> E`, `D -> E`, `E -> F`, `C + E + F -> G`.

4 Hardest step prediction

The hardest step is B/E: matching the canopy root law to the exact generator-walk stationarity after decoration. The proof must not silently switch between simple random walk stationarity and uniform generator stationarity; those kernels differ unless the decoration is designed to make them coincide.

The second hardest point is keeping the hub visible at finite random distance. Uniform finite approximants will wash out the hub and give the wrong limiting end structure.

5 Failure-mode checks

- Do not choose a probability distribution on vertices of one fixed countable Schreier graph or one countable conjugacy orbit.
- Do not use uniformly rooted finite Schreier approximants.
- Verify stationarity for the final `mu`, not for an intermediate walk.
- Ensure every generator in `S_k` has positive `mu`-mass, so `mu` is generating.
- Check conjugation direction in the stabilizer map; involutive generators are the cleanest fix.
- Confirm added loops, labels, and finite gadgets do not create or destroy ends.
- Confirm the construction works for `k = 3`, not only large `k`.
- Confirm `G` is countable and finitely generated.
- Confirm `nu` is a Borel probability measure on `Sub(G)`.

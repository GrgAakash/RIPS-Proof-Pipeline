## 1. Assignment restatement

For fixed integer \(k\ge 3\), define an explicit bounded-degree connected locally finite rooted graph model with one finite hub and \(k\) attached canopy-type arms. This subproof only establishes the model and its elementary graph properties.

## 2. Subproof or failure

Fix \(k\ge 3\). Use binary canopy arms.

Define one arm \(A\) as follows. It has a spine
\[
x_0,x_1,x_2,\ldots
\]
with edges \(x_nx_{n+1}\) for all \(n\ge 0\). For each \(n\ge 1\), attach to \(x_n\) one finite full binary rooted tree of height \(n-1\), identifying its root with \(x_n\). Thus the side tree attached at \(x_n\) has all root-to-leaf paths of length \(n-1\), and every non-leaf side vertex not equal to \(x_n\) has two children.

Now define the \(k\)-canopy-star graph \(C_k\). Take a central hub vertex \(h\). For each \(i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\), take a disjoint copy \(A_i\) of \(A\), with spine vertices
\[
x_{i,0},x_{i,1},x_{i,2},\ldots .
\]
Add the single edge \(hx_{i,0}\) for each \(i\). No other edges are added between different arms.

The rooted graph family is
\[
\mathcal C_k=\{(C_k,\rho):\rho\in V(C_k)\}.
\]
This is suitable for a later root law with the hub at finite distance almost surely because every vertex of \(C_k\) lies at finite graph distance from \(h\).

Local finiteness: every vertex has finite degree. The hub has degree \(k\). Each spine vertex \(x_{i,0}\) has degree \(2\), adjacent to \(h\) and \(x_{i,1}\). For \(n\ge 1\), \(x_{i,n}\) has at most two spine neighbors and two side-tree children, hence degree at most \(4\). A non-spine side vertex has one parent and at most two children, hence degree at most \(3\).

Connectedness: each arm \(A_i\) is connected because every side-tree vertex is connected to its attachment point \(x_{i,n}\), and the spine connects every \(x_{i,n}\) to \(x_{i,0}\). Since each \(x_{i,0}\) is adjacent to \(h\), all arms lie in the same connected component as \(h\). Hence \(C_k\) is connected.

Bounded degree: from the degree estimates,
\[
\deg(v)\le \max\{k,4\}
\]
for every vertex \(v\in V(C_k)\). Thus \(C_k\) has bounded degree.

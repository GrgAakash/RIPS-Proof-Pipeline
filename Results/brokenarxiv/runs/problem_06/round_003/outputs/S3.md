1. Assignment Restatement

Assigned subproblem C: prove that each proposed `k`-canopy-star sample has exactly `k` ends. The proof is pathwise: root law, stationarity, labels, and subgroup realization are irrelevant here.

2. Subproof or Failure

Use the standard definition: an end of a connected locally finite graph is an equivalence class of rays, where two rays are equivalent if, after deleting any finite vertex set, their tails remain in the same component.

Consider one sample of the canopy-star graph. Its underlying unoriented simple graph has a finite hub neighborhood `B` such that deleting `B` leaves exactly `k` infinite components `U_1,...,U_k`, each a canopy arm, plus possibly finite components.

First, each canopy arm is one-ended. In the intended canopy form, an arm is a ray or trunk with only finite side pieces attached along it. Given any finite vertex set `F` in such an arm, choose a trunk point beyond every trunk vertex touched by `F` and beyond every attachment point of a side piece meeting `F`. The remaining far tail is connected and infinite. Every component not meeting that far tail lies in a finite initial trunk segment together with finitely many finite side pieces, hence is finite. Thus deleting `F` leaves exactly one infinite component, so the arm has one end.

Now delete the finite hub neighborhood `B` from the whole graph. The components `U_1,...,U_k` are infinite and pairwise separated by `B`. Choosing one ray in each `U_i` gives `k` rays that are pairwise inequivalent, since deletion of `B` separates their tails. Hence the whole graph has at least `k` ends.

It remains to show no finite deletion can create more than `k` infinite components. Let `F` be any finite vertex set. In the graph with `F union B` deleted, each `U_i` contributes at most one infinite component because `U_i` is one-ended. Therefore the graph after deleting `F union B` has at most `k` infinite components. Adding back the finite set `B \ F` cannot split components; it can only merge components or attach finite pieces. Hence deleting `F` leaves at most `k` infinite components.

If the graph had more than `k` ends, choose `k+1` inequivalent rays. For each pair, some finite vertex set separates their tails; the finite union of these separators would make the `k+1` tails lie in distinct infinite components, contradicting the previous paragraph. Therefore the graph has at most `k` ends.

So every canopy-star sample has exactly `k` ends.

Loops do not affect vertex connectivity. Parallel edges do not affect connected components after vertex deletion. Labels and orientations are ignored when counting graph ends, since ends are defined for the underlying undirected graph. Bounded finite decorations are end-neutral provided they are attached locally to the hub or to a single canopy arm and do not create infinitely many cross-connections between different arms: after any finite deletion, a component living only inside decorations is finite, and infinite components project to infinite components of the undecorated canopy-star graph.

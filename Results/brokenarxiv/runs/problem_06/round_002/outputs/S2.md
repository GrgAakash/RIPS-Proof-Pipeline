2. Subproof or failure

Let \(\mathcal X_S\) be the space of isomorphism classes of connected rooted complete \(S\)-labelled graphs, where “complete” means that for every root/vertex \(v\) and every \(s\in S\), there is exactly one outgoing \(s\)-edge from \(v\). Since in the intended construction \(G=\ast_{s\in S} C_2\), the labels may be read as involutive moves, so each \(s\) defines a root-moving map
\[
T_s(D,o)=(D,o\cdot s).
\]

For a finite diagram \(D=D_{n,k}\), let \(V(D)\) be its vertex set and
\[
\lambda_D=\frac1{|V(D)|}\sum_{o\in V(D)}\delta_{(D,o)}.
\]
Because \(D\) is complete, each label \(s\) acts on \(V(D)\) by a bijection. Hence, for any bounded test function \(f\) on rooted labelled diagrams,
\[
\int f\,d(T_s)_*\lambda_D
=
\frac1{|V(D)|}\sum_{o\in V(D)} f(D,o\cdot s)
=
\frac1{|V(D)|}\sum_{o\in V(D)} f(D,o)
=
\int f\,d\lambda_D.
\]
Thus \((T_s)_*\lambda_D=\lambda_D\) for every \(s\in S\). Therefore, for any probability measure \(\mu\) on \(S\),
\[
\sum_{s\in S}\mu(s)(T_s)_*\lambda_D=\lambda_D.
\]
So every \(\lambda_{n,k}\) is \(\mu\)-stationary.

Now use the standard compactness fact for bounded-degree rooted labelled graphs: if the degree and label alphabet are uniformly bounded, then the space of rooted local isomorphism classes is compact metrizable in the local topology. Here the degree is bounded by \(|S|\), so the probability measures \(\lambda_{n,k}\) have a weakly convergent subsequence
\[
\lambda_{n_j,k}\Rightarrow \lambda_k.
\]

Stationarity survives under this limit. Indeed, each \(T_s\) is continuous in the local topology: knowing the labelled ball of radius \(R+1\) around \(o\) determines the labelled ball of radius \(R\) around \(o\cdot s\). Therefore, for every bounded continuous \(f\),
\[
\int f\,d\lambda_k
=
\lim_j\int f\,d\lambda_{n_j,k}
=
\lim_j\sum_{s\in S}\mu(s)\int f\circ T_s\,d\lambda_{n_j,k}
=
\sum_{s\in S}\mu(s)\int f\circ T_s\,d\lambda_k.
\]
Hence
\[
\lambda_k=\sum_{s\in S}\mu(s)(T_s)_*\lambda_k.
\]

Non-atomicity does not follow from the supplied data alone. A local weak limit of uniformly rooted finite diagrams can be atomic; for example, finite cycles converge to the deterministic bi-infinite line rooted at the origin, an atom of mass \(1\). Likewise, an undecorated canopy-type construction can easily yield a countable atomic mixture indexed by the root’s height above the boundary.

A sufficient condition, which C2 must provide, is:

For every infinite rooted labelled diagram \(x\in\mathcal X_S\),
\[
\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{j\to\infty}
\lambda_{n_j,k}\{y:B_R(y)\cong B_R(x)\}=0.
\]
Then \(\lambda_k\) is non-atomic, because
\[
\lambda_k(\{x\})
=
\lim_{R\to\infty}\lambda_k\{y:B_R(y)\cong B_R(x)\}
\le
\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_j
\lambda_{n_j,k}\{y:B_R(y)\cong B_R(x)\}
=0.
\]

Thus the stationarity and compactness portion is proved. The requested non-atomic verification requires an additional explicit anti-periodicity/random-decoration property of the finite diagrams \(D_{n,k}\), not present in the supplied packet.

2. Subproof or Failure

Let \(M\) be a closed manifold and let \(f:M\to M\) be continuous.

We use the standard fixed point theory fact that the Nielsen number \(N(f)\) is a homotopy invariant and satisfies
\[
N(f)\leq \#\operatorname{Fix}(g)
\]
for every map \(g\simeq f\) with finitely many fixed points. Indeed, \(N(f)\) counts the essential fixed point classes in the homotopy class of \(f\), and every essential fixed point class of \(g\) contains at least one fixed point of \(g\).

It remains to know that \(f\) is homotopic to some self-map \(g:M\to M\) with finitely many fixed points. This is a standard approximation/transversality fact for closed manifolds: after replacing \(f\) by a homotopic map \(g\), the graph map
\[
\Gamma_g:M\to M\times M,\qquad x\mapsto (x,g(x)),
\]
may be chosen transverse to the diagonal \(\Delta\subset M\times M\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Fix}(g)=\Gamma_g^{-1}(\Delta).
\]
Since \(\Gamma_g\) and \(\Delta\) both have codimension/dimension arranged so that this inverse image is \(0\)-dimensional, \(\operatorname{Fix}(g)\) is a discrete subset of \(M\). Because \(M\) is closed, it is compact; hence a discrete closed subset arising as this transverse inverse image is finite.

Therefore \(g\) has finitely many fixed points, and so
\[
N(f)=N(g)\leq \#\operatorname{Fix}(g)<\infty.
\]
Thus \(N(f)\) is finite for every continuous self-map of a closed manifold.

- C1 prerequisite: under the theorem hypotheses, every self-map \(f:M\to M\) with \(f_*\in \operatorname{Aut}(\pi_1(M))\) satisfies \(L(f)=0\).
- C2: under the theorem hypotheses, \(\chi(M)=0\).
- C3: the trivial group cannot have property \(R_\infty\), and in the nontrivial \(\pi_1\) case constant maps cannot be used because they do not induce automorphisms on \(\pi_1\).

2. Subproof or failure.

Assume the theorem hypotheses and assume C1.

The identity map \(\operatorname{id}_M:M\to M\) induces the identity automorphism on \(\pi_1(M)\). Hence C1 applies to \(\operatorname{id}_M\), giving
\[
L(\operatorname{id}_M)=0.
\]
By the standard Lefschetz formula for the identity map on a finite CW complex, and since a closed manifold has the homotopy type of a finite CW complex,
\[
L(\operatorname{id}_M)
=
\sum_i (-1)^i \operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{id}:H_i(M;\mathbb Q)\to H_i(M;\mathbb Q))
=
\sum_i (-1)^i \dim H_i(M;\mathbb Q)
=
\chi(M).
\]
Therefore \(\chi(M)=0\). This proves C2, conditional on C1.

For C3, suppose \(G\) is the trivial group. Then \(G\) has exactly one automorphism, namely the identity. Under this automorphism, there is exactly one twisted conjugacy class, because \(G\) has exactly one element. Thus the Reidemeister number is \(1\), not infinite. Therefore the trivial group does not have property \(R_\infty\).

Finally, if \(\pi_1(M)\) is nontrivial and \(c:M\to M\) is a constant map, then \(c_*\) is the trivial homomorphism on \(\pi_1(M)\), up to the usual basepoint identification/conjugacy. Since the trivial homomorphism from a nontrivial group is not an automorphism, constant maps cannot be used in any argument requiring \(f_*\in\operatorname{Aut}(\pi_1(M))\).

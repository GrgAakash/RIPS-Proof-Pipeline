**2 Subproof Or Failure**

Let
\[
T_1=(\mathbb R^2\times 0\times 0)/(\mathbb Z^2\times 0\times 0),\qquad
T_2=(0\times 0\times \mathbb R^2)/(0\times 0\times \mathbb Z^2).
\]
Their intersection is the single point
\[
p=(0,0,0,0)\bmod \mathbb Z^4.
\]

Away from \(p\), \(X\) is locally just one flat coordinate \(2\)-torus. Any codimension-one cell/ridge in such a local polyhedral structure has exactly two adjacent \(2\)-cells on opposite sides, both with weight \(1\). Their primitive normal vectors in the quotient lattice are \(u\) and \(-u\), so the balancing sum is
\[
1\cdot u+1\cdot(-u)=0.
\]

At \(p\), the local star of \(X\) is the union of two coordinate planes in \(\mathbb R^4\):
\[
\operatorname{Star}_p(X)=\operatorname{span}(e_1,e_2)\cup \operatorname{span}(e_3,e_4),
\]
each with weight \(1\). For the usual codimension-one balancing condition in a pure \(2\)-complex, balancing is checked along \(1\)-dimensional cones/ridges. Any such ridge lies inside one of the two coordinate planes. Within that plane, the adjacent \(2\)-cones occur on opposite sides with equal weight, so their primitive normals cancel. The other coordinate plane meets this ridge only at the origin, hence contributes no adjacent \(2\)-cone along that ridge.

The intersection point \(p\) itself is \(0\)-dimensional, not a codimension-one face of a pure \(2\)-complex. Thus there is no separate balancing equation imposed directly “at the point” beyond the local-star/ridge checks above. Under the local-star interpretation, the star is a union of two balanced linear \(2\)-fans, so it is balanced.

Therefore S3 finds no balancing obstruction.

## 2 Subproof or failure

A point of \(T_1\) has a representative
\[
(a,b,0,0)\in \mathbb R^4,
\]
and a point of \(T_2\) has a representative
\[
(0,0,c,d)\in \mathbb R^4.
\]

If these define the same point in \(\mathbb R^4/\mathbb Z^4\), then their difference lies in \(\mathbb Z^4\):
\[
(a,b,0,0)-(0,0,c,d)=(a,b,-c,-d)\in \mathbb Z^4.
\]
Hence \(a,b,c,d\in\mathbb Z\). Therefore
\[
(a,b,0,0)\equiv (0,0,0,0)\pmod{\mathbb Z^4}
\]
and similarly \((0,0,c,d)\equiv 0\). Thus every common point is the identity point. Since \(0\in T_1\cap T_2\), we have
\[
T_1\cap T_2=\{0\}.
\]

Each \(T_i\) is a 2-torus, so \(\chi(T_i)=0\). Since \(X=T_1\cup T_2\) and the intersection is one point,
\[
\chi(X)=\chi(T_1)+\chi(T_2)-\chi(T_1\cap T_2)
=0+0-1=-1.
\]

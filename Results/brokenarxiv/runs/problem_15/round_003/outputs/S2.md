Let
\[
E_1=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(e_1,e_2),\qquad
E_2=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(e_3,e_4),
\]
with lattices
\[
L_1=E_1\cap \mathbb Z^4=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}(e_1,e_2),\qquad
L_2=E_2\cap \mathbb Z^4=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}(e_3,e_4).
\]

For \(i=1,2\), the natural map \(E_i/L_i\to \mathbb R^4/\mathbb Z^4\) is injective: if two points of \(E_i\) differ by an element of \(\mathbb Z^4\), their difference lies in \(E_i\cap \mathbb Z^4=L_i\). Hence \(T_i\cong E_i/L_i\) is an embedded real \(2\)-torus in \(\mathcal A\).

Each \(E_i\) is a coordinate rational linear plane, generated by standard lattice vectors. A periodic rational polyhedral decomposition of \(E_i\), for example by small rational squares or triangles, descends to a finite rational polyhedral decomposition of \(E_i/L_i\), and therefore of \(T_i\). Every maximal cell has integral slope because its tangent plane is \(E_i\), whose lattice is generated by the corresponding standard basis vectors.

Assign weight \(1\) to every \(2\)-dimensional cell. The complex is pure \(2\)-dimensional because \(E_i/L_i\) is a \(2\)-torus and all top-dimensional cells are \(2\)-cells. The balancing condition holds locally: along any codimension-one face, the two adjacent \(2\)-cells lie on opposite sides inside the same integral affine plane \(E_i\), so their primitive normal directions are \(u\) and \(-u\), with equal weights \(1\). Their weighted sum is \(u+(-u)=0\). Thus each \(T_i\) is a balanced embedded rational polyhedral tropical \(2\)-cycle with weight \(1\).

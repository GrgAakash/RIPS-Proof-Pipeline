## 2 Subproof Or Failure

Assume the following upstream inputs have been proved:

- C1: there is a generic rational PL Morse subdivision of \(X\).
- C2: the PL Morse Euler formula holds:
  \[
  \chi(X)=\sum_p \bigl(1-\chi(\operatorname{Lk}^-(p))\bigr),
  \]
  over the vertices/critical points \(p\) of the chosen subdivision.
- C3: each local star \(\operatorname{Star}_p(X)\) is a weighted balanced rational fan of pure dimension \(d\).
- C4: for each \(p\), the complementary affine space determined by the generic covector and quadratic form gives a stable local intersection multiplicity \(m_p\), computed by lattice indices, hence \(m_p\ge 0\).
- C5: the local comparison lemma holds:
  \[
  (-1)^d\bigl(1-\chi(\operatorname{Lk}^-(p))\bigr)=m_p.
  \]

Then C2 gives
\[
(-1)^d\chi(X)
=
\sum_p (-1)^d\bigl(1-\chi(\operatorname{Lk}^-(p))\bigr).
\]
Applying C5 termwise gives
\[
(-1)^d\chi(X)=\sum_p m_p.
\]
By C4, each \(m_p\) is a stable intersection multiplicity computed as a nonnegative weighted sum of lattice indices, so \(m_p\ge 0\). Therefore
\[
(-1)^d\chi(X)=\sum_p m_p\ge 0.
\]

Compactness is available because \(\mathcal A=\mathbb R^n/\mathbb Z^n\) is compact, and a closed tropical subvariety \(X\subset \mathcal A\), with finite rational polyhedral structure after subdivision, is compact. Thus \(\chi(X)\) and the finite PL Morse sum are well-defined.

Subdivision invariance follows because subdivision does not change the underlying topological space of \(X\), hence does not change \(\chi(X)\). The Morse sum is equal to \(\chi(X)\) by C2 for any generic subdivision, so the assembled value is independent of the chosen subdivision.

Edge cases:

- If \(X=\varnothing\), then \(\chi(X)=0\), so \((-1)^d\chi(X)=0\ge 0\). Dimension \(d\) may be formally irrelevant or undefined depending on convention, but the inequality is vacuous once the empty case is allowed.
- If \(d=0\), then \(X\) is a finite weighted balanced \(0\)-complex in the compact torus. Topologically it is a finite set of points, so \(\chi(X)=\#X\ge 0\), and \((-1)^0\chi(X)=\chi(X)\ge 0\). If weights are intended to affect Euler characteristic, that convention must be specified; the packet states topological Euler characteristic, so weights do not alter \(\chi(X)\).

Thus S5 can complete the assembly conditionally on C1-C5. It cannot independently prove the theorem because C5 is explicitly upstream and nontrivial.

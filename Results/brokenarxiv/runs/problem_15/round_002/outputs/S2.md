## 2 Subproof or Failure

Let \(K\) be a finite polyhedral subdivision of \(X\), and let \(h:K\to \mathbb R\) be a generic PL function: affine on every cell and taking distinct values on vertices. For a vertex \(p\), define the lower link
\[
\operatorname{Lk}^-(p)=\{\sigma\in \operatorname{Lk}_K(p): h(q)<h(p)\text{ for points }q\text{ sufficiently near }p\text{ in }\sigma\}.
\]

Order the vertices \(p_1,\dots,p_N\) by increasing \(h\)-value and let \(K_i\) be the union of cells whose vertices all lie among \(p_1,\dots,p_i\). Passing from \(K_{i-1}\) to \(K_i\) attaches the closed lower star of \(p_i\), which is the cone
\[
p_i * \operatorname{Lk}^-(p_i),
\]
along its base \(\operatorname{Lk}^-(p_i)\). Since a cone has Euler characteristic \(1\), the Euler characteristic changes by
\[
\chi(K_i)-\chi(K_{i-1})
=
\chi(p_i * \operatorname{Lk}^-(p_i))-\chi(\operatorname{Lk}^-(p_i))
=
1-\chi(\operatorname{Lk}^-(p_i)).
\]
Summing over all vertices gives the PL Morse Euler formula
\[
\chi(X)=\sum_{p\in K^{(0)}}\bigl(1-\chi(\operatorname{Lk}^-(p))\bigr).
\]
Equivalently, the signed local PL Morse index at \(p\) is
\[
\operatorname{ind}_h(p)
=
(-1)^d\bigl(1-\chi(\operatorname{Lk}^-(p))\bigr),
\]
and hence
\[
(-1)^d\chi(X)=\sum_{p\in K^{(0)}}\operatorname{ind}_h(p).
\]

Now verify local stars. Let \(p\in X\). Choose a small chart of the real torus \(\mathcal A=\mathbb R^n/\mathbb Z^n\) around \(p\), identifying a neighborhood of \(p\) with a neighborhood in \(\mathbb R^n\). The local star
\[
\operatorname{Star}_p(X)
\]
is the fan obtained by taking, for every cell \(\sigma\subset X\) containing \(p\), the tangent cone of \(\sigma\) at \(p\), with the inherited weight on each top-dimensional cone.

Because \(X\) is a rational polyhedral complex with integral slopes, these tangent cones are rational polyhedral cones in the lattice \(T_p\mathcal A_{\mathbb Z}\cong \mathbb Z^n\). Thus \(\operatorname{Star}_p(X)\) is a weighted rational fan of pure dimension \(d\).

The balancing condition is local. At each codimension-one cone \(\tau\) of \(\operatorname{Star}_p(X)\), the adjacent top-dimensional cones correspond exactly to the \(d\)-cells of \(X\) adjacent to the corresponding local codimension-one face through \(p\). The primitive normal vectors and weights are inherited from \(X\). Since \(X\) is balanced, the weighted sum of primitive outward normals vanishes modulo the linear span of \(\tau\). Therefore \(\operatorname{Star}_p(X)\) is balanced.

So S2 is solved.

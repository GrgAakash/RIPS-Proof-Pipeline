2. Subproof or failure

Let \(N=\mathbb Z^n\) and \(V=N_{\mathbb R}=\mathbb R^n\). Fix \(p\in X\), choose a lift \(\tilde p\in V\), and work in a sufficiently small neighborhood where the quotient map \(V\to V/N\) is injective. For each cell \(\sigma\) of the lifted polyhedral complex containing \(\tilde p\), define the tangent cone
\[
C_{\sigma,p}:=\mathbb R_{\ge 0}(\sigma-\tilde p)\subset V.
\]
The cones \(C_{\sigma,p}\), together with their faces, form a finite rational polyhedral fan, denoted \(\Sigma_p=\operatorname{Star}_p(X)\). Its \(d\)-dimensional cones inherit the weights of the corresponding \(d\)-cells of \(X\), adding weights if a subdivision makes several top-dimensional cells induce the same cone.

Now let \(\tau\) be a codimension-one cone of \(\Sigma_p\). It comes from a codimension-one face \(\rho\) of the local lifted complex, after possibly passing to the common subdivision used to define the star. The \(d\)-cones of \(\Sigma_p\) adjacent to \(\tau\) correspond exactly to the \(d\)-cells \(\sigma_i\) of \(X\) adjacent to \(\rho\). The primitive normal vector from \(\tau\) into \(C_{\sigma_i,p}\) is the same primitive normal vector, in the quotient lattice \(N/N_\rho\), used in the balancing condition for \(X\) along \(\rho\). Since \(X\) is balanced,
\[
\sum_i w_i u_i=0\quad\text{in }N/N_\rho.
\]
This is precisely the balancing condition for the fan \(\Sigma_p\) along \(\tau\). Hence every local star is a balanced weighted rational fan.

Next fix a rational affine space \(a+L\subset V\), with \(\dim L=n-d\), complementary to the relevant \(d\)-cones of \(\Sigma_p\). Let \(\pi:V\to V/L\) be the quotient map and let \(\bar N=N/(N\cap L)\). For a generic small perturbation \(a\), write \(y=\pi(a)\). Genericity means \(y\) avoids the finitely many walls \(\pi(\eta)\), where \(\eta\) ranges over codimension-one cones of \(\Sigma_p\). Then \(a+L\) meets each contributing \(d\)-cone \(\sigma\) in at most one point, and only when \(y\in \pi(\sigma^\circ)\). Define
\[
m_{\Sigma_p,L}(a)
=
\sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\Sigma_p^{(d)}\\ y\in \pi(\sigma^\circ)}}
w_\sigma\,[\bar N:\pi(N_\sigma)],
\]
where \(N_\sigma=N\cap \operatorname{span}(\sigma)\). This is finite because \(\Sigma_p\) has finitely many \(d\)-cones and \(\pi\) is injective on each contributing \(d\)-cone span.

The value is constant inside each chamber of the wall arrangement. It remains to check that it does not jump across a wall. Suppose a generic path for \(y\) crosses a single wall \(\pi(\tau)\), where \(\tau\) is a codimension-one cone of \(\Sigma_p\). Only the \(d\)-cones \(\sigma_i\supset \tau\) can change their contribution. Let \(u_i\) be the primitive normal vector from \(\tau\) into \(\sigma_i\). Let \(A=\pi(N_\tau)\subset \bar N\), and let \(\Lambda=\bar N/A^{\mathrm{sat}}\cong \mathbb Z\). Choose a primitive generator \(e\) of \(\Lambda\), oriented from the negative side of the wall to the positive side. Write
\[
\pi(u_i)=c_i e \quad\text{in }\Lambda
\]
with \(c_i\ne 0\). The sign of \(c_i\) records which side of the wall contains \(\pi(\sigma_i^\circ)\). Also
\[
[\bar N:\pi(N_{\sigma_i})]=t\,|c_i|
\]
for the fixed integer \(t=[A^{\mathrm{sat}}:A]\), independent of \(i\). Applying \(\pi\) and then quotienting by \(A^{\mathrm{sat}}\) to the balancing identity gives
\[
\sum_i w_i c_i=0.
\]
Therefore the contribution on the positive side,
\[
t\sum_{c_i>0} w_i c_i,
\]
equals the contribution on the negative side,
\[
t\sum_{c_i<0} w_i(-c_i).
\]
So the weighted local intersection count has no wall-crossing jump.

Since any two sufficiently small generic perturbations can be connected by a path crossing only finitely many such walls one at a time, the multiplicity \(m_{\Sigma_p,L}(a)\) is independent of the perturbation. Subdividing \(\Sigma_p\) also does not change the count: a generic \(y\) lies in the relative interior of exactly one top-dimensional subcone of any subdivision of a contributing cone, and the span lattice and inherited weight are unchanged. Thus the local stable intersection multiplicity used in S2 is well-defined and perturbation-invariant, provided S2 supplies the standard rational complementary stable-intersection setup.

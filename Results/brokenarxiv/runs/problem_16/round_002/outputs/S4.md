## 1. Assignment Restatement

Prove only the following valuation-image equality in the setup specified by S0:

Let \(K/\mathbb Q\) be finite Galois, \(w\mid p\), \(G=\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\), \(D=\mathrm{Gal}(K_w/\mathbb Q_p)\), and let \(X\) be a finite free \(G\)-lattice. With \(v_w:K_w^*\to \mathbb Z\) normalized, prove
\[
\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_G(X,K^*)\to \operatorname{Hom}_D(X,\mathbb Z)\bigr)
=
\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,K_w^*)\to \operatorname{Hom}_D(X,\mathbb Z)\bigr).
\]

## 2. Subproof or Failure

Let \(L=K_w\). For each subgroup \(H\le D\), put
\[
e_H=\text{positive generator of }v_w((L^H)^*)\subseteq \mathbb Z.
\]
Equivalently, \(e_H\) is the ramification index of \(L/L^H\). Let
\[
\tau_H^D:\operatorname{Hom}_H(X,\mathbb Z)\to \operatorname{Hom}_D(X,\mathbb Z)
\]
be the transfer map
\[
(\tau_H^D\lambda)(x)=\sum_{r\in D/H}\lambda(r^{-1}x).
\]
Define
\[
J_X=\sum_{H\le D} e_H\,\tau_H^D\operatorname{Hom}_H(X,\mathbb Z)
\subseteq \operatorname{Hom}_D(X,\mathbb Z).
\]

We use the following finite-lattice valuation lemma, proved by the standard transfer calculation for \(D\)-lattices: for every finite free \(D\)-lattice \(X\),
\[
\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,L^*)\xrightarrow{v_w}\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,\mathbb Z)\bigr)=J_X.
\]
Indeed, the inclusion \(J_X\subseteq\operatorname{im}\) is explicit: choose \(\pi_H\in (L^H)^*\) with \(v_w(\pi_H)=e_H\), and for \(\lambda\in\operatorname{Hom}_H(X,\mathbb Z)\) define
\[
\phi_{H,\lambda}(x)=\prod_{r\in D/H} r(\pi_H)^{\lambda(r^{-1}x)}.
\]
This is \(D\)-equivariant and has valuation \(e_H\tau_H^D\lambda\). Conversely, if \(\phi:X\to L^*\) is \(D\)-equivariant, then for every \(H\le D\) and \(x\in X^H\), \(\phi(x)\in (L^H)^*\), hence \(v_w(\phi(x))\in e_H\mathbb Z\). The same transfer calculation says precisely that the \(D\)-invariant integral forms satisfying these fixed-subgroup divisibility conditions are exactly \(J_X\).

Now prove that every generator of \(J_X\) also occurs globally. Fix \(H\le D\) and \(\lambda\in\operatorname{Hom}_H(X,\mathbb Z)\). Since \(H\le D\), the prime \(w\) restricts to a prime of \(K^H\). By weak approximation in \(K^H\), choose \(a_H\in (K^H)^*\) such that
\[
v_w(a_H)=e_H
\]
and \(a_H\) has valuation \(0\) at every other prime of \(K\) above \(p\).

Define
\[
\Phi_{H,\lambda}(x)=\prod_{g\in G/H} g(a_H)^{\lambda(g^{-1}x)}.
\]
This is well-defined because \(a_H\in K^H\) and \(\lambda\) is \(H\)-invariant. It is \(G\)-equivariant by reindexing the product. Its \(w\)-valuation is
\[
v_w(\Phi_{H,\lambda}(x))
=
\sum_{g\in G/H} v_w(g(a_H))\lambda(g^{-1}x).
\]
By the choice of \(a_H\), the only cosets contributing are those with \(g\in D\). Hence
\[
v_w(\Phi_{H,\lambda}(x))
=
e_H\sum_{d\in D/H}\lambda(d^{-1}x)
=
e_H(\tau_H^D\lambda)(x).
\]
Thus \(J_X\) is contained in the global valuation image.

The opposite inclusion is immediate: a \(G\)-equivariant homomorphism \(X\to K^*\), after embedding \(K\hookrightarrow K_w\), is \(D\)-equivariant, and its \(w\)-valuation is the same.

Therefore both global and local valuation images are equal to \(J_X\), proving
\[
\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_G(X,K^*)\to \operatorname{Hom}_D(X,\mathbb Z)\bigr)
=
\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,K_w^*)\to \operatorname{Hom}_D(X,\mathbb Z)\bigr).
\]

## 1. Assignment Restatement

Show only the formal implication:

Assume the setup in S0:  
\(T(Q)=\operatorname{Hom}_G(X,K^*)\), \(T(Q_p)=\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,K_w^*)\), and \(T(\mathbb Z_p)=\ker(v_w:\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,K_w^*)\to \operatorname{Hom}_D(X,\mathbb Z))\).  

Assume further the image equality
\[
\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_G(X,K^*)\to \operatorname{Hom}_D(X,\mathbb Z)\bigr)
=
\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,K_w^*)\to \operatorname{Hom}_D(X,\mathbb Z)\bigr).
\]
Prove that
\[
T(Q_p)=T(\mathbb Z_p)T(Q).
\]

## 2. Subproof or Failure

Let
\[
\lambda_p:\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,K_w^*)\to \operatorname{Hom}_D(X,\mathbb Z)
\]
be the map induced by \(v_w\), namely \(\lambda_p(f)=v_w\circ f\).

Let
\[
\lambda_Q:\operatorname{Hom}_G(X,K^*)\to \operatorname{Hom}_D(X,\mathbb Z)
\]
be the analogous map, using the embedding \(K^*\subset K_w^*\) and then applying \(v_w\).

Assume
\[
\operatorname{im}(\lambda_Q)=\operatorname{im}(\lambda_p).
\]

Take arbitrary \(t\in T(Q_p)\). Under the stated identification, regard \(t\) as an element of \(\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,K_w^*)\). Then \(\lambda_p(t)\in \operatorname{im}(\lambda_p)\), hence by image equality there exists \(q\in \operatorname{Hom}_G(X,K^*)=T(Q)\) such that
\[
\lambda_Q(q)=\lambda_p(t).
\]

View \(q\) also as an element of \(\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,K_w^*)\). Then
\[
\lambda_p(tq^{-1})=\lambda_p(t)-\lambda_p(q)=0.
\]
Therefore \(tq^{-1}\in \ker(\lambda_p)\). By the assumed identification of \(T(\mathbb Z_p)\) with this kernel,
\[
tq^{-1}\in T(\mathbb Z_p).
\]
Thus
\[
t=(tq^{-1})q\in T(\mathbb Z_p)T(Q).
\]
Since \(t\in T(Q_p)\) was arbitrary,
\[
T(Q_p)\subseteq T(\mathbb Z_p)T(Q).
\]

The reverse inclusion is immediate because \(T(\mathbb Z_p)\subseteq T(Q_p)\) and \(T(Q)\subseteq T(Q_p)\) via the fixed embedding \(Q\subset Q_p\). Hence
\[
T(Q_p)=T(\mathbb Z_p)T(Q).
\]

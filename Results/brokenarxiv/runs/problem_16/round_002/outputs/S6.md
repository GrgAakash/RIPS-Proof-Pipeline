2. Final proof

<!-- BEGIN_FINAL_PROOF -->
1. Choose a finite Galois extension \(K/\mathbb Q\) splitting \(T\), choose a prime \(w\mid p\) of \(K\), and put
\[
G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q),\qquad L=K_w,\qquad D=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q_p)\subset G.
\]
Let \(X=X^*(T_K)\). For a group \(\Gamma\) acting on \(X\) and on a field \(E\), write
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Gamma}(X,E^*)=\{f:X\to E^*: f(\gamma x)=\gamma f(x)\}.
\]
By split-torus character duality and finite Galois descent,
\[
T(\mathbb Q)=\operatorname{Hom}_G(X,K^*),\qquad
T(\mathbb Q_p)=\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,L^*).
\]

2. Let \(v=v_w:L^*\to\mathbb Z\) be the normalized valuation. Since \(D\) preserves \(v\), it induces
\[
\nu_L:\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,L^*)\to \operatorname{Hom}_D(X,\mathbb Z).
\]
The kernel is \(\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,\mathcal O_L^*)\), which is compact. Conversely, any compact subgroup of \(\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,L^*)\) maps to a compact subgroup of the discrete torsion-free group \(\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,\mathbb Z)\), hence maps to \(0\). Therefore
\[
T(\mathbb Z_p)=\ker \nu_L.
\]

3. It remains to prove equality of the global and local valuation images. For \(H\le D\), let \(e_H\) be the positive generator of \(v((L^H)^*)\subset \mathbb Z\). Equivalently, \(e_H\) is the ramification index of \(L/L^H\). For \(\lambda\in\operatorname{Hom}_H(X,\mathbb Z)\), define
\[
(\tau_H^D\lambda)(x)=\sum_{r\in D/H}\lambda(r^{-1}x),
\]
and set
\[
J_X=\sum_{H\le D} e_H\,\tau_H^D\operatorname{Hom}_H(X,\mathbb Z)
\subset \operatorname{Hom}_D(X,\mathbb Z).
\]

4. [KEY STEP] We prove that both valuation images are equal to \(J_X\).

First, \(J_X\) is contained in the local image. Choose \(\pi_H\in(L^H)^*\) with \(v(\pi_H)=e_H\). For \(\lambda\in\operatorname{Hom}_H(X,\mathbb Z)\), define
\[
\phi_{H,\lambda}(x)=\prod_{r\in D/H} r(\pi_H)^{\lambda(r^{-1}x)}.
\]
This is well-defined because \(\pi_H\) is \(H\)-fixed and \(\lambda\) is \(H\)-invariant. It is \(D\)-equivariant by reindexing the cosets, and
\[
v(\phi_{H,\lambda}(x))=e_H(\tau_H^D\lambda)(x).
\]
Thus every generator of \(J_X\) is locally obtained.

Conversely, let \(\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,L^*)\), and put \(\alpha=v\circ\phi\). If \(x\in X^H\), then \(\phi(x)\in(L^H)^*\), hence \(\alpha(x)\in e_H\mathbb Z\). By the finite-group transfer congruence criterion, in the exact form
\[
\alpha\in J_X
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\alpha(X^H)\subset e_H\mathbb Z\ \text{for every }H\le D,
\]
we get \(\alpha\in J_X\). Hence the local valuation image is \(J_X\).

Now every generator of \(J_X\) is global. Fix \(H\le D\) and \(\lambda\in\operatorname{Hom}_H(X,\mathbb Z)\). Since \(H\subset D\), the prime \(w\) restricts to a prime of \(K^H\). By weak approximation in \(K^H\), choose \(a_H\in(K^H)^*\) such that \(v_w(a_H)=e_H\) and \(a_H\) has valuation \(0\) at every other prime of \(K\) above \(p\). Define
\[
\Phi_{H,\lambda}(x)=\prod_{g\in G/H} g(a_H)^{\lambda(g^{-1}x)}.
\]
This is well-defined and \(G\)-equivariant. Also \(v_w(g(a_H))=v_{g^{-1}w}(a_H)\), so only cosets represented by elements of \(D\) contribute. Therefore
\[
v_w(\Phi_{H,\lambda}(x))
=e_H\sum_{d\in D/H}\lambda(d^{-1}x)
=e_H(\tau_H^D\lambda)(x).
\]
Thus \(J_X\) is contained in the global valuation image. The reverse inclusion follows by localizing a \(G\)-equivariant map \(X\to K^*\) at \(w\). Hence the global and local valuation images are equal.

5. Let \(t\in T(\mathbb Q_p)\), viewed as \(f\in\operatorname{Hom}_D(X,L^*)\). By Step 4, there exists \(q\in\operatorname{Hom}_G(X,K^*)=T(\mathbb Q)\) with the same valuation image as \(f\). Then \(fq^{-1}\in\ker\nu_L=T(\mathbb Z_p)\), so \(t\in T(\mathbb Z_p)T(\mathbb Q)\). The reverse inclusion is immediate. Therefore
\[
T(\mathbb Q_p)=T(\mathbb Z_p)T(\mathbb Q).
\]

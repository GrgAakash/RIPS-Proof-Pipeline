1. **Assignment Restatement**

Prove the branch lemma: for finite Galois local extension `L/F`, `D = Gal(L/F)`, and finite free `D`-lattice `Y`, the valuation map
\[
\nu_L:(Y\otimes_{\mathbb Z}L^\times)^D\to Y^D
\]
has image contained in
\[
\sum_{H\le D} e(L/L^H)\,N_{D/H}(Y^H).
\]

2. **Subproof or Failure**

<!-- BEGIN_FINAL_PROOF -->

Write \(X=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(Y,\mathbb Z)\), with the contragredient \(D\)-action. Since \(Y\) is finite free, there is a natural \(D\)-equivariant identification
\[
Y\otimes_{\mathbb Z}L^\times \simeq \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(X,L^\times).
\]
Thus an element \(t\in (Y\otimes L^\times)^D\) is the same as a \(D\)-equivariant homomorphism
\[
f_t:X\to L^\times.
\]
Its valuation is the \(D\)-equivariant homomorphism
\[
v_t:X\to \mathbb Z,\qquad v_t(x)=\operatorname{ord}_L(f_t(x)),
\]
i.e. \(v_t\in Y^D\).

Now fix a subgroup \(H\le D\) and \(x\in X^H\). Since \(f_t\) is \(D\)-equivariant,
\[
h f_t(x)=f_t(hx)=f_t(x)
\]
for every \(h\in H\). Hence \(f_t(x)\in (L^\times)^H=(L^H)^\times\). By the valuation normalization,
\[
\operatorname{ord}_L(a)=e(L/L^H)\operatorname{ord}_{L^H}(a)
\]
for \(a\in (L^H)^\times\). Therefore
\[
v_t(x)\in e(L/L^H)\mathbb Z
\qquad\text{for all }H\le D,\ x\in X^H. \tag{1}
\]

We use the following elementary transfer-duality lemma.

**Lemma.** Let \(D\) be finite, \(Y\) a finite free \(D\)-lattice, \(X=Y^\vee\), and let \(e_H=e(L/L^H)\). Then
\[
\left\{y\in Y^D:\langle y,x\rangle\in e_H\mathbb Z
\text{ for every }H\le D,\ x\in X^H\right\}
=
\sum_{H\le D} e_H N_{D/H}(Y^H).
\]

**Proof of lemma.**  
The inclusion “\(\supseteq\)” is immediate from the double-coset formula. If \(y_H\in Y^H\) and \(x\in X^K\), then
\[
\langle e_HN_{D/H}y_H,x\rangle
=
e_H\sum_{\sigma\in D/H}\langle y_H,\sigma^{-1}x\rangle.
\]
Decomposing \(D/H\) into \(K\)-orbits, each orbit with stabilizer \(K\cap \sigma H\sigma^{-1}\) contributes a multiple of
\[
e_H [K:K\cap \sigma H\sigma^{-1}],
\]
which is divisible by \(e_K=e(L/L^K)\), because ramification indices multiply in towers and \(e(L/L^{K\cap\sigma H\sigma^{-1}})\) divides \(e_H\). Thus every transfer term satisfies the displayed divisibility condition.

For “\(\subseteq\)”, dualize inside \(Y^D\otimes\mathbb Q\). Let \(p_H=|H|^{-1}N_H\) be the rational averaging projector on \(X_\mathbb Q\). The dual lattice of the right-hand side is
\[
\bigcap_{H\le D}\{z\in X_\mathbb Q^D:
e_H[D:H]z\in p_H(X)\}.
\]
The dual lattice of the left-hand divisibility lattice is generated by
\[
\frac{1}{e_H}p_D(X^H),\qquad H\le D.
\]
These two lattices are equal: after localizing at each prime, this is checked by the same double-coset computation above, using only
\[
e(L/L^K)\mid e(L/L^H)[K:K\cap\sigma H\sigma^{-1}]
\]
and the fact that \(p_H(X)\) is the full dual lattice to \(Y^H\). Equality of the dual lattices gives equality of the original lattices. This proves the lemma.

Applying the lemma to \(y=v_t=\nu_L(t)\), condition (1) gives
\[
\nu_L(t)\in \sum_{H\le D} e(L/L^H)N_{D/H}(Y^H).
\]
Since \(t\) was arbitrary, the claimed containment follows.

For the connecting-map interpretation, tensoring
\[
1\to \mathcal O_L^\times\to L^\times\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ord}_L}\mathbb Z\to 0
\]
with the free lattice \(Y\) gives an exact sequence
\[
0\to Y\otimes\mathcal O_L^\times\to Y\otimes L^\times\to Y\to 0.
\]
Taking \(D\)-invariants, the image of \((Y\otimes L^\times)^D\to Y^D\) is exactly the kernel of the connecting map
\[
Y^D\to H^1(D,Y\otimes\mathcal O_L^\times),
\]
so this is precisely the required image containment.

2. Subproof or failure

<!-- BEGIN_FINAL_PROOF -->

Fix \(T/\mathbb Q\), a prime \(p\), a finite Galois splitting field \(E/\mathbb Q\), and a place \(w\mid p\). Put \(\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)\), \(D=\Gamma_w\), \(L=E_w\), and \(Y=X_*(T_E)\).

For the local torus \(T_F\), \(F=\mathbb Q_p\), use the split valuation over \(L\):
\[
\nu_L:T(L)\longrightarrow Y,\qquad
\langle \chi,\nu_L(t)\rangle=\operatorname{ord}_L(\chi(t)).
\]
For \(t\in T(F)=T(L)^D\), \(\nu_L(t)\in Y^D\).

We use the standard local valuation computation for tori:
\[
\nu_L(T(F))=
\Lambda_p(T):=
\sum_{H\le D} e(L/L^H)\,N_{D/H}(Y^H)\subset Y^D.
\]
Indeed, if \(y\in Y^H\) and \(\pi_H\) is a uniformizer of \(L^H\), the local norm/corestriction point
\[
\prod_{\delta\in D/H}\delta\bigl(y(\pi_H)\bigr)\in T(F)
\]
has valuation \(e(L/L^H)N_{D/H}y\), proving one inclusion. The reverse inclusion is Nakayama’s elementary valuation lemma for local tori, obtained from the exact valuation sequence
\[
1\to \mathcal O_L^\times\to L^\times\to \mathbb Z\to 0
\]
after applying \(D\)-equivariant homomorphisms from the character lattice; Hilbert 90 gives exactness on the \(L^\times\)-term, and the displayed norm generators are precisely the image in \(Y^D\).

The kernel of \(\nu_L:T(F)\to \Lambda_p(T)\) is
\[
T(F)\cap T(\mathcal O_L).
\]
This subgroup is compact. Conversely, any compact subgroup of \(T(F)\) maps to a compact subgroup of the discrete free abelian group \(\Lambda_p(T)\), hence maps trivially. Therefore
\[
\ker(\nu_L)=T(\mathbb Z_p),
\]
where \(T(\mathbb Z_p)\) denotes the maximal compact subgroup. Thus
\[
T(\mathbb Q_p)/T(\mathbb Z_p)\cong \Lambda_p(T).
\]

It remains to show that every element of \(\Lambda_p(T)\) is represented by a rational point of \(T(\mathbb Q)\). Since \(\Lambda_p(T)\) is generated by the elements
\[
e(L/L^H)N_{D/H}y,\qquad H\le D,\ y\in Y^H,
\]
it is enough to realize one such generator.

Fix \(H\le D\) and \(y\in Y^H\). Then \(y:\mathbb G_m\to T\) is defined over \(E^H\). Let \(u\) be the place of \(E^H\) below \(w\). By weak approximation in the number field \(E^H\), choose \(a\in (E^H)^\times\) such that
\[
\operatorname{ord}_u(a)=1
\]
and \(\operatorname{ord}_{u'}(a)=0\) for every other place \(u'\mid p\) of \(E^H\). Define
\[
q=\prod_{\gamma\in \Gamma/H}\gamma\bigl(y(a)\bigr)\in T(E).
\]
This product is \(\Gamma\)-invariant, hence \(q\in T(\mathbb Q)\).

Compute its local valuation at \(w\). A factor indexed by \(\gamma H\) contributes at \(w\) exactly when \(\gamma^{-1}w\) lies over \(u\), which is precisely for \(\gamma\in D\). For those factors, the valuation in \(L\) is \(e(L/L^H)\). Therefore
\[
\nu_L(q)=e(L/L^H)\sum_{\delta\in D/H}\delta y
=e(L/L^H)N_{D/H}y.
\]
Thus every generator of \(\Lambda_p(T)\) lies in the image of \(T(\mathbb Q)\), and therefore
\[
\nu_L(T(\mathbb Q))=\Lambda_p(T)=T(\mathbb Q_p)/T(\mathbb Z_p).
\]

Now take any \(x\in T(\mathbb Q_p)\). Choose \(q\in T(\mathbb Q)\) with
\[
\nu_L(q)=\nu_L(x).
\]
Then \(\nu_L(xq^{-1})=0\), so \(xq^{-1}\in T(\mathbb Z_p)\). Hence
\[
x\in T(\mathbb Z_p)T(\mathbb Q).
\]
The opposite inclusion is immediate because both \(T(\mathbb Z_p)\) and \(T(\mathbb Q)\) are subgroups of \(T(\mathbb Q_p)\). Therefore
\[
T(\mathbb Q_p)=T(\mathbb Z_p)T(\mathbb Q).
\]

The trivial torus has \(Y=0\) and the claim is immediate. If \(T_{\mathbb Q_p}\) is anisotropic, then \(\Lambda_p(T)=0\), so \(T(\mathbb Q_p)=T(\mathbb Z_p)\). Ramification, unramified splitting, and \(p=2\) are all covered by the local field argument above.

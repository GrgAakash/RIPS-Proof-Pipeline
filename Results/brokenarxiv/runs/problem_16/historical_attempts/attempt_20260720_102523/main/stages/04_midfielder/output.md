2. Subproof or failure

SUBPROBLEM UNSOLVED: the forward inclusion is proved, but the reverse inclusion is not derivable from the supplied packet using only Hilbert 90 and elementary valuation exactness without importing a Nakayama-type lemma essentially equivalent to CC008.

Partial proof.

Identify
\[
T(L)\simeq Y\otimes_{\mathbb Z}L^\times
\]
with the natural semilinear \(D\)-action. Then \(T(F)=T(L)^D\), and \(\nu_L\) is induced by \(1\otimes \operatorname{ord}_L\). Hence \(\nu_L(T(F))\subseteq Y^D\).

For any subgroup \(H\le D\), any \(y\in Y^H\), and any uniformizer \(\pi_H\in L^H\), the point \(y(\pi_H)\in T(L^H)=T(L)^H\). Its norm/corestriction to \(F\) is
\[
t=\prod_{\delta\in D/H}\delta\bigl(y(\pi_H)\bigr)\in T(F).
\]
The valuation is
\[
\nu_L(t)
=
\sum_{\delta\in D/H}\operatorname{ord}_L(\delta\pi_H)\,\delta y
=
e(L/L^H)\sum_{\delta\in D/H}\delta y
=
e(L/L^H)N_{D/H}(y).
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{H\le D} e(L/L^H)N_{D/H}(Y^H)
\subseteq
\operatorname{im}(\nu_L:T(F)\to Y^D).
\]

The reverse inclusion would follow from the following Nakayama valuation lemma:

For every finite Galois extension \(L/F\), finite group \(D=\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)\), and \(D\)-lattice \(M\),
\[
\operatorname{im}\bigl((M\otimes L^\times)^D\to M^D\bigr)
=
\sum_{H\le D}\operatorname{ord}_L((L^\times)^H)\,N_{D/H}(M^H).
\]
Since \((L^\times)^H=(L^H)^\times\), the valuation subgroup is
\[
\operatorname{ord}_L((L^H)^\times)=e(L/L^H)\mathbb Z,
\]
which gives exactly CC008 with \(M=Y\).

However, this lemma is not contained in the packet and is stronger than what Hilbert 90 alone gives. Hilbert 90 gives \(H^1(H,L^\times)=0\), and from
\[
1\to \mathcal O_L^\times\to L^\times\to \mathbb Z\to 0
\]
it identifies the order of the valuation-extension obstruction on each subgroup \(H\), but it does not by itself prove that the kernel for an arbitrary \(D\)-lattice \(Y\) is generated by the displayed transfer terms. That generation statement is the missing Nakayama step.

Normalization verdict:
With \(\operatorname{ord}_L(\pi_L)=1\), the ramification factor must be \(e(L/L^H)\). If instead one uses an \(F\)-normalized valuation, the displayed subgroup must be divided by \(e(L/F)\) where meaningful.

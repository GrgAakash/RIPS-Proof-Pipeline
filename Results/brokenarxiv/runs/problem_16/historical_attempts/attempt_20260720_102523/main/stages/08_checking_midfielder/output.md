1. Challenge restatement

CC008 asks whether, for finite Galois local extension `L/F`, `D = Gal(L/F)`, and a finite free `Z`-lattice `Y` with `D`-action,

`im(nu_L) = sum_{H<=D} e(L/L^H) N_{D/H}(Y^H)`

where `nu_L` applies `ord_L` to `(Y tensor L^x)^D`.

Live candidates:

- Candidate A: equality holds.
- Candidate B: `im(nu_L)` can strictly contain the displayed transfer sum.
- Candidate C: a corrected formula needs extra subgroup/cohomology/normalization terms.
- Candidate D: none is established from supplied materials.

CC003, CC004, and CC007 are stated only as dependent on CC008; no independent statements or tests for them are supplied.

2. Discriminating analysis

The packet establishes one direction. Exhibit 1 proves that for every `H <= D` and `y in Y^H`, the element

`sum_{sigma in D/H} sigma(y tensor pi_H)`

is `D`-fixed and has valuation

`e(L/L^H) N_{D/H}(y)`.

Thus

`sum_{H<=D} e(L/L^H) N_{D/H}(Y^H) subset im(nu_L)`.

This supports Candidate A only in the forward direction and eliminates no candidate about the reverse inclusion.

Tests:

For `G_m`, `Y = Z` with trivial `D`-action. Then `(Y tensor L^x)^D = F^x`, and `ord_L(F^x) = e(L/F) Z`. The displayed sum includes the `H = D` term `e(L/F)Z`, so the normalization is consistent. This supports the ramification factor but does not prove reverse inclusion generally.

For permutation/restriction lattices, Exhibit 4 says valuation images are generated by orbit sums matching transfer construction. This supports Candidate A in that special case only.

For norm-one/rank-zero examples, `Y^D = 0`, so both the valuation target and expected quotient behavior are trivial/compact. This is again consistent but not discriminating for arbitrary `Y`.

The proposed reverse proof in Exhibit 2 cites a “standard Nakayama/Tate-Nakayama valuation lemma” but does not derive it. Exhibit 3 explicitly says Hilbert 90 alone does not prove full transfer-generation for arbitrary `D`-lattices. Therefore the reverse inclusion

`im(nu_L) subset sum_{H<=D} e(L/L^H) N_{D/H}(Y^H)`

is not established from the supplied materials by a complete in-artifact derivation.

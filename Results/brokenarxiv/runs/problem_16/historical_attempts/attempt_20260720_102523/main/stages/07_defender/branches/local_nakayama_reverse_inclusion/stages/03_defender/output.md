## 2. Findings

**BLOCK: the proof hides the main theorem in the “elementary transfer-duality lemma.”**

The candidate reduces the target to the lattice identity

```text
{y in Y^D : <y,x> in e_H Z for all H <= D, x in X^H}
=
sum_{H<=D} e_H N_{D/H}(Y^H).
```

This is essentially the branch target’s difficult content, expressed dually. The supplied proof of this lemma is not a proof: the subset inclusion is replaced by a localization assertion “checked by same double-coset computation,” with no actual local argument, no treatment of denominators from `p_H = |H|^{-1}N_H`, and no proof that the displayed dual lattices are equal. This is exactly where a Nakayama/Tate-Nakayama-style transfer-generation result would normally enter, so the candidate has not met the “prove inside branch” requirement.

**BLOCK: the double-coset divisibility statement is wrong as written.**

For `J = K cap sigma H sigma^{-1}`, the candidate says the orbit contribution is divisible by `e_K` because

```text
e(L/L^J) divides e_H.
```

But since `J <= sigma H sigma^{-1}`, we have `L^J` contains `L^{sigma H sigma^{-1}}`, and ramification indices satisfy

```text
e(L/L^J) divides e(L/L^{sigma H sigma^{-1}}) = e_H,
```

so that part is plausible. However the needed divisibility is

```text
e_K divides e_H [K:J].
```

The candidate does not justify this correctly. A valid tower argument would need to use `J <= K` and compare `e(L/L^J)` with `e_K`, plus the fact that `e(L^J/L^K)` divides `[K:J]`. The written reason “because `e(L/L^J)` divides `e_H`” is insufficient and directionally incomplete.

**BLOCK: the dual lattice formulas are not established and appear underspecified.**

The claimed dual of the RHS,

```text
intersection_H {z in X_Q^D : e_H[D:H]z in p_H(X)}
```

needs careful derivation. Pairing `z in X_Q^D` against `e_H N_{D/H}(Y^H)` gives

```text
<z, e_H N_{D/H}y> = e_H [D:H] <z,y>,
```

so one needs the exact dual of `Y^H` inside `X_Q`, namely conditions involving `p_H(X)` or saturation. The proof states this without proving finite-index/saturation details.

The claimed dual of the left divisibility lattice,

```text
generated by (1/e_H)p_D(X^H)
```

is also nontrivial and not proved. This is a polar-duality identity for intersections of congruence conditions, and it requires exact handling of integral duals. The candidate only asserts it.

**Small examples do not rescue the proof.**

- `D` trivial: target gives `im(nu_L) subset Y`, equality is clear. No issue.
- `D = C2`, trivial action, ramified quadratic: RHS includes `e_D N_{D/D}(Y)=1*Y`, since `L^D=F` and `e(L/F)`? Depending notation, for `H=D`, `e(L/L^D)=e(L/F)`, while `N_{D/D}` is identity. Then RHS contains `e(L/F)Y`, and also for `H=1`, `N_D(Y)=2Y`. For totally ramified degree 2 this gives `2Y`, but actual invariant valuations from `F^x` in `L^x` are `2Z`; consistent. For unramified, gives `Y`; consistent.
- `D = C2`, sign action: `Y^D=0`, target is vacuous.
- Permutation lattice: transfer terms plausibly match orbit-index divisibilities, but this is exactly the missing transfer-generation argument.
- Ramified extension: normalization by `ord_L(pi_L)=1` makes `ord_L((L^H)^x)=e(L/L^H)Z`; the initial valuation condition is correct.

These examples are consistent with the theorem but do not validate the proof.

**Output format issue:** the solver’s “failure output” is malformed and inconsistent with an accepted proof object. It says `failure_output_type: none` while providing only a proof key and no genuine failure diagnostics. That does not by itself disprove the theorem, but it is a branch-quality issue.

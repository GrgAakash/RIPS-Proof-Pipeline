1. Assignment restatement

S-ID: S3

assigned subclaim(s): Charging bound for same-colored disjoint edge-pairs in \(G[U]\).

what must be proved: Given a \(P_4\)-rainbow edge-coloring of \(G\), for every unordered pair of disjoint same-colored edges in \(G[U]\), choose a missing cross-edge in \(H=\overline G\) and charge the pair to it. For each fixed missing edge \(xy\), bound the number of charged unordered pairs for which \(xy\) is one cross-edge between the two \(G\)-edges by \(O(|U|)\), ideally at most \(2(|U|-2)\). Conclude the number of same-colored disjoint edge-pairs in \(G[U]\) is \(O(e(H)|U|)=O(n^{3-\epsilon})\) when \(e(H)=\lfloor n^{2-\epsilon}\rfloor\).

declared prerequisite subclaims: None needed. I prove the needed missing-cross-edge fact inside this subproof.

2. Subproof or failure

Let \(G\) be an \(n\)-vertex graph with an edge-coloring such that every copy of \(P_4\) in \(G\) is rainbow. Let \(H=\overline G\), and let \(U\subseteq V(G)\). Write \(u=|U|\).

Call an unordered pair \(\{e,f\}\) of edges of \(G[U]\) admissible if \(e\) and \(f\) are vertex-disjoint and have the same color. First note the following elementary fact.

Claim. If \(ab,cd\in E(G)\) are disjoint and have the same color, then at least one of
\[
ac,\ ad,\ bc,\ bd
\]
is not an edge of \(G\).

Proof. If, for example, \(ac\in E(G)\), then \(b-a-c-d\) is a copy of \(P_4\) in \(G\), using edges \(ba,ac,cd\). Its first and third edges \(ab\) and \(cd\) have the same color, so this copy of \(P_4\) is not rainbow, contradiction. The same argument applies to any of the four possible cross-edges, by ordering the path appropriately:
\[
ac: b-a-c-d,\quad
ad: b-a-d-c,\quad
bc: a-b-c-d,\quad
bd: a-b-d-c.
\]
Thus all four cross-edges cannot be present, proving the claim. In fact, no cross-edge between \(\{a,b\}\) and \(\{c,d\}\) can be present. \(\square\)

For each admissible pair \(\{ab,cd\}\), choose one missing cross-edge among \(ac,ad,bc,bd\), and charge \(\{ab,cd\}\) to that edge of \(H\). Since all vertices lie in \(U\), every charged missing edge also has both endpoints in \(U\). Hence only missing edges in \(H[U]\) receive charges.

Fix a missing edge \(xy\in E(H[U])\). We count admissible unordered edge-pairs \(\{e,f\}\) that could be charged to \(xy\) with \(xy\) serving as a cross-edge between \(e\) and \(f\).

For \(xy\) to be a cross-edge between two disjoint edges \(e,f\), one of \(e,f\) must contain \(x\), and the other must contain \(y\). Thus the pair has the form
\[
\{xz,yw\}
\]
where \(z,w\in U\setminus\{x,y\}\), \(xz,yw\in E(G)\), and the edges \(xz\) and \(yw\) are disjoint. Disjointness requires \(z\neq w\). Also, since \(xz\) and \(yw\) are same-colored and disjoint, the earlier claim applied to these two edges says that every cross-edge between \(\{x,z\}\) and \(\{y,w\}\) is missing from \(G\). In particular, \(zw\notin E(G)\), so \(zw\in E(H)\).

For each possible charged pair \(\{xz,yw\}\), the ordered choice of \(z\) determines at most one value of \(w\) if one is counting through the actual pair? That alone is not enough to bound by \(O(u)\), because \(z\) could pair with many \(w\). However, the stronger observation is that every such pair yields a missing edge \(zw\in E(H[U])\). Therefore the number of pairs charged to a fixed \(xy\) is at most the number of ordered pairs \((z,w)\) with \(z,w\in U\setminus\{x,y\}\), \(z\neq w\), and \(zw\in E(H)\), which is at most \(2e(H[U])\). This gives only
\[
\#\{\text{charges to fixed }xy\}\le 2e(H[U]),
\]
not \(O(u)\) in general.

The proposed bound \(2(u-2)\) is false without additional hypotheses. Here is a concrete obstruction.

Let \(U\) contain distinct vertices \(x,y\), sets
\[
Z=\{z_1,\dots,z_t\},\qquad W=\{w_1,\dots,w_t\},
\]
and suppose \(G[U]\) contains the edges \(xz_i\) and \(yw_j\) for all \(1\le i,j\le t\), with all these \(2t\) edges assigned the same color. To preserve the \(P_4\)-rainbow condition for these same-colored disjoint pairs, put all edges \(z_iw_j\) into \(H\), and also \(xy\in H\). Then for every \(i,j\), the unordered pair \(\{xz_i,yw_j\}\) has \(xy\) as a missing cross-edge. Thus \(xy\) is a possible charge target for \(t^2\) same-colored disjoint pairs.

Taking \(t\) large gives \(t^2\), while \(2(|U|-2)=2(2t)=4t\). For \(t\ge 5\), \(t^2>4t\). Therefore the claimed \(O(|U|)\) per fixed missing edge cannot be proved from the stated assumptions alone.

This construction can be made into a full \(P_4\)-rainbow-colored graph as follows. Let \(G\) have vertex set \(\{x,y\}\cup Z\cup W\), edges \(xz_i\) and \(yw_j\) all colored with one common color \(\alpha\), and no other edges. Then \(G\) contains no copy of \(P_4\), because every connected component is a star. Hence the coloring vacuously satisfies the condition that every copy of \(P_4\) is rainbow. The complement \(H\) contains \(xy\) and every \(z_iw_j\). Again, \(xy\) is a cross-edge for all \(t^2\) same-colored disjoint pairs \(\{xz_i,yw_j\}\). This disproves the desired per-edge \(O(|U|)\) charging bound in the generality assigned.

Thus the assigned conclusion
\[
\#\{\text{same-colored disjoint pairs in }G[U]\}=O(e(H)|U|)
\]
does not follow from this charging scheme without additional structure or a different counting argument.

SUBPROBLEM UNSOLVED: the requested fixed-missing-edge bound \(O(|U|)\) is false under the allowed assumptions.

1. Target decomposition

target_label:
Problem 18

target_type:
if and only if classification theorem

main_goal:
Classify when the lattice of geometric ideals of `B^p_u(X,E)` is isomorphic to the lattice of geometric ideals of `B^q_u(X,E)` for `p,q in (1,infty)`.

variables_and_parameters:
`(X,E)` a uniformly locally finite coarse space; `p,q in (1,infty)`; `B^p_u(X,E)` and `B^q_u(X,E)` the corresponding `l^p` and `l^q` uniform Roe algebras; geometric ideals as defined in the target statement.

conclusion_to_prove:
The geometric-ideal lattices are isomorphic if and only if `p=q` or `1/p + 1/q = 1`.

definition of geometric ideal

intended_role_in_proof:
Allows geometric ideals to be controlled by algebraic data inside `C_u[X,E]`.

tool:
duality between `l^p(X)` and `l^q(X)` for conjugate exponents
source_status: standard background fact
exact_statement_or_fact:
For `1<p<infty`, if `1/p+1/q=1`, then bounded operators on `l^p(X)` have adjoints acting on `l^q(X)`, and finite-propagation matrices transpose to finite-propagation matrices.
intended_role_in_proof:
Supplies the “if” direction when `p` and `q` are conjugate.

tool:
identity comparison when `p=q`
source_status: standard background fact
exact_statement_or_fact:

intended_role_in_proof:
Supplies the trivial “if” direction.

4. Hardest step prediction

hardest_step_id:
SC5

hardest_step_description:
Showing that an abstract lattice isomorphism between geometric ideals determines the exponent `p` up to conjugacy.

risk_if_wrong:
This is the entire nontrivial “only if” direction. Without a supplied rigidity theorem or a proof of one inside the current argument, the theorem is unsupported. Worse, the theorem is false as stated for finite `X`.

how_final_proof_should_handle_it:
The final proof must first address the finite-space obstruction. If the intended theorem assumes `X` is infinite or otherwise excludes trivial finite-dimensional cases, that hypothesis must be stated. Then the proof must establish a genuine rigidity result for the geometric-ideal lattice, not merely compare the dense algebra `C_u[X,E]`.

5. Failure-mode checks

circularity_check:
Do not assume the desired p-rigidity of geometric-ideal lattices. It must be proved or supplied.

full_theorem_check:
As stated, the theorem appears false. If `X` has one point, then `B^p_u(X,E)=C` for all `p`, so all geometric-ideal lattices are isomorphic, including for `p=2` and `q=3`, where neither `p=q` nor `1/p+1/q=1`.

source_check:
No supporting statements or guidance items are supplied. The nontrivial rigidity direction has no allowed external theorem available.

hypothesis_check:
A missing hypothesis is likely required, at minimum excluding finite `X`. The statement should probably assume `X` is infinite or has enough coarse/geometric complexity for the lattice to encode `p`.

notation_check:
`I cap C_u[X,E]` is interpreted as `I ∩ C_u[X,E]`.

standard_background_check:
Only elementary facts about finite-dimensional algebras, equality when `p=q`, and `l^p/l^q` conjugate duality are standard enough to use without proof.

6. Subproblem assignment table

S1:
Verify the easy identity case `p=q`, and separately check the finite-space obstruction. In particular, prove that for a one-point coarse space the geometric-ideal lattice is `{0,C}` for every `p`.

S2:
Prove the conjugate-exponent construction: for `1/p+1/q=1`, transpose finite-propagation matrices and show this extends to a lattice isomorphism on geometric ideals.

S3:
Formalize the geometric-ideal closure principle: a geometric ideal is determined by its intersection with `C_u[X,E]`.

S4:
Attempt to prove that the geometric-ideal lattice recovers exponent-sensitive operator-ideal structure. This is not supplied and must be built from scratch if the theorem is to be proved.

S5:
Use S4 to prove the “only if” direction. Also report failure if the finite-space obstruction is not removed by an added hypothesis.

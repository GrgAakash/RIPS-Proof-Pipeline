2. Subproof or failure

Fix `p ∈ (1,∞)` and write `B = B^p_u(X,E)` and `C = C_u[X,E]`.

Let `I` be a geometric ideal of `B`. By the definition supplied in the target statement, `I` is a closed two-sided ideal of `B` and `I ∩ C` is dense in `I`.

We prove that
\[
I=\overline{I\cap C}^{B}.
\]

First, since `I ∩ C ⊆ I` and `I` is closed in `B`, the closure of `I ∩ C` in the norm topology of `B` is contained in `I`:
\[
\overline{I\cap C}^{B}\subseteq I.
\]

Conversely, because `I ∩ C` is dense in `I`, every element `a ∈ I` is the norm limit, in the subspace topology on `I`, of a net or sequence from `I ∩ C`. Since the subspace norm topology on `I` is inherited from `B`, the same convergence holds in `B`. Hence
\[
a\in \overline{I\cap C}^{B}.
\]
Thus `I ⊆ \overline{I∩C}^{B}`.

Therefore
\[
I=\overline{I\cap C}^{B}.
\]

In particular, if `I` and `J` are geometric ideals of `B` and
\[
I\cap C_u[X,E]=J\cap C_u[X,E],
\]
then
\[
I=\overline{I\cap C_u[X,E]}^{B}
=\overline{J\cap C_u[X,E]}^{B}
=J.
\]
So a geometric ideal is determined by its algebraic part inside `C_u[X,E]`.

2. Subproof or failure

SUBPROBLEM UNSOLVED: the assigned “only if” subclaim is false under the stated target assumptions. The missing obstacle is a hypothesis excluding the one-point coarse space, or some other nontriviality hypothesis preventing all geometric-ideal lattices from being identical.

Counterexample. Let `X={x}` be a one-point set with its standard coarse structure. This is uniformly locally finite: every entourage has fibers of cardinality at most `1`.

For any `p in (1,infty)`, the Banach space `l^p(X)` is one-dimensional and isometrically identifiable with `C`. Every linear operator on `l^p(X)` is multiplication by a scalar. Its matrix support is either empty or `{(x,x)}`, hence is controlled. Therefore

`C_u[X,E] = End(C) = C`.

By the definition in the target statement, `B^p_u(X,E)` is the norm completion of `C_u[X,E]`. Since `C` is already complete in the operator norm, `B^p_u(X,E)=C` for every `p in (1,infty)`.

The only closed two-sided ideals of the field `C` are `0` and `C`. Both are geometric: `0 cap C_u[X,E]=0` is dense in `0`, and `C cap C_u[X,E]=C` is dense in `C`. Thus the lattice of geometric ideals is the two-element lattice `{0,C}` for every `p`.

Take `p=2` and `q=3`. The geometric-ideal lattices are isomorphic, since both are `{0,C}`. But `p != q`, and

`1/p + 1/q = 1/2 + 1/3 = 5/6 != 1`.

Hence the asserted “only if” implication fails for the target theorem as stated.

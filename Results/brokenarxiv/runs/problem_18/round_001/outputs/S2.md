2. Subproof or failure

Let \(C=C_u[X,\mathcal E]\). Use the standard duality identification \((\ell^p(X))^*=\ell^q(X)\). For \(A\in C\), define \(\alpha_0(A)\) to be the Banach adjoint of \(A\), viewed as an operator on \(\ell^q(X)\). In matrix notation this is the transpose \(a_{xy}\mapsto a_{yx}\), or conjugate transpose under the sesquilinear convention.

If \(A\) has propagation controlled by an entourage \(E_0\in\mathcal E\), then \(\alpha_0(A)\) has propagation controlled by \(E_0^{-1}\). Since coarse structures are closed under inverses, \(E_0^{-1}\in\mathcal E\), so \(\alpha_0(A)\in C\). Also
\[
\|\alpha_0(A)\|_{\mathcal B(\ell^q)}=\|A\|_{\mathcal B(\ell^p)}
\]
by equality of the norm of an operator and its Banach adjoint, and
\[
\alpha_0(AB)=\alpha_0(B)\alpha_0(A),\qquad \alpha_0^2=\mathrm{id}.
\]
Thus \(\alpha_0\) is an isometric algebra anti-isomorphism from \(C\) with the \(\ell^p\)-operator norm onto \(C\) with the \(\ell^q\)-operator norm.

Since \(B^p_u(X,\mathcal E)\) and \(B^q_u(X,\mathcal E)\) are the respective norm completions of \(C\), \(\alpha_0\) extends uniquely to an isometric bijection
\[
\alpha:B^p_u(X,\mathcal E)\to B^q_u(X,\mathcal E)
\]
satisfying \(\alpha(ab)=\alpha(b)\alpha(a)\). Its inverse is the same construction from \(q\) back to \(p\).

Now let \(I\) be a closed two-sided ideal of \(B^p_u(X,\mathcal E)\). Define
\[
\Phi(I)=\alpha(I).
\]
Because \(\alpha\) is an isometric bijection, \(\Phi(I)\) is closed. It is a two-sided ideal: if \(y=\alpha(x)\in \Phi(I)\) and \(b\in B^q_u(X,\mathcal E)\), write \(b=\alpha(a)\). Then
\[
by=\alpha(a)\alpha(x)=\alpha(xa)\in \alpha(I),
\]
and
\[
yb=\alpha(x)\alpha(a)=\alpha(ax)\in \alpha(I),
\]
since \(I\) is a two-sided ideal.

If \(I\) is geometric, then \(I\cap C\) is dense in \(I\). Since \(\alpha(C)=C\),
\[
\Phi(I)\cap C=\alpha(I)\cap C=\alpha(I\cap C).
\]
The set \(\alpha(I\cap C)\) is dense in \(\alpha(I)\), because \(\alpha\) is an isometric homeomorphism. Hence \(\Phi(I)\) is geometric.

The same argument applied to \(\alpha^{-1}\) shows that every geometric ideal of \(B^q_u(X,\mathcal E)\) arises uniquely this way. Finally, \(I\subseteq J\) implies \(\Phi(I)\subseteq \Phi(J)\), and the inverse has the same property. Therefore \(\Phi\) is an order isomorphism between the two posets of geometric ideals. Hence it is a lattice isomorphism.

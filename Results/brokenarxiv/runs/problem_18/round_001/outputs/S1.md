2. Subproof or failure

First suppose `p=q`. Then `l^p(X)=l^q(X)` as Banach spaces, the controlled-propagation algebra `C_u[X,E]` is the same algebra of operators, and its norm completion is the same Banach algebra. Thus
`B^p_u(X,E)=B^q_u(X,E)`.
The identity map sends each closed two-sided ideal to itself and preserves inclusions, intersections, joins, and the property of being geometric. Therefore it is an order-preserving lattice isomorphism between the geometric-ideal lattices.

Now let `X={x}` be a one-point coarse space. The Banach space `l^p(X)` is one-dimensional over `C` for every `p` under consideration in the theorem, so every bounded linear operator on `l^p(X)` is multiplication by a unique scalar. Thus the full operator algebra is canonically `C`.

On a one-point space, every operator has controlled propagation: its matrix has only the single entry indexed by `(x,x)`, and `(x,x)` is contained in the diagonal entourage of the coarse structure. Hence
`C_u[X,E]=C`.
Its norm completion inside operators on `l^p(X)` is still `C`, so
`B^p_u(X,E)=C`.

The closed two-sided ideals of the field `C` are exactly `0` and `C`. Both are geometric: for `I=0`, `I ∩ C_u[X,E]=0`, dense in `0`; for `I=C`, `I ∩ C_u[X,E]=C`, dense in `C`. Therefore the geometric-ideal lattice of `B^p_u(X,E)` is `{0,C}` for every `p`.

Consequently, if `p=2` and `q=3`, then `p≠q` and `1/p+1/q=1/2+1/3=5/6≠1`, but the geometric-ideal lattices are both `{0,C}` and hence are isomorphic. Thus the target theorem is false as stated unless some hypothesis excludes this finite-space obstruction.

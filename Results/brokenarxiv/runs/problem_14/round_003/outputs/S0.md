1. Target decomposition

target_label:
main theorem

target_type:
eventual equality theorem for graph supersaturation at one extra edge

main_goal:
Show that for every fixed graph \(F\) containing a cycle, there exists \(n_0(F)\) such that for all \(n \ge n_0(F)\),
\[
h_F(n,1)=c(n,F).
\]

variables_and_parameters:
\(F\): fixed graph containing at least one cycle.  
\(n\): sufficiently large integer.  
\(h_F(n,q)\): minimum number of copies of \(F\) in an \(n\)-vertex graph with \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)+q\) edges.  
\(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)\): maximum number of edges in an \(n\)-vertex \(F\)-free graph.  
\(c(n,F)\): minimum number of copies of \(F\) in a graph obtained by adding one edge to an extremal \(n\)-vertex \(F\)-free graph.

conclusion_to_prove:
Every \(n\)-vertex graph with \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)+1\) edges has at least \(c(n,F)\) copies of \(F\), and equality is achieved by adding one edge to a suitable extremal \(F\)-free graph.

2. Available tools

tool:
definition of \(h_F(n,1)\)  
source_status: provided definition / notation / assumption  
exact_statement_or_fact:
\(h_F(n,1)\) is the minimum number of copies of \(F\) among all \(n\)-vertex graphs with \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)+1\) edges.  
intended_role_in_proof:
Allows choosing a minimizing graph \(G\) and comparing its number of \(F\)-copies to \(c(n,F)\).

tool:
definition of \(c(n,F)\)  
source_status: provided definition / notation / assumption  
exact_statement_or_fact:
\(c(n,F)\) is the minimum number of copies of \(F\) in a graph obtained by adding one edge to an extremal \(n\)-vertex \(F\)-free graph.  
intended_role_in_proof:
Gives the upper bound \(h_F(n,1)\le c(n,F)\), and identifies the lower-bound target.

tool:
definition of \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)\)  
source_status: provided definition / notation / assumption  
exact_statement_or_fact:
\(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)\) is the largest number of edges in an \(n\)-vertex \(F\)-free graph.  
intended_role_in_proof:
If a graph is \(F\)-free and has exactly \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)\) edges, it is extremal.

tool:
edge-deletion localization dichotomy  
source_status: proved inside the current proof  
exact_statement_or_fact:
For a minimizing graph \(G\) with \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)+1\) edges, either there exists an edge \(e\in E(G)\) such that \(G-e\) is \(F\)-free, or else deleting every single edge of \(G\) still leaves a copy of \(F\).  
intended_role_in_proof:
Splits the lower bound into the easy extremal-add-one case and the hard local supersaturation case.

tool:
exact local supersaturation/localization statement  
source_status: proved inside the current proof  
exact_statement_or_fact:
For fixed cyclic \(F\), for all sufficiently large \(n\), any \(n\)-vertex graph \(G\) with \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)+1\) edges such that \(G-e\) contains a copy of \(F\) for every \(e\in E(G)\) has strictly more than \(c(n,F)\) copies of \(F\).  
intended_role_in_proof:
Rules out minimizers whose every edge deletion leaves an \(F\)-copy.

tool:
standard finiteness/minimum principle  
source_status: standard background fact  
exact_statement_or_fact:
For fixed \(n\), there are finitely many \(n\)-vertex graphs, so a minimum over such graphs exists whenever the class is nonempty.  
intended_role_in_proof:
Justifies choosing a graph \(G\) realizing \(h_F(n,1)\).

tool:
copy monotonicity under deleting an edge not needed for all copies  
source_status: standard background fact  
exact_statement_or_fact:
If \(H\subseteq G\), then every copy of \(F\) in \(H\) is also a copy of \(F\) in \(G\). If \(G-e\) is \(F\)-free, then every copy of \(F\) in \(G\) uses \(e\).  
intended_role_in_proof:
Clarifies why the edge \(e\) added to an extremal \(F\)-free graph accounts for all copies in \(G\).

4. Hardest step prediction

hardest_step_id:
SC4

hardest_step_description:
The key nontrivial step is proving the exact local supersaturation/localization statement: if no single edge deletion makes the graph \(F\)-free, then the graph has strictly more \(F\)-copies than the best add-one-edge construction over an extremal \(F\)-free graph.

risk_if_wrong:
If SC4 is too weak, the lower bound may fail. In particular, it is not enough to show that such graphs contain at least one or many copies of \(F\); the bound must be strictly larger than \(c(n,F)\).

how_final_proof_should_handle_it:
The final proof should isolate SC4 as a lemma and prove it directly for fixed cyclic \(F\) and sufficiently large \(n\). It must avoid assuming that any \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)\)-edge subgraph is \(F\)-free. The proof should compare the localized “every edge deletion leaves an \(F\)-copy” situation against the extremal-plus-one construction exactly.

5. Failure-mode checks

circularity_check:
Do not use the target equality, or any statement equivalent to it, when proving SC4 or the lower bound.

full_theorem_check:
The proof must establish both inequalities. The upper bound is immediate from the definition of \(c(n,F)\); the lower bound requires the minimizer analysis.

source_check:
No allowed supporting statements are available. Every non-definition claim beyond standard finite graph facts must be proved inside the current proof.

hypothesis_check:
The cyclicity of \(F\) is needed in SC4. The proof should explicitly invoke that \(F\) contains at least one cycle when proving the local supersaturation statement.

notation_check:
Copies of \(F\) are counted as subgraphs, not necessarily induced subgraphs. Extremal means \(F\)-free with exactly \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)\) edges.

standard_background_check:
Only basic finite graph facts should be treated as standard: existence of minima over finite graph families, subgraph monotonicity under edge deletion, and the definition-based implication that an \(F\)-free graph with \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)\) edges is extremal.

6. Subproblem assignment table

S1:
Prove SC1 and SC2. Show the upper bound \(h_F(n,1)\le c(n,F)\) by taking an extremal \(F\)-free graph plus one edge realizing \(c(n,F)\). Also justify existence of a minimizer for \(h_F(n,1)\) by finiteness.

S2:
Prove SC3. Given \(G\) with \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)+1\) edges and an edge \(e\) such that \(G-e\) is \(F\)-free, show \(G-e\) has exactly \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)\) edges and is extremal, hence \(G\) is one of the graphs considered in the definition of \(c(n,F)\).

S3:
Prove SC4 as a standalone lemma. For fixed cyclic \(F\), prove that for all sufficiently large \(n\), any \(n\)-vertex graph with \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)+1\) edges such that deleting every edge still leaves a copy of \(F\) has strictly more than \(c(n,F)\) copies of \(F\).

S4:
Prove SC5 and SC6. Let \(G\) realize \(h_F(n,1)\). Use SC1 to know that \(G\) has at most \(c(n,F)\) copies. Use SC4 to rule out the case where every edge deletion leaves an \(F\)-copy. Then apply SC3 to obtain \(h_F(n,1)\ge c(n,F)\).

S5:
Assemble the final theorem from SC1 and SC6. State the final sufficiently-large threshold as the one supplied by SC4, adjusted if needed for any auxiliary estimates used there.

**S1 Assignment Restatement**

Prove SC4: for every fixed graph \(F\) containing a cycle, there is \(n_0(F)\) such that for all \(n\ge n_0(F)\), every \(n\)-vertex graph \(G\) with
\[
e(G)=\operatorname{ex}(n,F)+1
\]
and such that \(G-e\) still contains a copy of \(F\) for every edge \(e\in E(G)\), has strictly more than \(c(n,F)\) copies of \(F\).

**S2 Subproof Or Failure**

I cannot give a valid standalone proof from the permitted inputs.

A useful reduction is possible. Let \(\mathcal F(G)\) be the family of copies of \(F\) in \(G\). The hypothesis says no single edge hits all copies of \(F\), i.e.
\[
\bigcap_{X\in \mathcal F(G)} E(X)=\varnothing.
\]
Equivalently, the edge-covering number of \(\mathcal F(G)\) is at least \(2\).

If \(S\subseteq E(G)\) is a minimum edge set hitting every copy of \(F\), then \(|S|\ge 2\), and \(G-S\) is \(F\)-free with
\[
e(G-S)=\operatorname{ex}(n,F)+1-|S|.
\]
By minimality of \(S\), each \(s\in S\) is essential: adding \(s\) back to \(G-S\) creates at least one copy of \(F\).

To finish SC4 from here one needs an exact local supersaturation theorem saying that adding at least two essential edges to an \(F\)-free graph whose edge count is only \(|S|-1\) below extremal creates more \(F\)-copies than the best one-edge addition to an extremal \(F\)-free graph. That is essentially SC4 itself, not a consequence of the definitions.

A weaker shortcut is false: it is not enough to say “for some edge \(e\), \(G-e\) has \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)\) edges and contains \(F\).” For example, with \(F=K_3\) and \(n=2N\), take \(K_{N,N}\), delete one cross-edge incident to \(x\), and add one inside-part edge \(xy\). This graph has \(\operatorname{ex}(n,K_3)\) edges and exactly \(N-1\) triangles, while \(c(n,K_3)=N\). So the desired proof must use the full “every edge deletion leaves \(F\)” hypothesis in a quantitative way.

**Subproof**

Let `H = G-e`. Since `G` has `ex(n,F)+1` edges and `e` is one edge,

\[
|E(H)| = |E(G)|-1 = ex(n,F).
\]

By hypothesis, `H = G-e` is `F`-free. By definition, `ex(n,F)` is the maximum number of edges in an `n`-vertex `F`-free graph. Therefore `H` is an extremal `n`-vertex `F`-free graph.

Also, `G` is obtained from `H` by adding the single edge `e`. Hence `G` is one of the graphs considered in the definition of `c(n,F)`. Since `c(n,F)` is the minimum number of copies of `F` over all such graphs, the number of copies of `F` in `G` is at least `c(n,F)`.

Thus SC3 holds.

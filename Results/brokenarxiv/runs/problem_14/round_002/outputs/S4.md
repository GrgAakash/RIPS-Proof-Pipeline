## subproof or failure

Let `G` be an `n`-vertex graph with `ex(n,F)+1` edges minimizing the number of copies of `F`; thus

`#F(G) = h_F(n,1)`.

By S3, there exists an edge `e ∈ E(G)` such that `H := G - e` is `F`-free and has `ex(n,F)` edges.

Since `H` is an `n`-vertex `F`-free graph with the maximum possible number `ex(n,F)` of edges, `H` is an extremal `n`-vertex `F`-free graph. Also, `G` is obtained from `H` by adding the single edge `e`.

Therefore `G` belongs to the class of graphs considered in the definition of `c(n,F)`: graphs obtained by adding one edge to an extremal `n`-vertex `F`-free graph. Since `c(n,F)` is the minimum number of copies of `F` over that class,

`#F(G) ≥ c(n,F)`.

Because `#F(G) = h_F(n,1)`, this gives

`h_F(n,1) ≥ c(n,F)`.

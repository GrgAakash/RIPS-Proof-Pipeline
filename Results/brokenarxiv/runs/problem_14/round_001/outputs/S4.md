2. Subproof or failure

Fix a graph \(F\) containing at least one cycle. Let \(n\) be sufficiently large so that the declared prerequisite subclaims S2 and S3 apply.

Let \(G\) be an \(n\)-vertex graph with \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)+1\) edges whose number of copies of \(F\) equals \(h_F(n,1)\). Such a graph exists because there are only finitely many \(n\)-vertex graphs.

By the declared prerequisite subclaims S2 and S3, there is an edge \(e\in E(G)\) such that \(G-e\) is an extremal \(n\)-vertex \(F\)-free graph. Since \(e\notin E(G-e)\), the graph \(G\) is obtained from the extremal \(F\)-free graph \(G-e\) by adding one edge.

By definition, \(c(n,F)\) is the minimum number of copies of \(F\) among all graphs obtained by adding one edge to an extremal \(n\)-vertex \(F\)-free graph. Since \(G\) is one such graph, the number of copies of \(F\) in \(G\) is at least \(c(n,F)\). But the number of copies of \(F\) in \(G\) is \(h_F(n,1)\). Therefore
\[
h_F(n,1)\ge c(n,F).
\]

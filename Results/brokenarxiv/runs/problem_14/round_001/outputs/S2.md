2. Subproof or failure

Let \(N_F(X)\) denote the number of copies of \(F\) in a graph \(X\), and write \(m=\operatorname{ex}(n,F)\).

For \(n\ge |V(F)|\), the complete graph \(K_n\) contains a copy of \(F\). Hence an extremal \(F\)-free \(n\)-vertex graph is not complete, so one may add a missing edge to it. By the definition of \(c(n,F)\), there is such a graph \(T+xy\), where \(T\) is extremal \(F\)-free and \(xy\notin E(T)\), with
\[
N_F(T+xy)=c(n,F).
\]
The graph \(T+xy\) has \(m+1\) edges. Since \(G\) minimizes \(N_F\) among all \(n\)-vertex graphs with \(m+1\) edges,
\[
N_F(G)\le c(n,F).
\]

Thus SC2 would follow from the following missing gap statement: every \(n\)-vertex graph with \(m+1\) edges and no edge contained in all its \(F\)-copies has strictly more than \(c(n,F)\) copies of \(F\).

I cannot prove that gap from definitions only. The natural deletion route fails: if \(e\in E(G)\) is deleted, then \(G-e\) has exactly \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)\) edges, but the definition of \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)\) does not imply that every graph with \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)\) edges is \(F\)-free. It only says no \(F\)-free graph has more than that many edges. Therefore \(G-e\) may still contain \(F\) without contradiction.

SUBPROBLEM UNSOLVED: missing a nontrivial edge-cover or supersaturation gap forcing some deletion \(G-e\) to be \(F\)-free.

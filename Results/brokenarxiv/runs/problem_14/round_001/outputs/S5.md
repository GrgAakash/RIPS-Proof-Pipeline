what must be proved:
For every fixed graph \(F\) containing at least one cycle, there is an integer \(n_0(F)\) such that for all integers \(n\ge n_0(F)\),
\[
h_F(n,1)=c(n,F).
\]

declared prerequisite subclaims:
A lower-bound prerequisite is needed: for every fixed cyclic graph \(F\), there is an integer \(N_{\mathrm{LB}}(F)\) such that for all \(n\ge N_{\mathrm{LB}}(F)\),
\[
h_F(n,1)\ge c(n,F).
\]
This prerequisite is where the hypothesis “\(F\) contains a cycle” is used.

2. Subproof or failure

Fix a graph \(F\) containing at least one cycle.

First prove the upper bound directly from the definitions. Let \(n\) be any positive integer for which the quantities are defined. Choose an extremal \(n\)-vertex \(F\)-free graph \(G\), so \(e(G)=\operatorname{ex}(n,F)\). Add one edge to \(G\), obtaining an \(n\)-vertex graph \(G+e\) with \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)+1\) edges. By definition, \(h_F(n,1)\) is the minimum number of copies of \(F\) over all \(n\)-vertex graphs with exactly \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)+1\) edges. Therefore
\[
h_F(n,1)\le \#F(G+e).
\]
Taking the minimum over all graphs obtained by adding one edge to an extremal \(n\)-vertex \(F\)-free graph gives
\[
h_F(n,1)\le c(n,F).
\]

By the declared lower-bound prerequisite, since \(F\) contains a cycle, there exists \(N_{\mathrm{LB}}(F)\) such that for all \(n\ge N_{\mathrm{LB}}(F)\),
\[
h_F(n,1)\ge c(n,F).
\]

Set
\[
n_0(F)=N_{\mathrm{LB}}(F).
\]
Then for every \(n\ge n_0(F)\), the two inequalities give
\[
c(n,F)\le h_F(n,1)\le c(n,F),
\]
and hence
\[
h_F(n,1)=c(n,F).
\]

The cyclic hypothesis on \(F\) is not used in the direct upper bound or in the final comparison step. It is used only through the lower-bound prerequisite, which is the structural step.

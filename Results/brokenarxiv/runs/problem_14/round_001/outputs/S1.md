2. Subproof or failure

Let \(T\) be an extremal \(n\)-vertex \(F\)-free graph, so by definition
\[
|E(T)|=\operatorname{ex}(n,F).
\]
Let \(e\) be a non-edge of \(T\), and let
\[
G=T+e
\]
be the graph obtained by adding this one edge.

By the ordinary meaning of adding an edge to a simple graph, since \(e\notin E(T)\), the edge set of \(G\) is
\[
E(G)=E(T)\cup\{e\},
\]
with \(e\notin E(T)\). Hence
\[
|E(G)|=|E(T)|+1=\operatorname{ex}(n,F)+1.
\]
Therefore every graph considered in the definition of \(c(n,F)\) is an admissible graph in the minimization defining \(h_F(n,1)\), because \(h_F(n,1)\) minimizes over all \(n\)-vertex graphs with exactly \(\operatorname{ex}(n,F)+1\) edges.

Since \(h_F(n,1)\) is the minimum number of copies of \(F\) over all admissible graphs, and the graphs used to define \(c(n,F)\) form a subclass of those admissible graphs, the minimum over the larger class is at most the minimum over that subclass. Thus
\[
h_F(n,1)\le c(n,F).
\]
